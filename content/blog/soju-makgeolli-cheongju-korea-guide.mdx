Soju is Korea's best-selling spirit, makgeolli is a milky rice wine, and cheongju is a clear, refined rice wine, each with its own foods and occasions. Pour for others rather than yourself, accept a drink with two hands from elders, and pace yourself.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Soju:** clear, around 13–17% alcohol, the default with BBQ and stews
- **Makgeolli:** cloudy rice wine, around 6–8%, classic with pancakes on rainy days
- **Somaek:** soju mixed into beer, a party favorite
- **Avoid:** pouring your own drink in formal company

</KeyTakeaways>

## Korean drinks compared

| Drink | Korean | Alcohol (approx.) | Taste | Best with |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Soju** | 소주 | 13–17% | Clean, slightly sweet | Korean BBQ, stews, raw fish |
| **Fruit soju** | 과일소주 | Lower | Sweet, fruity | Easy drinking, first-timers |
| **Makgeolli** | 막걸리 | 6–8% | Milky, sweet, fizzy | Jeon, pajeon, after hiking |
| **Cheongju** | 청주 | 13–16% | Clear, smooth, rice aroma | Holiday meals, ancestral rites |
| **Somaek** | 소맥 | Mixed | Soju plus beer | Group dinners, celebrations |

Prices are low by international standards: a bottle of soju costs a couple of thousand won in convenience stores and a few times more in restaurants.

## Drinking etiquette

At a Korean table, people pour for each other. Hold the bottle with two hands, or one hand with the other supporting your arm, when pouring for someone older. Receive a drink with two hands, and turn your head slightly away from elders when you drink. Keep an eye on others' glasses and refill them when empty.

<Callout type="tip" title="Saying no politely">
You don't have to drink. Leave your glass a little full so no one refills it, or say 저는 술을 잘 못 마셔요 (I can't drink much). A soft drink in your glass is fine for toasts.
</Callout>

## Drinking food and rounds

In Korea, drinking almost always comes with food. Anju (안주), food eaten with alcohol, ranges from Korean BBQ and fried chicken to pancakes, stews and dried squid. Bars and pubs expect you to order at least one anju dish.

A night out often moves through rounds (차): dinner with soju (1차), then a pub for beer and chicken (2차), and sometimes noraebang (3차). You can leave after any round; saying you have an early start tomorrow is a polite exit.

- **Soju pairings:** samgyeopsal, stews, raw fish, tteokbokki
- **Makgeolli pairings:** pajeon, bindaetteok, tofu kimchi
- **Beer pairings:** fried chicken, dried snacks, fruit platters

## Toasting and the first glass

Soju is traditionally served in small shot glasses, and bottles are shared rather than ordered per person. Before opening a fresh bottle, some people swirl it or flick the neck, a playful habit rather than a rule. Fruit soju and lower-alcohol options suit first-timers well. The first glass is often drunk together after a toast. 건배 (*geonbae*, cheers) is the standard toast, and 원샷 (one shot) means drinking it all at once, though nobody should pressure you to.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How strong is soju?**
Most standard soju is around 13–17% alcohol, weaker than vodka but much stronger than beer or wine.

**What is somaek?**
Soju mixed into beer, usually in a ratio the pourer likes. It's popular at group dinners.

**Can I buy soju at convenience stores?**
Yes, cheaply, at any convenience store. You must be of legal drinking age, and ID may be checked.

**Do I have to drink at Korean work dinners?**
No. It's common to decline politely or drink slowly. Keeping your glass partly full stops people from refilling it.
