Drink names in Korean cafes are borrowed from English: 아메리카노, 라떼, 카푸치노. Say them the Korean way and you're understood. The words that trip people up are the ones around the drink, and ten of them cover a whole visit.

## At the counter

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 따뜻한 | ttatteutan | hot |
| 사이즈 | saijeu | size |
| 큰 / 작은 | keun / jageun | large / small |
| 포장 | pojang | to go, takeout |
| 머그컵 | meogeukeop | mug |

Iced is **아이스**, which sounds like English. Hot is usually **따뜻한**, and you'll be asked which one if you don't say it. **핫** is also understood.

**사이즈는요?** ("what size?") is the second question you'll get. You don't need the cafe's size names: **큰 사이즈로 주세요** (large) or **작은 사이즈로 주세요** (small) works everywhere.

**포장** answers the question every barista asks, **드시고 가세요?** ("for here?"). Staying usually means a **머그컵**, a real mug or glass, since Korea has restricted disposable cups for dine-in since 2018.

## At your table

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 콘센트 | konsenteu | power outlet |
| 와이파이 | waipai | wifi |
| 화장실 | hwajangsil | restroom |

**콘센트** is the least guessable word here, and the only word Koreans use for a wall socket. Ask **콘센트 자리 있어요?** ("is there a seat with an outlet?") and you'll be pointed toward a wall.

**화장실** literally means "makeup room". It's simply the standard word. In many buildings the restroom is shared and locked, so the next question is **비밀번호 있어요?** ("is there a code?").

## On the way out

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 반납대 | bannapdae | cup return station |
| 또 오세요 | tto oseyo | come again |

Most Korean cafes expect you to take your cup to the **반납대**, usually near the register.

## What staff will say to you

| You'll hear | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 드시고 가세요? | For here? |
| 사이즈는요? | What size? |
| 저쪽에 있어요. | It's over there. |
| 또 오세요! | Come again! |

You don't need to reply to **또 오세요**. A nod or **감사합니다** is plenty.

All of these are polite speech. Staff add the honorific **-시-** (드시고), and you answer with plain **-요** forms; you never need casual speech (반말) at the counter.

## Three sentences from the list

- **따뜻한 라떼 하나 주세요.** — One hot latte, please.
- **콘센트 자리 있어요?** — Is there a seat with an outlet?
- **컵은 어디에 반납해요?** — Where do I return the cup?
