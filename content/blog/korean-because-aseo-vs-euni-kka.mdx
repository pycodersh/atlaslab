English has one everyday "because." Korean has two that you'll hear all the time, **-아/어서** and **-(으)니까**, and textbooks often translate both as "because, so." They are not interchangeable, though, and the difference shows up in the most ordinary sentences: an apology, a suggestion, a reason to leave early.

The short version: **-아서 explains, -(으)니까 argues.**

## How to build them

**-아/어서** follows the same vowel rule as [아요/어요](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-verb-conjugation-ayo-eoyo): take the polite form, drop 요, add 서.

| Verb | -아/어서 | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 늦다 (to be late) | 늦어서 | because (I'm) late |
| 바쁘다 (to be busy) | 바빠서 | because (I'm) busy |
| 하다 (to do) | 해서 | because (I) do |
| 오다 (to come) | 와서 | because (it) comes |

**-(으)니까** depends on the last sound of the stem: **-니까** after a vowel, **-으니까** after a consonant. A final ㄹ drops before 니까.

| Verb | -(으)니까 | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 바쁘다 | 바쁘니까 | since (I'm) busy |
| 없다 | 없으니까 | since there isn't |
| 춥다 | 추우니까 | since it's cold (ㅂ irregular) |
| 멀다 | 머니까 | since it's far (ㄹ drops) |

---

## Rule 1: requests and suggestions take -(으)니까

This is the big one. If the second half is a **request, an order, a suggestion or "let's"**, only -(으)니까 works.

| ✗ | ✓ | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 비가 와서 우산 가져가세요. | 비가 **오니까** 우산 가져가세요. | It's raining, so take an umbrella. |
| 배고파서 밥 먹을까요? | 배고**프니까** 밥 먹을까요? | I'm hungry, so shall we eat? |
| 멀어서 택시 타요. (let's) | **머니까** 택시 타요. | It's far, so let's take a taxi. |

With -아서, the same first halves are fine when the second half is a plain statement: **비가 와서 집에 있었어요** ("It rained, so I stayed home").

## Rule 2: no past tense before -아서

-아서 never takes 았/었, even when the cause is in the past. The final verb carries the tense.

- ✗ 늦었어서 죄송해요 → ✓ **늦어서 죄송해요** (Sorry I was late.)
- ✗ 비가 왔어서 → ✓ **비가 와서** 못 갔어요 (It rained, so I couldn't go.)

-(으)니까 is happy to take tense: **어제 많이 먹었으니까 오늘은 조금만 먹을게요** ("I ate a lot yesterday, so I'll eat light today").

<Callout type="tip" title="Apologies and thanks: always -아서">

Fixed politeness phrases use -아/어서: **늦어서 죄송해요**, **도와주셔서 감사합니다**, **기다리게 해서 미안해요**. Putting -(으)니까 there turns the reason into a justification, a bit like "I was late, *so*…", which is the opposite of an apology.

</Callout>

## Rule 3: -(으)니까 sounds like *your* reasoning

Because -(으)니까 presents the reason as the speaker's own judgment, it can sound pointed in plain statements, especially in answers:

- 왜 안 왔어요? — Why didn't you come?
- **바빠서요.** — I was busy. (neutral, explanatory)
- **바쁘니까요.** — Because I was busy. (can sound like "obviously")

When you're simply explaining, -아서 is the safer default.

## Rule 4: -(으)니까 can mean "when I did…"

With a past action, -(으)니까 can also mean "when I did X, I found Y":

- **가 보니까 문이 닫혀 있었어요.** — When I went, the door was closed.
- **먹어 보니까 맛있었어요.** — When I tried it, it was good.

This combines with [-아/어 보다](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-have-you-ever-eo-boda) all the time.

## Common mistakes

**-아서 before a request.** 피곤해서 쉬세요 should be 피곤하니까 쉬세요 ("You're tired, so rest").

**Past tense before -아서.** 먹었어서 → 먹어서.

**-(으)니까 in apologies.** 늦었으니까 죄송해요 → 늦어서 죄송해요.

**Forgetting the ㄹ drop.** 멀으니까 → 머니까, 살으니까 → 사니까.

## 2-minute recap

- **-아/어서** = because, as a natural cause. Also the form for **apologies and thanks**.
- **-(으)니까** = because, as your reason. **Required before requests, suggestions and "let's."**
- No 았/었 before -아서: **늦어서**, not 늦었어서.
- -(으)니까 after a vowel is **-니까**, after a consonant **-으니까**; ㄹ drops: **머니까**.
- With a past action, -(으)니까 can mean "when I did it, I found…": **가 보니까**.
