Korean has two everyday ways to say "because": **-아/어서** and **-(으)니까**. Textbooks often translate both as "because, so", but they don't swap. The difference shows up in the most ordinary sentences: an apology, a suggestion, a reason to leave early.

The short version: **-아서 explains a cause. -(으)니까 gives your reason, usually for what comes next.**

## How to build them

**-아/어서** follows the same vowel rule as [아요/어요](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-verb-conjugation-ayo-eoyo): take the polite form, drop 요, add 서. **-(으)니까** is **-니까** after a vowel and **-으니까** after a consonant; a final ㄹ drops.

| Verb | -아/어서 | -(으)니까 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 늦다 (be late) | 늦어서 | 늦으니까 | regular |
| 바쁘다 (be busy) | 바빠서 | 바쁘니까 | ㅡ drops before 아 |
| 피곤하다 (be tired) | 피곤해서 | 피곤하니까 | 하다 → 해서 |
| 춥다 (be cold) | 추워서 | 추우니까 | ㅂ irregular |
| 멀다 (be far) | 멀어서 | 머니까 | ㄹ drops before 니까 |
| 없다 (not have) | 없어서 | 없으니까 | consonant → 으니까 |

## Rule 1: requests and suggestions take -(으)니까

If the second half is a **request, an order, a suggestion or "let's"**, only -(으)니까 works.

| ✗ | ✓ | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 비가 와서 우산 가져가세요. | 비가 **오니까** 우산 가져가세요. | It's raining, so take an umbrella. |
| 배고파서 밥 먹을까요? | 배고**프니까** 밥 먹을까요? | I'm hungry, so shall we eat? |
| 멀어서 택시 타자. | **머니까** 택시 타자. | It's far, so let's take a taxi. |
| 피곤해서 쉬세요. | 피곤**하니까** 쉬세요. | You're tired, so get some rest. |

The same first halves are fine with -아서 when the second half just states a fact: **비가 와서 집에 있었어요** (*biga waseo jibe isseosseoyo*, "it rained, so I stayed home").

## Rule 2: no past tense before -아서

-아서 never takes 았/었, even for a past cause. The final verb carries the tense.

| ✗ | ✓ | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 늦었어서 죄송해요. | **늦어서** 죄송해요. | Sorry I was late. |
| 비가 왔어서 못 갔어요. | 비가 **와서** 못 갔어요. | It rained, so I couldn't go. |

-(으)니까 does take tense: **어제 많이 먹었으니까 오늘은 조금만 먹을게요** (*eoje mani meogeosseunikka oneureun jogeumman meogeulgeyo*, "I ate a lot yesterday, so I'll eat light today").

<Callout type="tip" title="Apologies and thanks: always -아서">

Fixed politeness phrases use -아/어서: **늦어서 죄송해요**, **도와주셔서 감사합니다**, **기다리게 해서 미안해요**. With -(으)니까 the reason turns into a justification, roughly "I was late, *so*…", which is the opposite of an apology.

</Callout>

## Rule 3: -(으)니까 sounds like your own reasoning

-(으)니까 presents the reason as the speaker's judgment, so in a plain answer it can sound pointed.

| Answer to 왜 안 왔어요? | Romanization | How it sounds |
|---|---|---|
| 바빠서요. | bappaseoyo | "I was busy." Neutral, explaining. |
| 바빴으니까요. | bappasseunikkayo | "Because I was busy, obviously." Can sound defensive. |

When you're simply explaining, -아서 is the safer default.

## Rule 4: -(으)니까 can mean "when I did…, I found…"

With a past action, -(으)니까 also means you discovered something:

- **가 보니까 문이 닫혀 있었어요.** (*ga bonikka muni dachyeo isseosseoyo*) When I went, the door was closed.
- **먹어 보니까 맛있었어요.** (*meogeo bonikka masisseosseoyo*) When I tried it, it was good.

This pairs with [-아/어 보다](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-have-you-ever-eo-boda) all the time.

<Callout type="info" title="-아서 doesn't always mean 'because'">

-아/어서 also links two actions in order, when the second happens on the back of the first: **가서 먹어요** (*gaseo meogeoyo*, "go there and eat"), **앉아서 기다려 주세요** (*anjaseo gidaryeo juseyo*, "please sit and wait"), **친구를 만나서 영화를 봤어요** ("I met a friend and we saw a movie"). Here a request after -아서 is fine, because there's no "because" in it. For two separate actions, Korean uses -고 instead: the [-고 vs -(으)면서 guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-connecting-go-vs-myeonseo) compares them.

</Callout>

## Common mistakes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 피곤해서 쉬세요. | 피곤하니까 쉬세요. | A request needs -(으)니까. |
| 먹었어서 | 먹어서 | No past tense before -아서. |
| 늦었으니까 죄송해요. | 늦어서 죄송해요. | Apologies use -아서. |
| 멀으니까, 살으니까 | 머니까, 사니까 | ㄹ drops before 니까. |
