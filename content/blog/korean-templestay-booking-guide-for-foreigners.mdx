A templestay lets you spend a day or a night at a Korean Buddhist temple, joining monks for meditation, ceremonies and simple vegetarian meals. Choose a short program near Seoul or an overnight stay in the mountains, book through the official Templestay website, and follow the temple's quiet routine.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Types:** half-day programs, or one to two nights at a temple
- **Price:** roughly 50,000–100,000 KRW per night including meals, varying by temple
- **Booking:** the official Templestay site lists programs with English support
- **Avoid:** skipping the early wake-up; the dawn ceremony is the heart of the stay

</KeyTakeaways>

## Program types

| Program | Length | What you do | Best for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Day program** | 2–4 hours | Temple tour, tea, meditation, lantern or bead making | A taste without staying overnight |
| **Rest-type stay** | 1–2 nights | Free time with optional activities | Relaxing |
| **Experience-type stay** | 1–2 nights | Full schedule with ceremonies, bows and meals | The complete experience |

City temples like Bongeunsa and Jogyesa in Seoul offer short programs, while mountain temples like Haeinsa, Beopjusa and Golgulsa offer overnight stays.

## A typical overnight schedule

| Time | Activity |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Afternoon** | Check in, receive temple clothes, orientation |
| **Evening** | Dinner, evening ceremony (예불), tea with a monk |
| **~21:00** | Lights out |
| **~03:30–04:00** | Wake up for the dawn ceremony |
| **Morning** | 108 bows or meditation, breakfast (sometimes a formal monastic meal), walking meditation |
| **Late morning** | Clean up and check out |

<Callout type="info" title="Temple meals">
Meals are vegetarian, without garlic or onion in many temples. During a formal monastic meal (발우공양), you eat in silence and finish everything in your bowl.
</Callout>

## Booking, step by step

1. **Search the official Templestay website** by region, date and program type.
2. **Check the language** and whether the program runs in English.
3. **Book and pay online**, then note the check-in time and directions.
4. **Arrive on time**; programs start together and late arrivals miss the orientation.

## Etiquette

| Rule | How |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Clothing** | Wear the temple clothes provided; avoid revealing outfits on arrival |
| **Greeting** | Put your palms together and bow slightly (합장) |
| **Silence** | Keep voices low, especially in halls and at meals |
| **Shoes** | Remove them before entering halls |
| **Photos** | Ask before photographing monks or inside halls |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I have to be Buddhist to do a templestay?**
No. Templestays welcome people of all backgrounds, and participation in ceremonies is respectful rather than religious.

**Is a templestay in English?**
Many temples run programs with English-speaking staff or volunteers. Check the language options when booking.

**How hard are the 108 bows?**
They're tiring but done at your own pace. You can rest if needed.

**What should I bring?**
Toiletries, a towel, a warm layer, comfortable socks and an open mind. Temple clothes are provided.
