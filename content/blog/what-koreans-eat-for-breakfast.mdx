A traditional Korean breakfast is rice, a hot soup and a few side dishes, with no separate "breakfast foods." Today many Koreans eat something quicker: street toast, gimbap, porridge or just coffee on the way to work.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Traditional:** rice, soup or stew, kimchi and banchan
- **On the go:** egg toast, triangle gimbap, porridge, or an iced americano
- **Budget:** street toast or gimbap for about 3,000–6,000 KRW
- **Avoid:** expecting pancakes or cereal at a local restaurant; look for soup shops instead

</KeyTakeaways>

## The traditional Korean breakfast

In Korean, the word for a meal, 밥 (bap), also means cooked rice, and breakfast follows the same pattern as lunch and dinner. A home breakfast is often leftovers from the night before, freshened with a new soup.

| Part | Typical choices | Why it's there |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rice (밥)** | White rice, or rice mixed with grains | The base of every meal |
| **Soup (국)** | Bean sprout soup, seaweed soup, doenjang soup | Warm, light and easy to eat |
| **Kimchi** | Cabbage kimchi or kkakdugi | On the table at every meal |
| **Banchan** | Rolled omelet, namul, braised tofu or potatoes | Small sides made ahead |
| **Protein** | Grilled fish, fried egg, spam slices | Quick to cook in the morning |

Mild, clear soups are common in the morning. Bean sprout soup and gukbap are also classic hangover breakfasts.

## What Koreans eat on the go

Busy commuters rarely sit down for rice and soup on weekdays. These are the most common quick breakfasts.

- **Street egg toast (길거리 토스트):** buttered bread with an egg-and-cabbage omelet, ham, cheese and a little sugar and ketchup. Chains such as Isaac Toast sell it near subway stations.
- **Gimbap:** a seaweed rice roll from a gimbap shop, or a triangle gimbap from a convenience store
- **Juk (죽):** rice porridge from porridge chains, gentle on the stomach
- **Bakery bread and coffee:** increasingly common, especially among young office workers
- **Sweet potatoes and eggs:** steamed goguma or roasted eggs (구운 계란) from the convenience store

<Callout type="tip" title="Early-morning soup shops">
Gukbap, haejangguk and seolleongtang restaurants often open early, and some stay open around the clock. They're the most reliable sit-down breakfast for travelers outside hotels.
</Callout>

## Ordering breakfast as a traveler

Street toast stalls and gimbap shops are fast and friendly to first-timers. Most have picture menus or kiosks.

<Dialogue title="At a toast stall">
  <Line me who="You" ko="햄치즈 토스트 하나 주세요." rom="Haem chijeu toseuteu hana juseyo." en="One ham and cheese toast, please." />
  <Line who="Staff" ko="드시고 가세요, 포장이세요?" rom="Deusigo gaseyo, pojang-iseyo?" en="For here or to go?" />
  <Line me who="You" ko="포장이요. 설탕은 조금만 넣어 주세요." rom="Pojang-iyo. Seoltang-eun jogeumman neoeo juseyo." en="To go. Just a little sugar, please." />
</Dialogue>

Korean street toast is sweeter than an American egg sandwich. If you prefer savory, ask for less sugar (설탕 조금만) or no sugar (설탕 빼 주세요).

## Korean breakfast at home

A Korean breakfast is easier than it looks if you cook rice ahead and keep a few banchan in the fridge. These recipes each take 15 minutes or less:

- [Egg rice bowl (계란밥)](/kpantry/en/recipes/egg-rice-bowl): hot rice, a fried egg, soy sauce and sesame oil
- [Gyeran-mari](/kpantry/en/recipes/gyeran-mari): a rolled omelet that keeps well for lunch boxes
- [Bean sprout soup](/kpantry/en/recipes/kongnamul-guk): a light, clear soup ready in about 15 minutes
- [Kimchi fried rice](/kpantry/en/recipes/kimchi-fried-rice): the best use for last night's rice

For a full table, add kimchi and one or two banchan made at the weekend.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a typical Korean breakfast?**
Traditionally rice, a soup, kimchi and a few side dishes. Many people now eat toast, gimbap or porridge on weekdays.

**Do Koreans eat kimchi for breakfast?**
Yes. Kimchi is served at every meal, including breakfast.

**What is Korean street toast?**
A grilled sandwich with an egg-and-cabbage omelet, ham, cheese, a sprinkle of sugar and ketchup, sold at stalls and chains.

**Where can I eat breakfast in Seoul without a hotel?**
Toast stalls, gimbap shops, porridge chains and early-opening soup restaurants, plus convenience stores open 24 hours.
