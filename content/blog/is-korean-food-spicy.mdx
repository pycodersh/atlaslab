Korean food has a spicy reputation, but plenty of classic dishes contain no chili at all. Use the heat chart below to order with confidence, and ask for 덜 맵게 (less spicy) whenever a dish can be adjusted.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Not everything is spicy:** bulgogi, galbi, samgyetang, seolleongtang and japchae have no chili heat
- **Where heat comes from:** gochugaru (chili flakes), gochujang (chili paste) and fresh chilies
- **Ask for mild:** 덜 맵게 해 주세요 (*deol maepge hae juseyo*), or 순한맛 where levels are offered
- **Avoid:** testing yourself with "extra spicy" franchise dishes on day one

</KeyTakeaways>

## Heat levels of popular dishes

| Heat | Dishes | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **None** | Bulgogi, galbi, samgyetang, seolleongtang, japchae, gimbap, mandu, gyeran-jjim | Safe for anyone avoiding spice |
| **Mild** | Doenjang jjigae, bibimbap (add gochujang to taste), soy-garlic chicken | Gentle warmth at most |
| **Medium** | Kimchi jjigae, tteokbokki, dakgalbi, yangnyeom chicken, kimchi | Noticeable heat, balanced by sweetness |
| **Hot** | Spicy sundubu, jjamppong, yukgaejang, spicy pork bulgogi | Properly spicy |
| **Very hot** | Buldak noodles, "extra spicy" franchise tteokbokki, spicy jjim | For confirmed chili lovers |

Restaurants differ a lot, so the same dish can be mild in one place and fiery in the next. When in doubt, ask.

## Where the heat comes from

Most Korean spice comes from three ingredients. **Gochugaru** (chili flakes) gives kimchi and stews their red color and a fruity, building heat. **Gochujang** (chili paste) adds heat with sweetness and depth, and is the base of tteokbokki and bibimbap sauce. **Fresh green and red chilies** are sliced into stews or eaten raw on the side with ssamjang, and these can be the hottest surprise on the table.

<Callout type="tip" title="How to cool down">
Rice, steamed egg (gyeran-jjim), cheese toppings, pickled radish and sweet drinks like sikhye calm the heat better than water. Many spicy dishes come with a mild side for exactly this reason.
</Callout>

## Why Korean food got spicy

Chili peppers arrived in Korea only around the late 16th or 17th century, but they spread quickly into kimchi, pastes and stews. Before that, Korean food relied on ginger, garlic, black pepper and fermented sauces for flavor. Today, gochugaru and gochujang are central to Korean cooking, and many Koreans enjoy a level of heat that surprises visitors.

Spicy food is also tied to mood. Koreans often say they crave spicy food when stressed (스트레스 받을 때 매운 거), and "extra spicy" challenges are a popular trend on social media. At the same time, everyday home cooking includes plenty of mild soups, steamed dishes and seasoned vegetables.

- **Mild by tradition:** royal court cuisine and temple food use little or no chili
- **Regional differences:** Jeolla cooking is known for bold, rich flavors; Gangwon food is often milder
- **Kids' menus:** many restaurants offer non-spicy versions for children

## Ordering mild

Many dishes can be made milder if you ask before ordering. Stews and tteokbokki are easy to adjust; marinated meats and kimchi aren't. For bibimbap, ask for the chili paste on the side and add it yourself.

<Dialogue title="Ordering at a restaurant">
  <Line me who="You" ko="이거 많이 매워요?" rom="Igeo mani maewoyo?" en="Is this very spicy?" />
  <Line who="Staff" ko="조금 매워요." rom="Jogeum maewoyo." en="It's a little spicy." />
  <Line me who="You" ko="덜 맵게 해 주실 수 있어요?" rom="Deol maepge hae jusil su isseoyo?" en="Could you make it less spicy?" />
  <Line who="Staff" ko="네, 순한맛으로 해 드릴게요." rom="Ne, sunhanmasseuro hae deurilgeyo." en="Sure, I'll make it mild." />
</Dialogue>

Watch for the word 매운 (*maeun*, spicy) on menus, and 불 (*bul*, fire) in dish names like buldak; both are clear warnings.

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Start with non-spicy classics like bulgogi and samgyetang
- Ask 매워요? (is it spicy?) before ordering
- Keep rice and mild sides nearby
- Try a small bite of kimchi before a big one

</Do>
<Dont>

- Order "extra spicy" on your first try
- Eat the raw green chilies on the side without testing them
- Assume red color always means very hot
- Drink lots of water to cool down; rice works better

</Dont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is all Korean food spicy?**
No. Many popular dishes, like bulgogi, galbi, japchae and samgyetang, have no chili heat at all.

**What should I order if I can't eat spicy food?**
Try bulgogi, galbi, samgyetang, seolleongtang, japchae, gimbap or mandu. Ask for bibimbap with the chili paste on the side.

**How do I say "not spicy" in Korean?**
안 맵게 해 주세요 (*an maepge hae juseyo*) for no spice, or 덜 맵게 (*deol maepge*) for less spicy.

**Is kimchi spicy?**
Most cabbage kimchi is moderately spicy. White kimchi (백김치) and water kimchi (물김치) contain no chili.
