The secret to a first trip to Seoul is grouping sights by area: the historic north first, the creative west next, and south of the Han River only when you're ready for Gangnam. Start with the 3-day core below, then add days 4 and 5 if you have them.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **3 days:** palaces and Bukchon, Gwangjang Market, Hongdae, N Seoul Tower, a Han River evening
- **4 days:** add Seongsu-dong and Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP) at night
- **5 days:** add Gangnam, Starfield Library and a Yeouido sunset picnic
- **Where to stay:** Myeongdong or Hongdae for first-timers
- **Getting around:** a transit card and Naver Map or KakaoMap; cross the Han River at most once a day

</KeyTakeaways>

Seoul covers about six times the area of Paris, so bouncing between the palaces and Gangnam twice in a day can cost hours on the subway. Pick your [transit card](/blog/en/k-patto/climate-card-vs-t-money-seoul) on day 1 and set up a [map app that works in Korea](/blog/en/k-patto/google-maps-alternatives-korea) before you land.

## Choosing your trip length

| Length | Best for | What you'll cover |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **3 days** | Stopovers and long weekends; a fast pace, often 20,000+ steps a day | Gyeongbokgung, Bukchon, Gwangjang, Hongdae, N Seoul Tower, Myeongdong |
| **4 days** | Most first-timers; balanced and photo-friendly | The 3-day core plus Seongsu-dong and DDP at night |
| **5 days** | K-culture fans and slower travelers | All of the above plus Gangnam, COEX and Yeouido |

## The 3-day core itinerary

### Day 1: Palaces, hanok alleys and market food

- **Morning (09:30): Gyeongbokgung Palace.** Arrive before 10:00 for the Royal Guard changing ceremony at Gwanghwamun Gate. Wearing a rented *hanbok* gets you free entry to the palaces.
- **Lunch (12:30): ginseng chicken soup or knife-cut noodles.** Tosokchon, a short walk west of the palace, is famous for *samgyetang*; expect a queue.
- **Afternoon (14:00): Bukchon Hanok Village and Insadong.** Walk the hanok alleys of this Joseon-era neighborhood, then head down to Insadong for tea houses and craft shops. Keep your voice down in Bukchon: people live in these houses.
- **Evening (18:30): Gwangjang Market.** Mung bean pancakes (*bindaetteok*), twisted doughnuts (*kkwabaegi*) and mini seaweed rolls (*mayak gimbap*). See the [Gwangjang Market guide](/blog/en/k-patto/gwangjang-market-guide) for prices and seating.

### Day 2: Hongdae, Namsan and Myeongdong

- **Morning (10:30): Yeonnam-dong and Gyeongui Line Forest Park.** A converted railway line lined with bakeries and coffee shops.
- **Afternoon (13:30): Hongdae.** Street fashion, thrift shops and dance crews busking on the main walking street.
- **Late afternoon (17:00): Namsan cable car to N Seoul Tower.** Go up just before sunset and watch the city light up.
- **Dinner (19:30): Myeongdong.** Korean BBQ, then the evening street food stalls. The [Myeongdong street food guide](/blog/en/k-patto/myeongdong-street-food-guide) has prices and a budget plan.

### Day 3: Museums, Hannam-dong and the Han River

- **Morning (10:30): National Museum of Korea or Leeum Museum of Art.** Celadon and royal crowns, or contemporary art in Hannam-dong.
- **Lunch (13:00): Itaewon and Hannam-dong.** Designer fragrance shops, fashion showrooms and international food.
- **Afternoon (15:30): Ikseon-dong Hanok Alley.** 1920s hanok turned into dessert cafes and craft beer bars.
- **Evening (19:00): Banpo Hangang Park.** Picnic by the river and watch the Moonlight Rainbow Fountain on Banpo Bridge, which runs from spring to autumn. Try [convenience store ramyeon by the river](/blog/en/k-patto/han-river-parks-seoul-guide).

<Callout type="tip" title="Tapped in on the wrong platform?">

Some older stations have separate gates for each direction. If you tap in on the wrong side, tap out and back in on the correct side within 15 minutes at the same station and line, and it counts as a free transfer on a transit card. Single-journey tickets don't qualify.

</Callout>

## Adding days 4 and 5

### Day 4: Seongsu-dong and Dongdaemun at night

- **Morning to afternoon: Seongsu-dong (Line 2).** Former shoe workshops and red-brick factories now hold pop-up stores, flagship shops such as Dior Seongsu, and warehouse cafes like Daelim Changgo. The [Seongsu guide](/blog/en/k-patto/seongsu-dong-pop-up-cafe-guide) shows how to skip the lines.
- **Evening: Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP).** Zaha Hadid's curving building is at its best lit up at night, and the nearby shopping malls stay open late.

### Day 5: Gangnam and a Yeouido sunset

- **Morning: Bongeunsa Temple,** a quiet Buddhist temple across the street from Gangnam's towers.
- **Midday: Starfield COEX Mall,** home to the 13-meter bookshelves of the Starfield Library.
- **Afternoon: Apgujeong Rodeo and Dosan Park** for Korean fashion concept stores, including Gentle Monster's Haus Dosan and the Tamburins flagship.
- **Evening: Yeouido Hangang Park** for a sunset picnic; order fried chicken to the riverside.

## Korean for getting around

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **실례지만, 명동역 어느 쪽이에요?** | Sillyejiman, Myeongdongyeok eoneu jjogieyo? | Excuse me, which way is Myeongdong Station? |
| **몇 번 출구예요?** | Myeot beon chulguyeyo? | Which exit number is it? |
| **여기서 걸어갈 수 있어요?** | Yeogiseo georeogal su isseoyo? | Can I walk there from here? |

Locals usually answer with directions like **저쪽 사거리에서 오른쪽으로 가세요** (*jeojjok sageorieseo oreunjjogeuro gaseyo*), "turn right at that intersection".

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which neighborhood should I stay in for my first visit?**
Myeongdong or Hongdae. Myeongdong is on Line 4, a short walk from Line 2 at Euljiro 1-ga, with airport buses and easy access to the palaces and markets. Hongdae has nightlife, late-night food and the AREX airport train.

**Is 3 days enough for Seoul?**
Three days covers the essentials: Gyeongbokgung, Bukchon, Hongdae, Myeongdong and N Seoul Tower. Four or five days lets you add Seongsu and Gangnam at a comfortable pace.

**How do I pay for the subway and buses?**
Use a transit card. A T-money card from a convenience store is topped up with cash; a Climate Card can be bought at station machines, many of which now accept foreign credit cards. Tapping a foreign credit card directly at the gates doesn't work yet.
