Korean beef marinades like bulgogi and galbi almost always start with grated Asian pear (배, *bae*). It does two jobs at once: a mild enzyme softens the meat, and its juicy, clean sweetness balances soy sauce and garlic without tasting sugary.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Why pear:** a gentle protein-softening enzyme plus clean, light sweetness
- **How much:** about half a medium pear per 500g (1 lb) of beef
- **Best substitute:** grated Fuji apple for sweetness; a little kiwi only for tough cuts
- **Avoid:** marinating overnight with kiwi or pineapple; the meat turns mushy

</KeyTakeaways>

## Why the Asian pear works

The Asian pear is round, crisp and very watery, closer to an apple in texture than the soft European pears sold in Western supermarkets. Like several fruits, it contains protease enzymes that break down meat proteins. The difference is strength: pineapple, kiwi and papaya break meat down fast, while pear works slowly, so beef softens without losing its bite.

Its sugars are mostly fructose and glucose, which taste light and fresh rather than heavy. That lets soy sauce, garlic and sesame oil carry the flavor, and it helps the meat caramelize on the grill. Historically, Korean beef from working cattle was lean and tough, and grated pear was one of the ways home cooks made it tender.

## Asian pear vs common substitutes

| Substitute | Tenderizing | Flavor | Best for | How to use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Asian pear** | Gentle | Clean, juicy sweetness | Bulgogi, galbi, pork | ½ pear per 500g; up to overnight |
| **Fuji apple** | Mild | Sweet, slightly tart | Bulgogi, pork | Same amount; cut added sugar slightly |
| **Bosc or Anjou pear** | Very mild | Sweet, floral | Thin beef, chicken | Use firm, slightly underripe fruit |
| **Kiwi** | Very strong | Tart, bright | Tough short ribs | About 1 tbsp purée per 500g; 30 minutes max |
| **Pineapple juice** | Very strong | Sweet, acidic | Quick pork marinades | 1–2 tbsp; never overnight |
| **Bottled pear juice (배즙)** | Mild | Sweet | Any beef marinade | Replace grated pear one-to-one |

## How to prepare pear for a marinade

**1. Choose and peel.** Pick a pear that feels heavy for its size and has no soft spots; heavier means juicier. Peel it completely, since the skin is thick and slightly bitter.

**2. Grate.** Cut the flesh away from the sour core and grate it finely on a microplane or box grater, or blend it into a smooth purée.

**3. Strain if you're grilling over high heat.** For charcoal or a very hot pan, press the pulp through a sieve and use only the juice. Pulp clinging to the meat burns long before the beef is done.

![Grating a peeled Asian pear and straining the pulp through a fine-mesh sieve](https://eecvvgkihtcgfikaimao.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/blog-covers/why-korean-marinades-use-asian-pear-step-02.jpg)

<Callout type="tip" title="A basic bulgogi marinade for 500g beef">
½ cup grated pear (about half a medium pear), ½ cup soy sauce, 2 tbsp minced garlic, 1 tbsp minced ginger, 2 tbsp sesame oil, 1–2 tbsp brown sugar and black pepper. Taste the pear first; if it's very sweet, use less sugar.
</Callout>

## How long to marinate

Thin-sliced beef for bulgogi needs only 30 minutes to 2 hours. Thicker, bone-in galbi benefits from at least 4 hours, or overnight in the fridge. A pear-based marinade is forgiving, so an extra few hours won't ruin the texture.

Strong substitutes are the opposite. Kiwi's actinidin and pineapple's bromelain keep working the whole time the meat sits in them. Leave beef in a kiwi marinade overnight and it comes out soft and chalky. If you use them, keep the amount small and the time under 30 minutes, or mix them with apple so the sweetness comes from somewhere gentler.

## Buying Asian pears in Korea and abroad

In Korea, pears are sold in supermarkets and traditional markets, often wrapped individually in foam netting. They're large and peak in autumn, and gift boxes of pears are a classic Chuseok present. Outside Korea, look for them at Asian grocers and many mainstream supermarkets, sometimes labeled "nashi pear."

When fresh pears are expensive or out of season, Korean supermarkets sell pouches of 100% pear juice (배즙) in the beverage or health-food aisle. They're a convenient marinade base and keep in the pantry until opened.

- **배 (bae):** Asian pear
- **갈비 양념 (galbi yangnyeom):** short rib marinade
- **연육 (yeonyuk):** tenderizing meat, often printed on marinade bottles
- **배즙 (baejeup):** pear juice

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why do Korean marinades use Asian pear?**
Its gentle enzymes soften the meat, and its light, juicy sweetness balances soy sauce and garlic.

**Can I use a regular pear instead of an Asian pear?**
Yes, a firm Bosc or Anjou pear works for sweetness, though it tenderizes less. Grated Fuji apple is often closer.

**How long should I marinate bulgogi with pear?**
Thin bulgogi slices need 30 minutes to 2 hours; thick galbi can marinate 4 hours to overnight.

**Is kiwi a good substitute for Asian pear?**
Only in small amounts and for under 30 minutes. Its enzyme is much stronger and can make beef mushy.
