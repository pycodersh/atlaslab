Korean table manners are relaxed in casual restaurants, but a few habits matter, especially with older people. Use the spoon for rice and soup and chopsticks for side dishes, share from the middle, and pour drinks for others rather than yourself.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Spoon:** rice and soup; **chopsticks:** side dishes and noodles
- **Rice bowl stays on the table:** don't lift it to your mouth
- **Elders first:** wait for the oldest person to start eating
- **Avoid:** sticking chopsticks upright in rice; it resembles funeral offerings

</KeyTakeaways>

## Spoon and chopsticks

| Utensil | Use it for | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Spoon (숟가락)** | Rice, soup, stews | Koreans eat rice with a spoon, not chopsticks |
| **Chopsticks (젓가락)** | Side dishes, noodles, meat | Metal and flat; take time to get used to |
| **Both together** | Not at once | Hold one at a time; rest the other on the table or a holder |
| **Scissors and tongs** | Cutting meat, kimchi, noodles | Used by whoever is grilling or serving |

<Callout type="warn" title="The one big taboo">
Never stand your chopsticks or spoon upright in a bowl of rice. It looks like the incense and rice offered to ancestors at memorial rites. Lay them on the table or a rest instead.
</Callout>

## Sharing dishes

| Situation | Etiquette |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Shared stew in the middle** | Use the ladle, or your own spoon only if everyone does; ask for a small plate (앞접시) |
| **Side dishes** | Take a little at a time from the shared plates |
| **Last piece** | Offer it to others before taking it |
| **Refills** | Ask staff; don't empty a shared dish without offering |

## Casual vs formal meals

How strict the manners are depends on who you're eating with. With friends your own age at a casual restaurant, almost anything goes. With older people, a boss or a partner's family, the traditional rules matter much more.

| Setting | How formal | What matters most |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Friends, casual restaurant** | Relaxed | Sharing fairly, no upright chopsticks |
| **Work dinner (회식)** | Formal | Waiting for seniors, pouring drinks, receiving with two hands |
| **Partner's family** | Very formal | Elders start first, polite speech, thanking the host |
| **Street food and markets** | Very relaxed | Returning sticks and cups to the vendor |

If you're unsure, watch the people around you and follow their lead. Koreans are generally forgiving of visitors, and a sincere effort, like waiting for the oldest person or offering to pour, goes a long way. Nobody expects a first-time visitor to know every rule.

## Drinking etiquette

| Rule | How |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pour for others** | Fill others' glasses, not your own |
| **Receive with two hands** | Hold your glass with both hands when an older person pours |
| **Turn away to drink** | With elders, turn your head slightly away when you drink |
| **Keep glasses topped up** | Refill a neighbor's empty glass |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do Koreans eat rice with chopsticks?**
Mostly with a spoon. Chopsticks are for side dishes, noodles and meat.

**Is it rude to lift my bowl?**
Lifting the rice or soup bowl to your mouth is considered impolite in Korea, unlike in Japan. Keep it on the table and use your spoon.

**What should I say before and after a meal?**
Say 잘 먹겠습니다 (*jal meokgetseumnida*) before eating, and 잘 먹었습니다 (*jal meogeotseumnida*) after, especially when someone else is paying.

**Do I have to follow drinking etiquette with friends?**
Among friends your own age it's relaxed. The rules matter most with older people, bosses and in-laws.
