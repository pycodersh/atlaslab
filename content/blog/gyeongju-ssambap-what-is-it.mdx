Gyeongju ssambap (경주 쌈밥) is a set meal of rice, a basket of fresh leaves and a table crowded with small side dishes, plus grilled pork and a bubbling stew. It's the classic lunch after sightseeing in Korea's ancient capital, and one order feeds you with dozens of flavors.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **What you get:** rice, wrapping leaves, ssamjang, grilled meat or fish, stew and many banchan
- **Where:** ssambap restaurants clustered near Daereungwon tomb park and Hwangnam-dong
- **Price:** usually a per-person set, often with a two-person minimum
- **Avoid:** filling up on the first few dishes; dozens more keep coming

</KeyTakeaways>

## What's on a Gyeongju ssambap table

| Part of the meal | Examples | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Leaves** | Lettuce, perilla, napa cabbage, kale, steamed cabbage | Refills are usually free |
| **Sauce** | Ssamjang, sometimes gang-doenjang (thick soybean stew sauce) | Spoon onto each wrap |
| **Main** | Grilled spicy pork, bulgogi or grilled fish | Wrap with rice and leaves |
| **Stew** | Doenjang jjigae | Eaten with rice between wraps |
| **Banchan** | Namul, kimchi, braised potatoes, pancakes, seaweed, salted seafood | Often 15–20 dishes or more |
| **Rice** | White rice or mixed-grain rice | Sometimes in a stone pot |

## Why so many dishes?

Gyeongju was the capital of the Silla kingdom for nearly a thousand years, and today it's one of Korea's busiest heritage destinations. Ssambap restaurants near the tomb parks grew up feeding visitors a generous, healthy meal of local vegetables, and the "table full of small dishes" became a selling point. It reflects a wider Korean idea that a good meal balances many flavors, textures and colors at once.

- **Seasonal leaves:** the leaf basket changes with the season
- **Vegetable-heavy:** most banchan are namul and pickles, making it a lighter meal
- **Group-friendly:** perfect for families and tour groups sharing one big table

<Callout type="tip" title="Pace yourself">
Dishes keep arriving after you sit down. Try a little of everything first, then go back to your favorites and ask for refills of the ones you love.
</Callout>

## Making a day of it in Gyeongju

Ssambap fits naturally into a Gyeongju sightseeing day. Most visitors explore Daereungwon's grassy tomb mounds, Cheomseongdae observatory and Woljeonggyo bridge, all within walking distance of the ssambap restaurants. After lunch, Hwangnidan-gil's cafes and bakeries are a short stroll away, and Gyeongju bread (황남빵), a small pastry filled with red bean paste, makes a good souvenir.

- **Morning:** Bulguksa Temple and Seokguram grotto, a bus ride from the center
- **Lunch:** ssambap near Daereungwon
- **Afternoon:** tomb park, Cheomseongdae and Hwangnidan-gil
- **Evening:** Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond, lit up beautifully after dark

## Where to find it

The main cluster of ssambap restaurants is around Daereungwon (the royal tomb park) and nearby Hwangnidan-gil, a short walk from Cheomseongdae. Many have English or picture menus and set prices per person. Lunch is the busiest time, so arriving a little early or late helps.

Building a wrap is the same as anywhere in Korea: leaf in your palm, a spoonful of rice, a piece of meat, a dab of ssamjang, then fold and eat it in one bite. See our [ssambap guide](/blog/en/k-pantry/korean-ssambap-dining-guide) for step-by-step tips.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is Gyeongju ssambap?**
A set meal of rice, wrapping leaves, ssamjang, grilled meat, stew and many side dishes, famous in Gyeongju.

**How many side dishes come with ssambap?**
It varies, but Gyeongju restaurants often serve 15–20 or more small dishes.

**Is Gyeongju ssambap vegetarian?**
Most banchan are vegetables, but the main dish is meat or fish and some sides contain seafood. Ask about options.

**Where are the ssambap restaurants in Gyeongju?**
Mostly near Daereungwon tomb park and Hwangnidan-gil, close to the main historic sites.
