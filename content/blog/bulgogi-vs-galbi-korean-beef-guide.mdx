Bulgogi is thinly sliced marinated beef cooked quickly in a pan or on a grill, while galbi is marinated short ribs grilled on the bone. Both use a sweet soy marinade and neither is spicy, making them the safest Korean meat dishes for first-timers.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Bulgogi:** thin slices of beef, often cooked with broth and vegetables, usually cheaper
- **Galbi:** short ribs, thicker and chewier, grilled at the table, more premium
- **Both:** sweet soy-garlic marinade, not spicy
- **Avoid:** confusing beef galbi (소갈비) with cheaper pork galbi (돼지갈비)

</KeyTakeaways>

## Bulgogi vs galbi

| | Bulgogi (불고기) | Galbi (갈비) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cut** | Thinly sliced beef, often sirloin or brisket | Short ribs, on the bone or butterflied |
| **Marinade** | Soy sauce, sugar, garlic, sesame oil, pear | Similar, often thicker and sweeter |
| **Cooking** | Pan or domed grill, sometimes with broth | Charcoal or gas grill at the table |
| **Texture** | Tender, juicy | Meaty, slightly chewy |
| **Price** | Moderate | Higher, especially beef galbi |
| **Best for** | Rice bowls, lunch sets, first-timers | BBQ dinners, special meals |

There's also pork galbi (돼지갈비), marinated pork ribs that taste similar and cost much less.

## How restaurants serve them

Bulgogi often arrives as a set meal or in a shallow pan with broth, glass noodles and vegetables, eaten with rice. Seoul-style bulgogi is brothy; Gwangyang and Eonyang styles are grilled over charcoal. Galbi is grilled at your table at BBQ restaurants, cut with scissors, and wrapped in lettuce with ssamjang.

<Callout type="tip" title="Why pear?">
Korean marinades often include grated Asian pear, which tenderizes the meat and adds sweetness. See [why Korean marinades use Asian pear](/blog/en/k-pantry/why-korean-marinades-use-asian-pear).
</Callout>

## Regional and modern versions

Bulgogi has many regional styles. **Seoul-style** bulgogi is cooked in a dome-shaped pan with a moat of broth, glass noodles and mushrooms, so the meat stays juicy and you can spoon the broth over rice. **Eonyang** (near Ulsan) and **Gwangyang** (in South Jeolla) bulgogi are grilled over charcoal, giving a smoky, caramelized flavor. There's also **bulgogi burger**, **bulgogi pizza** and **bulgogi kimbap**, everyday fusion foods across Korea.

Galbi has its own famous towns. **Suwon galbi** is known for huge, butterflied beef short ribs, often seasoned with salt rather than soy. **Pocheon idong galbi** is famous for marinated ribs served in big portions, and **LA galbi** refers to short ribs cut across the bone into thin strips, popular at home and for picnics.

- **Lunch sets (정식):** bulgogi set meals with rice and banchan are an affordable way to try it
- **Galbi restaurants:** often specialize in beef or pork, so check the sign
- **Galbitang:** a clear soup made from beef short ribs, a mild option at galbi restaurants

## Making them at home

For bulgogi, slice beef very thin (partly frozen beef is easier to cut), marinate for 30 minutes to a few hours, and stir-fry quickly over high heat. For galbi, marinate the ribs overnight, then grill or broil until caramelized. Both work with store-bought marinades if you're short on time. Serve them with rice, lettuce leaves, ssamjang and a simple green onion salad.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is bulgogi spicy?**
No. Classic bulgogi uses a sweet soy marinade. Spicy pork bulgogi (제육볶음) is a different dish.

**Which is more expensive, bulgogi or galbi?**
Galbi, especially beef short ribs. Bulgogi uses cheaper cuts sliced thin.

**What is the difference between beef galbi and pork galbi?**
Beef galbi uses beef short ribs and costs more; pork galbi uses pork ribs with a similar marinade and is much cheaper.

**Can I make bulgogi without a grill?**
Yes. A hot frying pan works well for thin bulgogi slices.
