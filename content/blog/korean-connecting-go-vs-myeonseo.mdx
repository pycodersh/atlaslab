English strings actions together with "and," "then," and "while." Korean does the same job by changing the verb itself, so you attach one ending to the first verb and keep going.

Two of those endings do most of the work, and they sound similar to English speakers: **-고** and **-(으)면서**. The difference is about time. One lists or sequences actions, and the other says they happen together.

## -고: and, and then

-고 attaches to the verb stem with no changes at all. It never depends on whether the stem ends in a vowel or a consonant.

| Verb | + 고 | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 가다 | 가고 | go, and |
| 먹다 | 먹고 | eat, and |
| 읽다 | 읽고 | read, and |
| 하다 | 하고 | do, and |

It has two main jobs.

**1. Listing things**

- 저는 커피도 좋아하고 차도 좋아해요. — I like coffee and I like tea.
- 이 식당은 맛있고 싸요. — This restaurant is tasty and cheap.

**2. Doing things in sequence**

- 밥을 먹고 커피를 마셨어요. — I ate a meal and then had coffee.
- 손을 씻고 와요. — Wash your hands and come.

The sequence reading is the one that surprises learners. When two actions follow each other, -고 means "and then." You finish the first, then the second begins.

---

## Where the tense goes

Here is a rule that makes -고 easy: **the tense goes on the last verb only.**

- 어제 밥을 먹고 영화를 봤어요. — I ate and watched a movie yesterday.

You don't need 먹었고 (though it's grammatically possible, it sounds heavy). The final verb sets the tense for the whole sentence. This works for polite endings too: the 요 only appears once, at the end.

## -(으)면서: while

-(으)면서 means two things happen **at the same time**, with the same subject.

- -면서 after a stem that ends in a vowel
- -으면서 after a stem that ends in a consonant

| Verb | + (으)면서 | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 가다 | 가면서 | while going |
| 보다 | 보면서 | while watching |
| 먹다 | 먹으면서 | while eating |
| 듣다 | 들으면서 | while listening (irregular) |

Examples:

- 음악을 들으면서 공부해요. — I study while listening to music.
- 걸으면서 전화했어요. — I made a phone call while walking.
- 밥을 먹으면서 TV를 봐요. — I watch TV while eating.

The subject has to be the same for both actions. 동생은 TV를 보면서 나는 공부해요 sounds wrong because two different people are involved.

---

## The key test

Compare these two sentences:

| Korean | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 밥을 먹고 TV를 봤어요. | I ate, and then I watched TV. |
| 밥을 먹으면서 TV를 봤어요. | I watched TV while eating. |

In the first, there are two separate events in order. In the second, one overlaps with the other.

A quick check: **can you swap in "and then"?** If yes, use -고. If you'd say "while" or "at the same time," use -(으)면서.

---

## Two jobs for -고 that surprise people

**1. Contrasts.** Sometimes -고 joins two things that differ:

- 저는 커피를 마시고 친구는 차를 마셔요. — I drink coffee and my friend drinks tea.

**2. Ongoing states.** With some verbs, -고 plus 있다 means "is doing," and it appears in the progressive:

- 지금 밥을 먹고 있어요. — I'm eating right now.

Both are common in everyday speech.

## Useful travel and dining examples

- 지하철을 타고 가요. — I go by subway. (Literally "take the subway and go.")
- 택시를 타고 왔어요. — I came by taxi.
- 메뉴를 보고 주문할게요. — I'll look at the menu and then order.
- 이거 먹고 갈게요. — I'll eat this and go. (A common thing to say at a food stall.)
- 커피를 마시면서 기다릴게요. — I'll wait while drinking coffee.

The 타고 pattern is worth learning early. Korean doesn't say "go by bus." It says "ride the bus and go."

---

## Common mistakes

**Putting the tense on both verbs.** 먹었고 봤어요 is heavy. Say 먹고 봤어요.

**Using -면서 with two different subjects.** It works only when the same person does both actions.

**Using -고 when you mean "at the same time."** 걷고 전화했어요 means you walked, and then you made a call. Use 걸으면서 for simultaneous actions.

**Forgetting irregular changes.** 듣다 becomes 들으면서 and 걷다 becomes 걸으면서, while 만들다 (a stem ending in ㄹ) takes -면서: 만들면서.

---

## 2-minute recap

- **-고** attaches to any verb stem: it lists things or says one action follows another ("and then").
- **-(으)면서** means "while": two actions at the same time, by the same person.
- Tense and politeness go on the **last verb only**.
- Quick test: could you say "and then"? Use -고. Could you say "while"? Use -(으)면서.
- 타고 가요 ("ride and go") is the standard way to say "go by bus, subway, or taxi."
- -고 있다 means "is doing right now."
