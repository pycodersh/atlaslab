A Han River picnic means a mat, instant ramyeon from the park convenience store's cooking machine, and maybe fried chicken delivered to the riverbank. Buy everything at the store inside the park and take your trash back with you.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **Where:** about a dozen riverside parks; Yeouido (Yeouinaru Station, Line 5) is the classic
- **How long:** an afternoon or an evening
- **Cost:** the parks are free; ramyeon from the machine costs a few thousand won
- **Booking:** none; delivery goes to the park's marked delivery zone (배달존), not your mat
- **Avoid:** leaving trash on the grass; bins near the stores sort general waste, recycling and food scraps

</KeyTakeaways>

## Pick your park

Every park has convenience stores, restrooms and bike stations; they differ in mood more than facilities. Search the park name with **한강공원** in Naver Map or Kakao Map for the walk from the station.

| Park | Nearest subway | Best for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Yeouido** | Yeouinaru (Line 5) | The big classic picnic lawns, spring cherry blossoms |
| **Banpo** | Express Bus Terminal (Lines 3, 7, 9), then a walk | The Moonlight Rainbow Fountain on evenings in the warmer months |
| **Ttukseom** | Jayang Station, also signed Ttukseom Hangang Park (Line 7) | Water sports, a younger crowd, sunset views |
| **Mangwon** | Mangwon or Hapjeong (Line 6) | A quieter local scene near Mangwon Market |

<Callout type="info" title="Fountain shows are seasonal">
The Banpo Bridge fountain usually runs from spring to autumn, several times each evening, and pauses in winter and bad weather. Check the current schedule on the Seoul city website before you plan around it.
</Callout>

## How to use the ramyeon machine

The machine at the park convenience store boils water and cooks the noodles for you. It takes three to four minutes.

| Step | What to do | Detail |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Buy** | Pick a packet of ramyeon and a cooking bowl at the counter | The foil or paper bowl is sold or handed out next to the noodles |
| **2. Fill** | Put the noodles, soup powder and flakes in the bowl | Add an egg or a slice of cheese now if you bought them |
| **3. Cook** | Set the bowl on the machine and press start (or scan the bowl's barcode) | It adds the right amount of water and starts boiling |
| **4. Wait** | Stir halfway with chopsticks | The machine stops on its own and beeps |
| **5. Clear up** | Pour leftover soup into the food scrap bin | Never pour broth into general trash |

Shin Ramyun is the classic choice. Jin Ramen (mild) is easier if you don't like heat. Our guide to [how spicy Korean food is](/blog/en/k-pantry/is-korean-food-spicy) helps you choose.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 라면 기계 어떻게 써요? | ramyeon gigye eotteoke sseoyo? | How do I use the ramyeon machine? |
| 돗자리 있어요? | dotjari isseoyo? | Do you have picnic mats? |
| 쓰레기 어디에 버려요? | sseuregi eodie beoryeoyo? | Where do I throw away trash? |

You'll hear **그릇에 면 넣고 버튼 누르시면 돼요** ("put the noodles in the bowl and press the button"). The [Han River picnic phrases](/blog/en/k-patto/han-river-picnic-korean-phrases) have more.

## Delivery, mats and tents

Chicken and beer (chimaek) is the other Han River classic. A driver can't find one mat among hundreds, so every park has marked **delivery zones** near the entrances: pick the zone as your address and walk over when the driver messages you. Most delivery apps need a Korean phone number; the [delivery app guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-delivery-apps-tourists) explains the workarounds.

Small shade tents are allowed only in marked zones and set hours, and must stay open on at least two sides.

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Sort trash at the bins near the convenience store
- Meet delivery drivers at the delivery zone
- Keep music to your own group's volume
- Check the weather; parks close low areas after heavy rain

</Do>
<Dont>

- Pour ramyeon broth on the grass or into general trash
- Set up a closed tent outside the marked zones
- Leave valuables alone on the mat for long periods
- Ride a shared bike on the walking paths

</Dont>
</DoDont>

## Getting around the river

Seoul's public bikes (Ddareungi) are parked along every park and are the easiest way to hop between spots: buy a pass in the app, scan the QR code on the bike, and return it to any station. Walking paths and bike lanes are separate, so stay in your lane.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I drink alcohol at Han River parks?**
Yes. Drinking is generally allowed and common, especially chicken and beer on summer evenings. Some areas restrict it, and behavior that disturbs others can get you asked to leave, so keep it relaxed.

**Is the ramyeon machine available all year?**
Yes. The machines are inside the convenience stores, which open all year and often late into the night. Winter evenings are quiet, and a hot bowl by the cold river is part of the appeal.

**Can I bring my own food?**
Yes. Bringing food from outside is completely normal, whether it's gimbap, fruit or a full picnic. Just take the packaging home or sort it at the park bins.

**Is it safe at night?**
Han River parks are well lit and busy until late on most evenings. Normal city sense applies: keep your phone and wallet with you, and avoid the water's edge after drinking.
