In K-dramas and K-pop, you hear quick phrases like **물 줘!** ("Give me water!") all the time. But say that to an older server in a real Seoul restaurant, and everyone freezes. Korean etiquette expects polite endings with strangers and service workers, and one missing syllable is the whole difference between polite dining and accidental rudeness.

## The exchange

**손님 (customer):** 저기요. 물 줘.  
*(Excuse me. Give me water.)*  
**직원 (staff):** 네? 손님, 반말하시면 안 돼요.  
*(Pardon? Sir, you shouldn't talk down to me.)*  
**손님:** 아, 죄송해요! 물 주세요... 휴, 깜짝이야.  
*(Ah, I'm so sorry! Water, please... Phew, that gave me a fright.)*  
**직원:** 여기 있습니다.  
*(Here you go.)*

## Polite or casual?

Dropping **-요 (-yo)** turns your speech into **반말** (casual speech), which is for close friends, younger people and kids. Always keep **-요** when speaking to restaurant staff.

| Casual (friends only) | Polite (staff, strangers) | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 저기! | 저기요. | Excuse me (calling attention). |
| 물 줘. | 물 주세요. | Water, please. |
| 미안해. | 죄송해요. / 죄송합니다. | I'm sorry. |
| 여기 있어. | 여기 있습니다. / 여기요. | Here it is. |

> **깜짝이야! (kkamjjagiya!):**
> An instinctive exclamation meaning **"You scared me!"** or **"What a shock!"** Native speakers say it when they drop something, hear a loud sound, or catch themselves in a sudden social slip.

## What to say before and during your meal

Essential phrases for getting attention, water and extra side dishes without friction:

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **저기요, 물 좀 주세요.** | *jeogiyo, mul jom juseyo* | Excuse me, some water please. |
| **반찬 좀 더 주실 수 있나요?** | *banchan jom deo jusil su innayo?* | Could we get more side dishes? |
| **앞접시 두 개만 주세요.** | *apjeopsi du gaeman juseyo* | Just two small sharing plates, please. |
| **물은 셀프인가요?** | *mureun selpeu-ingayo?* | Is water self-service? |

## What staff will tell you

Many casual Korean eateries don't bring water automatically. Look out for these signs and phrases:

| You'll hear or see | Meaning | Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **물은 셀프입니다.** | Water is self-service. | Grab a stainless cup and fill it at the water purifier. |
| **네, 바로 갖다 드릴게요.** | Yes, I'll bring it right away. | When you ask for extra items. |
| **몇 분이세요?** | How many people? | Asked at the entrance. |

> **물은 셀프 (self-service water):**
> If no water bottle reaches your table within a couple of minutes, check the walls. A sign saying **물은 셀프** means you walk up to the purifier and take cups yourself.

## What to actually say

- Calling a server: **저기요!** (*Excuse me!*)
- Asking for water politely: **물 좀 주세요.** (*Water, please.*)
- Apologizing for a slip: **아, 죄송해요!** (*Ah, I'm sorry!*)
- Asking if you should get it yourself: **물은 셀프예요?** (*Is water self-service?*)
