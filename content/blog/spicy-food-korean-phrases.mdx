Korean menus don't always warn you. A dish that looks mild can arrive glowing red, and the first bite tells you everything. Here is what Koreans actually say in that moment, and what to say before it happens.

## The exchange

**A:** 으아 매워! 입에서 불이 나요!  
*(Ugh, it's so spicy! My mouth is on fire!)*  
**B:** 너무 맵지 않아? 괜찮니? 물 좀 마셔.  
*(Isn't it too spicy? Are you okay? Drink some water.)*  
**A:** 근데 계속 당기는 맛이야!  
*(But I can't stop eating it!)*  
**B:** 우리 애기 다 컸네 진짜!  
*(My baby is really all grown up now!)*

This is a parent and child, so most lines are casual speech (반말). The table below gives the polite version of each one: use those with staff, strangers, and anyone older. The last line is a family joke. If you can handle spicy food, you're not a baby anymore.

## Polite or casual?

| Casual (friends, children) | Polite (staff, strangers, older people) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 매워! | 매워요! | It's spicy! |
| 괜찮아? | 괜찮으세요? | Are you okay? |
| 물 좀 마셔. | 물 좀 드세요. | Have some water. |
| 계속 당겨. | 계속 당겨요. | I can't stop eating it. |

**입에서 불이 나요** (*ibeseo buri nayo*, "fire is coming out of my mouth") works in both: it already ends in -요.

## Before you order

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 이거 많이 매워요? | igeo mani maewoyo? | Is this very spicy? |
| 맵기 조절 돼요? | maepgi jojeol dwaeyo? | Can you adjust the spice level? |
| 순한 맛으로 주세요. | sunhan mas-euro juseyo | The mild one, please. |
| 덜 맵게 해 주세요. | deol maepge hae juseyo | Less spicy, please. |

Many tteokbokki, jjamppong and chicken places list spice levels on the menu: **순한 맛** (mild), **보통 맛** (medium), **매운 맛** (hot), sometimes numbered **1단계 · 2단계**. If you can't eat spice at all, the [guide to ordering non-spicy food](/blog/en/k-patto/order-non-spicy-food-korea) covers that conversation in full.

## What staff will tell you

| You'll hear | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 조금 매워요. | It's a little spicy. |
| 하나도 안 매워요. | It's not spicy at all. |
| 이건 좀 매운 편이에요. | This one is on the spicy side. |

Be careful with **조금 매워요**: a Korean "a little" can be a lot for a first-timer.

<Callout type="tip" title="당기는 맛, said 땡기는 맛">

**당기는 맛** (*danggineun mat*) is a taste that keeps pulling you back. In speech you'll almost always hear it pronounced **땡기는 맛**. The same verb covers cravings: **매운 게 땡겨** — "I'm craving something spicy."

</Callout>

## When it's too much

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 물 좀 더 주세요. | mul jom deo juseyo | More water, please. |
| 우유 있어요? | uyu isseoyo? | Do you have milk? |
| 저 매운 거 잘 못 먹어요. | jeo maeun geo jal mon meogeoyo | I'm not good with spicy food. |

Water spreads the burn; milk calms it. Many spicy-food spots also sell **쿨피스**, a sweet peach drink made for exactly this.

You'll also hear **맵찔이** (*maepjjiri*), friendly slang for someone who can't handle spice. "저 맵찔이예요" is a self-deprecating way to say it about yourself.

## What to actually say

- First bite hits hard: **으아, 매워요! 입에서 불이 나요!**
- Checking before you order: **이거 많이 매워요?**
- Choosing a level: **순한 맛으로 주세요.**
- Painful but delicious: **맵지만 맛있어요!**
- Need relief: **물 좀 더 주세요.**
