Korean spice has a reputation. Your first bowl of buldak, your first chip of fire noodles — there's a moment where your eyes water and something in you breaks open.

And then you reach for more.

## The exchange

**A:** 으아 매워! 입에서 불이 나요!  
*(Ugh, it's so spicy! My mouth is on fire!)*  
**B:** 너무 맵지 않아? 괜찮니? 물 좀 마셔.  
*(Isn't it too spicy? Are you okay? Drink some water.)*  
**A:** 근데 계속 당기는 맛이야!  
*(But I can't stop eating it, it's so addictive!)*  
**B:** 우리 애기 다 컸네 진짜!  
*(My baby is really all grown up now!)*

> **Key Nuances**
> - **입에서 불이 나요**: Literally "fire from my mouth" — the standard Korean expression when spice levels peak.
> - **-지 않아? / -니?**: Warm, informal question endings typically used by friends or parents showing care.
> - **당기는 맛**: "A pulling taste" — addictive spice that keeps you reaching for more despite the burn.
> - **다 컸네**: "You're all grown up" — Mom's logic: if you can handle spicy food, you're no longer a baby.

## How Koreans talk about spicy food

A few phrases that come up constantly around Korean food:

**맵지만 맛있어!** — "It's spicy but delicious!"
The standard response when something burns and you love it anyway.

**저 매운 거 못 먹어요** — "I can't eat spicy food"
Useful to know if that's you — staff at Korean restaurants respond well to this.

**덜 맵게 해주실 수 있어요?** — "Could you make it less spicy?"
Works at most Korean restaurants. Ask when you order.

**이거 얼마나 매워요?** — "How spicy is this?"
Before you order something unfamiliar.

## The **당기는 맛** effect

**당기는 맛** is why Koreans eat tteokbokki at 11pm even when they're not hungry. It's why the last few bites of buldak happen even when your eyes are watering. It's the thing that makes Korean food distinctly hard to stop eating.

The same word appears in other contexts too — **생각이 당겨** (I'm craving it, it's pulling at my thoughts) — but **당기는 맛** at the dinner table is immediately understood by every Korean speaker.

## What to actually say

When something hits harder than expected:
**으아 매워! 입에서 불이 나요!**

When someone looks like they're struggling:
**괜찮아? 물 좀 마셔.**

When it's painful but you can't stop:
**근데 계속 당기는 맛이야...**

When a child does something that marks a new stage:
**우리 애기 다 컸네 진짜!**
