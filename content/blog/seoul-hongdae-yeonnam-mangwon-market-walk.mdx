Hongdae is Seoul's student and indie-music district, and the neighbourhoods around it are some of the most pleasant in the city to walk. Start on the leafy old rail line in Yeonnam-dong, cross into Hongdae's busy streets, slow down in the cafe lanes of Sangsu and Hapjeong, and end at Mangwon Market, where locals do their shopping.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **The route:** Yeonnam-dong Forest Trail → Hongdae Walk Street → Sangsu & Hapjeong cafe lanes → Mangwon Market, about 4 km on foot
- **Getting around:** walk the whole way; Hongik Univ. Station (Line 2) is the starting point
- **Cost:** free; budget for coffee and market snacks
- **Best timing:** start late morning, reach Hongdae by mid-afternoon, Mangwon by early evening
- **Avoid:** arriving at Mangwon Market late at night, when most stalls have shut

</KeyTakeaways>

The Gyeongui Line Forest Park follows a railway that now runs underground. The Yeonnam-dong section, a strip of lawn and trees running north from Hongik Univ. Station, is lined with cafes, small restaurants and wine bars on both sides. It's the easy, green start to the day. On sunny weekends, people bring a mat and sit on the grass.

Most Korean cafes are self-service: you order at the counter, then return your tray when you leave. Our guides to [ordering coffee in Korea](/blog/en/k-patto/order-iced-americano-korea) and [busing your own table](/blog/en/k-patto/return-tray-korean-cafe) cover the basics.

Hongdae is named after Hongik University, the art school at the centre of the area. Its main pedestrian street is known for busking: dancers and singers set up in the evening and draw crowds. During the day it's a maze of youth fashion shops, photo booths, cosmetics stores and snack stalls.

## Sangsu and Hapjeong Cafe Lanes

South of Hongdae, toward the Han River, the streets around Sangsu Station and Hapjeong Station are quieter. The side lanes hold independent cafes, bakeries, bookshops and small studios, a calmer place to sit for an hour than the main Hongdae strip. There's no single sight here: wander the side streets between the two stations and stop wherever looks good.

Sangsu Station (Line 6) is one stop from Hapjeong Station (Lines 2 and 6), so it's easy to start or end the walk by subway.

## Mangwon Market

Mangwon Market is a covered neighbourhood market that local residents still use every day. It's long and narrow, with stalls selling vegetables, side dishes, rice cakes, fried snacks and chicken. Prices are lower than in the tourist districts, and it's a good place to graze on hot snacks for an early dinner.

From the market it's a short walk to the Mangwon Hangang Park riverside. If you buy snacks, you can eat them by the water.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I walk from Hongdae to Mangwon Market?**
Yes. It's about 2–3 km depending on your route, or one stop on Line 6 from Hapjeong to Mangwon.

**When is the best time for busking in Hongdae?**
Evenings, especially on weekends.

**Is the Yeonnam-dong forest trail free?**
Yes. It's a public park, open to everyone.

**Does Mangwon Market take cards?**
Many shops do, but small snack stalls are faster with cash.
