Goguma (고구마) are Korean sweet potatoes, with reddish-purple skin and pale yellow flesh that turns sweet and golden when roasted. Koreans eat them as a healthy snack, from street-cart roasted goguma in winter to convenience store packs and goguma lattes.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Taste:** sweeter and denser than many Western sweet potatoes
- **Winter snack:** roasted goguma (군고구마) from street carts
- **Everywhere:** convenience stores sell cooked goguma, and cafes serve goguma lattes
- **Avoid:** storing them in the fridge; cold makes them hard and less sweet

</KeyTakeaways>

## Goguma varieties

| Type | Korean | Texture | Best for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bam goguma** | 밤고구마 | Dry, fluffy, chestnut-like | Roasting, steaming |
| **Hobak goguma** | 호박고구마 | Moist, very sweet, orange flesh | Roasting, desserts |
| **Kkul goguma** | 꿀고구마 | Sticky, honey-sweet | Roasting, snacks |
| **Purple goguma** | 자색고구마 | Firm, mildly sweet | Lattes, baking, color |

You can tell varieties apart at the market by their labels and shape: bam goguma are rounder with drier flesh, hobak goguma are slimmer with deep orange insides, and purple goguma are easy to spot by their color. Many sellers let you ask which is sweetest that week.

## How Koreans eat goguma

The classic winter snack is **roasted goguma** from a street cart, sold in paper bags and eaten hot. At home, people steam or air-fry goguma for breakfast or a snack, often with kimchi or a glass of milk. **Goguma mattang** (고구마맛탕) are candied sweet potato chunks, and **goguma latte** is a creamy sweet potato drink sold in cafes.

- **Diet snack:** goguma is popular among people eating healthily or working out
- **Goguma cheese:** sweet potato baked with cheese, a popular side at chicken and pizza places
- **Goguma pizza:** sweet potato mousse crusts and toppings are a Korean pizza favorite

<Callout type="tip" title="Goguma and kimchi">
Eating hot goguma with cold, sour kimchi is a classic Korean combination: sweet and tangy, warm and cool.
</Callout>

## Goguma in Korean food culture

Sweet potatoes have a special place in Korean memory. In leaner decades they were a filling, affordable food, and roasted goguma by the stove on winter nights is a nostalgic image for many older Koreans. Today goguma is linked with healthy eating: athletes, office workers and anyone watching their diet often keep a few steamed goguma for a snack or light lunch.

Goguma also appears in many modern foods. Bakeries sell goguma bread and cakes, convenience stores stock goguma chips and drinks, and Korean pizza chains offer goguma crust and goguma mousse toppings.

- **Dried goguma (고구마말랭이):** chewy, naturally sweet dried slices, sold as a snack
- **Goguma stems (고구마줄기):** the stems are blanched and seasoned as a namul side dish
- **Goguma glass noodles:** dangmyeon in japchae is made from sweet potato starch
- **Seasonal tip:** freshly harvested goguma appear in autumn and are best after a few weeks of storage, when they turn sweeter

## Cooking goguma at home

Wash the goguma and roast them whole at around 200°C for 40–60 minutes, until soft and caramelized at the edges. An air fryer works well too. For steamed goguma, steam for about 30 minutes. Let them rest a little, because the sweetness develops as they cool slightly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is goguma?**
Korean sweet potato, with reddish-purple skin and pale yellow flesh that becomes very sweet when roasted.

**How is goguma different from Western sweet potatoes?**
It's usually drier, denser and sweeter than orange-fleshed sweet potatoes.

**Where can I buy goguma in Korea?**
Street carts in winter, convenience stores, markets and supermarkets all year.

**How do I store goguma?**
Keep raw goguma in a cool, dry, dark place, not the fridge.
