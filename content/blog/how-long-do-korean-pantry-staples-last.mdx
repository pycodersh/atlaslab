Most Korean pantry staples last far longer than people expect: the fermented pastes keep for a year or more in the fridge, and kimchi doesn't so much expire as get more sour. The two things that actually lose quality are the oils and gochugaru, and they rarely look spoiled when they do.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Gochujang and doenjang:** 1–2 years refrigerated after opening; darkening is normal
- **Gochugaru:** keep it in the freezer, or it fades in months
- **Kimchi:** keeps fermenting; old, sour kimchi is perfect for cooking
- **Avoid:** keeping opened perilla oil in the cupboard; it goes rancid fast

</KeyTakeaways>

## Shelf life at a glance

| Item | Where to store after opening | Roughly how long | Sign it's past its best |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gochujang** | Fridge | 1–2 years | Fuzzy mold, sharp alcohol smell |
| **Doenjang** | Fridge | 2 years or more | Fuzzy mold |
| **Ssamjang** | Fridge | 6 months–1 year | Sour or off smell |
| **Gochugaru** | Freezer | 1 year+ (months at room temperature) | Brownish color, dusty smell |
| **Soy sauce** | Fridge or cool cupboard | 1–2 years | Flat flavor |
| **Sesame oil** | Cool, dark cupboard | 6–12 months | Sharp, crayon-like smell |
| **Perilla oil** | Fridge | A couple of months | Paint-like smell |
| **Dried anchovies** | Freezer | 6 months+ | Rancid, fishy smell |
| **Dried kelp** | Airtight cupboard | 1 year+ | Damp or moldy patches |
| **Rice cakes (tteok)** | Freezer | About 3 months | Mold spots |
| **Sweet potato noodles** | Cupboard | Up to 2 years | Damp or bugs |

## Fermented pastes: change, don't spoil

Gochujang is shelf-stable before opening but belongs in the fridge once open. Over time it darkens, tastes deeper and gets a little saltier as moisture evaporates. None of that is a problem. Throw it out if you see fuzzy mold or smell a sharp alcohol note. A thin white film is usually harmless surface yeast; scraping it off is a judgment call.

![Gochujang](https://eecvvgkihtcgfikaimao.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/pantry-ingredients/gochujang.png)

Doenjang is even sturdier. Traditional doenjang was aged for years on purpose, so darkening is expected. Ssamjang has a shorter life because it's mixed with sesame oil, garlic and other ingredients.

<Callout type="tip" title="Clean spoon, flat surface">
Always use a dry, clean spoon and smooth the paste flat after scooping. Wet spoons and exposed peaks are where mold starts.
</Callout>

## Gochugaru: freeze it

Chili flakes don't go bad in a food-safety sense, but they lose their color and fruity aroma within months at room temperature, and that's the reason you bought them. Korean households keep gochugaru in an airtight bag in the freezer. It doesn't clump if it was dry when it went in, and you can use it straight from frozen. If yours has turned brownish and smells like dust, replace it; it will make dull, muddy food.

## Kimchi: it doesn't expire, it changes

Kimchi keeps fermenting in the fridge, and under normal storage there's no point where it suddenly becomes unsafe. What changes is how you use it.

- **First 1–2 weeks:** crisp and lightly sour. Best eaten as a side dish.
- **1–2 months:** clearly sour and softer. Still good fresh, and now great for cooking.
- **3 months and beyond:** very sour and soft. Ideal for kimchi jjigae, fried rice and pancakes.

Keep it pressed down under its own liquid, since air contact causes surface mold. Discard it if you see fuzzy colored mold or it smells rotten rather than sour. Strong and pungent is normal; putrid isn't.

## Oils: the ones that really go bad

Sesame oil and perilla oil are the two staples that genuinely spoil. They go rancid rather than moldy, so they look fine but smell sharp, like old crayons or paint. Perilla oil is especially fragile because of its high unsaturated fat content, so keep it in the fridge and buy small bottles. Sesame oil is fine in a cool, dark cupboard, but its aroma fades once opened, so smaller bottles are better there too.

## Dried goods and frozen items

Dried anchovies hold up best in the freezer, where their oils stay fresh. Kelp is happy in an airtight container in the cupboard. Fresh rice cakes harden quickly in the fridge, so freeze them in portions and soak or boil them from frozen. Sweet potato noodles for japchae keep for a long time in a dry cupboard.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does gochujang go bad?**
Rarely. Refrigerated after opening, it keeps 1–2 years. Discard it only if you see fuzzy mold or smell alcohol.

**How long does kimchi last in the fridge?**
Months. It keeps getting more sour, and very sour kimchi is best for cooking.

**Should gochugaru be refrigerated?**
The freezer is best. It keeps the color and aroma for a year or more.

**How do I know if sesame oil is rancid?**
It smells sharp, like crayons or old paint, instead of nutty and roasted.
