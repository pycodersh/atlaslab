Korean convenience stores (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven, Emart24) sell everything you need to build a hot meal, plus microwaves, hot water and a counter to eat at. Pick a combo from the table below, pay first, then cook and eat in the store.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Most famous combo:** the Mark meal (마크정식): spicy noodles, sausage and cheese, around 5,000–7,000 KRW
- **Equipment:** hot water dispenser, microwave and an eating counter in most stores
- **Rule:** pay before you open or heat anything
- **Avoid:** pouring leftover soup into the trash; use the liquid bin

</KeyTakeaways>

## Popular combos

| Combo | What you buy | How to make it | Rough total |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mark meal (마크정식)** | Spicy tteokbokki or Buldak cup noodles, a sausage stick, string cheese | Cook the noodles, drain most of the water, add sliced sausage and cheese, microwave 30–40 sec | 5,000–7,000 KRW |
| **Ramyeon + triangle gimbap** | Cup ramyeon, tuna-mayo triangle gimbap | Eat the noodles, then drop the gimbap into the leftover broth | 3,000–4,500 KRW |
| **Buldak + mayo rice** | Buldak cup noodles, instant rice, a mayo packet | Microwave the rice, mix with the drained noodles and mayo to tame the heat | 4,500–6,000 KRW |
| **Dosirak meal** | A boxed lunch (dosirak) and a drink | Microwave per the label, usually 1.5–3 min | 4,500–6,500 KRW |
| **Ice cup drink** | A pouch drink and a cup of ice from the freezer | Pour the pouch over the ice | 1,500–2,500 KRW |

The Mark meal got its name from a K-pop idol's go-to order, and the stores still stock the ingredients side by side.

## Brands and their strengths

All four big chains sell similar basics, but each has its own private-label items that locals seek out. You don't need to plan around them; just know that the dosirak and snacks differ a little from store to store, so it's worth trying more than one chain during your trip.

| Chain | Known for |
| :--- | :--- |
| **CU** | A wide range of private-label snacks and frequent viral new products |
| **GS25** | Popular dosirak boxed lunches and fresh food |
| **7-Eleven** | Imported snacks and drinks alongside Korean staples |
| **Emart24** | Products linked to the Emart supermarket range |

## Using the equipment

| Equipment | Use it for | How |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hot water dispenser** | Cup noodles, instant soup | Fill to the line inside the cup and close the lid for 3–4 minutes |
| **Microwave** | Dosirak, gimbap, sausages, rice | Follow the time printed for the wattage on the label |
| **Ramyeon cooker** | Pouch ramyeon (in some stores) | Place the paper bowl on the machine and press start |
| **Eating counter** | Eating in | Clear your space when you're done |

<Callout type="tip" title="Microwave wattage">
Labels often list two times, for 700W and 1,000W. Store microwaves are usually the stronger 1,000W kind (marked on the door), so use the shorter time. Gimbap needs only about 15–20 seconds.
</Callout>

## Cleaning up and sorting trash

| Waste | Where it goes |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Leftover soup and broth** | The liquid or food-waste bin (often labeled 음식물 or 국물) |
| **Plastic cups and trays** | Plastic recycling (플라스틱) |
| **Cans and bottles** | Can and bottle recycling (캔 · 병) |
| **Wrappers, chopsticks, napkins** | General waste (일반쓰레기) |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the Mark meal very spicy?**
It depends on the noodles. With Buldak it's hot; with tteokbokki noodles it's milder. The cheese softens the heat either way.

**Can I eat inside every convenience store?**
Most have a small counter or a few tables, and some have outdoor seating. Very small branches may have no seating.

**Do convenience stores take foreign cards?**
Yes, Visa and Mastercard are widely accepted, and contactless payment works at most registers.

**What does 1+1 mean on a sticker?**
Buy one, get one free. 2+1 means buy two, get a third free. Grab the free item yourself before paying.
