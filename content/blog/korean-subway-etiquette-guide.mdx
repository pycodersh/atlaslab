The Seoul subway is clean, cheap and signposted in English, so most visitors find it easy after one ride. Learn the transfer signs, check whether your train is express, and leave the colored priority seats free.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Fare:** pay by tapping a transit card; transfers within a set time are discounted or free
- **Signs:** every line has a number and color; follow the color to transfer
- **Express trains:** Line 9 and some other lines skip stations, so check before boarding
- **Avoid:** sitting in pink pregnancy seats or the priority seats at the ends of each car

</KeyTakeaways>

## Transfers and train types

| Situation | What to look for | Tip |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Transferring** | Orange transfer signs (갈아타는 곳) with the line color | Some transfers are long walks; allow 5–10 minutes |
| **Direction** | The name of the last station on the platform sign | Check the next station name too |
| **Express vs local** | 급행 (express) vs 일반 (local) on the train and platform screen | Line 9 express skips many stations; local stops everywhere |
| **Last train** | Around midnight on most lines | Check the app; times differ by line and day |
| **Transit card** | T-money or Climate Card | See [which card to buy](/blog/en/k-patto/climate-card-vs-t-money-seoul) |

## Fixing gate problems

Some stations have separate entrances for each direction, so it's easy to tap in on the wrong side.

| Problem | What to do |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Tapped in on the wrong side** | Tap out at the same station within a few minutes; you usually aren't charged. Then walk to the correct entrance and tap in again |
| **Gate won't open** | Press the call button (호출) on the gate; staff answer over the intercom and can open it remotely |
| **Card shows an error** | Try again slowly, or take it to the station office (역무실) |
| **Not enough balance** | Top up at the machine next to the gates, usually with cash |

## Reading the station

Every station has a name in Korean, English, Chinese and Japanese, plus a line number and a station code. Exits are numbered, and locals give directions by exit number, so note yours when you arrange to meet someone or look up a restaurant. Large stations have maps showing which exit leads to which building.

| Sign | What it tells you |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Line color and number** | Which line you're on, such as green for Line 2 |
| **Station code (e.g. 201)** | A short number used on maps and screens |
| **Exit numbers (출구)** | Which street you come out on; restaurants list their nearest exit |
| **Platform screens** | When the next two trains arrive and whether they're express |

## Seat etiquette

| Seat | Where | Who it's for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pink pregnancy seats** | Middle of each car, marked in pink | Pregnant passengers; locals leave them empty even when the train is full |
| **Priority seats** | Ends of each car, different color | Elderly, disabled, pregnant passengers and people with small children |
| **Regular seats** | Everywhere else | Anyone; offer yours to someone who needs it |

## Riding etiquette

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Let passengers off before boarding
- Wear your backpack on your front in crowded cars
- Stand on the right on escalators if people are walking on the left
- Use headphones for music and videos

</Do>
<Dont>

- Sit in pink or priority seats when you don't need them
- Talk loudly on the phone
- Eat full meals on the train
- Rush through closing doors; the next train is usually minutes away

</Dont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which app should I use for the subway?**
Naver Map and KakaoMap give accurate routes, transfer walking times and last-train times. Google Maps works for transit, but the Korean apps are more detailed.

**Can I pay the fare with a credit card?**
Some foreign contactless cards now work at the gates, but support varies. A T-money or Climate Card is the reliable option.

**Is it OK to eat or drink on the subway?**
A drink with a lid is fine. Strong-smelling food and full meals are frowned upon.

**What happens if I miss my stop?**
Get off at the next station, cross to the opposite platform, and ride back. Many stations let you change direction without leaving the gates.
