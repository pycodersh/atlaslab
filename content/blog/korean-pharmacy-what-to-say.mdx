Korean pharmacies sell a lot over the counter: cold medicine, painkillers, stomach remedies, patches. You usually don't name a brand. You describe what's wrong, the pharmacist asks a question or two, and then chooses something for you. The conversation is short, but it is a conversation.

## Finding one

Look for **약국** (*yakguk*) and a green cross. Most close in the evening, though some areas have late-night pharmacies. [Late-night medicine in Korea](/blog/en/k-patto/late-night-medicine-korea-clinics-guide) explains where to go after hours. Convenience stores also sell a small range of basics, such as painkillers, cold medicine and digestive aids, around the clock.

## Describing what's wrong

The pattern is **~이/가 아파요** ("~ hurts").

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 머리가 아파요. | meoriga apayo | I have a headache. |
| 배가 아파요. | baega apayo | I have a stomachache. |
| 목이 아파요. | mogi apayo | I have a sore throat. |
| 열이 나요. | yeori nayo | I have a fever. |
| 기침을 해요. | gichimeul haeyo | I have a cough. |
| 콧물이 나요. | konmuri nayo | I have a runny nose. |
| 소화가 안 돼요. | sohwaga an dwaeyo | I have indigestion. |
| 설사를 해요. | seolsareul haeyo | I have diarrhea. |

## Asking for medicine

**~ 있어요?** ("do you have ~?") works for all of these.

| Korean | Romanization | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 감기약 | gamgiyak | cold medicine |
| 진통제 | jintongje | painkiller |
| 해열제 | haeyeolje | fever reducer |
| 소화제 | sohwaje | digestive aid |
| 연고 | yeongo | ointment |
| 파스 | paseu | medicated patch |
| 밴드 | baendeu | adhesive bandage |

## What the pharmacist will ask

| You'll hear | Meaning | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 어디가 불편하세요? | What's bothering you? | 목이 아파요. |
| 기침도 하세요? | Do you have a cough too? | 네, 조금요. |
| 언제부터 그러셨어요? | Since when? | 어제부터요. |
| 알레르기 있으세요? | Any allergies? | 아니요, 없어요. |
| 드시는 약 있으세요? | Are you taking any medicine? | 아니요. |

Short answers are fine: **네**, **아니요**, **어제부터요** ("since yesterday").

## Understanding the instructions

This is the part worth learning properly, because it comes at the end, when you've stopped concentrating.

| You'll hear | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 하루 세 번 드세요. | Take it three times a day. |
| 식후에 드세요. | Take it after meals. |
| 식전에 드세요. | Take it before meals. |
| 이건 좀 졸려요. | This one makes you drowsy. |
| 운전하시면 안 돼요. | You shouldn't drive. |

**식후** (*sikhu*, after eating) and **식전** (*sikjeon*, before eating) are on almost every packet. If you didn't catch it, ask **다시 한번 말해 주세요** ("please say that again").

## Polite or casual?

Pharmacists use honorific **-시-** with you (**드세요**, **있으세요?**). Polite **-요** is all you need back. With a friend: **나 감기 걸렸어** ("I've caught a cold").

<Callout type="info" title="If it's more than a cold">

If symptoms are serious or don't improve, the pharmacist may send you to a clinic: **병원에 가 보세요** ("you should see a doctor"). [Seeing a doctor in Korea](/blog/en/k-patto/seeing-a-doctor-in-korea) covers that visit. In an emergency, call **119**.

</Callout>

## A full visit

> **You:** 목이 아픈데, 감기약 있어요?  
> **Pharmacist:** 기침도 하세요?  
> **You:** 네, 조금요.  
> **Pharmacist:** 이건 졸려요. 운전하시면 안 돼요.  
> **You:** 그럼 밤에 먹을게요.  
> **Pharmacist:** 하루 세 번, 식후에 드세요.
