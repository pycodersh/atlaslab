The hard part of Korean consonants isn't the number. It's that Korean makes a three-way distinction where English makes two, and English speakers can't hear the third option at first. Get that system and the 19 letters organize themselves.

## The shapes are diagrams

The five base consonants sketch what your mouth does.

| Letter | Shows | Built from it |
|---|---|---|
| ㄱ | the back of the tongue rising | ㅋ ㄲ |
| ㄴ | the tongue tip behind the teeth | ㄷ ㅌ ㄸ ㄹ |
| ㅁ | the mouth | ㅂ ㅍ ㅃ |
| ㅅ | a tooth | ㅈ ㅊ ㅉ ㅆ |
| ㅇ | the open throat | ㅎ |

You don't have to believe it to learn the letters, but it makes them stick.

## The three-way system

Take the *k* family:

| Type | Letter | How it sounds |
|---|---|---|
| Plain | ㄱ | soft, almost no air |
| Aspirated | ㅋ | a strong puff of air |
| Tense | ㄲ | no air, throat tight, sharp |

English has plain and aspirated sounds too (the *p* in "spin" vs "pin") but treats them as one letter. Korean gives each its own letter, and they change meaning.

| Plain | Aspirated | Tense |
|---|---|---|
| ㄱ | ㅋ | ㄲ |
| ㄷ | ㅌ | ㄸ |
| ㅂ | ㅍ | ㅃ |
| ㅈ | ㅊ | ㅉ |

ㅅ has a tense partner, ㅆ, but no aspirated one.

| Word pair | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 사다 · 싸다 | *sada · ssada* | to buy · to be cheap |
| 자다 · 짜다 | *jada · jjada* | to sleep · to be salty |
| 달 · 딸 | *dal · ttal* | moon · daughter |

**The paper test:** hold a sheet of paper an inch from your mouth. ㅋ should move it, ㄲ should leave it still, ㄱ barely flutters it. Tense sounds also start with a brief tightness in the throat, like holding back a cough.

## The rest

- **ㄴ, ㅁ:** plain *n* and *m*.
- **ㅎ:** *h*, but it weakens or drops between vowels. 좋아요 is said *joayo*.
- **ㅇ:** silent at the start of a syllable, *ng* at the bottom. 아 is *a*; 앙 is *ang*.
- **ㄹ:** not an English R. Between vowels it's a quick tap, like the *tt* in American "butter". At the end of a syllable it's closer to an L. 라면 starts with a tap; 물 ends with an L.

## At the bottom of a block

A consonant in the final position isn't released. 밥 ends with your lips closed and nothing after. And several letters collapse into one sound: ㅅ, ㅆ, ㅈ, ㅊ, ㅌ all end as a stopped *t*, so 옷, 있 and 낮 end the same way. The [batchim guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-batchim-final-consonants-explained) covers this properly.

<Callout type="tip" title="What to practice first">

Start with plain vs aspirated (ㄱ vs ㅋ), which is closest to something English already does. Tense sounds come from listening: hear 싸다 enough times and your mouth starts reaching for the right shape on its own.

</Callout>
