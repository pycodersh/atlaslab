**주세요** is probably the first Korean word most travelers learn. 물 주세요, "water, please." What fewer people notice is that the same 주세요 can follow a verb, and then it stops meaning "give me" and starts meaning "please do this for me."

That one move covers most of what you ask strangers to do: stop the taxi here, make it less spicy, take our photo, wrap this up.

## Two kinds of 주세요

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **noun + 주세요** | please give me (a thing) | 물 주세요. — Water, please. |
| **verb + 아/어 주세요** | please do (this) for me | 물 좀 갖다 주세요. — Could you bring some water? |

주다 means "to give," and as a helper verb it adds the idea of doing something **for someone's benefit**, usually yours. That's why it's the natural way to make a request.

---

## Building it

Start from the polite present you already know ([the 아요/어요 rule](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-verb-conjugation-ayo-eoyo)), drop the 요, and add 주세요.

| Verb | 아요/어요 form | Request |
|---|---|---|
| 세우다 (to stop a car) | 세워요 | **세워 주세요** — please stop |
| 열다 (to open) | 열어요 | **열어 주세요** — please open |
| 찍다 (to take a photo) | 찍어요 | **찍어 주세요** — please take |
| 기다리다 (to wait) | 기다려요 | **기다려 주세요** — please wait |
| 하다 (to do) | 해요 | **해 주세요** — please do |
| 바꾸다 (to change) | 바꿔요 | **바꿔 주세요** — please change |

Any 하다 verb works the same way: 포장하다 → **포장해 주세요**, 계산하다 → **계산해 주세요**.

## Lines for the taxi

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 이 주소로 가 주세요. | i juso-ro ga juseyo | Please go to this address. |
| 여기 세워 주세요. | yeogi sewo juseyo | Please stop here. |
| 트렁크 좀 열어 주세요. | teureongkeu jom yeoreo juseyo | Could you open the trunk? |
| 에어컨 좀 켜 주세요. | eeokeon jom kyeo juseyo | Could you turn on the AC? |
| 천천히 가 주세요. | cheoncheonhi ga juseyo | Please drive slowly. |

## Lines for the restaurant and shop

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 덜 맵게 해 주세요. | deol maepge hae juseyo | Please make it less spicy. |
| 이거 빼 주세요. | igeo ppae juseyo | Please leave this out. |
| 포장해 주세요. | pojanghae juseyo | To go, please. |
| 계산해 주세요. | gyesanhae juseyo | The bill, please. |
| 봉투에 담아 주세요. | bongtu-e dama juseyo | Could you put it in a bag? |

<Callout type="tip" title="좀: the word that makes it polite">

Slip **좀** (jom, literally "a little") in front of the verb and the request softens from "do this" to "could you…?": **사진 좀 찍어 주세요**, **창문 좀 닫아 주세요**. It costs one syllable and Koreans use it constantly.

</Callout>

## Softer still

For a stranger, a favor that takes effort, or anyone clearly older, step up the politeness:

| Form | Feel | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -아/어 주세요 | polite, everyday | 사진 좀 찍어 주세요. |
| -아/어 주시겠어요? | more polite, a question | 사진 좀 찍어 주시겠어요? |
| -아/어 주실 수 있어요? | "would you be able to…?" | 사진 좀 찍어 주실 수 있어요? |

The last one combines this pattern with [-(으)ㄹ 수 있다](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-can-su-itda-eopda).

---

## Why not just -(으)세요?

**-(으)세요** is also polite, but it tells the other person what to do for their own sake: **앉으세요** ("please have a seat"), **들어오세요** ("come in"). Hosts and staff say it to you.

When *you* want something done for *you*, it can sound like an order. **여기 세우세요** to a taxi driver sounds like "stop here." **여기 세워 주세요** sounds like "please stop here for me."

---

## Common mistakes

**Keeping the 요.** 찍어요 주세요 doubles the ending. Drop it: 찍어 주세요.

**Using -(으)세요 for favors.** 기다리세요 to a stranger is an instruction. Ask with 기다려 주세요.

**Forgetting 하다 → 해.** 포장하 주세요 is wrong. Say 포장해 주세요.

**Noun + 아/어 주세요.** 물 어 주세요 doesn't exist. Nouns take plain 주세요: 물 주세요.

---

## 2-minute recap

- **noun + 주세요** = please give me: 물 주세요.
- **verb + 아/어 주세요** = please do it for me: 세워 주세요, 해 주세요.
- Build it from the 아요/어요 form minus 요.
- Add **좀** to soften: 사진 좀 찍어 주세요.
- More polite: **-아/어 주시겠어요?**
- **-(으)세요** is for telling people what to do for themselves, so it's not the right way to ask a favor.
