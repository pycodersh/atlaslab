If you'll stay in Korea for more than 90 days, you need to register for an Alien Registration Card (ARC, now often called a Residence Card) within 90 days of arrival. Book an appointment at your local immigration office through HiKorea, bring the documents below, and expect to wait a few weeks for the card.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Deadline:** register within 90 days of arriving in Korea
- **Where:** the immigration office for your area, booked through the HiKorea website
- **Fee:** around 30,000 KRW (check the current amount on HiKorea)
- **Avoid:** waiting until the deadline; appointments in university areas book up fast

</KeyTakeaways>

## Why the ARC matters

| You need an ARC to... | Note |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Open a full bank account** | Many banks require it |
| **Get a phone plan in your name** | Long-term contracts usually need it |
| **Join National Health Insurance** | Linked to your registration |
| **Re-enter Korea easily** | Registered residents can leave and return during their stay |
| **Sign leases and use some apps** | Identity verification often needs it |

## Documents to bring

| Document | Note |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Passport** | Original, with your visa |
| **Application form** | Available at the office or online |
| **Photo** | Passport-style, 3.5 × 4.5 cm, white background |
| **Certificate of enrollment** | From your university's international office |
| **Proof of address** | Lease, dorm certificate or host confirmation |
| **Fee** | Paid at the office, usually by card or revenue stamp |

Requirements can vary by visa type and office, so check the latest list on HiKorea or with your university before your appointment.

<Callout type="tip" title="Ask your university first">
Many universities run group ARC applications for new international students, which saves you booking an appointment yourself. Check with the international office before you book.
</Callout>

## Step by step

| Step | What to do | Tip |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Book** | Reserve a visit on HiKorea for your local immigration office | Book as soon as you arrive |
| **2. Prepare** | Gather documents and photos | Bring copies as well as originals |
| **3. Visit** | Submit documents and fingerprints | Arrive early; offices are busy |
| **4. Wait** | Processing usually takes a few weeks | Your passport stays with you |
| **5. Collect** | Pick up in person or choose delivery if offered | Bring your receipt |

While you wait, keep your application receipt with your passport. It shows you've applied if a bank, phone shop or employer asks.

## After you get your card

| Task | Deadline / note |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Report an address change** | Within a set period after moving, usually 14 days |
| **Extend your stay** | Apply before your permitted stay expires |
| **Lost card** | Report it and apply for a replacement |
| **Leaving Korea for good** | Return the card at departure immigration |

- Book your appointment as soon as you arrive
- Bring originals and copies of every document

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need an ARC for a short stay?**
No. Visitors staying 90 days or less don't need one. Registration applies to longer stays.

**How long does the ARC take?**
Usually a few weeks after your appointment, depending on the office and season. Start of semester is busiest.

**Can I travel abroad while my ARC is being processed?**
Check with the immigration office first. Travel rules during processing can depend on your visa and situation.

**What happens if I miss the 90-day deadline?**
You may be fined and it can complicate future applications. Register as soon as possible and explain the delay at the office.
