Andong jjimdak (안동찜닭) is chicken braised in a sweet, savory soy sauce with potatoes, carrots, glass noodles and a few dried chilies. Unlike many red Korean chicken dishes, it's dark brown and only mildly spicy, which makes it a great choice if you're avoiding heat.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **What it is:** soy-braised chicken with vegetables and chewy glass noodles
- **Spice:** mild; dried chilies add a gentle kick that you can ask to reduce
- **Portion:** usually ordered by size (half, whole) to share
- **Avoid:** ordering it expecting a fiery red dish; it's sweet and savory

</KeyTakeaways>

## Why it's soy-based

Andong is an inland city in North Gyeongsang Province, known for its Confucian heritage and traditional Hahoe Village. The dish is said to have grown out of Andong's old market alleys, where cooks braised chicken in soy sauce with vegetables to serve generous portions cheaply. Soy sauce and dried chilies gave it a savory, glossy sauce rather than the gochujang-red style of other regions, and it spread nationwide as a franchise favorite in the late 1990s and 2000s.

Glass noodles (당면) soak up the sauce and are many people's favorite part. Potatoes and carrots soften into the braise, and green onion goes on at the end.

- **Sweetness:** comes from sugar or corn syrup in the soy sauce
- **Heat:** from a handful of dried red chilies, easy to avoid
- **Comfort food:** often eaten with plain rice to soak up the sauce

<Callout type="tip" title="Ask for less heat">
If you're very sensitive to spice, ask for fewer chilies (고추 빼 주세요 or 덜 맵게 해 주세요). The dish stays just as flavorful.
</Callout>

## Making jjimdak at home

Jjimdak is a forgiving one-pot dish to cook at home. Brown bone-in chicken pieces, then add a sauce of soy sauce, sugar or corn syrup, garlic, ginger and water. Add potato and carrot chunks with a few dried red chilies and simmer, covered, for about 25 minutes. Soak glass noodles in water while it cooks, then add them in the last few minutes with onion and green onion so they absorb the sauce.

- **Chicken:** bone-in thighs and drumsticks stay juicy
- **Noodles:** add late, or they'll soak up all the sauce
- **Spice:** use fewer dried chilies for a milder dish

## Ordering jjimdak

Jjimdak is usually ordered by size: a half (반 마리) for one to two people, a whole (한 마리) for two to three. Many restaurants let you add extra glass noodles, cheese or rice cakes. It's served in a big platter or pot to share, with rice and pickled radish.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Andong jjimdak spicy?**
It's mild. Dried chilies add a little heat, and you can ask for fewer.

**What does jjimdak taste like?**
Sweet and savory, with a glossy soy-garlic sauce, tender chicken and chewy glass noodles.

**How much jjimdak should I order?**
A half chicken for one to two people, a whole for two to three. Add rice and extra noodles.

**Where is Andong?**
An inland city in North Gyeongsang Province, also known for Hahoe Folk Village and salted mackerel.
