Seoul's city buses reach places the subway doesn't, and they're cheap and frequent. Board at the front and tap your card, press the stop button before your stop, and tap again as you leave through the back door so your transfer discount works.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Board at the front, exit at the back** on most city buses
- **Tap twice:** on boarding and when getting off, for free or discounted transfers
- **Bus colors** tell you the route type: blue, green, red and yellow
- **Avoid:** boarding with a takeout coffee; drivers can refuse open drinks

</KeyTakeaways>

## Bus colors and types

| Color | Type | Route |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Blue** | Trunk (간선) | Long routes across the city along major roads |
| **Green** | Branch (지선) | Shorter routes linking neighborhoods to subway stations |
| **Red** | Express / metropolitan (광역) | Seoul to satellite cities, fewer stops |
| **Yellow** | Circular (순환) | Loops around central areas |
| **Small green** | Village bus (마을버스) | Very short local routes, often uphill neighborhoods |

## How to ride, step by step

| Step | What to do | Tip |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Find the stop** | Use Naver Map or KakaoMap for the stop and arrival time | Stops show route numbers and next stops |
| **2. Signal** | Step forward or raise your hand as the bus approaches | Buses may skip stops if nobody signals |
| **3. Board at the front** | Tap your card on the reader by the driver | Cash is being phased out on many routes |
| **4. Ride** | Hold on; buses brake suddenly | Screens and announcements show the next stop in English |
| **5. Press the stop button** | Before your stop | Buttons are on poles and walls |
| **6. Tap out and exit** | Tap at the back door reader as you leave | Needed for transfer discounts |

## Transfers

The rules below apply to Seoul's city buses and subway, and similar systems run in most Korean cities.

Tapping out unlocks transfer discounts between buses and the subway. Transfers usually need to happen within about 30 minutes of getting off (longer late at night), and you pay only for the extra distance.

| Situation | What happens |
| :--- | :--- |
| **You tap out, then tap into a subway or bus within the time limit** | Transfer discount applies |
| **You forget to tap out** | You may be charged a full fare on the next ride |
| **Several people on one card** | Possible on buses if you tell the driver; easier with separate cards |

<Callout type="warn" title="Takeout cups">
Seoul buses can refuse passengers carrying open takeout drinks, such as coffee or bubble tea, for safety and cleanliness. Finish your drink or bring a sealed bottle.
</Callout>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to tap out on Seoul buses?**
Yes, if you want transfer discounts. Tap the reader by the back door as you leave.

**Can I pay the bus fare with cash?**
Some buses still take cash, but many routes are going card-only. A T-money or Climate Card is the reliable option.

**Are there English announcements?**
Yes. Most city buses announce major stops in English and show them on screens.

**How late do buses run?**
Most routes run until around midnight. Night buses (marked N) cover major routes after that.
