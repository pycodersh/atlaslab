Korean-Chinese food (중식) is a cuisine of its own, and the big three are jajangmyeon (black bean noodles), jjamppong (spicy seafood noodle soup) and tangsuyuk (sweet and sour pork). Can't choose between the noodles? Order jjamjjamyeon, a split bowl with both.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Jajangmyeon:** savory-sweet black bean sauce, not spicy, around 7,000–9,000 KRW
- **Jjamppong:** spicy red seafood broth, around 9,000–12,000 KRW
- **Can't decide:** 짬짜면 (jjamjjamyeon), half of each in a divided bowl
- **Avoid:** pouring sauce over the whole tangsuyuk plate before asking your table

</KeyTakeaways>

## The big three compared

| Dish | Korean | What it is | Spice | Best for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Jajangmyeon** | 짜장면 | Wheat noodles in thick black bean sauce with pork and onion | None | First-timers, kids, comfort food |
| **Jjamppong** | 짬뽕 | Noodles in spicy seafood and vegetable broth | Medium to hot | Spice lovers, hangovers |
| **Tangsuyuk** | 탕수육 | Crispy fried pork with sweet and sour sauce | None | Sharing between two or more |
| **Jjamjjamyeon** | 짬짜면 | Half jajangmyeon, half jjamppong | Mixed | The indecisive |
| **Bokkeumbap** | 볶음밥 | Fried rice, usually with a little jajang sauce and soup | None | A rice option |

## Pour or dip? The tangsuyuk debate

| Style | Korean | How | Who prefers it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pour** | 부먹 (*bumeok*) | Sauce poured over the whole plate | Those who like soft, saucy pieces |
| **Dip** | 찍먹 (*jjikmeok*) | Dip each piece in the sauce bowl | Those who want them crispy |

<Callout type="tip" title="Ask before you pour">
Pour versus dip is a genuine, light-hearted debate in Korea. Ask 부먹이에요, 찍먹이에요? before pouring the sauce over a shared plate.
</Callout>

## Delivery and Black Day

Jajangmyeon is Korea's original delivery food: it arrives in covered bowls, and the restaurant may even collect the dishes later. On April 14, "Black Day", single people traditionally eat jajangmyeon together as a joke counterpart to Valentine's and White Day.

| Occasion | What people order |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Moving day** | Jajangmyeon delivered to the new home |
| **Black Day (April 14)** | Jajangmyeon among single friends |
| **Group lunch** | Noodles each, plus tangsuyuk to share |

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Mix the jajang sauce thoroughly before eating
- Order tangsuyuk to share, sized 소 · 중 · 대 (small, medium, large)
- Ask whether the table prefers pour or dip
- Use scissors to cut long noodles if offered

</Do>
<Dont>

- Expect Chinese food from China; this is a Korean style
- Pour sauce over shared tangsuyuk without asking
- Worry about slurping noodles; it's normal
- Wear white when eating jajangmyeon; the sauce splashes

</Dont>
</DoDont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is jajangmyeon spicy?**
No. The black bean sauce is savory and slightly sweet. Jjamppong is the spicy one.

**What is the difference between Korean and Chinese jajangmian?**
Korean jajangmyeon uses a sweeter black bean paste (춘장) and a thick, glossy sauce. It developed in Korea's Chinese communities and is distinct from the Chinese original.

**What size tangsuyuk should two people order?**
A small (소) usually suits two people having noodles each. Choose medium for three or four.

**Can I get jajangmyeon delivered?**
Yes. It's one of the most common delivery foods in Korea, usually arriving within 30–40 minutes.
