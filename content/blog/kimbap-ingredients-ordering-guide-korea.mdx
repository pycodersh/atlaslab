Kimbap (김밥) is rice and fillings rolled in seaweed and sliced into rounds: Korea's go-to quick meal, picnic food and road-trip snack. Order a roll at a kimbap shop for about 4,000–6,000 KRW, or grab a triangle version from any convenience store.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Classic roll:** around 3,500–5,000 KRW at a kimbap shop
- **Where:** kimbap chains, bunsik snack shops, markets and convenience stores
- **Fillings:** pickled radish, egg, carrot, spinach, fish cake, ham or crab stick
- **Avoid:** confusing it with Japanese sushi; kimbap rice is seasoned with sesame oil, not vinegar

</KeyTakeaways>

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Classic** | 기본 김밥 / 야채김밥 | Pickled radish, egg, carrot, spinach, ham, fish cake | 3,500–5,000 KRW |

## Kimbap vs sushi

| | Kimbap | Sushi rolls |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rice seasoning** | Sesame oil and salt | Vinegar, sugar, salt |
| **Fillings** | Mostly cooked or pickled | Often raw fish |
| **Eaten with** | Nothing; just pick up a slice | Soy sauce and wasabi |
| **Occasion** | Quick meals, picnics, school trips | Restaurant meals |

<Callout type="tip" title="Opening a triangle kimbap">
Convenience store triangles have numbered tabs: pull 1 down the middle, then 2 and 3 to the sides. The seaweed stays crisp because it's wrapped separately until you open it.
</Callout>

## What goes inside a classic roll

The standard roll balances crunchy, sweet, savory and fresh fillings. Knowing them helps if you need to avoid an ingredient.

| Filling | Korean | Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Yellow pickled radish** | 단무지 | Crunch and sweetness |
| **Egg strip** | 계란 | Soft, mild |
| **Carrot** | 당근 | Color and sweetness |
| **Spinach** | 시금치 | Seasoned with sesame oil |
| **Fish cake** | 어묵 | Savory and chewy |
| **Ham or crab stick** | 햄 · 맛살 | Saltiness |
| **Burdock root** | 우엉 | Sweet-soy, earthy |

To leave something out, say [ingredient] 빼 주세요, for example 햄 빼 주세요 (no ham, please).

## Ordering at a kimbap shop

Kimbap shops such as the Kimbap Cheonguk (김밥천국) chain sell rolls alongside cheap hot dishes, making them a great budget stop.

| Also on the menu | What it is |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ramyeon** | Instant noodles cooked to order |
| **Tteokbokki** | Spicy rice cakes |
| **Dolsot bibimbap** | Rice bowl in a hot stone pot |
| **Donkatsu** | Breaded pork cutlet with sauce |
| **Rabokki** | Tteokbokki with ramyeon noodles |

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Count rolls with 줄 (*jul*): 한 줄, 두 줄
- Eat kimbap the same day; rice hardens in the fridge
- Pair it with ramyeon or tteokbokki for a full meal
- Take kimbap to go for picnics and day trips

</Do>
<Dont>

- Dip kimbap in soy sauce like sushi
- Leave kimbap in a hot car for hours
- Expect raw fish inside
- Throw away hard leftover kimbap; dip it in egg and pan-fry it

</Dont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is kimbap the same as sushi?**
No. Kimbap rice is seasoned with sesame oil, fillings are mostly cooked or pickled, and it's eaten without soy sauce.

**Is kimbap vegetarian?**
The classic roll usually contains ham, fish cake or crab stick. Ask for 야채김밥 and check, or ask staff to leave out the meat.

**How long does kimbap keep?**
It's best eaten the same day. Refrigerated rice turns hard, so leftovers are often dipped in egg and pan-fried.

**What does 마약김밥 mean?**
Literally "drug kimbap", a playful name meaning addictive. They're small, simple rolls served with a mustard-soy dip, famous at Gwangjang Market.
