Korean prices look familiar on a tag: 12,900원, ₩35,000. The trouble starts when someone says them out loud. English groups big numbers by the thousand; Korean groups them by the **ten thousand**, so 35,000 comes out as "three ten-thousands and five thousand". Once that clicks, every price is easy to read and to hear.

## The units

Money always uses **Sino-Korean** numbers (일, 이, 삼, 사, 오…), never native ones. The place-value words are:

| Value | Korean | Romanization |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 십 | sip |
| 100 | 백 | baek |
| 1,000 | 천 | cheon |
| **10,000** | **만** | **man** |
| 100,000 | 십만 | simman |
| 1,000,000 | 백만 | baengman |
| 10,000,000 | 천만 | cheonman |
| 100,000,000 | 억 | eok |

The key row is **만**. Korean has no single word for "a hundred thousand" or "a million." Those are 십만 (ten 만) and 백만 (a hundred 만).

One more rule: when the number in front of 십, 백, 천 or 만 is one, you drop it. It's **천 원**, not 일천 원, and **만 원**, not 일만 원.

## Reading any price in two steps

1. **Cut the number four digits from the right.** 35,000 becomes 3 | 5000.
2. **Read the left part + 만, then the right part.** 삼만 + 오천 = **삼만 오천 원**.

| On the tag | Split | Say it |
|---|---|---|
| 3,500원 | 3500 | 삼천오백 원 |
| 9,900원 | 9900 | 구천구백 원 |
| 12,900원 | 1 \| 2900 | 만 이천구백 원 |
| 50,000원 | 5 \| 0000 | 오만 원 |
| 128,000원 | 12 \| 8000 | 십이만 팔천 원 |
| 1,500,000원 | 150 \| 0000 | 백오십만 원 |

Korean banknotes come in 1,000, 5,000, 10,000 and 50,000 won, so the words **천 원, 오천 원, 만 원, 오만 원** are the four you'll hear most at a till.

To get a feel for what those amounts buy, from street food to a sit-down meal, try the [Korean Won Price Sense tool](/tools/krw-price-sense).

## At the counter

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 이거 얼마예요? | igeo eolmayeyo? | How much is this? |
| 다 해서 얼마예요? | da haeseo eolmayeyo? | How much is it altogether? |
| 카드 돼요? | kadeu dwaeyo? | Can I pay by card? |
| 현금으로 할게요. | hyeongeumeuro halgeyo | I'll pay in cash. |
| 영수증 주세요. | yeongsujeung juseyo | A receipt, please. |

Staff usually answer with the total and the word **입니다** or **이에요**: 만 이천 원입니다 ("That's 12,000 won").

<Callout type="tip" title="Hearing prices fast">

Listen for **만** first. Whatever comes before it is the number of 10,000s, and whatever comes after is under 10,000. 사만 칠천 원 is 4 × 10,000 + 7,000 = 47,000 won. If a number goes by too fast, **다시 한번 말해 주세요** ("could you say that once more?") is always fine, or just hold up your phone calculator.

</Callout>

## Haggling, briefly

Fixed prices are the norm in shops, cafes and convenience stores. In traditional markets and some street stalls, a friendly ask is fine:

- **조금만 깎아 주세요.** — *jogeumman kkakka juseyo* — Could you knock a little off?
- **두 개 사면 얼마예요?** — *du gae samyeon eolmayeyo?* — How much if I buy two?

Don't try it in department stores or chains. The [counters guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-counters-gae-myeong-jan-byeong) covers 두 개 and the other counters you'll need when buying more than one, and the [market guide](/blog/en/k-patto/shopping-at-a-korean-traditional-market) has the rest of the market phrases.

## Common mistakes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 십천 원 | 만 원 | 10,000 has its own word. |
| 일천 원, 일만 원 | 천 원, 만 원 | Drop the "one" before 천 and 만. |
| 다섯 천 원 | 오천 원 | Money is always Sino-Korean. |
| 백이십팔천 | 십이만 팔천 | Split at 10,000, not at the comma. |
