Bingsu (빙수) is a mountain of finely shaved milk ice topped with fruit, red beans or roasted soybean powder, and it's Korea's best-known dessert. One bowl is meant for two or three people, and most cafes serve it year-round.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Classic flavor:** patbingsu, with sweet red beans and chewy rice cake
- **Budget:** about 10,000–18,000 KRW at a dessert cafe; hotel versions cost far more
- **Share it:** one bingsu for two or three people is normal
- **Avoid:** ordering one per person; you'll leave most of it

</KeyTakeaways>

## Bingsu flavors to know

Modern bingsu uses frozen milk instead of plain water, so the ice is soft, fluffy and creamy rather than crunchy. These are the flavors you'll see most often.

| Flavor | Toppings | Taste | Good for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Patbingsu (팥빙수)** | Sweet red beans, rice cake, condensed milk | Earthy, sweet, nostalgic | The classic first try |
| **Injeolmi bingsu (인절미빙수)** | Roasted soybean powder, chewy rice cake, almonds | Nutty, like peanut butter | People who don't like red beans |
| **Mango bingsu (망고빙수)** | Fresh or frozen mango, sometimes mango sorbet | Bright, tropical | Summer, photos |
| **Strawberry bingsu (딸기빙수)** | Fresh strawberries, strawberry milk | Fresh, creamy | Winter and spring, peak strawberry season |
| **Coffee or matcha bingsu** | Espresso or green tea syrup, red beans | Bittersweet | After a heavy meal |

Hotels in Seoul compete with luxury mango bingsu every summer, and the most famous versions now cost well over 100,000 KRW. A dessert-cafe bingsu is a better first taste.

## How to order and eat it

Bingsu arrives with a few long spoons, one per person. Most dessert cafes take orders at a counter or kiosk, then call your number when it's ready.

Mix the toppings into the ice as you eat, or keep them on top and dig in from the edge. Koreans do both and will happily argue about which is correct.

<Callout type="tip" title="Eat it fast">
Milk ice melts much faster than water ice. Take photos first, then eat within 10 minutes.
</Callout>

## Other Korean desserts worth trying

Korean desserts are generally less sweet than American ones, with flavors like roasted grain, red bean, honey, cinnamon and sesame.

- **Yakgwa (약과):** honey-soaked, deep-fried wheat cookies with ginger; popular again with younger Koreans
- **Injeolmi (인절미):** soft rice cake coated in roasted soybean powder
- **Hotteok (호떡):** a pan-fried pancake filled with brown sugar, cinnamon and nuts; try our [hotteok recipe](/kpantry/en/recipes/hotteok)
- **Bungeoppang (붕어빵):** fish-shaped waffle filled with red bean, a winter street classic
- **Dalgona (달고나):** a brittle honeycomb candy of sugar and baking soda
- **Ttungcaron (뚱카롱):** "fat" macarons, overstuffed with buttercream

Traditional drinks also double as dessert: sikhye, a sweet rice drink, and sujeonggwa, a cinnamon-ginger punch, are often served at the end of a meal.

## Making bingsu at home

You don't need a bingsu machine for a good home version.

1. **Freeze** milk sweetened with a little condensed milk in a shallow container.
2. **Scrape** it with a fork every 30 minutes, or freeze it solid and shave it with a box grater.
3. **Top** it with canned sweet red beans, chopped fruit or roasted soybean powder.
4. **Finish** with a drizzle of condensed milk and serve right away.

Canned sweet red beans (단팥) and roasted soybean powder (콩가루) are sold at Korean and most Asian grocery stores.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is bingsu made of?**
Finely shaved frozen milk or water, topped with sweet red beans, fruit, rice cake, roasted soybean powder or condensed milk.

**Is bingsu only a summer dessert?**
No. Cafes serve it year-round, and strawberry bingsu is a winter favorite.

**What's the difference between bingsu and a snow cone?**
Bingsu ice is shaved much finer and usually made from milk, so it's soft and creamy. Snow cones use crunchy water ice and syrup.

**How much does bingsu cost in Seoul?**
Usually about 10,000–18,000 KRW at a dessert cafe. Luxury hotel bingsu can cost many times more.
