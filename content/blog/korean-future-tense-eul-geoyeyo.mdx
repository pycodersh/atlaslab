After the present (먹어요) and the past (먹었어요), the future is one ending: **-(으)ㄹ 거예요**. It's short to build, and it behaves differently from English: the same ending covers plans and guesses.

## The rule

Take the verb stem (the verb minus 다), then add:

- **-ㄹ 거예요** if the stem ends in a vowel
- **-을 거예요** if the stem ends in a consonant

| Verb | Future | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 가다 | 갈 거예요 *gal geoyeyo* | I'll go |
| 보다 | 볼 거예요 *bol geoyeyo* | I'll see, watch |
| 하다 | 할 거예요 *hal geoyeyo* | I'll do |
| 먹다 | 먹을 거예요 *meogeul geoyeyo* | I'll eat |
| 읽다 | 읽을 거예요 *ilgeul geoyeyo* | I'll read |
| 입다 | 입을 거예요 *ibeul geoyeyo* | I'll wear |

Vowel stems gain a ㄹ on the last syllable (가 + ㄹ = 갈). Consonant stems add 을 as a separate syllable.

## The three tenses side by side

| Verb | Present · Past | Future |
|---|---|---|
| 가다 | 가요 · 갔어요 | 갈 거예요 |
| 먹다 | 먹어요 · 먹었어요 | 먹을 거예요 |
| 하다 | 해요 · 했어요 | 할 거예요 |

That's the whole system for everyday polite speech. The [conjugation guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-verb-conjugation-ayo-eoyo) and the [past tense guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-past-tense-explained) cover the first two.

## What it really means

The ending is built from 거 ("thing") plus 이에요 ("it is"), so 갈 거예요 is literally "it is the thing of going." That explains a feature English speakers don't expect: **-(으)ㄹ 거예요 covers both plans and guesses.**

**A plan or intention:**

- 내일 친구를 만날 거예요. — I'm going to meet a friend tomorrow.
- 주말에 쉴 거예요. — I'll rest this weekend.

**A prediction or guess:**

- 비가 올 거예요. — It's going to rain. / It will probably rain.
- 맛있을 거예요. — It'll probably be delicious.

The context tells you which one you mean. If you're talking about yourself, it usually means a plan. If you're talking about the weather or about someone else, it usually means a guess.

## 이다 and adjectives

The ending works with 이다 and adjectives too, though in this case it nearly always means a guess.

- 학생일 거예요. — He's probably a student.
- 바쁠 거예요. — She's probably busy.
- 괜찮을 거예요. — It'll be fine.

This is the natural way to say "probably" in Korean, and you can use it for the present as well as the future.

## Irregular verbs

A few verbs change before this ending. The most common:

| Verb | Future | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 만들다 (to make) | 만들 거예요 | I'll make |
| 살다 (to live) | 살 거예요 | I'll live |
| 걷다 (to walk) | 걸을 거예요 | I'll walk |
| 돕다 (to help) | 도울 거예요 | I'll help |

Verbs that end in ㄹ keep their stem as it is (만들 + 거예요), since the ㄹ is already there. The ㄷ and ㅂ irregular verbs change the same way they do in the other endings.

## Other ways to talk about the future

Korean often uses the plain present with a time word, just as English says "I leave tomorrow."

- 내일 가요. — I'm going tomorrow. (a schedule or a decision made)
- 내일 갈 거예요. — I'm going to go tomorrow. (a plan or intention)

The present form sounds like a fixed schedule. The -(으)ㄹ 거예요 form sounds like an intention.

When you're making a decision on the spot, use **-(으)ㄹ게요** ("I'll do it"):

- 제가 할게요. — I'll do it. (offering, promising)
- 먼저 갈게요. — I'll head out first.

And for suggestions, use **-(으)ㄹ까요?** ("Shall we...?"):

- 같이 갈까요? — Shall we go together?

## Common mistakes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| (offering) 제가 할 거예요 | 제가 할게요 | A promise to the person in front of you takes -(으)ㄹ게요. |
| 가을 거예요 | 갈 거예요 | Vowel stems take just ㄹ. |
| 갈거예요 | 갈 거예요 | 거 is a separate word. |
| 내일은 화요일일 거예요 | 내일은 화요일이에요 | For a fact, -(으)ㄹ 거예요 sounds like "probably". |
