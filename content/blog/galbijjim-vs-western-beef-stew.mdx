Galbijjim (갈비찜) is beef short ribs braised slowly in a sweet soy sauce with radish, carrots, chestnuts and jujubes until the meat falls off the bone. It's a celebration dish for holidays and family gatherings, and spicy versions have become a popular restaurant meal.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **What it is:** soy-braised beef short ribs, sweet and savory
- **When:** holidays like Seollal and Chuseok, birthdays and special dinners
- **Restaurant versions:** classic soy or spicy (매운 갈비찜), often with cheese
- **Avoid:** confusing it with grilled galbi; galbijjim is braised

</KeyTakeaways>

## Galbijjim vs Western beef stew

| | Galbijjim (갈비찜) | Western beef stew |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cut** | Beef short ribs on the bone | Chuck or stewing beef, boneless |
| **Liquid** | Soy sauce, sugar, garlic, pear, sesame oil | Stock, wine, tomato |
| **Vegetables** | Radish, carrots, chestnuts, jujubes, shiitake | Carrots, potatoes, onions, celery |
| **Flavor** | Sweet, savory, glossy | Savory, rich, often winey |
| **Served with** | Rice and banchan | Bread, mashed potatoes |
| **Occasion** | Holidays and special meals | Everyday comfort food |

Galbijjim also has a reputation as a premium dish because beef short ribs are expensive in Korea, especially hanwoo (Korean native beef). That's why it's associated with holidays, weddings and honoring guests, while everyday braises use cheaper cuts or chicken.

## How galbijjim is made

The short ribs are soaked in cold water to remove blood, then often parboiled to clear the broth. They're braised in a sauce of soy sauce, sugar or honey, grated Asian pear, garlic and sesame oil, along with chunks of radish and carrot. Chestnuts and jujubes go in later, and the dish simmers until the sauce reduces to a glossy glaze. Pear helps tenderize the meat and adds natural sweetness.

- **Time:** a couple of hours on the stove, faster in a pressure cooker
- **Garnish:** egg strips, pine nuts and green onion for holiday tables
- **Leftovers:** taste even better the next day as the flavors deepen

<Callout type="tip" title="Spicy galbijjim">
Many restaurants serve a spicy version made with gochugaru, often topped with melted cheese and finished with fried rice in the same pot. It's very popular with younger diners and groups.
</Callout>

## Other Korean jjim dishes

Jjim (찜) means braised or steamed, and galbijjim is just one of many jjim dishes. If you enjoy it, try these on Korean menus:

- **Andong jjimdak (안동찜닭):** soy-braised chicken with glass noodles, mild and saucy
- **Agujjim (아구찜):** spicy braised monkfish with bean sprouts, a seafood favorite
- **Haemuljjim (해물찜):** spicy braised mixed seafood, served in big platters for groups
- **Dakbokkeumtang (닭볶음탕):** spicy braised chicken with potatoes, sometimes called dakdoritang
- **Gyeran-jjim (계란찜):** fluffy steamed egg, a mild side dish

Many jjim restaurants finish the meal by making fried rice in the leftover sauce, just like dakgalbi and BBQ restaurants. Ask for 볶음밥 when the main dish is nearly done.

## Where to eat galbijjim

Galbijjim restaurants serve it by portion size or per person, often with a two-serving minimum. Spicy galbijjim chains are common in Seoul, and traditional Korean restaurants serve the classic soy version as part of set meals. Daegu's Dongin-dong is famous for spicy galbijjim served in aluminum pots.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is galbijjim?**
Beef short ribs braised in sweet soy sauce with radish, carrots, chestnuts and jujubes.

**Is galbijjim spicy?**
The classic version isn't. Spicy galbijjim, made with chili flakes, is a separate restaurant favorite.

**What's the difference between galbi and galbijjim?**
Galbi is grilled; galbijjim is braised slowly in sauce until tender.

**When do Koreans eat galbijjim?**
On holidays like Seollal and Chuseok, birthdays and special family dinners.
