Namul (나물) means seasoned vegetables, and one simple method makes dozens of Korean side dishes: blanch the vegetable, squeeze it dry, and toss it with garlic, sesame oil and salt or soy sauce. Master it once and you can make spinach, bean sprout and fern namul for banchan or bibimbap.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Basic method:** blanch, rinse, squeeze, season
- **Core seasoning:** garlic, sesame oil, salt or soup soy sauce, sesame seeds
- **Uses:** banchan, bibimbap toppings, lunch boxes
- **Avoid:** skipping the squeeze; watery namul tastes bland

</KeyTakeaways>

## The basic method

1. **Blanch** leafy vegetables briefly in salted boiling water, until just wilted.
2. **Rinse** in cold water to stop the cooking and keep the color bright.
3. **Squeeze** out as much water as possible with your hands.
4. **Season** with minced garlic, sesame oil, a pinch of salt or soup soy sauce, and sesame seeds.
5. **Toss with your hands** so the seasoning coats every piece; Koreans call this 무치다 (to dress).

Raw namul (생채) like spicy radish skip the blanching, while dried namul like bracken and radish greens are soaked and simmered first.

<Callout type="tip" title="Season with your hands">
Korean cooks toss namul by hand, sometimes with food-safe gloves. It coats the vegetables evenly and lets you feel when the seasoning is right.
</Callout>

## Seasonal namul

Namul changes with the seasons, and many Koreans look forward to the first wild greens of spring. In spring, markets fill with shepherd's purse (냉이), mugwort (쑥), wild chives (달래) and other mountain greens, often seasoned simply or added to doenjang soup. Summer brings cucumber, eggplant and zucchini namul, while autumn and winter rely on dried vegetables preserved from the harvest, like dried radish greens and bracken fern.

Dried namul (묵나물) is soaked and simmered before seasoning. It was a way to keep vegetables through winter and is still eaten on the first full moon of the lunar year (정월대보름), when families prepare nine kinds of namul.

- **Spring:** naengi, dallae and ssuk, fresh and slightly bitter
- **Summer:** cucumber, eggplant, zucchini
- **Winter:** dried namul like siraegi (radish greens) and gosari (bracken)
- **Perilla oil:** often used instead of sesame oil for dried namul

## Using namul

Namul is one of the most common banchan, served with almost every Korean meal. Several namul together form the toppings for **bibimbap**. On holidays, families prepare a set of three colors of namul (삼색나물): white (bean sprouts or bellflower), green (spinach) and brown (bracken). Namul keeps in the fridge for 2–3 days and is perfect for lunch boxes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is namul?**
Seasoned vegetables, usually blanched or stir-fried and dressed with garlic, sesame oil and salt or soy sauce.

**How long does namul keep?**
About 2–3 days in the fridge in a sealed container.

**Is namul vegetarian?**
Usually, though some versions use salted shrimp or fish sauce. Check when buying.

**Which namul go in bibimbap?**
Typically spinach, bean sprouts, bracken fern, zucchini and carrot, plus others by season.
