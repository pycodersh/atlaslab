Tteokbokki (떡볶이) is chewy rice cakes in a sweet-spicy red sauce, and it's the star of bunsik (분식), Korea's cheap snack-food menu. Order the classic 떡튀순 combo (tteokbokki, fried snacks and sundae) and dip the fried pieces in the tteokbokki sauce.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Price:** tteokbokki around 4,000–6,000 KRW per portion; combos around 10,000–15,000 KRW
- **Spice:** sweet first, then a building heat; most stalls are medium
- **The combo:** 떡튀순 = tteokbokki + twigim (fried snacks) + sundae
- **Avoid:** ordering extra-hot franchise tteokbokki on your first try

</KeyTakeaways>

## The bunsik menu

| Dish | Korean | What it is | Spice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tteokbokki** | 떡볶이 | Rice cakes and fish cake in red sauce | Medium |
| **Twigim** | 튀김 | Battered fried vegetables, squid, seaweed rolls, egg | None |
| **Sundae** | 순대 | Korean blood sausage with glass noodles, often with liver and lung | None |
| **Eomuk** | 어묵 | Fish cake skewers in hot broth | None |
| **Gimbap** | 김밥 | Rice rolls with fillings | None |
| **Rabokki** | 라볶이 | Tteokbokki with ramyeon noodles | Medium |

## How spicy is tteokbokki?

| Style | Where | Heat |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Street stall** | Markets, street carts | Medium; sweet and spicy |
| **Bunsik shop** | Neighborhood snack shops | Medium, often adjustable |
| **Franchise "extra spicy"** | Chains known for heat | Very hot; levels from mild to extreme |
| **Rose / cream** | Trendy shops | Mild and creamy |
| **Gungjung (royal)** | Traditional restaurants | Not spicy; soy-based |

## Twigim: what to pick

The fried snacks at bunsik shops sit in a glass case. Point at what you want, and the cook re-fries it to order.

| Twigim | Korean | What it is |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Seaweed roll** | 김말이 | Glass noodles wrapped in seaweed, then fried |
| **Squid** | 오징어튀김 | Battered squid strips |
| **Sweet potato** | 고구마튀김 | Sweet slices, a safe pick |
| **Vegetable** | 야채튀김 | A tangle of mixed shredded vegetables |
| **Boiled egg** | 계란 | Often served in the tteokbokki sauce |
| **Dumpling** | 만두튀김 | Fried dumplings |

Kimmari (seaweed roll) dipped in tteokbokki sauce is the combination most Koreans name as their favorite.

## Eating it like a local

| Tip | How |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Dip the fried snacks** | Dunk twigim in the tteokbokki sauce, or ask for it mixed in (버무려 주세요) |
| **Free broth** | At fish cake stalls, the hot broth is free; use the paper cup and ladle |
| **Sundae dip** | Salt, chili salt or ssamjang, depending on the region |
| **Share** | Combos are meant for the table; ask for extra forks or toothpicks |

<Callout type="info" title="Sundae differs by region">
In Seoul, sundae is usually dipped in salt with chili powder; in other regions it comes with ssamjang or a soy-based dip. See [why sundae dips change by region](/blog/en/k-pantry/sundae-differs-by-region).
</Callout>

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Try the 떡튀순 combo to taste everything at once
- Dip fried snacks in the tteokbokki sauce
- Help yourself to free fish cake broth at eomuk stalls
- Ask for 순한맛 (mild) if you're unsure

</Do>
<Dont>

- Order the hottest franchise level first
- Double-dip a bitten skewer in a shared sauce
- Expect sundae to taste like Western sausage; it's mild and chewy
- Leave skewers on the table; return them to the cup

</Dont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is tteokbokki too spicy for beginners?**
Most street and snack-shop tteokbokki is medium: sweet first, then warm. Order mild, rose or soy-based versions if you're cautious.

**What is sundae made of?**
Korean sundae is pig intestine stuffed with glass noodles, pork blood and seasonings. It's mild and chewy, often served with liver and lung.

**What does 떡튀순 mean?**
It's short for tteokbokki, twigim and sundae: the classic bunsik combo.

**Is fish cake broth really free?**
At most street eomuk stalls, yes. Paying for skewers includes the broth; pour yourself a cup.
