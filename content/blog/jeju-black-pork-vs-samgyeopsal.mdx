Jeju black pork (흑돼지, *heukdwaeji*) comes from a native black-haired pig breed raised on Jeju Island, prized for its firm, chewy texture and rich, nutty fat. It costs more than regular pork belly, but for many visitors a black pork BBQ dinner is the highlight of a Jeju trip.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **What it is:** pork from Jeju's black-haired pigs, grilled like samgyeopsal
- **Taste:** firmer meat, thicker skin, richer and less greasy fat than regular pork
- **Price:** noticeably higher than regular samgyeopsal, especially on the island's famous streets
- **Avoid:** ordering only one serving; most BBQ restaurants require two

</KeyTakeaways>

## Jeju black pork vs regular samgyeopsal

| | Jeju black pork (흑돼지) | Regular samgyeopsal (삼겹살) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pig** | Native black-haired Jeju breed | Common commercial breeds |
| **Texture** | Firm, chewy, skin often left on | Softer, more tender |
| **Fat** | Rich, nutty, less greasy | Juicy, can be heavier |
| **Cut thickness** | Often thick-cut | Usually thinner |
| **Classic dip** | Myeolchi-jeot (anchovy sauce) bubbling on the grill | Ssamjang, salt and sesame oil |
| **Price** | Higher | Lower |
| **Where** | Jeju, plus specialty restaurants in Seoul | Everywhere |

## How it's served on Jeju

Jeju black pork restaurants grill thick-cut slices over charcoal, often with the skin on, and staff usually cook it for you. A small dish of melted anchovy sauce (멜젓, *meljeot*) sits on the grill, and you dip each piece in it. It comes with lettuce, perilla leaves, garlic and kimchi, and many places finish with kimchi fried rice on the grill.

Jeju City has a well-known **Black Pork Street** (흑돼지 거리) near Jeju City Hall and in Seogwipo, lined with restaurants. Prices there are on the higher side, but quality is generally good.

<Callout type="tip" title="Try the anchovy dip">
The bubbling meljeot (salted anchovy sauce) is the Jeju way to eat black pork. It's salty and pungent, so dip lightly; it pairs surprisingly well with the rich fat.
</Callout>

Expect a lively, smoky atmosphere at popular black pork restaurants, especially in the evening. Reservations aren't usually needed, but famous spots can have a wait.

## More Jeju food to try

Black pork is Jeju's most famous dish, but the island has much more. Combine a black pork dinner with these local specialties during your trip.

- **Gogi-guksu (고기국수):** noodles in a rich pork broth topped with sliced pork, a Jeju classic
- **Okdom-gui (옥돔구이):** grilled tilefish, a prized local fish
- **Jeonbok (전복):** abalone, served as porridge (전복죽), grilled or raw
- **Haemul ttukbaegi (해물뚝배기):** a seafood stew in an earthenware pot
- **Hallabong (한라봉):** Jeju's sweet, large citrus fruit, sold everywhere in winter and spring
- **Haenyeo seafood:** fresh catch from Jeju's famous women divers, sold near the coast

Dongmun Market in Jeju City and Olle Market in Seogwipo are the easiest places to try many of these in one visit.

## Is it worth it?

If you're on Jeju, yes, at least once. The firmer texture and richer fat are different from mainland pork, and eating it on the island is part of the experience. In Seoul, black pork restaurants exist, but quality and price vary, so check reviews in Naver Map first.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is Jeju black pork?**
Pork from a native black-haired pig breed raised on Jeju Island, known for its firm texture and rich fat.

**Is Jeju black pork more expensive?**
Yes, it usually costs noticeably more than regular samgyeopsal.

**What is ogyeopsal?**
Pork belly with the skin left on, giving five layers. It's a popular cut for Jeju black pork.

**What sauce comes with Jeju black pork?**
Meljeot, a salted anchovy sauce warmed on the grill, plus the usual ssamjang and salt.
