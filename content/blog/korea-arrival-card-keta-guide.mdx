Most visitors need exactly one thing before flying to Korea: either a K-ETA or a free online e-Arrival Card, not both. Check which applies to your passport, file it from the official site only, and you'll walk through Incheon without filling in anything on the plane.

<KeyTakeaways title="Quick summary">

- **K-ETA exempt (22 countries and regions, through Dec 31, 2026):** including the US, Canada, the UK, Japan, Taiwan, Australia and most of Western Europe. Submit the free e-Arrival Card instead
- **Everyone else visiting visa-free:** apply for a K-ETA (10,000 KRW, valid 3 years). A K-ETA replaces the arrival card
- **e-Arrival Card:** free, online, within 3 days before you land (Korea time). No printout needed
- **Avoid:** look-alike sites charging 5–20 times the official fee. Use only `k-eta.go.kr` and `e-arrivalcard.go.kr`

</KeyTakeaways>

## Do you need a K-ETA?

Since 2021, travelers from Korea's visa-free countries need a K-ETA (Korea Electronic Travel Authorization) to board. A temporary exemption covers 22 countries and regions, and it has been extended through **December 31, 2026**.

| Your situation | What to submit | Cost |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Exempt list:** US (incl. Guam), Canada, UK, Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Macau, Singapore, Australia, New Zealand, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Netherlands, Belgium, Austria, Denmark, Finland, Norway, Sweden, Poland | e-Arrival Card (or an optional K-ETA) | Free |
| **Other visa-free countries** | K-ETA, before you fly | 10,000 KRW |
| **You need a visa for Korea** | Your visa, plus the e-Arrival Card | Visa fee |
| **Korean residence card holders** | Nothing extra | Free |

Exemptions are reviewed every year, so confirm your status on the official K-ETA site a few weeks before travel. If you're exempt but apply for a K-ETA anyway, it still works: it skips the arrival card and some travelers find it smoother at the gate.

**Applying for a K-ETA:** use [k-eta.go.kr](https://www.k-eta.go.kr) or the official K-ETA app. The government fee is 10,000 KRW, approval is valid for three years (or until your passport expires), and you should apply at least 72 hours before departure. Korea's foreign ministry has warned about agency sites charging far more; the government has not appointed any agency.

<Callout type="warn" title="Fake K-ETA and arrival card sites">

Search ads often show look-alike sites first. The e-Arrival Card is always **free**, and the K-ETA is **10,000 KRW**. If a site asks for more, or the address isn't `.go.kr`, close it.

</Callout>

## How to fill in the e-Arrival Card

The e-Arrival Card was introduced in February 2025. File it at [e-arrivalcard.go.kr](https://www.e-arrivalcard.go.kr). It takes about five minutes.

| Step | What to enter | Tip |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Timing** | Submit within 3 days before arrival, Korea time (GMT+9) | Landing on the 31st? The earliest is the 29th |
| **2. Passport** | Name, number and nationality exactly as printed | A typo means filing again |
| **3. Flight** | Flight number and arrival date | Check the arrival date in Korea time |
| **4. Address in Korea** | Hotel name plus its English road-name address | Copy it from your booking confirmation |
| **5. Phone** | Your hotel's number, or your eSIM/roaming number | Use the +82 format for Korean numbers |
| **6. Purpose and stay** | Tourism, business, visiting family; length of stay | Match your return ticket |

A submitted card is valid for 72 hours. If your trip is delayed beyond that, submit a new one. Parents or guardians file for children under 14, and groups of 10 or more can use the group form. Korean addresses come in two formats; the [road-name vs lot-number guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-road-name-address-vs-lot-number-guide) shows which one to copy.

## Paper cards, customs and Q-CODE on the plane

Crew may still hand out paper forms. Here's which ones you need:

| Form | Do you need it? |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Paper arrival card** | No, if you've filed the e-Arrival Card or hold a K-ETA. Paper cards ran alongside the online form through 2025; filing online avoids any doubt |
| **Customs declaration** | Only if you have items to declare (above the duty-free allowance, large cash, certain foods). One per family. It can also be filed online through the Korea Customs app |
| **Q-CODE (health declaration)** | Only if you've recently been in an area on Korea's quarantine watch list, or you have symptoms. File at [q-code.kdca.go.kr](https://q-code.kdca.go.kr); the list changes, so check before flying |

## Getting through immigration at Incheon

After landing, follow the signs to Arrivals and immigration. There are two kinds of lanes.

**Smart Entry Service (e-gates):** automated gates for eligible passports. As of March 2026, they cover 42 countries and regions for travelers aged 17 and over. Some nationalities still have to register first (US citizens need Global Entry, for example). If the screen rejects your passport, join the staffed line.

**Foreigners line (staffed counter):** the officer scans your passport and takes your fingerprints and a photo. Most visitors are through in a minute or two. Questions are short:

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need both a K-ETA and an e-Arrival Card?**
No. A valid K-ETA replaces the arrival card. If you're on the exempt list and don't have a K-ETA, file the free e-Arrival Card.

**Is the Korea e-Arrival Card free?**
Yes. The official site, e-arrivalcard.go.kr, charges nothing. Sites that charge a fee are third-party agencies.

**How early can I submit the e-Arrival Card?**
From 3 days before your arrival date in Korea time. It stays valid for 72 hours, so file again if your trip is postponed.

**Is the K-ETA exemption ending?**
The temporary exemption for the 22 countries and regions runs through December 31, 2026. Check the official K-ETA site before trips in 2027, since the list is reviewed each year.
