Most foreign residents staying in Korea long-term must join National Health Insurance (NHI), either through their employer or as a local subscriber. Once you're enrolled, you pay a monthly premium and get the same reduced medical costs as Korean citizens.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Who:** employees through work; other long-term residents and students as local subscribers
- **Cost:** employees split premiums with the employer; local subscribers pay a monthly premium, with discounts for students
- **Coverage:** doctor visits, hospital care and prescriptions at reduced cost
- **Avoid:** missing payments; arrears can affect visa extensions

</KeyTakeaways>

## Employee vs local subscriber

| | Employee (직장가입자) | Local subscriber (지역가입자) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Who** | People employed by a Korean company | Students, self-employed and others without workplace coverage |
| **Premium** | A percentage of salary, split with the employer | Set by NHIS based on income and assets, with a minimum for foreigners |
| **Payment** | Deducted from salary | Monthly bill, paid by transfer, card or auto-debit |
| **Students** | — | Usually receive a significant discount |
| **Dependents** | Eligible family can be added | Rules differ; check with NHIS |

Premium amounts change each year, so check the NHIS website or ask your university or employer for the current figures.

## When you're enrolled

| Situation | Typical rule |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Students (D-2, D-4 visas)** | Generally enrolled from registration |
| **Other long-term residents** | Enrolled after a set period of residence, often 6 months |
| **Employees** | Enrolled from the start of employment |
| **Short-term visitors** | Not eligible; use travel insurance |

<Callout type="info" title="Check your own case">
Rules depend on your visa type, length of stay and whether you have other coverage. NHIS offers a foreign-language helpline and English information on its website.
</Callout>

## Using your insurance

| Step | What happens |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Visit a clinic or hospital** | Show your ARC; you're identified as insured |
| **2. Pay your share** | A portion of the cost, much lower than uninsured prices |
| **3. Pharmacy** | Prescriptions are also discounted |
| **4. Keep receipts** | Useful for private insurance or tax records |

## Common changes to report

Your premium and coverage depend on your details, so keep NHIS updated when things change. Many updates happen automatically through immigration records, but it's worth checking.

| Change | What to do |
| :--- | :--- |
| **New job with workplace coverage** | Your employer enrolls you; your local-subscriber bill should stop |
| **Moving house** | Report your new address to immigration; NHIS records follow |
| **Becoming a student** | Submit enrollment proof for the student discount |
| **Leaving Korea permanently** | Settle any unpaid premiums and confirm the end of coverage |
| **Family members arriving** | Ask whether they can be added as dependents |

## What's covered

| Covered (with co-payment) | Usually not covered |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Clinic and hospital visits** | Cosmetic procedures |
| **Hospital stays and surgery** | Some dental and vision treatments |
| **Prescription medicine** | Certain advanced or elective treatments |
| **Regular health checkups (eligible ages)** | Treatment abroad |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is National Health Insurance mandatory for foreigners?**
For most long-term residents, yes. Students and employees are generally enrolled automatically, while others join after a set period.

**How much does NHI cost for a student?**
Students usually pay a reduced local-subscriber premium. The exact amount changes, so check with NHIS or your university.

**What happens if I don't pay?**
Unpaid premiums can limit your benefits and may affect visa extensions or renewals.

**Can I use NHI at any hospital?**
Yes, at clinics and hospitals across Korea. You pay a share of the cost, and NHI covers the rest.
