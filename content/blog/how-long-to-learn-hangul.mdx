Korean grammar takes English speakers a long time. The alphabet doesn't. Reading at all takes days; reading comfortably takes a few months.

## A realistic timeline

| When | What you can do | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–2 | recognize the letters | 14 consonants, 10 vowels |
| Days 3–5 | build syllable blocks | 한 = ㅎ + ㅏ + ㄴ |
| Weeks 1–2 | read signs and menus, slowly | sounding out |
| Months 1–3 | read without decoding | lots of reading |

The last stage is just mileage: blocks stop being decoded and start being recognized whole, the way you read English words.

## Why it's this fast

Hangul was invented on purpose in the 15th century rather than evolving from pictures, and the design shows.

- **Consonants copy the mouth.** ㄱ is the tongue at the back of the mouth, ㅁ is the lips. Related sounds share a shape plus a stroke: ㄱ, ㅋ, ㄲ.
- **Vowels are built from a few pieces.** ㅏ ㅓ ㅗ ㅜ are the same two strokes in different positions.
- **Spelling is close to the sound.** There's no *through / though / tough* problem.

The [scientific alphabet guide](/blog/en/k-patto/why-hangul-is-called-scientific-alphabet) explains the design in more detail.

## The two things that actually slow people down

**1. Look-alike letters.** ㅓ and ㅏ, ㅗ and ㅜ, ㅂ and ㅁ, ㄹ and ㄷ are easy to mix up at speed. Read them in contrast pairs (어/아, 오/우) rather than one at a time.

**2. Sound changes.** Korean isn't always said the way it's written, because neighboring sounds affect each other.

| Written | Said | Romanization |
|---|---|---|
| 신라 | [실라] | *silla* |
| 좋다 | [조타] | *jota* |
| 한국말 | [한궁말] | *hangungmal* |

Beginners often think they've learned Hangul wrong when this happens. They haven't. These are regular rules, picked up gradually by reading aloud with audio. The [batchim guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-batchim-final-consonants-explained) covers them.

## What not to do

- **Don't lean on romanization.** Reading *annyeonghaseyo* instead of 안녕하세요 feels faster for a week and slows you down for months. The [romanization guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-romanization-two-spellings) explains why.
- **Don't learn every letter before reading anything.** Learn ten letters, then read words made from those ten.
- **Don't skip audio.** Silent reading trains your eyes but not your ears.

## A practical first week

| Days | Do this |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Basic consonants and vowels; read two-letter blocks aloud. |
| 3–4 | Blocks with a final consonant: 사람, 학교, 친구. |
| 5–6 | Double consonants and complex vowels; read shop signs from photos. |
| 7 | Read a short dialogue aloud, slowly, with no romanization. |

Slow is normal. Speed comes from months of reading, not more study of the letters. The [one-day reading plan](/blog/en/k-patto/how-to-read-hangul-in-one-day) is the place to start.
