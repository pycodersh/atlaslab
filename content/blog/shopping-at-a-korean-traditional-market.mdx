A traditional market is where the price on the sign isn't always final, where vendors talk to you first, and where cash still sometimes matters. It's also a great place to practice: vendors are usually patient with foreigners and pleased when you try.

## Browsing without committing

Vendors greet anyone who slows down. If you're just looking, say **구경할게요** (*gugyeonghalgeyo*, "I'm just looking around") or **그냥 볼게요** (*geunyang bolgeyo*). Both are normal, and nobody is offended.

## Asking about things

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 이거 뭐예요? | igeo mwoyeyo? | What is this? |
| 이거 얼마예요? | igeo eolmayeyo? | How much is this? |
| 매워요? | maewoyo? | Is it spicy? |
| 먹어 봐도 돼요? | meogeo bwado dwaeyo? | Can I try some? |

At food stalls, asking for a taste is normal. The [prices guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-prices-money-eolmayeyo) helps when the answer comes back fast.

## What the vendor will say

| You'll hear | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 어서 오세요! 뭐 찾으세요? | Welcome! What are you looking for? |
| 한 봉지에 오천 원이요. | 5,000 won a bag. |
| 맛보고 가요. | Have a taste. |
| 몇 개 드려요? | How many shall I give you? |
| 하나 더 넣어 드렸어요. | I put in an extra one. |

## Asking for a discount

This works at traditional markets. At shops, department stores and convenience stores it's awkward.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 좀 깎아 주세요. | jom kkakka juseyo | Give me a little discount. |
| 좀 싸게 해 주세요. | jom ssage hae juseyo | Make it a bit cheaper. |
| 두 개 사면 얼마예요? | du gae samyeon eolmayeyo? | How much if I buy two? |

Tone matters more than words. Said lightly, with a smile, when you really mean to buy, it's part of the exchange. Buying more is the easiest way to get a better price.

## 덤: the free extra

Instead of lowering the price, vendors often add a bit more. That's **덤** (*deom*). If you buy mandarins and two extra land in the bag, **감사합니다** is all you need to say.

## Counters

| Counter | Used for |
|---|---|
| 개 | general items |
| 봉지 | a bag of something |
| 팩 | a pack |
| 근 | a traditional weight for meat and produce |
| 마리 | fish, whole animals |

**근** (*geun*) is about 600 g for meat and less for vegetables. **한 근 주세요** is what you'll hear at the butcher. The [counters guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-counters-gae-myeong-jan-byeong) covers the rest.

## Paying

Ask **카드 돼요?** (*kadeu dwaeyo?*, "do you take card?") before you order. Most stalls take cards now, but some small ones prefer or only take cash, so carry some.

## Polite or casual?

Older vendors often switch into casual speech or call you **학생** or **아가씨**. It's friendly, not rude. Keep using polite **-요** yourself.

## A full exchange

> **Vendor:** 어서 오세요! 뭐 찾으세요?  
> **You:** 그냥 구경할게요.  
> **You:** (later) 이거 얼마예요?  
> **Vendor:** 한 봉지에 오천 원이요.  
> **You:** 두 개 사면 좀 깎아 주세요.  
> **Vendor:** 그럼 구천 원에 드릴게요.  
> **You:** 감사합니다. 카드 돼요?

**Where to go:** **광장시장** is famous for food: bindaetteok and mayak gimbap. **남대문시장** is bigger and more general. Neighborhood markets are quieter and better for practice.
