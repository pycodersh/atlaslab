Gukbap (국밥) is hot soup with rice in it, and juk (죽) is soft rice porridge: the two dishes Koreans reach for when it's cold, after a late night, or when they feel unwell. Gukbap is a hearty, cheap meal; juk is gentle food you can buy in single portions at porridge chains and take home.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Gukbap:** filling soup with rice, around 9,000–12,000 KRW, often open late
- **Juk:** smooth rice porridge, around 9,000–15,000 KRW at porridge chains
- **When sick:** juk is the classic Korean comfort food, easy on the stomach
- **Avoid:** skipping the seasoning; many gukbap broths come lightly salted

</KeyTakeaways>

## Gukbap types

| Type | Korean | What it is | Best for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pork soup with rice** | 돼지국밥 | Milky pork broth, a Busan specialty | A hearty, cheap meal |
| **Blood sausage soup** | 순대국밥 | Pork broth with sundae and offal | Adventurous eaters |
| **Bean sprout soup** | 콩나물국밥 | Light, clean broth, often with a raw egg | Hangover mornings |
| **Beef soup with rice** | 소고기국밥 | Spicy or clear beef broth | Cold days |

## Juk types

| Type | Korean | Flavor | Best for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Plain rice** | 흰죽 | Very mild | Upset stomachs |
| **Abalone** | 전복죽 | Rich, savory, a premium choice | Recovering, a treat |
| **Pumpkin** | 호박죽 | Sweet and smooth | Breakfast, a light meal |
| **Vegetable and beef** | 야채소고기죽 | Savory, balanced | A full but gentle meal |
| **Red bean** | 팥죽 | Sweet-savory, eaten on the winter solstice | Seasonal tradition |

<Callout type="info" title="Why juk when you're sick">
In Korea, juk is what friends and family bring when someone is ill, much like chicken soup elsewhere. Porridge chains are full of people buying single portions to take to a sick relative.
</Callout>

<KpattoCta />

Gukbap restaurants usually serve a set of side dishes that go with the soup: radish kimchi, cabbage kimchi, raw onion with gochujang, and sometimes salted shrimp (새우젓) for seasoning pork broth. Add the shrimp a little at a time; it's very salty, and a small spoonful is enough for a whole bowl.

## Ordering juk to go

| Tip | Why |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ask to split it** | Ask staff to divide one large portion into two or three containers (나눠 담아 주세요) so it lasts several meals |
| **Side dishes** | Porridge comes with small sides like jangjorim (soy-braised beef) and kimchi |
| **Reheating** | Add a splash of water and reheat gently; juk thickens as it cools |
| **Seasoning** | A soy or salt packet often comes on the side |

## Eating gukbap like a local

1. **Taste the broth** before seasoning.
2. **Add salt, shrimp paste or chili** from the table to taste.
3. **Add chopped green onion or garlic** if provided.
4. **Mix in the rice** if it's served on the side, or eat it from the bowl.
5. **Eat kimchi or radish kimchi** alongside to balance the richness.

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Season gukbap to taste at the table
- Ask to split juk into containers for later
- Choose plain or pumpkin juk for an upset stomach
- Look for 24-hour gukbap shops for late-night meals

</Do>
<Dont>

- Expect juk to be cheap; abalone versions are premium
- Over-salt before tasting
- Leave juk uncovered; it forms a skin and thickens
- Assume all gukbap is mild; some versions are spicy

</Dont>
</DoDont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What's the difference between gukbap and juk?**
Gukbap is soup with rice added, so the grains stay whole in the broth. Juk is rice cooked down into a smooth porridge.

**Where can I buy juk?**
Porridge chains such as Bonjuk (본죽) are everywhere in Korean cities, and some traditional markets sell it too.

**Is gukbap open late?**
Many gukbap restaurants open early and close late, and some run 24 hours, making them popular after a night out.

**Is juk good for a hangover?**
It's gentle on the stomach, but for hangovers Koreans usually prefer brothy soups such as bean sprout gukbap or haejangguk.
