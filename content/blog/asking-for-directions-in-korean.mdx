Asking for directions in a new language has an obvious trap: you ask a perfect question and then can't follow the answer. So the useful preparation isn't the question. It's the handful of reply patterns that cover almost every set of Korean directions.

## Getting attention

**저기요** (*jeogiyo*) is the standard way to address a stranger. Say it clearly, a little loudly; it isn't rude. **실례합니다** (*sillyehamnida*, "excuse me") is more formal and works too, but 저기요 is far more common on the street.

## The question

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 경복궁에 어떻게 가요? | gyeongbokgunge eotteoke gayo? | How do I get to Gyeongbokgung? |
| 지하철역 어디예요? | jihacheollyeok eodiyeyo? | Where's the subway station? |
| 여기서 멀어요? | yeogiseo meoreoyo? | Is it far from here? |
| 길을 잃었어요. | gireul ireosseoyo | I'm lost. |

Saying **길을 잃었어요** first changes the answer: people slow down and often walk you part of the way.

## What you'll hear back

**1. Straight on**

| You'll hear | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 쭉 가세요. | Go straight. |
| 이 길로 쭉 가면 돼요. | Just keep going along this road. |

**쭉** (*jjuk*) means "straight on, without stopping", and it's in almost every set of directions.

**2. Turning**

| You'll hear | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 왼쪽으로 가세요. | Go left. |
| 오른쪽으로 꺾으세요. | Turn right. |
| 저기 끝에서 왼쪽으로 꺾으면 돼요. | Turn left at the end there. |

**꺾다** (*kkeokda*) is "to turn a corner", more common in spoken directions than 돌다.

**3. Landmarks**

| You'll hear | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 편의점 지나서 | past the convenience store |
| 신호등에서 | at the traffic light |
| 사거리에서 | at the intersection |
| 건너편에 있어요. | It's on the other side. |

Koreans give directions by landmark much more than by distance, and convenience stores are the favorite because there's always one.

**4. Near or far**

| You'll hear | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 바로 저기예요. | It's right there. |
| 걸어서 오 분이요. | Five minutes on foot. |
| 좀 멀어요. 버스 타세요. | It's a bit far. Take the bus. |

## Direction words

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 왼쪽 / 오른쪽 | oenjjok / oreunjjok | left / right |
| 앞 / 뒤 / 옆 | ap / dwi / yeop | in front / behind / next to |
| 건너편 | geonneopyeon | across the street |
| 횡단보도 | hoengdanbodo | crosswalk |

## When you didn't catch it

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 다시 한번 말해 주세요. | dasi hanbeon malhae juseyo | Please say that again. |
| 천천히 말해 주세요. | cheoncheonhi malhae juseyo | Please speak slowly. |
| 잘 못 알아들었어요. | jal mot aradeureosseoyo | I didn't quite catch that. |

The last one is honest, and people respond to it well, usually by slowing down and pointing.

## Polite or casual?

Strangers will use polite speech with you, often with **-세요** (**가세요**, **타세요**). Use **-요** back. With a friend, you'd ask **어떻게 가?** ("how do we get there?").

## A real exchange

> **You:** 저기요, 길을 잃었어요. 경복궁에 어떻게 가요?  
> **Passerby:** 이 길로 쭉 가면 돼요. 저기 끝에서 왼쪽으로 꺾으면 돼요.  
> **You:** 감사합니다!
