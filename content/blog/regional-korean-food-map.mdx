Every Korean region has its own signature dishes, shaped by local farming, fishing and history. Use this map to plan your meals: eat milmyeon in Busan, bibimbap in Jeonju, dakgalbi in Chuncheon and black pork on Jeju.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Busan:** seafood, pork soup with rice and milmyeon
- **Jeonju:** bibimbap and bean sprout soup in Korea's food capital
- **Jeju:** black pork, seafood and tangerines
- **Avoid:** expecting the same dish to taste identical everywhere; regional versions differ

</KeyTakeaways>

## Regional specialties at a glance

| Region | Must-try dishes | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Seoul** | Seolleongtang, royal court cuisine, street food | The capital's mix of traditions and trends |
| **Busan** | Dwaeji gukbap, milmyeon, ssiat hotteok, fresh seafood | Port city cooking, wartime history |
| **Jeonju** | Jeonju bibimbap, kongnamul gukbap | Fertile Jeolla farmland, a UNESCO City of Gastronomy |
| **Jeju** | Black pork, abalone, okdom (tilefish), tangerines | Island ingredients |
| **Chuncheon** | Dakgalbi, makguksu | Gangwon mountain cooking |
| **Andong** | Andong jjimdak, heotjesa bibimbap, salted mackerel | Inland Confucian traditions |
| **Gyeongju** | Ssambap, Gyeongju bread (hwangnam-ppang) | Ancient capital, simple country food |
| **Gangneung** | Chodang sundubu (tofu made with seawater) | East coast specialty |

Many of these dishes are also available in Seoul, but eating them in their hometown is part of the experience. Local restaurants often serve larger portions, more banchan and lower prices, and the specialty is usually the dish every restaurant near the station or market is known for. Ask a taxi driver or market vendor where locals eat; they'll often have a strong opinion.

## Why regional food differs

Climate and geography shape Korean food. The warm southwestern Jeolla provinces, with rich farmland and coastline, are famous for bold, generous dishes and many banchan. Coastal Gyeongsang cooking uses lots of seafood and is often saltier and spicier. Mountainous Gangwon relies on potatoes, buckwheat and corn. Jeju's island isolation created unique dishes around pork and seafood.

<Callout type="tip" title="Eat local on day trips">
When planning a trip outside Seoul, look up the town's signature dish first. Restaurants near train stations and markets often serve it, and it's usually better and cheaper than in Seoul.
</Callout>

## Regional cooking styles

Beyond individual dishes, each province has a cooking style you'll notice at the table.

- **Jeolla (southwest):** generous meals with many banchan, bold fermented flavors, and famous kimchi; Jeonju and Gwangju are food destinations
- **Gyeongsang (southeast):** saltier, spicier food, lots of seafood along the coast, Busan's port-city dishes
- **Gangwon (northeast):** potatoes, buckwheat, corn and mountain vegetables; makguksu and potato pancakes
- **Chungcheong (central):** milder, simpler flavors and hearty home cooking
- **Gyeonggi and Seoul:** a mix of everything, plus royal court cuisine traditions
- **Jeju:** island food built around pork, seafood and citrus

Traditional markets are the best places to taste regional food cheaply, and many cities have a market food street worth visiting.

## Planning a food trip

Combine regional specialties with easy transport. Jeonju is a quick KTX ride from Seoul for bibimbap and a hanok village. Chuncheon makes an easy day trip for dakgalbi, and Busan and Gyeongju fit together on a southeast trip. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which Korean city has the best food?**
Jeonju is often called Korea's food capital and is a UNESCO City of Gastronomy, but every region has standout dishes.

**What should I eat in Busan?**
Dwaeji gukbap (pork soup with rice), milmyeon, fresh seafood and ssiat hotteok.

**What is Jeju famous for food-wise?**
Black pork, abalone, seafood and tangerines.

**Can I find regional dishes in Seoul?**
Yes, many restaurants serve regional specialties, but they're often best in their home region.
