Korean taxis are metered, reasonably priced and accept cards, so they're an easy option for short trips and late nights. Know which taxi type you're getting into, show the destination in Korean, and pay by card at the end.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Regular taxis:** the cheapest, metered, in orange, silver or white
- **Night surcharge:** roughly 20–40% extra late at night
- **The red light paradox:** a red 빈차 sign lit means the taxi is available
- **Avoid:** flagging deluxe black taxis by accident if you're on a budget

</KeyTakeaways>

## Taxi types compared

| Type | How to spot it | Fare | Best for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Regular (일반)** | Orange, silver or white sedans | Base fare plus meter; night surcharge | Everyday trips |
| **Deluxe (모범)** | Black with a yellow roof sign | Noticeably higher base and per-km fare | Comfort, airports, busy hotel stands |
| **Large / van** | Vans with taxi signs, usually booked by app | Varies, often dynamic | Groups and luggage |
| **International taxi** | Signs marked "International" | Set fares for some airport routes; English-speaking drivers | Airport transfers without Korean |

## Hailing a taxi: the red light paradox

| What you see | What it means |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Red 빈차 sign lit** | Available: wave it down |
| **Sign off, or green/other light** | Occupied or booked |
| **예약 (reserved)** | Booked by app, won't stop |

## Street taxi or app?

Street taxis are easy most of the day, but apps are better late at night, in bad weather and in nightlife districts. The main difference is that the app shows the destination to the driver and the route to you, so there's no language barrier.

| | Street taxi | Kakao T app |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **How** | Wave down a taxi with a lit red sign | Set pickup and destination, request a ride |
| **Destination** | Show the address on your phone | Sent to the driver automatically |
| **Payment** | Card or T-money in the car | In-app (foreign cards often work) or in the car |
| **Busy nights** | Hard to find a free taxi | Easier, sometimes with a small call fee |
| **Best for** | Daytime, quiet streets | Nights, rain, unfamiliar areas |

## Paying and the meter

| Item | What to know |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Meter** | Starts at the base fare and rises by distance and time |
| **Night surcharge** | Applies late at night; shown on the meter automatically |
| **Card** | Tap or insert at the card reader by the driver; foreign cards usually work |
| **T-money** | Accepted in many taxis |
| **Tolls** | Added for expressways and some tunnels, such as the airport route |
| **Tipping** | Not expected; see [how paying works in Korea](/blog/en/k-patto/zero-tipping-split-bills-korea) |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are taxis in Korea expensive?**
Short trips in regular taxis are affordable compared with many cities. Late-night surcharges, deluxe taxis and long airport runs cost more.

**Can I pay a taxi with a foreign credit card?**
Usually yes. Most taxis have card readers that accept Visa and Mastercard, and many accept T-money.

**What if I leave something in a taxi?**
Your card receipt or the app's ride history shows the taxi's details. Call 1330 for help contacting the taxi company.

**Do Korean taxi drivers speak English?**
Most speak little English. International taxis are the exception, and showing the address in Korean works everywhere.
