In Korean restaurants, staff won't hover or check on you, so you call them when you're ready. Press the table bell or say 저기요 (*jeogiyo*), order by the number of servings, and pay at the counter on your way out.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Calling staff:** press the call bell on the table, or say 저기요 clearly
- **Free:** water, side dishes (banchan) and their refills
- **Paying:** usually at the counter by the door, after eating
- **Avoid:** waiting silently for a server to come over; they won't unless called

</KeyTakeaways>

## How a meal works

| Step | What happens | Tip |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Seating** | Say the number of people, or staff point you to a table | Some places have a kiosk or tablet at the table |
| **2. Water and sides** | Water may be self-service; banchan arrive with the food | Look for a water cooler and cups near the counter |
| **3. Ordering** | Call staff, order by dish and number of servings (인분) | BBQ and stews often need two servings minimum |
| **4. During the meal** | Call staff for refills or extra items | Side dish refills are usually free |
| **5. Paying** | Take the bill to the counter, or pay at the kiosk | One person pays, or ask to split by item |

## Ways to call staff

| Method | When to use it |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Call bell (호출벨)** | A button on the table; press once and wait |
| **저기요 (*jeogiyo*)** | "Excuse me": say it clearly with a raised hand |
| **사장님 (*sajangnim*) / 이모 (*imo*)** | Casual ways to call the owner or an older server in local eateries |
| **Tablet or kiosk** | Order and pay on the screen; staff bring the food |

<Callout type="tip" title="Self-service is normal">
Water, cups, cutlery, side dish refills and aprons are often self-service. Look for a station marked 셀프 (self) before asking staff.
</Callout>

## Reading the menu

Menus are often in Korean only, with prices on the wall. A few words appear on almost every menu and tell you how dishes are sized and served.

| Word | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1인분** (*irinbun*) | One serving (per person) |
| **소 · 중 · 대** | Small, medium, large (for shared dishes) |
| **공깃밥** (*gonggitbap*) | A bowl of rice, usually ordered separately |
| **사리** (*sari*) | Extra noodles or add-ins for stews |
| **추가** (*chuga*) | Extra, added on |
| **포장** (*pojang*) | Takeout |

Photos on the wall or a translation app's camera mode help a lot when the menu has no English.

## Paying and splitting

| Situation | What to do |
| :--- | :--- |
| **One person pays** | Common among friends; others pay next time |
| **Splitting** | Ask 따로 계산할게요 (*ttaro gyesanhalgeyo*, we'll pay separately) |
| **Kiosk restaurants** | Pay first, then wait for your number |
| **Tipping** | Not expected; see [no tipping in Korea](/blog/en/k-patto/zero-tipping-split-bills-korea) |

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Call staff with the bell or 저기요
- Ask for side dish refills; they're free
- Take the receipt or table number to the counter when paying
- Check whether water and cutlery are self-service

</Do>
<Dont>

- Wait for staff to come and check on you
- Wave or snap fingers at servers
- Order one serving of a two-serving dish
- Leave a tip on the table

</Dont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is it rude to call 저기요 loudly?**
No. It's the normal way to get attention in a busy restaurant. Say it clearly, once, and raise your hand.

**Are side dishes free in Korea?**
Yes. Banchan come with the meal, and refills are usually free. Some places have a self-service banchan bar.

**Do I pay at the table?**
Usually not. Take your bill or table number to the counter by the entrance. Kiosk and tablet restaurants have you pay on screen.

**Can I ask for a dish to be less spicy?**
Yes: 덜 맵게 해 주세요 (*deol maepge hae juseyo*). Some dishes can't be changed, but many can be made milder.
