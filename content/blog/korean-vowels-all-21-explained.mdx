Twenty-one vowels sounds like a lot until you see how they're built. Six are new. The other fifteen are combinations you can read without memorizing them one by one.

## The six that matter

| Letter | Sound | Mouth |
|---|---|---|
| ㅏ | *a* | open, relaxed |
| ㅓ | *eo* | open, lips **not** rounded |
| ㅗ | *o* | lips rounded, pushed forward |
| ㅜ | *u* | lips rounded and tight |
| ㅡ | *eu* | lips spread, sound at the back |
| ㅣ | *i* | lips spread wide |

## Add a stroke: the y-vowels

A second short stroke adds a *y* in front: ㅏ → ㅑ *ya*, ㅓ → ㅕ *yeo*, ㅗ → ㅛ *yo*, ㅜ → ㅠ *yu*. Ten vowels, and these four needed no new sounds.

## Add ㅣ: the e-vowels

ㅏ + ㅣ = ㅐ and ㅓ + ㅣ = ㅔ. Both sound roughly like the *e* in "bed".

**In everyday Seoul speech, ㅐ and ㅔ have merged.** Most younger speakers don't distinguish them. You still need to know which words use which for spelling, but you don't need to hear a difference. Their *y*-versions ㅒ and ㅖ are rare.

## Add ㅗ or ㅜ: the w-vowels

Put ㅗ or ㅜ in front of another vowel and you get a *w* glide.

| Built from | Vowel | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| ㅗ + ㅏ | ㅘ | *wa* |
| ㅗ + ㅐ | ㅙ | *wae* |
| ㅗ + ㅣ | ㅚ | *oe*, said like *we* |
| ㅜ + ㅓ | ㅝ | *wo* |
| ㅜ + ㅔ | ㅞ | *we* |
| ㅜ + ㅣ | ㅟ | *wi* |

ㅙ, ㅚ and ㅞ sound nearly the same in modern speech, roughly *we*. Three spellings, one sound: a spelling problem, not a listening one.

## The odd one out: ㅢ

ㅡ + ㅣ = ㅢ, and it changes with its position.

| Where | Example | Said like |
|---|---|---|
| start of a word | 의사 (doctor) | *uisa* |
| later in a word | 회의 (meeting) | *hoei* |
| the particle 의 ("of") | 저의 (my) | *jeoe* |

This is one of the few places where spelling and speech split for everyone, native speakers included.

## The pairs that cause real trouble

| Pair | Example | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|---|
| ㅓ vs ㅗ | 서다 · 소다 | If your lips round, you said ㅗ. |
| ㅡ vs ㅜ | 그 · 구 | ㅜ is rounded, ㅡ is spread. |
| ㅐ vs ㅔ | 개 · 게 | Same sound now; learn the spelling. |

**서다** means "to stand", **소다** is "soda", **그** is "that", **구** is "nine". **개** is "dog" and **게** is "crab".

<Callout type="tip" title="Train your ear with pairs">

Charts teach the inventory; pairs train recognition. Say 서다 / 소다, 그 / 구, 벌 / 볼 back to back, out loud, and watch your lips. The [pronunciation mistakes guide](/blog/en/k-patto/common-pronunciation-mistakes-korean-learners-make) lists the other sounds worth drilling.

</Callout>
