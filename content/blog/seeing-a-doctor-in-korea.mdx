Seeing a doctor in Korea is usually quicker than visitors expect. Neighborhood clinics take walk-ins, waits are often short, and a basic visit at a small clinic is usually affordable. The conversation follows a fixed shape: reception, the doctor, the pharmacy.

## Where to go

| Korean | Romanization | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 의원 | uiwon | small neighborhood clinic |
| 병원 | byeongwon | clinic or hospital (general word) |
| 대학병원 | daehakbyeongwon | large university hospital |
| 응급실 | eunggeupsil | emergency room |

For anything ordinary, go to a small clinic. Large university hospitals are for serious cases and usually expect a referral. Clinic signs show the specialty:

| Korean | Specialty |
|---|---|
| 내과 | internal medicine (colds, fever, stomach) |
| 이비인후과 | ear, nose and throat |
| 피부과 | dermatology |
| 정형외과 | orthopedics |
| 치과 | dentist |

For a cold, stomach trouble or a fever, **내과** (*naegwa*) is the right door. In an emergency, call **119**.

## At reception

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 진료 받고 싶어요. | jillyo batgo sipeoyo | I'd like to see a doctor. |
| 예약 안 했어요. | yeyak an haesseoyo | I don't have an appointment. |
| 외국인이에요. | oegugineyo | I'm a foreigner. |
| 여권 여기 있어요. | yeogwon yeogi isseoyo | Here's my passport. |

You'll fill in a short form. Staff often help foreigners through it.

## What staff and the doctor will ask

| You'll hear | Meaning | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 처음 오셨어요? | Is this your first visit? | 네, 처음이에요. |
| 어디가 아프세요? | Where does it hurt? | 배가 아파요. |
| 언제부터 아프셨어요? | Since when? | 어제 저녁부터요. |
| 드시는 약 있으세요? | Are you taking any medicine? | 아니요, 없어요. |
| 알레르기 있으세요? | Any allergies? | 네, 페니실린이요. |

## Describing symptoms

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 열이 나요. | yeori nayo | I have a fever. |
| 계속 기침이 나요. | gyesok gichimi nayo | I keep coughing. |
| 어지러워요. | eojireowoyo | I feel dizzy. |
| 토했어요. | tohaesseoyo | I threw up. |
| 삼 일 됐어요. | sam il dwaesseoyo | It's been three days. |

## What you'll be told

| You'll hear | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 심하지 않아요. | It's not serious. |
| 푹 쉬세요. | Get plenty of rest. |
| 며칠은 매운 거 드시면 안 돼요. | No spicy food for a few days. |
| 처방전 드릴게요. | I'll give you a prescription. |
| 약국 가서 약 받으세요. | Get your medicine at the pharmacy. |

**드시면 안 돼요** ("you mustn't eat/take") is how doctors phrase restrictions, so it's worth recognizing.

## Paying and the pharmacy

You pay at the desk on the way out (**카드로 할게요**, "by card"), then take the prescription (**처방전**, *cheobangjeon*) to a pharmacy, often in the same building or next door. Ask for **영수증** (*yeongsujeung*, receipt) if you have travel insurance. The [pharmacy guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-pharmacy-what-to-say) covers the instructions you'll get there.

## Polite or casual?

Medical staff speak to you with honorific **-시-** (**아프세요?**, **드세요**). You only need polite **-요**. You'll never need casual speech here.

## A full visit

> **You:** 진료 받고 싶어요.  
> **Reception:** 어디가 아프세요?  
> **You:** 배가 아파요.  
> **Doctor:** 언제부터 아프셨어요?  
> **You:** 어제 저녁부터요.  
> **Doctor:** 며칠은 매운 거 드시면 안 돼요.
