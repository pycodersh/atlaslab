Yes, Korea is generally considered one of the safer places to travel, and most visitors never need more than directions. Save three numbers before you land: 112 for police, 119 for fire and ambulance, and 1330 for an English-speaking travel helpline.

| Number | Who answers | Call it for | Languages |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **112** | Police | Crime, theft, assault, or anytime you feel in danger | Korean; an interpreter can be connected |
| **119** | Fire and ambulance | Medical emergencies, fires, accidents | Korean; interpretation available |
| **1330** | Korea Travel Helpline | Directions, interpretation, complaints, finding tourist police | English and several other languages, 24 hours |
| **1345** | Immigration Contact Center | Visa and length-of-stay questions | Multiple languages, weekday office hours |

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Emergency:** 112 (police) or 119 (ambulance and fire); no area code needed
- **Not an emergency but need help:** 1330 answers in English around the clock
- **Lost something:** check the police Lost112 site and the subway lost-and-found centers
- **Biggest real risks:** crowded trains, delivery scooters on sidewalks, summer heat and typhoons

</KeyTakeaways>

## How safe is Korea, really?

Violent crime against visitors is uncommon, and city streets stay busy and well lit late into the evening. Most problems tourists report are everyday ones.

| Risk | Where it happens | What to do |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pickpocketing** | Packed trains, busy markets | Keep your phone and wallet in a front pocket or zipped bag |
| **Scooters and bikes** | Sidewalks, crosswalks | Look both ways, even on a green light |
| **Weather** | Summer heat and typhoons; icy steps in winter | Check alerts and carry water in summer |
| **Unposted prices** | Fish markets, some street stalls | Ask the total before you agree; see our [Noryangjin guide](/blog/en/k-pantry/noryangjin-fish-market-guide-how-to-eat-hoe) |

Many people travel alone in Korea, including solo women. The usual city precautions apply: stay aware of your surroundings, don't leave drinks unattended, and share your plans with someone.

<Callout type="tip" title="Night safety">

- Use an app taxi (Kakao T) or a metered taxi, and keep the receipt; it shows the car number
- Stick to main streets late at night; convenience stores are open, bright and staffed 24/7
- Last trains run around midnight; check the time before a late night out
- If you feel unsafe, step into a convenience store or call 112

</Callout>

## What to do in an emergency

1. **Call 112 or 119** and say which you need: police (경찰, *gyeongchal*) or ambulance (구급차, *gugeupcha*).
2. **Give your location:** the station name and exit number, the building name, or a landmark from your map app.
3. **Stay on the line.** If you don't speak Korean, keep holding; an interpreter can join the call.
4. **Need translation for a non-emergency?** Call **1330**, which can also help you talk to police or hospital staff.

## Lost and found

<Callout type="info" title="Where to look for lost items">

- **Police:** report it at a police box (파출소) and ask for a 분실 신고 (lost-item report). The police Lost112 website lists items handed in across the country.
- **Subway:** each line has a lost-and-found center, and station staff can call it for you. Note the time, line and car if you remember them.
- **Taxi:** your receipt or the app ride history shows the car number, so the company can contact the driver.
- **Passport:** report the loss to police, then contact your embassy in Seoul. Keep a photo of your passport on your phone.

</Callout>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do 112 and 119 operators speak English?**
Operators can connect an interpreter, so stay on the line even if the first voice speaks Korean. For non-emergencies, 1330 answers directly in English.

**Is it safe to walk around Seoul at night?**
Central areas stay busy and well lit late into the night, and convenience stores are open everywhere. Use normal city caution, stay on main streets, and take a metered or app taxi home.

**What is the loud alert on my phone?**
Korean phones receive emergency broadcasts for severe weather, fine dust and other warnings, usually in Korean. Screenshot the message, translate it, and follow the instructions.

**Where do I go if I lose my passport?**
Report it at a police station for a loss report, then contact your embassy for a replacement or an emergency travel document. A photo of your passport speeds this up.
