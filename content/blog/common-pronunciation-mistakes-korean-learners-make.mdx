Most pronunciation advice for Korean is too general to act on. "Listen carefully" does not tell you what you are getting wrong.

These are the seven that come up again and again with English speakers, in rough order of how much they affect being understood.

## 1. Collapsing ㅓ and ㅗ

English does not distinguish these cleanly, so learners often produce something in between. Korean treats them as completely separate vowels, and the difference changes words:

- 서다 (to stand) vs 소다 (soda)
- 벌 (bee, punishment) vs 볼 (cheek, ball)

ㅗ is rounded — lips pushed forward. ㅓ is not rounded at all; the mouth stays relaxed and open. If your lips move, you are saying ㅗ.

---

## 2. Turning ㅡ into "oo"

ㅡ has no close English equivalent, so learners reach for the nearest thing, usually ㅜ. But 그 and 구 are not the same word.

For ㅡ, spread your lips slightly as if starting a smile and make the sound at the back of your mouth. No rounding.

---

## 3. Treating ㄱ, ㅋ, ㄲ as one sound

English has one *k*. Korean has three, and they are not interchangeable:

- 갈비 vs 칼비 — one is a dish, one is nothing
- 사다 (to buy) vs 싸다 (to be cheap)

ㅋ is aspirated — a puff of air. ㄲ is tense — no air, throat tightened, sharper. ㄱ sits in the middle. Hold a sheet of paper in front of your mouth: it should move for ㅋ and stay still for ㄲ.

---

## 4. Releasing final consonants

In English, the *k* in "book" gets a small release. In Korean, a consonant at the end of a syllable is stopped, not released. 밥 ends with the lips closed and no puff. 책 ends with the tongue at the back and no release.

Adding an English-style release makes you sound like you are adding a syllable that is not there.

---

## 5. Making ㄹ an English R

ㄹ is not the English R. Between vowels it is a quick flap, close to the *tt* in American "butter." At the end of a syllable it is closer to an L.

- 라면 — flap at the start
- 물 — L at the end

The English R pulls the tongue back and rounds the lips, which makes 라면 nearly unrecognizable.

---

## 6. Ignoring sound changes across syllables

Korean spelling and Korean pronunciation are not identical. [Sounds assimilate](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-batchim-final-consonants-explained) when syllables meet:

- 한국말 is pronounced *han-gung-mal*, not *han-guk-mal*
- 좋아요 is pronounced *jo-a-yo* — the ㅎ drops
- 십만 is pronounced *sim-man*

This is the single biggest reason learners fail to recognize words they already know when they hear them at speed. You learned the spelling; you are hearing the pronunciation.

---

## 7. Importing English sentence melody

English uses heavy stress on particular syllables. Korean does not — syllables get much more even weight, and meaning rides on the ending rather than on emphasis.

Stressing Korean words the way you would stress English ones is often what makes an otherwise accurate sentence sound foreign.
