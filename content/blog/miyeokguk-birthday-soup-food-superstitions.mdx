Koreans eat miyeokguk (미역국), a seaweed soup, on their birthday because mothers eat it for weeks after giving birth, so the birthday bowl is a thank-you to Mom. The same soup is avoided before an exam, because slippery seaweed is said to make you "slip" and fail.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Birthday:** a bowl of miyeokguk in the morning, often made by a parent or partner
- **Exams:** no seaweed soup; sticky yeot and rice cakes help you "stick" (pass)
- **Weddings:** noodles for a long marriage
- **Avoid:** offering seaweed soup to someone before a test or job interview

</KeyTakeaways>

## Why miyeokguk is the birthday soup

Miyeok (미역) is the brown seaweed sold in the US as wakame. Korean mothers traditionally eat miyeokguk for weeks after childbirth. Seaweed is rich in minerals, and the light soup is easy to eat while recovering.

So when someone eats the same soup on their birthday, it's a reminder of the day they were born and of the person who carried them. Many Koreans living abroad still make a bowl on their birthday, even when they'll have cake later.

| **Beef (소고기 미역국)** | Beef sautéed in sesame oil, then simmered | The classic birthday version |
| **Seafood (조개·홍합)** | Clams or mussels | Coastal regions |

## Why you never eat it before an exam

Because seaweed is slippery, eating it before a big test is believed to make you slip, as in fail. The idiom 미역국을 먹다 ("to eat seaweed soup") actually means to fail an exam or be rejected.

Instead, students get sticky foods. Sticky yeot (엿, rice taffy) and chapssal-tteok (찹쌀떡, glutinous rice cakes) are gifted before the national college entrance exam in November, because 붙다 means both "to stick" and "to pass."

<Callout type="warn" title="Read the room">
Serving miyeokguk to a friend the morning of their driving test or job interview will get a laugh at best and a worried look at worst. Save it for birthdays.
</Callout>

## Other Korean food customs and superstitions

Many of these are jokes or old habits rather than beliefs, but you'll hear about them often.

- **Noodles at weddings (잔치국수):** long noodles stand for a long marriage. "When will you feed us noodles?" means "When are you getting married?"
- **Tteokguk on New Year's Day:** one bowl traditionally made you a year older
- **Red bean porridge (팥죽) on the winter solstice:** red beans were believed to drive away bad spirits
- **Jjajangmyeon on moving day:** quick delivery noodles eaten on the floor among the boxes
- **Pig's head at a gosa (고사):** a rite for a new business, car or film shoot; guests tuck cash into the pig's mouth
- **Chopsticks upright in rice:** don't; it resembles a funeral offering

Asking "Did you have seaweed soup?" is a warm, very Korean way to say happy birthday.

## Making birthday miyeokguk at home

Dried miyeok expands a lot, so a small handful is enough for two bowls.

1. **Soak** about 10 g of dried seaweed in cold water for 20 minutes, then squeeze and cut it.
2. **Sauté** thin beef strips and the seaweed in sesame oil for 2–3 minutes.
3. **Add** water and garlic, then simmer for 20 minutes.
4. **Season** with soup soy sauce and a little salt, and serve with rice.

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Make miyeokguk for a Korean friend's birthday
- Gift yeot or sticky rice cakes before an exam
- Sauté the beef and seaweed in sesame oil first
- Ask "미역국 먹었어요?" on someone's birthday

</Do>
<Dont>

- Serve seaweed soup before a test or interview
- Use a whole package of dried seaweed for one pot
- Stick chopsticks upright in a bowl of rice
- Take the superstitions too seriously; most are jokes

</Dont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why do Koreans eat seaweed soup on their birthday?**
Mothers eat it after childbirth, so eating it on your birthday honors your mother and the day you were born.

**What does "eating seaweed soup" mean in Korean slang?**
미역국을 먹다 means failing an exam or being rejected, because slippery seaweed makes you "slip."

**What do Koreans give students before an exam?**
Sticky foods like yeot (rice taffy) and chapssal-tteok, because 붙다 means both "stick" and "pass."

**Is miyeok the same as wakame?**
Yes. Dried wakame sold in US grocery stores works for miyeokguk.
