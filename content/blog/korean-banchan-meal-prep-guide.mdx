Korean home cooks have meal-prepped for generations: a weekend session of banchan (반찬, side dishes) means weeknight dinners need only rice and one hot soup or main. Five or six well-chosen banchan, cooked in the right order, take about 90 minutes.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Make:** 2 braised sides, 2 namul, 1 stir-fry and 1 fresh salad
- **Time:** about 90 minutes with one pot and one pan
- **Keeps:** braised sides about 5–7 days, namul and fresh salads 2–3 days
- **Avoid:** making all namul on Sunday; they're the first to spoil

</KeyTakeaways>

## Which banchan keep best

Salt, soy sauce and sugar help preserve banchan, so braised (jorim) and stir-fried (bokkeum) sides last much longer than lightly dressed vegetables.

These are home-kitchen guidelines for food kept cold in airtight containers. When in doubt, follow USDA guidance and finish cooked leftovers within 3–4 days.

## A 90-minute cooking order

The trick is to start the slowest dish first and reuse the same water and pan. This order works for the six dishes above.

1. **0:00 – Start the jangjorim.** Simmer the beef in water with garlic. It cooks mostly unattended for about 40 minutes.
2. **0:10 – Boil one pot of water for blanching.** Blanch the spinach for 30 seconds, then the bean sprouts in the same water for 4–5 minutes. Rinse both in cold water.
3. **0:25 – Season the namul.** Squeeze the vegetables dry and toss each with garlic, sesame oil and soy sauce or salt.
4. **0:40 – Cut and salt the radish** for musaengchae, then dress it at the end.
5. **0:50 – Make the gamja-jorim** in a pan, then wipe the pan.
6. **1:10 – Stir-fry the eomuk-bokkeum** in the same pan.
7. **1:20 – Finish the jangjorim** with soy sauce and sugar, then cool everything before packing.

<Callout type="tip" title="Cool before you cover">
Spread hot banchan on a plate for 10–15 minutes before you put the lid on. Trapped steam drips back into the container and shortens its fridge life.
</Callout>

## Storing and serving through the week

Glass containers with tight lids are standard in Korean kitchens, and many families keep them in a dedicated shelf or a kimchi refrigerator. Serve with clean chopsticks or spoons, never ones that have been in your mouth, so bacteria don't get into the whole batch.

- **Monday and Tuesday:** finish the namul and musaengchae first
- **Wednesday and Thursday:** braised potatoes and fish cake
- **Friday:** jangjorim, often chopped over rice with butter and a fried egg

Each night, add one hot dish: a stew such as [kimchi jjigae](/kpantry/en/recipes/kimchi-jjigae) or [doenjang jjigae](/kpantry/en/recipes/doenjang-jjigae), or a quick protein like [dubu-buchim](/kpantry/en/recipes/dubu-buchim).

## Buying banchan in Korea

In Korea, busy families also buy banchan by weight at banchan shops (반찬가게) and supermarket counters. It's a good way for travelers with a kitchen to try many sides at once.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does Korean banchan last in the fridge?**
Braised sides like jangjorim keep about 5–7 days; namul and fresh salads about 2–3 days. Keep them cold in airtight containers.

**Which banchan are best for meal prep?**
Jorim (braised) and bokkeum (stir-fried) sides keep longest. Namul is best eaten early in the week.

**Can you freeze banchan?**
Jangjorim and some braised dishes freeze reasonably well. Namul and fresh salads turn watery, so make those fresh.

**How many banchan should I make for a week?**
Five or six is plenty for one or two people, especially with kimchi and one hot dish each night.
