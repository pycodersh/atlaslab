Daegu, Korea's big inland city, is a basin ringed by mountains, and this route shows both sides: the busy downtown streets of Dongseongno and a mural lane honouring a much-loved singer, then a cable car up Apsan for the view at sunset and a night market to finish.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **The route:** Dongseongno & Spark Land → Kim Gwangseok Street → Apsan Observatory → Seomun Night Market
- **Getting there:** KTX from Seoul to Dongdaegu Station, then the subway downtown
- **Cost:** the streets and market are free; Spark Land and the Apsan cable car are paid
- **Best day:** Friday to Sunday, when Seomun Night Market runs
- **Avoid:** leaving Apsan too late for the cable car down

</KeyTakeaways>

## Dongseongno and Spark Land

Dongseongno is downtown Daegu's main shopping area: pedestrian streets of fashion shops, cosmetics stores, cafes and restaurants between Jungangno and Banwoldang stations. Spark Land, an entertainment building in the middle of it, has a Ferris wheel on its roof, and riding it is the quickest way to see the downtown from above.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Getting there** | Jungangno Station (Line 1), or Banwoldang Station (Lines 1 and 2) |
| **Tip** | Spark Land's rides and Ferris wheel are ticketed; check hours on the day |

## Kim Gwangseok Street

Kim Gwangseok Street, officially Kim Gwangseok Dasi-geurigi-gil, is a lane of about 350 metres beside Bangcheon Market, lined with murals, statues and lyrics honouring the folk singer Kim Gwangseok, who grew up in the neighbourhood. Small cafes and shops line the lane, and Bangcheon Market is right beside it.

It's best in the afternoon, before you head to Apsan. People live along the lane, so keep the noise down.

Apsan is the mountain on Daegu's southern edge. The Apsan cable car, about 790 metres long, carries you most of the way up, and a short walk from the top station leads to the observatory deck, which looks out over the whole basin city. Time it for sunset and you'll see the lights come on below.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Getting there** | Taxi to the cable car base station in Apsan Park |
| **Tickets** | The cable car is paid; check current fares and the last ride down before you go |
| **Tip** | Bring a layer; it's windy and cool on the deck after dark |

Seomun Market is one of Korea's oldest and largest traditional markets. In the evenings, the street beside it becomes Seomun Night Market, with rows of food stalls selling grilled skewers, dumplings, fried snacks and desserts, plus live performances on a small stage.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **When** | Fridays to Sundays from 7 pm in 2026, March to December |
| **Closes** | 11:30 pm on Fridays and Saturdays, 10:30 pm on Sundays |
| **Getting there** | Seomun Market Station (Line 3), right next to the market |

The night market's schedule has changed from year to year, so check before you go. Our guide to [ordering Korean street food](/blog/en/k-patto/ordering-korean-street-food) has the phrases for the stalls.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which days is Seomun Night Market open?**
Fridays to Sundays in 2026, from 7 pm, between March and December.

**How long is Kim Gwangseok Street?**
About 350 metres, an easy 20–30 minute stroll with stops.

**How do I get up to Apsan Observatory?**
Take the Apsan cable car most of the way, then walk a short distance from the top station.

**Is Daegu a good day trip from Seoul?**
Yes. The KTX to Dongdaegu takes under two hours.
