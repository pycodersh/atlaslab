Sundae (순대) is Korean blood sausage, usually pig intestine stuffed with glass noodles, pork blood and seasonings, then steamed and sliced. It's mild and chewy, sold at bunsik snack shops and markets, and what you dip it in depends on where in Korea you are.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **What it is:** a steamed sausage of glass noodles, pork blood and rice or barley
- **Taste:** mild, savory and chewy, not gamey for most people
- **Served with:** liver and lung slices, plus a regional dip
- **Avoid:** expecting a firm Western-style sausage; sundae is soft and springy

</KeyTakeaways>

## The dip changes by region

| Region | Typical dip | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Seoul and Gyeonggi** | Salt mixed with chili powder (소금 · 고춧가루) | The most common in Seoul bunsik shops |
| **Busan and Gyeongsang** | Ssamjang or makjang (soybean paste) | Thick, savory paste |
| **Jeolla** | Chojang (sweet-sour chili paste) | Bright and tangy |
| **Jeju** | Soy sauce or vinegar soy | Simple and salty |
| **Chungcheong** | Salted shrimp (새우젓) | Often with sundae soup |

These are general patterns, and many shops offer more than one dip.

Locals often have strong opinions about the "right" dip, and moving to another region can mean getting used to a new one. Many bunsik shops in Seoul offer both salt and ssamjang, so try them side by side.

## Types of sundae

The common bunsik-shop sundae is the inexpensive glass-noodle kind, sold by the plate with sliced liver and lung. Regional specialty sundae is richer: **Abai sundae** from Sokcho uses squid or large intestine casing, **Byeongcheon sundae** near Cheonan is famous for its vegetable-rich filling, and **Baegam sundae** in Yongin is known for its hearty, meaty texture.

- **Ojingeo sundae (오징어순대):** squid stuffed with vegetables and noodles, a Sokcho specialty
- **Sundae-guk (순댓국):** sundae in a hearty pork soup with rice, a filling meal
- **Sundae bokkeum (순대볶음):** stir-fried sundae with vegetables and chili paste, popular in Sillim-dong

<Callout type="tip" title="Ask for 'sundae only'">
At bunsik shops, sundae comes with liver and lung (내장) by default. If you'd rather skip the organs, ask for 순대만 주세요 (sundae only, please).
</Callout>

## How sundae is made

Traditional sundae starts with cleaned pig intestine, stuffed with a mixture of glass noodles, pork blood, glutinous rice or barley, and chopped vegetables such as cabbage, green onion and bean sprouts, seasoned with garlic, ginger and salt. The sausages are steamed until firm, then sliced into rounds. Mass-produced sundae at bunsik shops uses mostly glass noodles, which gives it a light, springy texture, while specialty sundae has more vegetables, rice and meat.

Sundae has a long history in Korea, with old cookbooks describing sausages made with various fillings. It became a cheap, everyday street food in the 20th century.

- **Liver and lung:** often served alongside, soft and mild
- **Fresh vs reheated:** steamed sundae is best eaten warm
- **Market tip:** traditional markets sell sundae by weight, cut to order

## Where to try sundae

Bunsik shops sell sundae next to tteokbokki and fried snacks, often in the 떡튀순 combo. Traditional markets like Gwangjang and Sillim's Sundae Town serve it in big portions. Sundae-guk restaurants are common, cheap and often open late.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is Korean sundae made of?**
Usually pig intestine stuffed with glass noodles, pork blood, barley or rice and seasonings.

**What does sundae taste like?**
Mild, savory and chewy, with a soft texture from the glass noodles.

**Why do people dip sundae differently?**
Regional food traditions differ: salt and chili powder in Seoul, ssamjang in Busan, chojang in Jeolla.

**Is sundae the same as the ice cream sundae?**
No. Korean 순대 is a savory sausage; the name just sounds similar in English.
