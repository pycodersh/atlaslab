Korean rentals come in two main forms: jeonse, where you pay a very large deposit and no monthly rent, and wolse, where you pay a smaller deposit plus monthly rent. Most foreigners rent wolse, and whichever you choose, protecting your deposit is the most important step.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Jeonse (전세):** a huge deposit, often a large share of the home's value, and no monthly rent
- **Wolse (월세):** a smaller deposit plus monthly rent; the common choice for foreigners
- **Protect your deposit:** check the property register, register your address and get a fixed date stamp
- **Avoid:** signing before checking the landlord's debts on the property register

</KeyTakeaways>

## Jeonse vs wolse vs banjeonse

| | Jeonse (전세) | Wolse (월세) | Banjeonse (반전세) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Deposit** | Very large | Smaller, often several million to tens of millions of won | Medium to large |
| **Monthly rent** | None | Yes | Lower than wolse |
| **Typical term** | 2 years | 1–2 years | 1–2 years |
| **Main risk** | Losing a large deposit if the landlord defaults | Higher monthly costs | Both, to a lesser degree |
| **Best for** | Long-term residents with large savings or loans | Most foreigners, students and newcomers | Those who want lower rent with a manageable deposit |

Deposits and rents vary hugely by city, neighborhood and building, so compare listings in the area you want.

## Protecting your deposit

| Step | Korean | Why it matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Check the property register** | 등기부등본 | Shows the owner and any mortgages or debts on the home |
| **Register your address** | 전입신고 | Establishes your residence at the property |
| **Get a fixed date stamp** | 확정일자 | Gives your deposit priority if the property is sold |
| **Consider deposit insurance** | 전세보증보험 | Guarantees the deposit's return in many cases |
| **Use a licensed agent** | 공인중개사 | Licensed agents have legal duties and insurance |

<Callout type="warn" title="Jeonse fraud">
Some tenants have lost deposits when landlords owed more than the property was worth. Always check the property register, and ask a licensed agent or a legal advisor if the numbers don't add up.
</Callout>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can foreigners rent with jeonse?**
Yes, but it requires a very large deposit, and many foreigners choose wolse instead. Jeonse loans may be harder to get without a long credit history in Korea.

**Is my wolse deposit returned?**
Yes, at the end of the contract, minus any agreed deductions such as damage or unpaid bills.

**What is banjeonse?**
A hybrid: a larger deposit than wolse in exchange for lower monthly rent.

**How much is the agent fee?**
It's capped by law and depends on the deposit and rent. Ask the agent for the exact amount before signing.
