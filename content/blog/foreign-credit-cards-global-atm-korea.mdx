Korea is close to cashless, and foreign Visa and Mastercard cards work at most shops, restaurants, taxis and cafes. Cash is still handy for street stalls, markets and topping up transit cards, so withdraw a small amount from a Global ATM.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Cards work:** most stores, cafes, restaurants, taxis and kiosks
- **Cash still needed:** some street food, traditional markets, T-money top-ups
- **For cash:** use ATMs marked "Global" or with your card network's logo
- **Avoid:** standard Korean ATMs that only serve local bank cards

</KeyTakeaways>

## Where foreign cards work

| Place | Card? | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Convenience stores, chains, malls** | Yes | Contactless usually works |
| **Restaurants and cafes** | Yes | Kiosks may reject some cards; pay at the counter instead |
| **Taxis** | Usually | Tap or insert at the driver's reader |
| **Street food and markets** | Sometimes | Carry some cash |
| **Transit card top-ups** | Mostly no | Machines usually take cash only |
| **Small family restaurants** | Usually | Occasionally cash or bank transfer only |

## Finding cash

| ATM type | Accepts foreign cards? | Where |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Global ATM (글로벌 ATM)** | Yes: Visa, Mastercard, and often UnionPay, JCB | Airports, big stations, bank branches, some convenience stores |
| **Standard Korean ATM** | Often no | Everywhere |
| **Exchange counters** | Cash in, won out | Airports, Myeongdong, near tourist areas |

<Callout type="tip" title="Fees">
Global ATMs charge an operator fee, typically a few thousand won per withdrawal, on top of your own bank's foreign fees. Fewer, larger withdrawals cost less than many small ones.
</Callout>

## How much cash to carry

Most days you can get by on cards alone, but a little cash saves trouble at markets and transit machines. Plan by what you'll actually do, and top up from a Global ATM when you run low rather than carrying a large amount.

| Your day | Cash to carry | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **City sightseeing, malls, cafes** | 10,000–20,000 KRW | Cards cover almost everything |
| **Street food and markets** | 30,000–50,000 KRW | Some stalls are cash-only |
| **Topping up T-money** | 10,000–20,000 KRW in notes | Top-up machines mostly take cash |
| **Day trips to small towns** | 50,000 KRW or more | Rural shops and buses are less card-friendly |

Keep notes small: 1,000 and 10,000 KRW notes are easiest for vendors to change, and many machines don't accept 50,000 KRW notes.

## When a card is declined

| Problem | Try this |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Kiosk rejects the card** | Pay at the counter with staff |
| **Online-only Korean payment** | Ask if the shop takes cash, or use a different card |
| **Card needs a PIN** | Most terminals take signatures or no-PIN taps; enter a 4-digit PIN if asked |
| **Bank blocked the payment** | Tell your bank before you travel |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Korea really cashless?**
For most purchases, yes. Cards and mobile payments dominate. Cash is still useful at markets, some street food stalls and for transit card top-ups.

**Which ATMs work with foreign cards?**
Look for ATMs labeled Global ATM or showing your card network's logo, found at airports, large stations, bank branches and some convenience stores.

**Should I pay in won or my home currency?**
Choose won. Paying in your home currency usually means a worse exchange rate set by the terminal.

**Can I use Apple Pay in Korea?**
Apple Pay works at some terminals with Korean-issued cards. For foreign cards, physical contactless cards are more reliable.
