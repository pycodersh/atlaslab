Seoul's subway is one of the easiest systems in the world to use without speaking Korean: lines are color-coded, signs are in English, and map apps do the routing. So most visitors never say a word on it, until something goes wrong and they need to ask.

## Asking the way

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 경복궁에 어떻게 가요? | gyeongbokgunge eotteoke gayo? | How do I get to Gyeongbokgung? |
| 어디서 타요? | eodiseo tayo? | Where do I get on? |
| 여기서 갈아타요? | yeogiseo garatayo? | Do I transfer here? |
| 이 방향 맞아요? | i banghyang majayo? | Is this the right direction? |

**~에 어떻게 가요?** is the most useful sentence for getting around. Start with **저기요** (*jeogiyo*, "excuse me") to get someone's attention. [Asking for directions](/blog/en/k-patto/asking-for-directions-in-korean) covers the answers you'll get above ground.

## What you'll hear back

| You'll hear | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 3호선으로 갈아타세요. | Transfer to Line 3. |
| 시청역에서 내리세요. | Get off at City Hall. |
| 반대편에서 타세요. | Take it from the other side. |
| 두 정거장 가세요. | Go two stops. |

If you catch the line number and the station name, you have enough. You don't need the rest of the sentence.

## Words on the signs

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 환승 | hwanseung | transfer |
| 방면 | bangmyeon | toward (direction) |
| 출구 | chulgu | exit |
| 급행 / 일반 | geupaeng / ilban | express / all stops |
| 교통카드 | gyotongkadeu | transit card |

**방면** saves you the most trouble. Platform signs like **왕십리 방면** ("toward Wangsimni") tell you which way the train goes, which is usually the thing people get wrong.

**출구** matters more than you'd expect. Big stations can have a dozen or more exits spread over several blocks, and Koreans give meeting points by exit number: **3번 출구에서 만나요** ("let's meet at Exit 3").

## The announcements

- **이번 역은 ○○역입니다.** This station is ○○.
- **다음 역은 ○○역입니다.** The next station is ○○.
- **내리실 문은 왼쪽입니다.** The doors open on the left.

These use the formal **-습니다** style of public announcements. You'll never need to say them, but catching **이번 역** and **다음 역** lets you stop staring at the map.

## Topping up your card

At a station machine or a convenience store: **만 원 충전해 주세요** (*man won chungjeonhae juseyo*, "please charge 10,000 won"). Top-ups are usually cash only. The [transit card guide](/blog/en/k-patto/seoul-subway-transfer-transit-card-guide) explains which card to buy and how transfers are charged.

## Polite or casual?

| Polite (strangers, staff) | Casual (friends) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 어디서 갈아타요? | 어디서 갈아타? | Where do we transfer? |
| 다음 역에서 내려요. | 다음 역에서 내리자. | Let's get off at the next stop. |

## When you're lost

**길을 잃었어요** (*gireul ireosseoyo*, "I'm lost") changes the answer you get: people slow down, and some will walk you to the right platform.
