Seoul's subway is one of the easiest systems in the world to use without speaking the language. Everything is colour-coded, signs are in English, and apps handle the routing.

Which means most visitors never say a word in it — right up until the moment something goes wrong and they need to ask.

---

## Asking how to get somewhere

**~에 어떻게 가요?** — *how do I get to ~?*

- 경복궁에 어떻게 가요? — How do I get to Gyeongbokgung?
- 홍대입구에 어떻게 가요? — How do I get to Hongdae?

This is the single most useful sentence for getting around. Add 저기요 in front to get someone's attention first.

**어디서 타요?** — *where do I get on?*

**여기서 갈아타요?** — *do I transfer here?*

---

## The answer you will get

Most answers use one of two patterns, so recognising them matters more than producing them.

**~으로 갈아타세요** — *transfer to ~*

- 여기서 3호선으로 갈아타세요. — Transfer to Line 3 here.

**~에서 내리세요** — *get off at ~*

- 시청역에서 내리세요. — Get off at City Hall.

If you catch the line number and the station name, you have enough. You do not need the rest of the sentence.

## Words on the signs

| Korean | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 환승 | transfer |
| 방면 | in the direction of |
| 출구 | exit |
| 급행 | express |
| 일반 | local (all stops) |
| 상행 / 하행 | inbound / outbound |
| 교통카드 | transit card |

**방면** is the one that saves you. Platform signs say 왕십리 방면 — *toward Wangsimni* — which tells you which direction the train goes, and that is usually the thing you got wrong.

**출구** matters more than you expect. Large stations have fifteen exits spread over several blocks, and taking the wrong one means a long walk. Exit numbers are the standard way Koreans give directions: 3번 출구에서 만나요 — *let's meet at Exit 3*.

## What the announcements say

The same few sentences repeat at every station.

- **이번 역은 ○○역입니다.** — This station is ○○.
- **내리실 문은 왼쪽입니다.** — The doors open on the left.
- **다음 역은 ○○역입니다.** — The next station is ○○.
- **안전선 밖으로 물러서 주십시오.** — Please stand back from the safety line.

These are in the formal -습니다 style used in public announcements. You will never need to say them, but recognising 이번 역 and 다음 역 lets you stop staring at the map.

## Topping up your card

At a convenience store or a station machine:

- 충전해 주세요. — Please charge it.
- 만 원 충전해 주세요. — Charge 10,000 won.
- 카드 주세요. — (buying one) A card, please.

T-money cards work on subways, buses, and many taxis, and you can buy one at any convenience store.

## When you are genuinely lost

- 길을 잃었어요. — I'm lost.
- 여기가 어디예요? — Where am I?
- 이 역 맞아요? — Is this the right station?

Koreans are unusually willing to help with directions, often walking you to the right platform. 저기요 to get attention, then the question.
