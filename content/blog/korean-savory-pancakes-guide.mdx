Jeon (전) are Korean savory pancakes and pan-fried fritters, and on rainy days Koreans pair them with makgeolli, a milky rice wine. Order a big pancake to share, dip it in soy-vinegar sauce, and gently swirl the makgeolli bottle before pouring.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Most popular:** haemul pajeon (seafood and green onion pancake), around 15,000–20,000 KRW
- **Rainy-day ritual:** pancakes and makgeolli when it rains
- **Makgeolli:** sweet, fizzy, around 6–8% alcohol; swirl the bottle before pouring
- **Avoid:** shaking a fizzy makgeolli bottle hard; it can spray when opened

</KeyTakeaways>

## Why pancakes on rainy days?

| Theory | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The sound** | The sizzle of batter in hot oil sounds like rain |
| **Comfort** | Hot, oily food feels good on cold, wet days |
| **Tradition** | Farmers couldn't work in the rain, so they cooked pancakes and drank makgeolli at home |

On rainy days, pancake and makgeolli bars fill up fast, especially in areas like Jongno and around universities.

<Callout type="tip" title="How to pour makgeolli">
The rice sediment settles at the bottom. Turn the bottle gently upside down a couple of times, or swirl it, then open it slowly over the table. It's traditionally drunk from shallow metal or ceramic bowls.
</Callout>

## Making pajeon at home

A seafood pancake is one of the easiest Korean dishes to cook abroad. The secret is a thin batter, a hot, well-oiled pan, and patience before flipping.

| Step | What to do | Tip |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Batter** | Mix pancake flour (부침가루) or plain flour with cold water until thin | Ice-cold water makes it crispier |
| **2. Green onions** | Lay whole green onions flat in the oiled pan | Cut them to pan length |
| **3. Pour and top** | Pour batter over, then add squid and shrimp | Use just enough batter to bind |
| **4. Fry** | Cook on medium-high until the bottom is golden | Add oil around the edges |
| **5. Flip** | Flip once, press down, and fry the other side | Don't flip repeatedly |

Serve it with a dip of soy sauce, vinegar and a little chili powder.

## Makgeolli basics

| Style | What to expect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Standard (생막걸리)** | Unpasteurized, fizzy, slightly sweet; keep it cold |
| **Sweet flavored** | Chestnut, banana or peach versions, lower alcohol taste |
| **Premium craft** | Less sweet, more complex, often from small breweries |
| **Dongdongju** | A cloudier, lightly filtered rice wine sometimes served in bowls |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What does makgeolli taste like?**
Milky, slightly sweet and tangy, with a light fizz. Most bottles are around 6–8% alcohol.

**Why do Koreans eat jeon when it rains?**
Popular explanations include the sizzling sound resembling rain, and the comfort of hot, oily food on a gloomy day.

**Is pajeon spicy?**
No. Seafood green onion pancake is savory and mild. Kimchi jeon has some tang and heat.

**Can I buy makgeolli at convenience stores?**
Yes. Convenience stores and supermarkets sell standard makgeolli cheaply, usually in the refrigerated section.
