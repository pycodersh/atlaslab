Dictionary verbs (가다, 먹다, 마시다, 공부하다) change shape in a sentence: 가요, 먹어요, 마셔요, 공부해요. One rule is behind all of it, and it comes down to a single vowel.

## Step 1: Find the stem

Every Korean verb in the dictionary ends in **다**. Remove it and you have the stem.

| Dictionary form | Stem |
|---|---|
| 가다 | 가 |
| 먹다 | 먹 |
| 마시다 | 마시 |
| 읽다 | 읽 |
| 하다 | 하 |

The stem never appears alone. It is a building block that endings attach to.

## Step 2: Look at the last vowel of the stem

This is the whole rule.

| Last stem vowel | Add | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ㅏ or ㅗ | 아요 | 가요 *gayo* · 앉아요 *anjayo* · 좋아요 *joayo* |
| anything else | 어요 | 먹어요 *meogeoyo* · 읽어요 *ilgeoyo* · 마셔요 *masyeoyo* |
| 하다 verbs | 해요 | 해요 *haeyo* · 공부해요 *gongbuhaeyo* · 일해요 *ilhaeyo* |

하다 is irregular, but so common that you learn it as its own case.

## Step 3: The contractions

This is where the rule seems to break, and it does not — the vowels just merge.

| Stem | Expected | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| 가 | 가아요 | **가요** |
| 오 | 오아요 | **와요** |
| 보 | 보아요 | **봐요** |
| 주 | 주어요 | **줘요** |
| 마시 | 마시어요 | **마셔요** |
| 배우 | 배우어요 | **배워요** |
| 되 | 되어요 | **돼요** |

The pattern:

- ㅏ + 아 → just ㅏ (가아 → 가)
- ㅗ + 아 → ㅘ (오아 → 와)
- ㅜ + 어 → ㅝ (주어 → 줘)
- ㅣ + 어 → ㅕ (마시어 → 마셔)

You do not have to memorise these as separate items. Say the two vowels quickly and they merge on their own — the spelling just records what your mouth already does.

## The verb 이다

이다 (to be) does not follow the pattern. It attaches directly to a noun and changes shape based on whether the noun ends in a consonant.

- 학생 + 이에요 → 학생이에요 (I'm a student)
- 의사 + 예요 → 의사예요 (I'm a doctor)

Consonant ending takes 이에요, vowel ending takes 예요.

## Why this rule is worth memorising properly

Because 아요/어요 is not one ending. It is a *form* that dozens of other endings attach to.

Once you know 먹어요, you get these almost for free:

| Form | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 먹었어요 | ate ([past tense](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-past-tense-explained)) |
| 먹어서 | because I ate |
| 먹어 주세요 | please eat |
| 먹어 봤어요 | I've tried eating |
| 먹어도 돼요 | you may eat |

All of them are built on 먹어. Learn the rule once and a large amount of grammar downstream stops requiring separate memorisation.

## Making it negative

Two ways, both easy.

**안 + verb** — the short one, most common in speech.

- 안 가요. — I don't go.
- 안 먹어요. — I don't eat.

For 하다 verbs, 안 goes between the noun and 하다: 공부 안 해요.

**Stem + 지 않아요** — slightly more formal.

- 가지 않아요.
- 먹지 않아요.

## Common mistakes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 마시아요 | 마셔요 | The last stem vowel is ㅣ, so 어요, and ㅣ + 어 merges into ㅕ. |
| 공부하아요 | 공부해요 | Every 하다 verb becomes 해요. |
| 가아요, 보아요 | 가요, 봐요 | Nobody says the unmerged form. |

The same base form also drives [irregular verbs](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-irregular-verbs-seven-types) and [requests with 주세요](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-requests-a-eo-juseyo).
