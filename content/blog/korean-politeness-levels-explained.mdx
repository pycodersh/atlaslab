Every Korean sentence ends in a speech level, and that ending says something about your relationship with the listener before the content arrives. Grammar books list several levels; in practice you need three.

<RuleCard badge="Critical rule" title="The golden rule of Korean politeness: start with 해요체">
  <RuleAvoid ko="반말" rom="banmal" note="Casual speech, like 어디 가? and 고마워." warn="With older people or staff, it sounds dismissive." />
  <RuleAvoid ko="합니다체" rom="hamnidache" note="Formal speech for announcements and business." warn="Used with friends, it keeps them at a distance." />
  <RuleInstead lead="Use 해요체 with everyone until someone invites you to speak casually.">
    <RuleExample ko="밥 먹었어요?" rom="bap meogeosseoyo?" en="Did you eat?" />
    <RuleExample ko="어디 가요?" rom="eodi gayo?" en="Where are you going?" />
    <RuleExample ko="고마워요." rom="gomawoyo" en="Thank you." />
    <RuleExample ko="말 놓으세요." rom="mal noeuseyo" en="Please speak casually to me. (how 반말 gets offered)" />
  </RuleInstead>
</RuleCard>

## The three that matter

| Level | Where you hear it | Endings |
|---|---|---|
| **해요체** (polite) | shops, taxis, colleagues, strangers, older people | -아요 · -어요 · -예요 |
| **합니다체** (formal) | announcements, news, presentations, staff scripts | -습니다 · -ㅂ니다 · -습니까 |
| **반말** (casual) | close friends, family, people clearly younger | -아 · -어 · plain forms |

**해요체** is the default: polite without being stiff, and right in almost every situation a visitor meets. **합니다체** you'll hear constantly in public spaces and rarely need to produce. **반말** is the one that causes offence when used with the wrong person.

## The same sentence, three ways

| Level | "Did you eat?" | "Thank you." |
|---|---|---|
| 합니다체 | 식사하셨습니까? | 감사합니다. |
| 해요체 | 밥 먹었어요? | 고마워요. |
| 반말 | 밥 먹었어? | 고마워. |

해요체 and 반말 often differ by a single syllable: 요 does most of the work. 감사합니다 is the one formal phrase everyone uses everywhere, including in otherwise 해요체 conversations.

## Dropping 요: the mechanics

| 요 form | 반말 | Romanization |
|---|---|---|
| 먹어요 | 먹어 | *meogeo* |
| 좋아요 | 좋아 | *joa* |
| 가요? | 가? | *ga?* |
| 학생이에요 | 학생이야 | *haksaengiya* |
| 고마워요 | 고마워 | *gomawo* |

Most of the time you just drop 요. Nouns change a little: 이에요 → 이야, 예요 → 야. The grammar is the easy part; knowing when you're allowed to is the real skill.

## Honorifics are a separate axis

**Speech level** is about your relationship with the *listener*. **Honorifics** show respect for the *person you're talking about*, with -(으)시- and special verbs.

| Plain | Honorific | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 먹다 | 드시다 | 할머니가 드세요. (Grandma is eating.) |
| 자다 | 주무시다 | 할머니께서 주무세요. (Grandma is sleeping.) |
| 있다 | 계시다 | 선생님 계세요? (Is the teacher in?) |
| 말하다 | 말씀하시다 | 말씀하세요. (Please go ahead.) |

So you can speak casually to a friend while using honorifics about their father. The two systems are independent. Honorific endings are also how Koreans say a polite "you" without the word: the [guide to 당신 and 너](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-never-say-dangsin-neo) shows how.

## When 반말 is safe, and when it isn't

| Safe to use 반말 | Not safe |
|---|---|
| close friends your age, once agreed | anyone noticeably older, however friendly |
| children | someone you've just met |
| when the other person invites it | anyone senior to you at work |
| talking to yourself | service staff |

**The asymmetry matters.** An older person may speak 반말 to you while you answer with 요. That's normal, not an insult, and it doesn't mean you can switch.

## How the switch is offered

Koreans don't drift into casual speech; they agree on it out loud, because switching is a statement about the relationship.

| You'll hear | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 말 놓으세요. | Please speak casually to me. |
| 말 편하게 해요. | Let's talk comfortably. |
| 우리 동갑이네요! | We're the same age! (often the cue) |

Once an older person offers, both sides switch. If you're the one asking, **말 놓아도 될까요?** ("may I speak casually?") is the polite way. Finding out you're the same age (동갑) is a common trigger: the [age guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-age-sal-man-nai) explains how people work that out.

## What happens if you get it wrong

Less than you fear: people are forgiving with learners. The one thing worth avoiding is 반말 with someone older or in a service role, which reads as dismissive rather than as a language slip. The opposite risk is subtler: 합니다체 with friends keeps a distance you may not intend.

<Callout type="tip" title="Pick one level and keep it">

Erring polite costs nothing. The habit to avoid is **mixing levels with the same person**: ending one sentence with 요 and the next without sounds unsteady rather than friendly.

</Callout>
