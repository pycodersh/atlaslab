You've managed the order. You said the drink name, you got the size right, and then the barista asks one more thing you didn't catch.

It's almost always the same question, and the answer is two words.

## The question

**드시고 가세요?** — *deu-si-go ga-se-yo?* — "Are you having it here?"

Or the longer version:

**드시고 가세요, 아니면 가져가세요?** — "For here, or to go?"

Some baristas say **매장이세요, 포장이세요?** — *mae-jang-i-se-yo, po-jang-i-se-yo?* — literally "in-store or packaged?"

They all mean the same thing.

## The exchange

**A:** 드시고 가세요?  
*(Are you having it here?)*  
**B:** 여기서 먹고 갈게요.  
*(I'll have it here.)*  
**A:** 네, 머그컵에 드릴게요.  
*(Okay, I'll serve it in a mug.)*

> **Key Nuances**
> - **드시고 가세요? / 매장이세요, 포장이세요?**: Two ways baristas ask "for here or to go?" — same question.
> - **여기서요 / 포장이요**: The two-word answers: "for here" or "to go."
> - **머그컵**: Staying means a real mug — single-use cups are restricted inside cafes, so the question is mandatory.
> - **포장해 주세요**: "Please pack it up" — for changing your mind, or taking restaurant leftovers home.

## The answer

Two options, and either one is a complete answer on its own:

**여기서 먹고 갈게요** — *yeo-gi-seo meok-go gal-ge-yo* — "I'll have it here"

**포장이요** — *po-jang-i-yo* — "To go"

Shorter versions work fine too. **여기서요** (here) and **가져갈게요** (I'll take it) are both natural.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 여기서 먹고 갈게요 | yeo-gi-seo meok-go gal-ge-yo | I'll have it here |
| 여기서요 | yeo-gi-seo-yo | For here |
| 포장이요 | po-jang-i-yo | To go |
| 가져갈게요 | ga-jyeo-gal-ge-yo | I'll take it with me |

## Why they always ask

It isn't small talk. The answer changes what your drink comes in.

**매장 (for here)** — a glass or ceramic mug. Real cups, washed and reused.

**포장 (to go)** — a disposable plastic or paper cup with a lid.

Korea restricted single-use cups inside cafes, so if you're staying, you get a real mug. That's why the question is mandatory rather than polite.

The barista will often confirm: **네, 머그컵에 드릴게요** — "Okay, I'll serve it in a mug."

## If you change your mind

You can ask for a to-go cup partway through:

**포장해 주세요** — *po-jang-hae ju-se-yo* — "Please pack it up"

Some cafes charge a small fee for the disposable cup, usually a few hundred won. Others don't.

## The same question elsewhere

You'll hear this at bakeries, fast food counters, and takeout restaurants too. The wording shifts slightly but the shape is identical, and **포장이요** works everywhere.

At a restaurant, asking to take leftovers home uses the same word: **포장해 주세요**.

## What to actually do

1. Order your drink
2. When they ask something you don't catch, it's probably this
3. Answer **여기서요** or **포장이요**
4. Expect a mug if you stay, a disposable cup if you go

One of these two words will get you through every cafe counter in Korea.
