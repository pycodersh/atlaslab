You've managed the order, and then the barista asks one more thing you didn't catch. It's almost always the same question, and the answer is two words.

## The exchange

**A:** 드시고 가세요?  
*(Are you having it here?)*  
**B:** 여기서 먹고 갈게요.  
*(I'll have it here.)*  
**A:** 네, 머그컵에 드릴게요.  
*(Okay, I'll serve it in a mug.)*

**먹고 갈게요** literally says "eat", but Koreans use it for drinks too. **마시고 갈게요** ("drink and go") is just as natural at a cafe.

## What staff will ask you

| You'll hear | Romanization | Literally |
|---|---|---|
| 드시고 가세요? | deusigo gaseyo? | Will you have it and go? |
| 드시고 가세요, 아니면 가져가세요? | ... animyeon gajyeogaseyo? | Have it here, or take it? |
| 매장이세요, 포장이세요? | maejangiseyo, pojangiseyo? | In-store or packaged? |

There are three common wordings, and they all mean "for here or to go?". **매장** means the store itself; **포장** means packaging, or takeout.

## Your answer

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 여기서요. | yeogiseoyo | For here. |
| 여기서 마시고 갈게요. | yeogiseo masigo galgeyo | I'll have it here. |
| 포장이요. | pojangiyo | To go. |
| 가져갈게요. | gajyeogalgeyo | I'll take it with me. |

Either short answer, **여기서요** or **포장이요**, is a complete reply on its own.

## Polite or casual?

| Staff (honorific) | You (polite) | With a friend (casual) |
|---|---|---|
| 드시고 가세요? | 여기서 마실게요. | 여기서 마시고 갈까? |
| 포장해 드릴까요? | 포장해 주세요. | 그냥 들고 가자. |

Staff use the honorific **드시다** for you. You never need it yourself: polite **-요** is right at any counter.

## Why they always ask

It changes what your drink comes in. Staying usually means a real mug or glass, and going means a disposable cup with a lid. Korea has restricted disposable cups for dine-in since 2018, so the question isn't small talk, though how strictly it's applied varies by cafe.

What you'll hear next confirms your answer:

- **머그컵에 드릴게요.** — I'll serve it in a mug.
- **컵홀더 필요하세요?** — Do you need a cup sleeve? (with a hot drink to go)

## If you change your mind

**포장해 주세요** (*pojanghae juseyo*, "please pack it up") turns a mug into a to-go cup. Some cafes charge a small fee for the disposable cup.

The same phrase works far beyond cafes: at bakeries, at fast-food counters, and for taking restaurant leftovers home.

## What to actually say

- For here: **여기서요.**
- To go: **포장이요.**
- Changed your mind: **포장해 주세요.**
