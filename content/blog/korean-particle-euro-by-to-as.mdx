If Korean particles were tools, (으)로 would be the Swiss Army knife. One small particle covers "by," "with," "to," "toward," "from," "as," and "in" (a language). English needs a different word for each. Korean uses one.

The easiest way to learn it is to meet its five jobs one at a time.

## Choosing 로 or 으로

The form depends on the last sound of the word before it:

- **로** after a vowel or after ㄹ: 버스로, 택시로, 카드로, 지하철로, 칼로
- **으로** after any other consonant: 손으로, 현금으로, 집으로

The ㄹ exception is the one that catches people. 지하철 ends in ㄹ, so it's **지하철로**, not 지하철으로.

---

## Job 1: means and tools ("by," "with")

This is the most common use.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 버스로 가요. | beoseu-ro gayo | I go by bus. |
| 카드로 결제할게요. | kadeu-ro gyeolje-halgeyo | I'll pay by card. |
| 현금으로 할게요. | hyeongeum-euro halgeyo | I'll pay in cash. |
| 손으로 먹어요. | son-euro meogeoyo | I eat with my hands. |
| 한국어로 말해요. | hangugeo-ro malhaeyo | I speak in Korean. |

Notice the last one. 로 also marks the language you use: 영어로 써 주세요 means "Please write it in English."

---

## Job 2: direction ("toward," "to")

로 shows which way you're heading.

- 이쪽으로 오세요. — Come this way.
- 오른쪽으로 가세요. — Go to the right.
- 앞으로 쭉 가세요. — Go straight ahead.
- 서울로 가요. — I'm heading to Seoul.

### 로 vs 에

Both can follow a place, and they feel different.

| Korean | Feel |
|---|---|
| 서울에 가요. | Seoul is the destination. |
| 서울로 가요. | I'm heading toward Seoul. |

에 marks the point where you arrive. 로 marks the direction you're moving in. For most travel talk, either works with places. 로 is the natural choice with directions (오른쪽으로) and when you give the driver an address: **이 주소로 가 주세요** ("Please go to this address").

---

## Job 3: materials ("made from")

- 쌀로 만들어요. — It's made from rice.
- 나무로 만든 의자 — a chair made of wood
- 밀가루로 만든 음식 — food made with flour

---

## Job 4: roles and status ("as")

This is the "as" meaning, and it's less obvious.

- 학생으로 왔어요. — I came as a student.
- 가이드로 일해요. — I work as a guide.
- 선물로 샀어요. — I bought it as a gift.
- 친구로 만났어요. — I met him as a friend.

---

## Job 5: choosing ("I'll go with this one")

When you're picking from options, 로 marks what you choose:

- **이걸로 주세요.** — I'll take this one, please.
- **이걸로 할게요.** — I'll go with this.
- 아이스로 주세요. — Iced, please.
- 매운 걸로 주세요. — The spicy one, please.

**이걸로 주세요** is one of the most useful sentences at a counter, a menu, or a market. It works every time you choose between two things.

---

## Handy sentences

- 이 주소로 가 주세요. — Please take me to this address.
- 영어로 된 메뉴 있어요? — Do you have a menu in English?
- 카드로 계산할게요. — I'll pay by card.
- 지하철로 얼마나 걸려요? — How long does it take by subway?
- 이쪽으로 가면 돼요? — Should I go this way?

---

## Common mistakes

**Using 에 for a method.** 버스에 가요 is wrong. Say 버스로 가요 or 버스를 타고 가요.

**Adding 으 after ㄹ.** 지하철으로 is wrong. Words ending in ㄹ take 로: 지하철로.

**Using 로 for the person you give something to.** 친구로 선물을 줬어요 means "I gave a gift as a friend." To say you gave it *to* a friend, use 친구에게 or 친구한테.

**Using 으로 after a vowel.** 버스으로 is wrong. Vowel-ending words take plain 로: 버스로, 택시로.

---

## 2-minute recap

- **로** after a vowel or ㄹ (버스로, 지하철로). **으로** after other consonants (손으로, 현금으로).
- Five jobs: **means/tools** (카드로), **direction** (오른쪽으로), **material** (쌀로), **role** (학생으로), **choice** (이걸로).
- 에 = the point where you arrive. 로 = the direction you're heading.
- 이걸로 주세요 and 이 주소로 가 주세요 are two sentences worth memorizing now.
- Don't use 로 for the person who receives something: use 에게 or 한테.
