English needs "by", "with", "toward", "in" and "as". Korean uses one particle for all of them: **(으)로**. You'll say it the first time you pay, take a taxi or point at a menu.

## 로 or 으로?

Look at the last sound of the word before it.

| Word ends in | Use | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| a vowel | **로** | 버스로, 카드로, 택시로 |
| ㄹ | **로** | 지하철로, 칼로, 서울로 |
| any other consonant | **으로** | 손으로, 현금으로, 오른쪽으로 |

The ㄹ row is the one that catches people: 지하철 ends in ㄹ, so it's **지하철로**, never 지하철으로.

**Listen for the link.** With 으로, the final consonant slides into the next syllable: 오른쪽으로 sounds like *oreunjjogeuro*, 현금으로 like *hyeongeumeuro*. That's why the romanization on this page has no hyphens.

## The five jobs

| Job | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Means**, tool, language: *how?* | 현금으로 할게요.<br />*hyeongeumeuro halgeyo* | I'll pay in cash. |
| **Direction**: *which way?* | 이쪽으로 오세요.<br />*ijjogeuro oseyo* | Come this way. |
| **Material**: *made of what?* | 쌀로 만들어요.<br />*ssallo mandeureoyo* | It's made from rice. |
| **Role**: *as what?* | 선물로 샀어요.<br />*seonmullo sasseoyo* | I bought it as a gift. |
| **Choice**: *which one?* | 아이스로 주세요.<br />*aiseuro juseyo* | Iced, please. |

### Means: how you do it

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 버스로 가요. | beoseuro gayo | I'm going by bus. |
| 지하철로 얼마나 걸려요? | jihacheollo eolmana geollyeoyo? | How long is it by subway? |
| 손으로 먹어도 돼요. | soneuro meogeodo dwaeyo | It's fine to eat with your hands. |
| 영어로 말해 주세요. | yeongeoro malhae juseyo | Please say it in English. |

The language you use counts as a tool too: **영어로 된 메뉴 있어요?** ("is there a menu in English?").

### Direction: which way you're heading

**이쪽으로**, **오른쪽으로**, **앞으로 쭉** are what you'll hear when someone gives you directions. The [directions guide](/blog/en/k-patto/asking-for-directions-in-korean) has the full set of replies.

### Choice: the counter sentence

**이걸로 주세요** ("this one, please") and **이걸로 할게요** ("I'll go with this one") work at every counter, menu and market stall. 이걸로 is 이것 + 으로, shortened in speech.

## 로 vs 에

Both can follow a place, but they look at the trip differently.

| Korean | Romanization | What it stresses |
|---|---|---|
| 서울에 가요. | seoure gayo | Seoul is where you'll arrive. |
| 서울로 가요. | seoullo gayo | Seoul is the direction you're heading. |
| 오른쪽으로 가세요. | oreunjjogeuro gaseyo | a direction, so only 으로 is natural |
| 2호선으로 갈아타세요. | ihoseoneuro garataseyo | Transfer to Line 2. |

With plain places, either works in everyday talk. With directions (오른쪽, 이쪽) and with transfers, (으)로 is the natural choice.

<Callout type="tip" title="Two more uses you'll hear">

**Changing into something:** **원화로 바꿔 주세요** (*wonhwaro bakkwo juseyo*, "please change it into won"). **A cause:** **감기로 못 왔어요** (*gamgiro mot wasseoyo*, "I couldn't come because of a cold"). The cause use is common in news and announcements: **사고로 지연되고 있습니다** ("delayed due to an accident").

</Callout>

## Common mistakes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 버스에 가요. | 버스로 가요. | The way you travel takes (으)로. |
| 지하철으로 | 지하철로 | ㄹ takes plain 로. |
| 버스으로 | 버스로 | A vowel takes plain 로. |
| 친구로 선물을 줬어요. | 친구한테 선물을 줬어요. | The person who receives takes 한테 or 에게. 친구로 means "as a friend". |

## Say it today

- Paying: **카드로 할게요.**
- In a taxi: **이 주소로 가 주세요.**
- At a counter: **이걸로 주세요.**
- In the subway: **몇 호선으로 갈아타요?** ("which line do I change to?")
