Yeouido is Seoul's island of offices, malls and riverside park; Yongsan, across the river, has the country's national museum and the boutique streets of Hannam-dong. Together they make a day of shopping, history and the Han River, with the river park saved for sunset if you prefer.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **The route:** The Hyundai Seoul → Yeouido Hangang Park → National Museum of Korea → Hannam-dong
- **Getting around:** walk between the first two, then subway or taxi across the river
- **Cost:** the park and the museum's permanent galleries are free
- **Best timing:** the museum is open late on Wednesdays and Saturdays, so on those days you can swap the order and finish at the river park at sunset
- **Avoid:** weekend afternoons at The Hyundai Seoul if you dislike crowds

</KeyTakeaways>

## The Hyundai Seoul

The Hyundai Seoul is one of Seoul's largest department stores, built around a tall, open atrium under a glass roof. Its upper floors have an indoor garden with real trees and lawn, and a big food hall in the basement. Even if you don't shop, it's worth a look for the space itself, and it hosts brand pop-ups that draw queues.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Getting there** | A short walk from Yeouido Station (Lines 5 and 9) |
| **Tip** | Go on a weekday morning; weekend afternoons are crowded |

Yeouido Hangang Park runs along the island's river side: wide lawns, cycle paths and convenience stores by the water. Seoulites come here to picnic, ride bikes and cook instant ramen in the stores' machines, then eat it facing the river. It's at its best around sunset.

Our [Han River picnic guide](/blog/en/k-patto/han-river-parks-seoul-guide) explains how the ramen machines work and where to sit, and our [Han River picnic phrases](/blog/en/k-patto/han-river-picnic-korean-phrases) cover the Korean you'll need for an evening by the river.

The National Museum of Korea holds the country's largest collection, from prehistoric tools and Silla gold crowns to Joseon paintings and celadon. Its permanent galleries are free, and the grounds include a pond with a traditional pavilion. You could spend a whole day here; two to three hours covers the highlights.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Getting there** | Ichon Station (Line 4 and Gyeongui-Jungang Line), with a covered walkway to the museum |
| **Fee** | Permanent galleries free; special exhibitions are ticketed |
| **Hours** | Opens daily, with late opening on Wednesdays and Saturdays; opening times changed in 2026, so check the official site |

## Hannam-dong

Hannam-dong, between Itaewon and the river, is where Seoul's designer boutiques, concept stores and stylish cafes cluster. The streets around Hangangjin Station and up toward Itaewon are best explored slowly on foot, browsing shops and stopping for coffee or dinner. The Leeum Museum of Art is also in the neighbourhood if you have time for one more museum.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Getting there** | Hangangjin Station (Line 6), or Hannam Station (Gyeongui-Jungang Line) |
| **When** | Late afternoon into the evening, when the cafes and restaurants are busy |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the National Museum of Korea free?**
The permanent galleries are free at the time of writing. Special exhibitions charge, and a general entry fee is under discussion for the future.

**When is the best time for Yeouido Hangang Park?**
Late afternoon into sunset, especially in spring and autumn.

**How do I get from Yeouido to the National Museum?**
By subway toward Ichon Station, or about 15–20 minutes by taxi depending on traffic.

**Is Hannam-dong near Itaewon?**
Yes. It's next to Itaewon, between it and the Han River.
