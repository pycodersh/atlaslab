Korean instant ramyeon becomes a real meal with a few simple additions: an egg, green onion, kimchi, a slice of cheese or some dumplings. Cook it in a pot with the right amount of water, add toppings at the right time, and finish with rice in the leftover broth.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Classic upgrade:** egg plus green onion in the last minute
- **Creamy and mild:** a slice of cheese on top of spicy noodles
- **Water:** about 500–550 ml per packet; too much makes the broth bland
- **Avoid:** overcooking; Koreans like their noodles firm and springy

</KeyTakeaways>

## Popular upgrades

| Add-on | When to add | What it does |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Egg** | Last minute; stir in or leave whole | Richness and protein |
| **Green onion** | Last 30 seconds | Freshness and aroma |
| **Kimchi** | With the noodles | Sour depth and crunch |
| **Cheese slice** | On top at the end | Creamy, softens spice |
| **Dumplings (mandu)** | At the start | Turns it into a full meal |
| **Rice cakes (tteok)** | At the start | Chewy texture |
| **Spam or ham** | At the start | Savory, budae-jjigae style |
| **Bean sprouts** | Last minute | Crunch, popular for hangovers |

## Cooking tips

Bring about 500–550 ml of water to a boil, add the soup base first, then the noodles. Cook for around 4 minutes, lifting the noodles with chopsticks a few times so they stay springy. Add the egg in the last minute and green onion at the end. Many Koreans eat straight from the pot, using the lid as a plate to cool the noodles.

- **Firm noodles:** lift and air them while cooking
- **Rich broth:** use slightly less water than the packet says
- **Finish with rice:** add a spoonful of rice to the leftover broth

<Callout type="tip" title="The rice finish">
When the noodles are gone, drop a bowl of rice into the remaining broth and eat it like soup. It's a classic, satisfying way to end the meal.
</Callout>

## Upgrades by mood

Different moods call for different upgrades. These pairings are popular in Korean homes and dorms:

- **Cold night:** Shin Ramyun with kimchi, egg and green onion
- **Hangover:** a mild ramyeon with bean sprouts and a cracked egg
- **Cheesy comfort:** spicy noodles topped with a slice of cheese and corn
- **Party pot:** budae jjigae-style, with Spam, sausage, tofu and kimchi
- **Light lunch:** cup noodles with a triangle kimbap dropped into the broth
- **Late-night snack:** Chapagetti with a fried egg on top

Some people also add a spoonful of gochugaru for extra heat, a drizzle of sesame oil at the end, or swap the broth water for milk in creamy versions.

## Combos locals love

Some combinations are so popular they have their own names. **Jjapaguri** mixes Chapagetti and Neoguri noodles, made famous by the film Parasite. **Cheese ramyeon** is a bunsik shop staple, and **rabokki** combines ramyeon with tteokbokki. On a cold night, many people add kimchi and an egg to Shin Ramyun, the most common upgrade in Korean homes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What's the best way to upgrade instant ramyeon?**
Add an egg and green onion at the end. Kimchi and cheese are popular next steps.

**How much water should I use?**
About 500–550 ml per packet for a rich broth. Check the packet and use slightly less if you like it strong.

**Why do Koreans put cheese in ramyeon?**
Cheese adds creaminess and softens the spice, a popular combination at bunsik shops.

**Can I make ramyeon in a convenience store?**
Yes. Use the hot water dispenser for cup noodles, or the ramyeon cooker in some stores and Han River parks.
