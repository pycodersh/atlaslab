Noryangjin Fisheries Market works in two stages: you buy live seafood from a stall on the market floor, then a restaurant upstairs serves it. You pay twice, once for the fish and once for the table, so knowing both prices up front is how you avoid overpaying.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **Getting there:** Noryangjin Station (Lines 1 and 9), then the footbridge to the market building
- **How long:** 1.5 to 2 hours to choose and eat
- **Cost:** two bills, one at the stall for the seafood and one at the restaurant for the table and cooking
- **Booking:** none; just walk in
- **Avoid:** buying at the first stall, and agreeing before you hear the total including slicing

</KeyTakeaways>

## The two-stage system

| Stage | What happens | Who you pay |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Choose** (market floor) | pick fish or shellfish from the tanks, priced by weight or piece | the stall |
| **2. Slice** (same stall) | most vendors slice raw fish (회, *hoe*) for you; ask if it's included | the stall |
| **3. Eat** (restaurant upstairs) | the vendor sends you to a restaurant; sides, sauces and leaves arrive | the restaurant |
| **4. Cook the rest** | bones become spicy stew; crab or shellfish can be steamed | the restaurant |

## What the restaurant charges

Restaurant fees are separate from the fish and are usually posted on a board at the entrance. The figures below are rough guides; prices change, so confirm before you sit down.

| Fee | What it covers | Rough price |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **상차림비** table setting | seat, side dishes, sauces, leaves | around 4,000–6,000 won a person |
| **매운탕** spicy fish stew | stew from your fish's bones and head | around 5,000–10,000 won a pot |
| **찜 · 구이** steaming, grilling | crab, shellfish or fish | by weight or portion; ask the rate |
| **음료 · 공깃밥** drinks, rice | soju, beer, soft drinks, rice | usual restaurant prices |

<Callout type="tip" title="Go as a group">
One large fish feeds three or four people, and the table fee is per person either way. Groups get much better value than solo diners.
</Callout>

## What to order

| Korean | Romanization | What it is |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **광어** | gwangeo | olive flounder: mild, firm, the safest first choice |
| **우럭** | ureok | rockfish: chewy and clean, great for stew afterward |
| **도미** | domi | sea bream: delicate and slightly sweet |
| **방어** | bangeo | amberjack: rich and fatty, best in winter |
| **전복 · 멍게** | jeonbok · meongge | abalone, and sea squirt (an acquired taste) |
| **대게** | daege | snow crab: order it steamed upstairs |
| **산낙지** | sannakji | live octopus: cut small and chew well; the suckers stick |

First time? Start with **광어** or **우럭** sliced as hoe, then turn the bones into stew.

## Handling vendors and ordering

Vendors call out to passers-by, and the same fish can be priced differently a few stalls apart. A polite "just looking" and a full lap of the floor are normal.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 그냥 둘러볼게요. | geunyang dulleobolgeyo | Just looking around. |
| 회 떠서 전부 얼마예요? | hoe tteoseo jeonbu eolmayeyo? | How much in total, sliced? |
| 매운탕거리도 싸 주세요. | maeuntanggeorido ssa juseyo | Please pack the bones for stew too. |

You'll hear **뭐 찾으세요? 싸게 해 줄게요!** ("what are you looking for? I'll give you a good price!") and a price like **사만 원이요** ("40,000 won"). The [traditional market guide](/blog/en/k-patto/shopping-at-a-korean-traditional-market) covers bargaining.

## How to eat hoe like a local

1. **Take a lettuce or perilla leaf** and place a slice of fish on it.
2. **Add sauce:** 초장 (*chojang*, sweet-sour chili paste) or soy sauce with wasabi.
3. **Add garlic or a sliver of green chili** if you like heat.
4. **Fold it and eat it in one bite**, ideally with soju or beer.
5. **Finish with 매운탕**, the spicy stew made from your fish's bones.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Noryangjin open at night?**
The retail floor keeps long hours and is busy into the evening, while the early-morning auction is for wholesale buyers. The market closes on New Year's Day and the main Lunar New Year and Chuseok holidays, so check before visiting around those dates.

**Can I pay by card?**
Most stalls and restaurants take cards. A few vendors prefer cash or bank transfer, so ask 카드 돼요? (*kadeu dwaeyo?*) before you agree to a price.

**Can I negotiate the price?**
Some bargaining is normal, especially on larger purchases. Comparing several stalls is more effective than haggling hard at one.

**How much should two people budget?**
A medium flounder sliced for two, plus table fees, stew and a couple of drinks, often comes to roughly 60,000–100,000 KRW. Crab and seasonal fish cost more.
