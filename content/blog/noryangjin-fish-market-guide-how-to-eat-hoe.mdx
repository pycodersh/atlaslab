Noryangjin Fisheries Market works in two stages: you buy live seafood from a stall on the market floor, then a restaurant upstairs slices, cooks and serves it. You pay twice, once for the fish and once for the table, so knowing both prices up front is how you avoid overpaying.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Getting there:** Noryangjin Station (Line 1 or Line 9), then the footbridge
- **Two bills:** the stall for the seafood, the restaurant for the table setting and cooking
- **Ask first:** get the total price, including slicing, before anything goes in a bag
- **Avoid:** buying at the first stall; walk the whole floor and compare

</KeyTakeaways>

## The two-stage system

| Stage | Where | What happens | Who you pay |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Choose** | Live-seafood stalls on the main market floor | Pick fish or shellfish from the tanks; prices go by weight or by piece | The stall |
| **2. Slice** | The same stall | Most vendors slice raw fish (회, *hoe*) for you; ask whether it's included | The stall (usually included) |
| **3. Carry up** | Restaurants upstairs | The vendor walks you to a partner restaurant or tells you where to go | — |
| **4. Eat** | Restaurant table | Sides, sauces, leaf wraps and drinks arrive | The restaurant |
| **5. Cook the rest** | Same restaurant | Bones and head become spicy stew; crab or shellfish can be steamed | The restaurant |

## What the restaurant charges

Restaurant fees are separate from the fish and are usually posted on a board at the entrance. The figures below are rough guides; prices change, so confirm before you sit down.

| Fee | Korean | What it covers | Rough price |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Table setting** | 상차림비 (*sangcharim-bi*) | Seat, side dishes, sauces, lettuce and perilla leaves | Around 4,000–6,000 KRW per person |
| **Spicy fish stew** | 매운탕 (*maeuntang*) | Stew made from your fish's bones and head | Around 5,000–10,000 KRW per pot |
| **Steaming** | 찜 (*jjim*) | Steaming crab, shellfish or lobster | Often charged by weight; ask the rate |
| **Grilling** | 구이 (*gui*) | Grilling shellfish or fish | Per portion; ask the rate |
| **Drinks and rice** | 음료 · 공깃밥 | Soju, beer, soft drinks, rice | Usual restaurant prices |

<Callout type="tip" title="Go as a group">
One large fish feeds three or four people, and the table fee is per person either way. Groups get much better value than solo diners.
</Callout>

## What to order

| Korean | Romanization | What it is |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **광어** | gwang-eo | Olive flounder: mild, firm, the safest first choice |
| **우럭** | u-reok | Rockfish: chewy and clean, great for stew afterward |
| **도미** | do-mi | Sea bream: delicate and slightly sweet |
| **방어** | bang-eo | Amberjack: rich and fatty, best in winter |
| **전복 · 멍게** | jeon-bok · meong-ge | Abalone, and sea squirt (an acquired taste) |
| **대게** | dae-ge | Snow crab: order it steamed upstairs |
| **산낙지** | san-nak-ji | Live octopus: cut small and chew well; the suckers stick |

First time? Start with **광어** or **우럭** sliced as hoe, then turn the bones into stew.

## Handling vendors and ordering

Vendors call out to passers-by, and the same fish can be priced differently a few stalls apart. A polite "just looking" and a full lap of the floor are normal.

## How to eat hoe like a local

1. **Take a lettuce or perilla leaf** and place a slice of fish on it.
2. **Add sauce:** 초장 (*chojang*, sweet-sour chili paste) or soy sauce with wasabi.
3. **Add garlic or a sliver of green chili** if you like heat.
4. **Fold it and eat it in one bite**, ideally with soju or beer.
5. **Finish with 매운탕**, the spicy stew made from your fish's bones.

<Do>

- Walk the whole floor before buying
- Ask the total price, including slicing, before agreeing
- Check the restaurant's fee board before you sit
- Report overcharging to the market's customer service center

<Dont>

- Feel pressured by vendors calling out
- Assume the table fee covers drinks or stew
- Swallow live octopus pieces without chewing
- Visit on Lunar New Year or Chuseok without checking; the market closes on major holidays

</Dont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Noryangjin open at night?**
The retail floor keeps long hours and is busy into the evening, while the early-morning auction is for wholesale buyers. The market closes on New Year's Day and the main Lunar New Year and Chuseok holidays, so check before visiting around those dates.

**Can I pay by card?**
Most stalls and restaurants take cards. A few vendors prefer cash or bank transfer, so ask 카드 돼요? (*kadeu dwaeyo?*) before you agree to a price.

**Can I negotiate the price?**
Some bargaining is normal, especially on larger purchases. Comparing several stalls is more effective than haggling hard at one.

**How much should two people budget?**
A medium flounder sliced for two, plus table fees, stew and a couple of drinks, often comes to roughly 60,000–100,000 KRW. Crab and seasonal fish cost more.
