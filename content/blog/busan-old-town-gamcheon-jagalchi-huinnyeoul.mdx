Busan's old harbor side is a different city from the beach towers of Haeundae. Climb into the painted hillside lanes of Gamcheon Culture Village, eat the catch of the day at Jagalchi, Korea's best-known fish market, and cross the bridge to Yeongdo for sunset over the sea at Huinnyeoul Culture Village.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **The route:** Gamcheon Culture Village → Jagalchi Market → Huinnyeoul Culture Village, about 6 km by road
- **Getting around:** subway Line 1 to Toseong and Jagalchi, village buses up to Gamcheon, a city bus over to Yeongdo
- **Cost:** all three are free to walk around; you only pay for food and drinks
- **Best timing:** Gamcheon in the morning, Jagalchi for lunch, Huinnyeoul for late afternoon light
- **Avoid:** walking up to Gamcheon from Toseong Station; it's steep, so take the village bus

</KeyTakeaways>

Gamcheon grew up on a steep hillside as a refugee neighborhood during the Korean War. Today its stacked houses are painted in pastel colors, and the lanes between them are full of murals, small galleries, cafes and viewpoints over the harbor.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fee** | Free |
| **Info center** | 9:00 am to 6:00 pm, open every day |
| **Getting there** | Toseong Station (Line 1), Exit 6, then village bus Saha 1-1, Seogu 2 or Seogu 2-2 |

Start at the information center for a map, then follow the main lane along the ridge before dropping down into the side alleys. People live in these houses, so keep the noise down and don't open gates or peer into windows.

Down on the waterfront next to Jagalchi Station (Line 1), Jagalchi Market is a long hall and maze of outdoor stalls selling fish, shellfish and sea squirts. The classic way to eat here: pick your seafood at a stall on the ground floor, have it prepared as raw fish (**hoe**, 회) or grilled, and eat it upstairs at one of the restaurants, which add a per-person table charge for side dishes and sauces.

Agree on the price before the fish is cut, and ask what the table charge is upstairs. The market building closes on some Tuesdays each month, so check before a Tuesday visit. If you want to practice first, our [Noryangjin fish market guide](/blog/en/k-pantry/noryangjin-fish-market-guide-how-to-eat-hoe) walks through the same buy-then-eat routine in Seoul.

</figure>

Across the bridge on Yeongdo island, Huinnyeoul is a narrow village of white-walled houses on top of a sea cliff, with a coastal walking path below and views out to the ships anchored offshore. It's at its best in the late afternoon and around sunset, and many of its small cafes close early in the evening.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fee** | Free, open all the time |
| **Getting there** | Buses such as 7, 71 and 508 from near Nampo Station; get off at the Huinnyeoul Culture Village stop |
| **Parking** | No car park in the village; use the public lots nearby and walk |

The lanes are steep with lots of steps, so wear comfortable shoes. It's a residential area: keep your voice down, don't photograph into homes, and take your rubbish with you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Gamcheon Culture Village free?**
Yes. The village is free to walk around, and the information center is open from 9 am to 6 pm every day.

**How do I eat raw fish at Jagalchi Market?**
Buy your seafood at a ground-floor stall, have it sliced or cooked, then eat upstairs, where restaurants charge a per-person table fee for sauces and side dishes.

**How do I get from Jagalchi to Huinnyeoul?**
Take a city bus from near Nampo Station across the bridge to Yeongdo, about 15 minutes, and get off at the Huinnyeoul Culture Village stop. A taxi is quicker.

**Can I combine this with Haeundae on the same day?**
It's possible, but rushed. The old town and the east coast are on opposite sides of the city, so give each its own day if you can.
