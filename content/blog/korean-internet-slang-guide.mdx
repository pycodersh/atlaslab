Korean internet slang mixes consonant-only shortcuts like ㅋㅋ with shortened words and phrases that come and go with trends. Learn the everyday ones below to follow chats and comments, but save them for friends rather than teachers, bosses or older people.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Consonant shortcuts:** ㅋㅋ (laughing), ㅇㅇ (yes), ㄱㅅ (thanks) are everywhere in texts
- **Shortened words:** Koreans clip long phrases into two or three syllables
- **Trends change fast:** some slang feels dated within a year or two
- **Avoid:** using slang with elders, teachers or in formal messages

</KeyTakeaways>

## Consonant shortcuts

| Shortcut | Stands for | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ㅋㅋ / ㅋㅋㅋ** | 크크 | Haha (more ㅋ = funnier) |
| **ㅎㅎ** | 흐흐 | Soft, friendly laugh |
| **ㅇㅇ** | 응응 | Yeah, okay |
| **ㄴㄴ** | 노노 | No, no |
| **ㄱㅅ** | 감사 | Thanks |
| **ㅈㅅ** | 죄송 | Sorry |
| **ㅇㅈ** | 인정 | Agreed, fair point |
| **ㄹㅇ** | 리얼 | For real |
| **ㅠㅠ / ㅜㅜ** | Crying eyes | Sad, touched |

<Callout type="tip" title="Read before you write">
Slang is great for understanding variety shows and comments. When writing, start with the safe shortcuts like ㅋㅋ and ㅇㅇ, and save edgier words until you've seen friends use them.
</Callout>

## Emoticons and stickers

Korean texting relies heavily on simple emoticons built from Korean letters and punctuation, plus KakaoTalk stickers. They soften messages and add tone that text alone can lack.

| Emoticon | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **^^** | A smile; friendly and polite, even with older people |
| **ㅠㅠ / ㅜㅜ** | Tears: sad, touched, or "please" |
| **;;** | Sweat drops: awkward or embarrassed |
| **ㅎㅇ** | 하이 (hi) |
| **ㅂㅂ / ㅃㅃ** | 바이바이 (bye-bye) |

KakaoTalk stickers (이모티콘) featuring characters like Ryan and Apeach are a huge part of chat culture, and many people buy sticker packs to match their mood. Sending a sticker instead of a reply is perfectly normal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What does ㅋㅋ mean in Korean?**
It's laughter, like "haha" or "lol". More ㅋ letters mean something is funnier.

**Is it rude to use slang with Koreans?**
With friends your age, it's friendly. With elders, teachers or at work, stick to standard polite Korean.

**Why do Koreans shorten words so much?**
Shortening long phrases into a few syllables is a long-running trend in Korean internet culture, partly for speed and partly for fun.

**How do I keep up with new slang?**
Watch variety shows, read webtoon and YouTube comments, and ask Korean friends. Trends move fast.
