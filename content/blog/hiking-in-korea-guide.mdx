Hiking is one of Korea's favorite pastimes, and Seoul is surrounded by mountains you can reach by subway. Pick a trail from the table below, start early, and finish like a local with pajeon and makgeolli at the bottom.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **By subway:** Bukhansan, Inwangsan and Achasan are all reachable on public transport
- **Start early:** trails are quieter and cooler in the morning
- **Bring:** water, snacks, layers, and proper shoes; trails are rocky
- **Avoid:** going off marked trails; parts of some parks are restricted

</KeyTakeaways>

## Trails near Seoul

| Mountain | Difficulty | Time (round trip) | Highlights |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Inwangsan** | Easy to moderate | About 2 hours | City views, fortress wall, close to Gyeongbokgung |
| **Achasan** | Easy | 1–2 hours | Han River views, popular at sunrise |
| **Namsan** | Easy | About 1 hour | Walk to N Seoul Tower |
| **Bukhansan (Baegundae)** | Challenging | 4–5 hours | Granite peaks and Seoul's highest summit |
| **Gwanaksan** | Moderate to hard | 3–4 hours | Rocky ridges, temple views |
| **Dobongsan** | Moderate to hard | 4 hours | Dramatic rock formations |

## What to bring

| Item | Why |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hiking shoes** | Granite slabs and steps can be slippery |
| **Water and snacks** | Few shops once you're on the trail |
| **Layers and a windbreaker** | It's colder and windier at the top |
| **Gloves** | Helpful on rope or rail sections |
| **Charged phone with a map app** | For trail maps and emergencies |

<Callout type="info" title="Korean hiking style">
Korean hikers are often fully kitted out in bright outdoor gear, even on easy trails. You don't need to match, but good shoes really do matter on rocky sections.
</Callout>

## Famous hikes beyond Seoul

If you have more time, Korea's national parks offer longer, more dramatic hikes. Most are reachable by express bus or KTX plus a local bus.

| Mountain | Region | Why go |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Seoraksan** | Gangwon (east coast) | Granite peaks and Korea's most famous autumn colors |
| **Hallasan** | Jeju | Korea's highest peak, a volcanic crater lake on top |
| **Jirisan** | South | Long ridge trails and mountain shelters |
| **Bukhansan** | Seoul | Big-mountain feel without leaving the city |

Hallasan's summit trails may require advance reservation, so check the national park website before you go.

## The after-hike ritual

At the bottom of popular trails, restaurants serve pajeon, tofu, noodle dishes and makgeolli. Sharing a pancake and a bottle of rice wine after a hike is a weekend tradition; see our [jeon and makgeolli guide](/blog/en/k-pantry/korean-savory-pancakes-guide).

## Safety

| Situation | What to do |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Getting lost** | Return to the last trail marker; markers have numbers you can report |
| **Injury** | Call 119 and give the nearest trail marker number |
| **Weather change** | Turn back early; summits get cold and windy |
| **Closing times** | Some trails close in the afternoon or during fire season |

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Start early and plan to be down before dark
- Greet other hikers with a nod or 안녕하세요
- Take your rubbish back down with you
- Note trail marker numbers as you go

</Do>
<Dont>

- Go off marked trails or into restricted areas
- Hike rocky routes in sneakers with no grip
- Light fires or smoke on the mountain
- Underestimate how hot summer hikes get

</Dont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I hike near Seoul without a car?**
Yes. Bukhansan, Inwangsan, Achasan and Gwanaksan all have trailheads near subway or bus stops.

**What's the best easy hike in Seoul?**
Inwangsan and Achasan are short, easy and have great city views.

**Do I need a permit to hike in Korea?**
Most trails are free and open without permits. Some sections close seasonally or during fire risk periods.

**Is it safe to hike alone?**
Popular trails near Seoul are busy and generally safe. Tell someone your plan, carry water and a charged phone, and stay on marked paths.
