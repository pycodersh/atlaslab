Perilla leaf (깻잎, *kkaennip*) is the large, jagged-edged green leaf you wrap around grilled meat at Korean barbecue. It's aromatic and slightly anise-like, and when you can't find it, green shiso is the closest stand-in.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Taste:** herbal, a little like anise or basil, with a mild bitterness
- **Not shiso:** related but different; Korean perilla is bigger, rounder and milder
- **Best substitutes:** green shiso for flavor, lettuce for wrapping, Thai basil in stews
- **Avoid:** mint; it pulls the dish in a completely different direction

</KeyTakeaways>

## What's the best substitute for perilla leaves?

Green shiso is the closest match, because it's the same plant species. For barbecue wraps, butter or red leaf lettuce keeps the function but not the flavor, and Thai basil brings the anise note to stews. Skip mint.

| Substitute | Best For | Flavor Profile | Closeness to Perilla |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Green shiso** | Raw wraps, pickling, salads, garnish | Sharp, citrusy, cinnamon-like | Closest (same species); use a little less |
| **"Sesame leaf"** | Anything | Often identical | Frequently perilla under another name |
| **Thai basil** | Added to stews at the end | Herbal, anise-like | Aroma only; too delicate for wraps |
| **Butter or red leaf lettuce** | BBQ wraps (ssam) | Mild, fresh | Wrap function only, no perilla flavor |
| **Red shiso** | Garnish | Sharper, more astringent | Weaker than green shiso |
| **Mint** | Not recommended | Cool, menthol | Wrong direction; makes the dish taste off |

## What perilla tastes like

Perilla belongs to the mint family, but the flavor goes somewhere unexpected: herbal and slightly anise-like, with notes of licorice and basil, and an earthy, gently bitter finish. The leaves are large, round, deeply veined and a little fuzzy. They're sturdy enough to fold around a piece of pork belly without tearing, which is a big part of why they're used as wraps. People tend to love it or dislike it; few feel neutral.

## Perilla is not shiso

Korean perilla and Japanese shiso are varieties of the same species, *Perilla frutescens*, and grocery labels often mix them up. Korean perilla leaves are large, round and soft with an anise flavor. Shiso leaves are smaller, pointed, more serrated and firmer, with a sharper, citrusy, cinnamon-like taste. Shiso is the best substitute you'll find, but you'll notice the difference immediately. If you see a pack labeled "sesame leaf" at an Asian grocer, check it; it's often perilla.

<Callout type="info" title="Leaf vs oil">
Perilla oil (들기름, *deulgireum*) is pressed from the seeds, not the leaves, and tastes nutty rather than herbal. It's used on vegetable side dishes and is not the same as sesame oil.
</Callout>

## How Koreans eat perilla

- **As a wrap (쌈):** grilled pork belly, a dab of ssamjang and a slice of garlic folded in a leaf. The slight bitterness cuts through the fat.
- **Pickled (깻잎장아찌):** leaves layered with soy sauce, garlic and chili. Peel off one leaf at a time and wrap it around a bite of rice. It keeps for weeks.
- **In stews:** torn into spicy stews and seafood dishes near the end to perfume the broth.
- **Pan-fried (깻잎전):** leaves stuffed with seasoned meat, dipped in flour and egg and fried.
- **In kimbap and salads:** a leaf inside a kimbap roll adds a fresh, herbal layer.

Perilla also appears in small packs at Korean convenience stores and supermarkets year-round, and BBQ restaurants usually put it in the basket of wrapping greens alongside lettuce.

## The easiest solution: grow it

Perilla grows fast from seed, does well in pots and is hard to kill. One plant gives more leaves than a household can eat over the summer, and it reseeds itself if you let some go to flower. Seeds are cheap online; look for Korean perilla or kkaennip seeds rather than shiso. Many Korean families abroad grow it on balconies for exactly this reason.

## Buying and storing perilla

Choose leaves that are flat, deep green and not wilted. Wrap them in a damp paper towel inside a container in the fridge, and they'll keep about a week. Wash them just before eating, and pat them dry for wraps so they don't make the meat soggy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is perilla leaf?
Kkaennip, a large herbal leaf from the mint family, used in Korea for wraps, pickles and stews.

### What's the difference between perilla leaf and shiso?
They're the same species, *Perilla frutescens*, but different varieties. Korean perilla is larger, rounder and softer, with a mild anise flavor. Shiso is smaller, more pointed and serrated, with a sharper, citrusy taste.

### Where can I buy pickled perilla leaves if I can't find fresh ones?
Look in the canned and jarred aisle of Korean grocers like H Mart, or search online for "canned seasoned perilla leaves" or 깻잎장아찌. The soy-pickled leaves are eaten straight from the can or jar, wrapped around rice, so they replace the banchan, not fresh leaves in a BBQ wrap.

### Can I grow perilla at home?
Yes. It grows easily from seed in pots and produces plenty of leaves all summer.
