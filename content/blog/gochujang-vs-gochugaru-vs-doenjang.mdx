Gochujang (고추장), gochugaru (고춧가루) and doenjang (된장) are the three seasonings at the heart of Korean cooking. Gochujang is a sweet, spicy chili paste, gochugaru is dried chili flakes, and doenjang is a salty, savory soybean paste, and many dishes use two or all three.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Gochujang:** thick red paste, sweet-spicy, fermented, for sauces and marinades
- **Gochugaru:** dried chili flakes or powder, fruity heat, for kimchi and stews
- **Doenjang:** fermented soybean paste, salty and earthy, for stews and dips
- **Avoid:** swapping one for another one-to-one; they behave very differently

</KeyTakeaways>

## The big three compared

| | Gochujang (고추장) | Gochugaru (고춧가루) | Doenjang (된장) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **What it is** | Fermented chili paste with rice and soybeans | Dried red chili flakes or powder | Fermented soybean paste |
| **Texture** | Thick, sticky paste | Flakes (coarse) or powder (fine) | Chunky to smooth paste |
| **Taste** | Sweet, spicy, savory | Fruity, smoky heat | Salty, earthy, deep |
| **Heat** | Medium | Mild to hot, varies | None |
| **Classic dishes** | Tteokbokki, bibimbap, spicy pork | Kimchi, kimchi jjigae, spicy soups | Doenjang jjigae, ssamjang |
| **Storage** | Fridge after opening | Freezer or fridge | Fridge after opening |

## How each one works in cooking

**Gochujang** adds body, sweetness and heat at once, so it's the base of sauces, marinades and dipping pastes. It can burn quickly in a hot pan, so cook it gently. **Gochugaru** gives color and cleaner heat without sweetness; coarse flakes go into kimchi, fine powder into soups and sauces. **Doenjang** brings deep, salty umami and is dissolved into broth for stews, or mixed with gochujang to make ssamjang for wraps.

- **Ssamjang:** doenjang + gochujang + garlic + sesame oil, the classic wrap sauce
- **Chojang:** gochujang + vinegar + sugar, a dip for raw fish and vegetables
- **Gochugaru heat levels:** labeled mild (순한맛) or hot (매운맛)

<Callout type="tip" title="Store gochugaru cold">
Gochugaru loses color and flavor at room temperature. Keep it sealed in the freezer, where it stays vibrant for months.
</Callout>

## Jang: the fermented foundation

Gochujang and doenjang belong to a family of fermented pastes and sauces called jang (장), along with soy sauce. Traditionally, families made them at home every year, fermenting soybean blocks (메주) in earthenware jars outdoors. Jang-making is so central to Korean food that it has been recognized as part of Korea's cultural heritage, and good jang is still seen as the secret to good home cooking.

Gochugaru isn't fermented, but it's just as important: it gives kimchi its color and heat, and every autumn families buy big bags of sun-dried chili powder for winter kimchi.

- **Cheonggukjang (청국장):** a pungent, quickly fermented soybean paste for strong stews
- **Makjang (막장):** a rustic soybean paste, used as a dip in Gyeongsang regions
- **Gochujang varieties:** some include barley, rice or other grains for different flavors
- **Homemade jang:** many families still receive jang from their parents' kitchens

## Buying tips

All three are sold in Asian grocery stores and many supermarkets abroad, usually in red plastic tubs (gochujang and doenjang) and bags (gochugaru). Check the label for heat level on gochujang and gochugaru. Traditional doenjang is stronger and chunkier; supermarket versions are milder and smoother. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**What's the difference between gochujang and gochugaru?**
Gochujang is a sweet, fermented chili paste; gochugaru is plain dried chili flakes.

**Is doenjang spicy?**
No. Doenjang is salty and savory, with no chili heat.

**Can I use gochujang instead of gochugaru?**
Sometimes, in small amounts, but gochujang adds sweetness and thickness that gochugaru doesn't.

**How long do these keep?**
Opened gochujang and doenjang keep for months in the fridge; gochugaru stays fresh longest in the freezer.
