Age matters in Korean conversation more than in English. It decides how people address each other and which speech level feels natural, so it comes up early, sometimes within the first few minutes of meeting someone.

Three things are worth knowing: how to say an age, how to ask politely, and which age people mean now that Korea has changed its official system.

## Saying your age: native number + 살

In everyday speech, age is a **native Korean number + 살** (sal, "years of age"). 살 is a counter, so the [counter rules](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-counters-gae-myeong-jan-byeong) apply: 1–4 and 20 shorten.

| Age | Korean | Romanization |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 한 살 | han sal |
| 4 | 네 살 | ne sal |
| 20 | 스무 살 | seumu sal |
| 21 | 스물한 살 | seumulhan sal |
| 25 | 스물다섯 살 | seumuldaseot sal |
| 30 | 서른 살 | seoreun sal |
| 40 | 마흔 살 | maheun sal |
| 50 | 쉰 살 | swin sal |

The decades are where learners stumble, because native tens have their own words: 열 (10), 스물 (20), 서른 (30), 마흔 (40), 쉰 (50), 예순 (60).

---

## 세: the formal version

On forms, in news reports and in announcements you'll see **세** (se) with **Sino-Korean** numbers: 25세 (이십오 세), 만 65세 이상 ("65 and over"). It means the same thing as 살, just in a written, official register. You rarely need to say it, but you'll read it everywhere.

## Asking politely

The question changes with the person you're asking.

| Who you're asking | Korean | Romanization |
|---|---|---|
| a child, or a friend your age | 몇 살이에요? | myeot sar-ieyo? |
| an adult, a stranger, someone new | 나이가 어떻게 되세요? | naiga eotteoke doeseyo? |
| someone clearly older | 연세가 어떻게 되세요? | yeonsega eotteoke doeseyo? |

Plenty of Koreans skip the number and ask the birth year instead: **몇 년생이에요?** ("what year were you born?"). The answer is the last two digits: **구십육 년생이에요** ("I was born in '96"). Because the year doesn't change between birthdays, it's the cleanest way to work out who is older.

<Callout type="info" title="What changed in June 2023">

Korea used to have a traditional count, often called "Korean age": everyone was one year old at birth and got a year older every January 1. From **June 28, 2023**, laws and official documents use **international age**, called **만 나이** (man nai), the same count as most of the world. A few rules, such as the youth protection limits on alcohol and tobacco, still count by birth year. In casual conversation, people now usually give 만 나이, and some still mention their birth year to compare. The [life guide to the Korean age system](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-age-system-explained) has the full background.

</Callout>

## Answering and following up

- **스물여덟 살이에요.** — I'm 28.
- **만으로 서른이에요.** — I'm 30 in international age.
- **동갑이네요!** — We're the same age!
- **제가 한 살 많아요.** — I'm a year older.
- **제가 동생이네요.** — So I'm the younger one.

Being the same age (동갑) is often the moment people suggest dropping the polite endings. The [politeness levels guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-politeness-levels-explained) covers when that switch is safe.

## Common mistakes

**Sino-Korean numbers with 살.** 이십 살 and 삼십 살 are wrong. Say 스무 살, 서른 살.

**Not shortening.** 스물 살 should be 스무 살, and 하나 살 should be 한 살.

**Asking elders 몇 살이에요?** It sounds like you're talking to a child. Use 나이가 어떻게 되세요? or, for someone clearly older, 연세가 어떻게 되세요?

**Mixing 살 and 세.** 스무 세 and 이십 살 mix the systems. Pair them: 스무 살, 이십 세.

## 2-minute recap

- Speech: **native number + 살**: 스무 살, 서른두 살.
- Writing and forms: **Sino-Korean + 세**: 25세.
- Native tens: **열, 스물, 서른, 마흔, 쉰, 예순**.
- Ask politely with **나이가 어떻게 되세요?**; for elders, **연세가 어떻게 되세요?**
- **몇 년생이에요?** asks the birth year, the easiest way to compare.
- Since June 28, 2023, official age is **만 나이**, international age.
