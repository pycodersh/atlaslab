A hanok stay means sleeping in a traditional Korean house: usually a mattress on a heated floor, rooms opening onto a small courtyard, and thin wooden doors. It's quieter and more personal than a hotel, and it works best if you know the house rules before you arrive.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **Where:** Bukchon and Seochon in Seoul, Jeonju Hanok Village, Gyeongju, Andong
- **Getting there (Seoul):** Bukchon is a short walk from Anguk Station (Line 3)
- **How long:** one or two nights is typical
- **Cost:** widely varied; budget rooms cost less than a mid-range hotel, private hanok far more
- **Booking:** book ahead for weekends; check bathroom, bed type and late check-in before paying

</KeyTakeaways>

## Hanok or hotel?

| | Hanok stay | Hotel |
|---|---|---|
| **Bed** | floor mattress (some rooms have beds) | Western bed |
| **Heating** | ondol underfloor heating | air units |
| **Bathroom** | private or shared | private |
| **Noise** | thin walls, paper doors | insulated |
| **Front desk** | often a host, not 24 hours | 24 hours |

## Where to stay

| Area | Why go | Getting there |
|---|---|---|
| **Bukchon, Seoul** | hanok alleys between two palaces | Anguk Station, Line 3 |
| **Seochon, Seoul** | quieter streets west of Gyeongbokgung | Gyeongbokgung Station, Line 3 |
| **Jeonju Hanok Village** | hundreds of hanok, food streets | KTX to Jeonju, then bus or taxi |
| **Gyeongju** | near royal tombs and temples | KTX to Singyeongju, then bus or taxi |
| **Andong Hahoe Village** | UNESCO-listed, very rural | bus from Andong |

Seoul hanok put you within walking distance of [Gyeongbokgung](/blog/en/k-patto/visiting-a-korean-palace-phrases); Jeonju is the place for [Jeonju bibimbap](/blog/en/k-pantry/jeonju-bibimbap-vs-regular-bibimbap); Gyeongju pairs with [ssambap](/blog/en/k-pantry/gyeongju-ssambap-what-is-it).

## Before you book

- **Check the room, not just the photos:** private or shared bathroom, floor bedding or bed, air conditioning in summer.
- **Ask about late arrival.** Many hanok guesthouses have no 24-hour desk; if you land late, message the host first.
- **Check the street rules in Bukchon.** Parts of Bukchon now limit tourist visits in the evening and early morning to protect residents. If your stay is inside that area, ask the host how guests come and go.
- **Weekends fill early** in Jeonju and Bukchon, especially in spring and autumn.

## Inside a hanok

| Feature | Korean | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Courtyard | 마당 *madang* | the open center the rooms face |
| Wooden hall | 대청마루 *daecheongmaru* | a raised veranda, cool in summer |
| Heated floor | 온돌 *ondol* | underfloor heating, now modernized |
| Tile roof | 기와 *giwa* | curved clay tiles |
| Floor bedding | 요 · 이불 *yo · ibul* | mattress and quilt, folded away by day |

Some hosts serve a simple breakfast and offer hanbok rental or tea; ask when you book.

## House etiquette

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Shoes off at the door | floors are for sitting and sleeping |
| Quiet after dark | sound carries through paper doors |
| Gentle with doors | paper screens tear easily |
| No cooking in rooms | wooden buildings, fire risk |
| No photos of residents' homes | many Bukchon hanok are private houses |

Don't turn the floor heating to maximum: ondol gets very hot.

## Korean for check-in

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 예약했는데요. | yeyakaenneundeyo | I have a reservation. |
| 신발 어디에 벗어요? | sinbal eodie beoseoyo? | Where do I take my shoes off? |
| 바닥 난방 어떻게 켜요? | badak nanbang eotteoke kyeoyo? | How do I turn on the floor heating? |
| 늦게 도착할 것 같아요. | neutge dochakal geot gatayo | I think I'll arrive late. |

What you'll hear from the host: **신발은 여기 벗어 주세요** ("please take your shoes off here") and **조절기는 벽에 있어요** ("the controller is on the wall"). The [hotel check-in guide](/blog/en/k-patto/hotel-check-in-korea) covers the rest of the front-desk Korean.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is sleeping on the floor in a hanok comfortable?**
Most people find it comfortable thanks to thick mattresses and a warm floor. Some hanok also have rooms with beds.

**Do hanok stays have private bathrooms?**
Many do, but some budget rooms share bathrooms. Check the listing.

**When is the best time for a hanok stay?**
Spring and autumn are pleasant. In winter the ondol heating is cozy; in summer, check for air conditioning.

**Can I book a hanok for one night?**
Yes. Most hanok guesthouses accept single nights, though weekends in Jeonju and Bukchon book up early.
