A hanok stay means sleeping in a traditional Korean house, usually on a mattress on a heated floor, around a small courtyard. Book in Seoul's Bukchon, Jeonju's Hanok Village or Gyeongju, take your shoes off at the door, and keep noise down at night.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Sleeping:** usually on floor mattresses (yo) over ondol underfloor heating
- **Where:** Bukchon and Seochon in Seoul, Jeonju Hanok Village, Gyeongju, Andong
- **Price:** roughly 80,000–250,000 KRW a night, depending on location and comfort
- **Avoid:** expecting hotel soundproofing; walls and doors are thin

</KeyTakeaways>

## Hanok vs hotel

| | Hanok stay | Hotel |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bed** | Floor mattress (some have beds) | Western bed |
| **Heating** | Ondol floor heating | Air conditioning and heating units |
| **Bathroom** | Private or shared, depending on the room | Private |
| **Noise** | Thin walls and paper doors | Good insulation |
| **Atmosphere** | Courtyards, wood, tile roofs | Modern |
| **Breakfast** | Often a simple Korean or continental breakfast | Varies |

## Where to stay

| Area | Why go |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Bukchon, Seoul** | Hanok alleys between two palaces; central but busy with visitors |
| **Seochon, Seoul** | Quieter hanok streets west of Gyeongbokgung |
| **Jeonju Hanok Village** | Hundreds of hanok, food streets, bibimbap |
| **Gyeongju** | Hanok near ancient tombs and temples |
| **Andong Hahoe Village** | A UNESCO-listed traditional village, very rural |

<Callout type="tip" title="Check the room details">
Hanok listings vary a lot. Check whether the room has a private bathroom, a bed or floor bedding, and air conditioning in summer before you book.
</Callout>

## Inside a hanok

Knowing the parts of a hanok makes the stay more interesting, and hosts are often happy to explain the details.

| Feature | Korean | What it is |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Courtyard** | 마당 (*madang*) | The open center that rooms face onto |
| **Wooden floor hall** | 대청마루 (*daecheongmaru*) | A raised wooden veranda, cool in summer |
| **Heated floor** | 온돌 (*ondol*) | Traditional underfloor heating, now usually modernized |
| **Paper doors** | 창호지 문 | Sliding doors with mulberry paper screens |
| **Tile roof** | 기와 (*giwa*) | Curved clay tiles, the signature hanok look |
| **Floor bedding** | 요 · 이불 | A mattress and quilt folded away in the daytime |

Many hosts serve a simple breakfast in the courtyard or a shared room, and some offer hanbok rental or tea ceremonies for guests. Ask when you book.

## House etiquette

| Rule | Why |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Shoes off** | Floors are for sitting and sleeping |
| **Quiet hours** | Sound travels easily through paper doors |
| **Be gentle with doors** | Sliding doors and paper screens tear easily |
| **No cooking in rooms** | Wooden buildings are a fire risk |
| **Respect neighbors** | In Bukchon, residents live in many hanok; keep voices down outside |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is sleeping on the floor in a hanok comfortable?**
Most people find it comfortable thanks to thick mattresses and warm floors. Some hanok also offer rooms with beds.

**Do hanok stays have private bathrooms?**
Many do, but some budget rooms share bathrooms. Check the listing.

**When is the best time for a hanok stay?**
Spring and autumn are pleasant. In winter the ondol heating is cozy; in summer check for air conditioning.

**Can I book a hanok for one night?**
Yes. Most hanok guesthouses accept single nights, though weekends in Jeonju and Bukchon book up early.
