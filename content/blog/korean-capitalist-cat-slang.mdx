Cats love on their own terms. One second they ignore your outstretched hand, and the next, the moment you grab a treat, they become your most devoted best friend. Koreans have the perfect, witty phrase for this: **자본주의 고양이** (*jabonjuui goyangi*, "capitalist cat").

Here is how native speakers playfully call out people (and pets) who only move when there's something in it for them.

## The exchange

**A:** 야옹아~ 이리 와 봐, 쓰다듬어 줄게!  
*(Kitty, come here! Let me pet you!)*  
**A:** 치... 비싸게 굴긴.  
*(Hmph... playing hard to get, huh?)*  
**A:** 츄르 먹을 사람?  
*(Who wants a Churu treat?)*  
**A:** 너 진짜 자본주의 고양이구나?!  
*(You really are a capitalist cat, aren't you?!)*

## Polite or casual?

In the video, the speaker talks casually to her cat (반말). If you want to use these playful expressions with people, here is how they shift between close friends and acquaintances or colleagues.

| Casual (friends, pets) | Polite (acquaintances, colleagues) | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 이리 와 봐. | 이리 와 보세요. | Come over here. |
| 쓰다듬어 줄게. | 쓰다듬어 봐도 돼요? | Let me pet you. / May I pet it? (asking the owner) |
| 비싸게 굴지 마. | 너무 비싸게 굴지 마세요. | Don't play hard to get. |
| 진짜 자본주의다! | 진짜 자본주의네요! | That's pure capitalism! (joking) |

> **비싸게 굴다 (acting expensive):**
> Literally "to act expensive," this is one of the most common everyday idioms in Korean. It means **playing hard to get**, or being aloof when someone invites you or asks a small favor.

## The "capitalism" slang in everyday Korea

Koreans often attach **자본주의** (capitalism) to everyday words to make self-deprecating or affectionate jokes about doing something purely for money or perks.

| Korean | Romanization | Literal meaning | How it's actually used |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **자본주의 미소** | *jabonjuui miso* | Capitalist smile | The polite, trained customer-service smile you put on only at work. |
| **자본주의 하트** | *jabonjuui hateu* | Capitalist finger heart | Doing cute poses or fan service only when the camera or paycheck arrives. |
| **자본주의 애교** | *jabonjuui aegyo* | Capitalist charm | Acting extra sweet just to get food, a discount, or a favor. |

## What friends and pet lovers will tell you

At cat cafes in Hongdae, or chatting with Korean pet owners, you'll often hear these:

| You'll hear | Romanization | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **간식 들고 있으면 인기 폭발이에요.** | *Gansik deulgo isseumyeon ingi pokbal-ieyo.* | You'll be super popular if you're holding treats. |
| **완전 개냥이네요!** | *Wanjeon gaenyangi-neyo!* | This cat acts just like a dog! (super affectionate) |
| **간식 줄 때만 친한 척해요.** | *Gansik jul ttaeman chinhan cheok-haeyo.* | It only pretends to like me when I hand out snacks. |
| **츄르 하나 사 올게요.** | *Churu hana sa olgeyo.* | Let me go buy a Churu tube. |

> **개냥이 (gaenyangi):**
> A blend of **개** (dog) and **고양이** (cat). Used all over Korea for a warm, cuddly cat that greets you at the door like a golden retriever.

## What to actually say

- Calling out someone acting cold: **비싸게 굴지 마요!** (*Stop playing hard to get!*)
- When a friend smiles brightly right after payday: **자본주의 미소 보소!** (*Look at that capitalist smile!*)
- Offering a pet a treat at a cafe: **츄르 줄까?** (*Want some Churu?*)
- Describing a friendly cat: **완전 개냥이에요!** (*Such an affectionate cat!*)
