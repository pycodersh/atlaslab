Korea sometimes has fine dust days (미세먼지, *misemeonji*), most often in late winter and spring, when air quality drops and the sky turns hazy. Check an air quality app each morning, wear a KF94 mask on bad days, and plan indoor sightseeing when levels are high.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **When:** most common from late winter through spring
- **Check:** Naver weather, AirKorea or a dedicated dust app every morning
- **Mask:** KF94 for bad days, KF80 for milder days
- **Avoid:** long outdoor hikes or runs on "bad" or "very bad" days

</KeyTakeaways>

## Reading air quality levels

Korean apps rate fine dust (PM10) and ultrafine dust (PM2.5) in four levels, usually shown with colors.

| Level | Korean | Color | What to do |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Good** | 좋음 | Blue | Enjoy outdoor plans |
| **Moderate** | 보통 | Green | Fine for most people |
| **Bad** | 나쁨 | Orange / yellow | Wear a mask outdoors; limit long exercise |
| **Very bad** | 매우 나쁨 | Red | Wear a KF94 mask; choose indoor sightseeing |

## Which mask to buy

| Mask | Filters fine particles? | Best for | Rough price |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **KF94** | Yes, high filtration | Bad and very bad days | Around 500–1,500 KRW each |
| **KF80** | Yes, moderate | Milder dust days, easier breathing | Around 500–1,200 KRW each |
| **KF-AD** | No (splash protection only) | Summer, hygiene | Cheap |
| **Fabric or fashion masks** | Little protection | Not for dust days | Varies |

Masks are sold at convenience stores, pharmacies and Daiso. Look for the KF number printed on the package.

<Callout type="tip" title="Plan around the forecast">
Dust levels change through the day and across regions. On bad days, swap a palace walk or hike for museums, malls, aquariums or a jjimjilbang, and save outdoor plans for clearer days.
</Callout>

## Indoor ideas for bad air days

A high dust reading doesn't have to ruin the day. Seoul has plenty of big indoor spaces where you can spend hours comfortably, and many link directly to subway stations.

| Place | Why it works |
| :--- | :--- |
| **National Museum of Korea** | Huge, free to enter, a full morning of history |
| **COEX and Starfield Library** | Shopping, an aquarium and the famous library under one roof |
| **Department store food halls** | Basements full of food stalls and bakeries |
| **Jjimjilbang** | Saunas and rest areas for an afternoon; see our [jjimjilbang guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-bathhouse-jjimjilbang-guide) |
| **Underground shopping arcades** | Long connected malls under Gangnam, Myeongdong and Express Bus Terminal |

## Who should be careful

People with asthma, heart or lung conditions, older travelers, pregnant travelers and small children feel fine dust most. If that's you, follow local health advice on bad days, carry any inhalers or medication you use, and keep strenuous outdoor activity for clearer days.

## Apps to check

| App | What it shows |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Naver weather** | Dust levels with the regular forecast |
| **AirKorea** | Official government air quality data |
| **Dedicated dust apps** | Hourly forecasts and color-coded maps |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When is fine dust worst in Korea?**
Usually late winter and spring, although bad days can happen at other times too.

**Is KF94 the same as N95?**
They're similar filtration standards. KF94 masks filter most fine particles and are widely available in Korea.

**Should I cancel plans on a fine dust day?**
Not necessarily. Wear a mask, cut back on long outdoor exercise, and shift some sightseeing indoors.

**Do Koreans wear masks on dust days?**
Many do, especially on bad and very bad days, so wearing one won't stand out.
