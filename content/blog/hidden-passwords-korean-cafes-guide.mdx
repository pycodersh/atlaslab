Korean cafes hide their most useful information in small places: the Wi-Fi password on a counter sign or receipt, and the restroom door code at the bottom of the receipt. Water and cutlery are usually self-service, and you return your tray when you leave.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Wi-Fi password:** on a sign at the counter, on the table, or printed on your receipt
- **Restroom code:** usually at the bottom of the receipt; the restroom may be outside the cafe
- **Water:** self-service from a dispenser near the counter
- **Avoid:** throwing away your receipt before you've used the restroom

</KeyTakeaways>

## Where to find the codes

| What you need | Where to look | Korean |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wi-Fi name and password** | Counter sign, table card, menu board or receipt | 와이파이 비밀번호 |
| **Restroom door code** | Bottom of the receipt, a sign by the door, or ask staff | 화장실 비밀번호 |
| **Restroom key** | Hanging at the counter in some older buildings | 화장실 열쇠 |
| **Order number** | On the receipt or the buzzer you're handed | 주문 번호 |

<Callout type="tip" title="The restroom might be outside">
In many small buildings, the restroom is shared by the whole floor and sits in the hallway, locked with a keypad. Follow the sign, enter the code from your receipt, and press * or # if the door doesn't open.
</Callout>

## Self-service habits

| Habit | How it works |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Water** | Free from a dispenser or jug near the counter, with paper or glass cups |
| **Napkins and straws** | At a station near the pickup counter |
| **Buzzer** | A vibrating pager tells you your drink is ready |
| **Tray return** | Take your tray and cups to the return shelf when you leave |
| **Leftover drinks** | Pour liquids and ice into the sink at the return station |

## Reading a cafe receipt

A Korean cafe receipt holds more than the price. Look for these lines before you throw it away.

| Line | Korean | What it tells you |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Order number** | 주문번호 | The number called or shown when your drink is ready |
| **Wi-Fi** | 와이파이 · WIFI | Network name and password |
| **Restroom code** | 화장실 비밀번호 | The keypad code for the restroom door |
| **Points or stamps** | 적립 | Loyalty points, usually needing a Korean phone number |
| **Payment** | 결제 | Card or cash details |

Snap a quick photo of the receipt so you still have the codes if it gets thrown away with your cup.

## Cafe types and what to expect

| Cafe type | Wi-Fi | Restroom | Water |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Big chains** | Usually free, password on the receipt | Inside, sometimes coded | Self-service |
| **Budget takeout chains** | Sometimes | Often none for customers | Ask staff |
| **Independent cafes** | Usually, sign at the counter | Often shared in the building | Self-service jug |
| **Study cafes** | Yes, part of the fee | Inside | Self-service |

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Keep your receipt until you've used Wi-Fi and the restroom
- Help yourself to water at the dispenser
- Return your tray and cups when you leave
- Pour leftover ice into the return-station sink

</Do>
<Dont>

- Ask staff for water at the table; it's usually self-service
- Leave cups and trays on the table
- Throw drinks into the general bin
- Use a cafe's restroom code without buying anything

</Dont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Wi-Fi free in Korean cafes?**
Usually yes for customers. The password is on a sign, a table card or your receipt.

**Why is the restroom outside the cafe?**
Many small buildings share one restroom per floor, kept locked with a keypad. The code is usually on your receipt.

**Do I have to clean up after myself?**
Yes. Taking your tray and cups to the return shelf is standard in most Korean cafes.

**Is water free?**
Yes, from a self-service dispenser or jug near the counter.
