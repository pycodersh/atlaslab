Korean convenience stores are not just places to buy things. Most have a counter by the window, a hot water dispenser, a microwave, and people eating at ten at night.

Cup ramen there costs about 1,500 won and is a genuine meal. The whole system runs on two questions.

## Can you eat inside?

**여기서 먹고 가도 돼요?**
*yeo-gi-seo meok-go ga-do dwae-yo?*
"Can I eat here?"

You'll almost always get yes. Look for a narrow counter facing the window or a few stools near the entrance.

You don't strictly have to ask — people just sit down. But asking tells you whether this particular branch has seating, and it's the polite move.

## The exchange

**A:** 여기서 먹고 가도 돼요?  
*(Can I eat here?)*  
**B:** 네, 젓가락도 가져가세요.  
*(Sure, and take chopsticks too.)*  
**A:** 뜨거운 물은 어디 있어요?  
*(Where's the hot water?)*  
**B:** 전자레인지 옆에 있어요.  
*(It's next to the microwave.)*

> **Key Nuances**
> - **먹고 가도 돼요?**: "Is it okay to eat here?" — the answer is almost always yes, and it tells you whether this branch has seats.
> - **젓가락**: Cup ramen doesn't come with chopsticks — grab a pair from the bin before you pour the water.
> - **뜨거운 물**: The hot water dispenser is free, and nobody checks where you bought the ramen.
> - **국물은 어디에 버려요?**: "Where do I pour the broth?" — leftover broth has its own bin, not the regular trash.

## Where the hot water is

**뜨거운 물은 어디 있어요?**
*tteu-geo-un mul-eun eo-di iss-eo-yo?*
"Where's the hot water?"

There's a dispenser near the microwave, usually a stainless steel box with a spout and a button. Free, and nobody checks whether you bought the ramen there.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 뜨거운 물 | tteu-geo-un mul | hot water |
| 전자레인지 | jeon-ja-re-in-ji | microwave |
| 젓가락 | jeot-ga-rak | chopsticks |
| 뚜껑 | ttu-kkeong | lid |
| 국물 | gung-mul | broth |

## The thing nobody tells you

**Cup ramen does not come with chopsticks.**

There's a bin of disposable wooden chopsticks near the counter or the microwave. Grab a pair *before* you pour the water, or you'll be standing there watching your noodles cook with nothing to eat them with.

Staff will often remind you — **젓가락도 가져가세요** (*jeot-ga-rak-do ga-jyeo-ga-se-yo*, "take chopsticks too") — but not always.

## The actual steps

1. Pick your cup ramen and pay at the counter
2. Grab chopsticks from the bin
3. Peel the lid back to the marked line, not all the way off
4. Fill with hot water to the inside line
5. Close the lid and wait — 3 minutes for most, 4 for thicker noodles
6. Eat at the counter

Some branches have a small weight to hold the lid down. If not, the chopsticks across the top work.

## What to add

This is where it gets good. The convenience store is a pantry, and people build on the base:

- A slice of **치즈** (cheese) melted on top of spicy ramen
- A **삼각김밥** (triangle kimbap) dipped in the broth
- A boiled egg from the counter by the register
- **만두** (dumplings) microwaved and dropped in

None of this is unusual. It's what the setup is for.

## Disposing of it

Look for a bin with a hole for liquid — you pour the leftover broth there, then put the cup in the trash. Don't pour broth into the regular bin.

If you can't find it: **국물은 어디에 버려요?** — "Where do I pour the broth?"

## Which ones to try

| Korean | What it is |
|---|---|
| 신라면 | The default. Spicy, beef-based |
| 진라면 | Milder. Comes in 매운맛 (spicy) and 순한맛 (mild) |
| 짜파게티 | Black bean noodles, not soup |
| 불닭볶음면 | Very spicy stir-fried. Not for a first try |
| 육개장 | Cup only. Thin noodles, beef broth |

If you want something that isn't spicy, ask **안 매운 거 있어요?** — "Do you have something not spicy?"

## What to actually do

1. Ask **여기서 먹고 가도 돼요?**
2. Get chopsticks before anything else
3. Ask **뜨거운 물은 어디 있어요?**
4. Fill to the line, wait three minutes
5. Pour the broth in the liquid bin, cup in the trash

It's one of the cheapest and most ordinary things you can do in Korea, and it's worth doing at least once at midnight.
