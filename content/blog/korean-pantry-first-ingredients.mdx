The hard part of cooking Korean food at home is rarely technique; it's that recipes assume a stocked pantry, so one dish turns into a big shopping trip. Buy in the right order, and each item unlocks several dishes instead of one.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **First four:** gochugaru, soy sauce, toasted sesame oil, garlic
- **Next four:** gochujang, doenjang, dried anchovies with kelp, short-grain rice
- **Cost:** roughly 50–70 USD for all eight, most of it lasting six months or more
- **Avoid:** buying single-recipe specialty items before you know what you'll cook

</KeyTakeaways>

## The eight essentials, in order

| Order | Ingredient | What it unlocks | Substitute if needed |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Gochugaru (고춧가루) | Kimchi, stews, namul, spicy stir-fries | Paprika + cayenne (a bridge only) |
| **2** | Soy sauce (진간장) | Marinades, braises, stir-fries | Japanese soy sauce |
| **3** | Toasted sesame oil (참기름) | Namul, bibimbap, dipping sauces | None that tastes the same |
| **4** | Garlic | Almost every savory dish | Frozen minced garlic cubes |
| **5** | Gochujang (고추장) | Tteokbokki, bibimbap, spicy marinades | Miso-gochugaru-honey mix |
| **6** | Doenjang (된장) | Doenjang jjigae, ssamjang, seasoned greens | Red miso, at reduced strength |
| **7** | Dried anchovies + kelp | Stock for most soups and stews | Stock packets (육수팩) |
| **8** | Short-grain rice | Every Korean meal | Japanese short-grain rice |

## Why this order

**Gochugaru comes first** because it appears in kimchi, most stews, seasoned vegetables and stir-fries, and nothing substitutes well. Buy the coarse grind and store it in the freezer.

![Gochugaru](https://eecvvgkihtcgfikaimao.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/pantry-ingredients/gochugaru.png)

**Soy sauce** is next, and a Japanese bottle you already own works fine; save your money for things you can't replace. **Toasted sesame oil** is a finishing oil, added off the heat in small amounts, and it's what makes food taste specifically Korean. **Garlic** goes in at volumes that surprise people, and pre-minced garlic is common in Korean homes too.

![Sesame Oil](https://eecvvgkihtcgfikaimao.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/pantry-ingredients/sesame-oil.png)

With just those four, you can already cook seasoned vegetables, simple marinated meat, stir-fries and egg dishes. That's enough to find out whether you'll keep cooking Korean food before committing to the rest.

<Callout type="tip" title="Split it into two trips">
Buy items 1–4 first and cook for a week or two. Then add items 5–8. You'll spread the cost and still be able to cook after the first trip.
</Callout>

## The second four

**Gochujang** opens up the spicy staples: tteokbokki, bibimbap, spicy pork and dipping sauces. One tub lasts a year in the fridge. **Doenjang** justifies itself with doenjang jjigae alone, and mixed with gochujang it makes ssamjang for lettuce wraps. **Dried anchovies and kelp** are bought as a pair because together they make the stock under most Korean soups; stock packets are a reasonable shortcut at first. **Short-grain rice** matters more than people expect, since Korean meals are built around slightly sticky rice. Japanese short-grain rice is the same type.

## What not to buy yet

These aren't wrong, just poor value at the start:

- **Perilla oil:** distinctive but used in fewer dishes, and it goes rancid quickly
- **Fish sauce (액젓):** mostly for kimchi making
- **Rice or corn syrup (물엿):** sugar and honey cover it
- **Rice cakes:** buy them when you make tteokbokki; they harden quickly
- **Specialty pastes and small stir-fry anchovies:** add once you know your favorite dishes

## Cost and where to buy

All eight cost about 50–70 USD at a Korean grocery store, and most last six months to a year. After that, the recurring costs are garlic, rice and vegetables.

- **Korean grocery stores** (such as H Mart in North America) are usually cheapest with the widest range.
- **General Asian grocers** have soy sauce, sesame oil, rice and often gochujang, but gochugaru and soup soy sauce are less reliable.
- **Online** works for shelf-stable items. Check that gochugaru packs say 고춧가루 and aren't generic chili flakes.

If you live in Korea, a large supermarket like E-Mart or Homeplus has everything here in one aisle, often in small sizes that suit a one-person kitchen.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What should I buy first for a Korean pantry?**
Gochugaru, soy sauce, toasted sesame oil and garlic. They cover a wide range of everyday dishes.

**How much does a basic Korean pantry cost?**
About 50–70 USD for eight essentials, and most last six months to a year.

**Can I use Japanese soy sauce for Korean cooking?**
Yes. It works well for most marinades, braises and stir-fries.

**Do I need both gochujang and gochugaru?**
Eventually, yes. Gochugaru is dry chili for seasoning and kimchi; gochujang is a fermented paste for sauces.
