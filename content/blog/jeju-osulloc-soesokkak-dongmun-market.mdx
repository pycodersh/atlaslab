This loop takes in Jeju's southwest and south coast and ends in Jeju City. Walk the tea fields at the Osulloc Tea Museum, ride a raft on the jade-green gorge of Soesokkak, then drive back over to Jeju City for street food at the Dongmun Market night market.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **The route:** Osulloc Tea Museum → Soesokkak → Dongmun Market, about 80 km by road
- **Getting around:** a rental car or taxi makes this loop easy; by bus it's a long day
- **Cost:** Osulloc and the Soesokkak gorge are free; rafts and kayaks at Soesokkak cost extra
- **Night market:** every day, 6:00 pm to midnight from November to April and 7:00 pm to midnight from May to October
- **Avoid:** planning water activities at Soesokkak on a windy day; they stop in rough weather

</KeyTakeaways>

The Osulloc Tea Museum sits beside wide green tea fields in Jeju's southwest. Inside are a small museum of tea culture, a tea shop and a cafe famous for green tea ice cream and roll cake; outside, paths lead into the rows of tea bushes. Entry and parking are free, and some tea experiences inside cost extra.

The fields are at their greenest from spring into summer. It's a relaxed stop of an hour or two, and it gets busy with tour groups by late morning, so arrive early.

Near Seogwipo on the south coast, Soesokkak is where a freshwater stream meets the sea in a narrow gorge of black lava rock and pine trees. The water is a deep jade green, and you can walk along the cliff paths for free.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fee** | Free to walk the gorge |
| **On the water** | Traditional rafts (**teu**, 테우) and kayaks, paid separately |
| **Weather** | Water activities stop in strong wind or waves |

Rides run during the day. Check the hours and prices on site, and go before mid-afternoon if you want to get on the water.

## Dongmun Market and the night market

<img src="https://eecvvgkihtcgfikaimao.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/blog-covers/jeju-osulloc-soesokkak-dongmun-market-dongmun.webp" alt="Produce stalls under the roof of Dongmun Market" loading="lazy" />

Dongmun Market in central Jeju City is the island's best-known traditional market, selling tangerines, hallabong oranges, seafood and dried goods by day. In the evening its **night market** sets up food stalls near Gate 8, selling grilled black pork, seafood, tangerine juice and other Jeju snacks.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Night market hours** | Every day, 6:00 pm to midnight from November to April, 7:00 pm to midnight from May to October (stalls close when sold out) |
| **Where** | Central Jeju City, around Gate 8 of Dongmun Market |
| **Day market** | Shop hours vary, roughly morning to evening |

Bring cash for the stalls, though many take cards. Our guide to [Jeju black pork](/blog/en/k-pantry/jeju-black-pork-vs-samgyeopsal) explains why it's on so many menus here.

At the night market you'll hear **카드 돼요?** ("do you take cards?"); answer with **네** or **현금만요** ("cash only") if a vendor asks you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the Osulloc Tea Museum free?**
Yes. Entry and parking are free; some tea classes and experiences inside are paid.

**What are the Dongmun night market hours?**
Every day, from 6 pm to midnight between November and April and from 7 pm to midnight between May and October. Stalls close when they sell out.

**Can I ride a raft at Soesokkak in winter?**
It depends on the season and the weather. Rides stop in strong wind or waves, so check the operating hours on site or by phone on the day.

**Can I do this loop by bus?**
Yes, but it's a long day with several transfers. A rental car or taxis between the three stops save a lot of time.
