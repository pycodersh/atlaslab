Korean BBQ means grilling meat at your table and wrapping it in lettuce with garlic and sauce. Pick a cut from the table below, order at least two servings, and let the staff take over the grill if they offer.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Most popular cut:** samgyeopsal (pork belly), around 15,000–20,000 KRW per serving
- **Two-serving minimum:** most BBQ restaurants won't grill a single portion
- **Free refills:** side dishes and lettuce are usually refillable at no cost
- **Avoid:** flipping the meat constantly; if staff are grilling, let them finish

</KeyTakeaways>

## The main cuts compared

| Cut | Korean | What it is | Flavor | Best sauce |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Samgyeopsal** | 삼겹살 | Pork belly, unmarinated | Rich, fatty, crispy edges | Ssamjang, or salt with sesame oil |
| **Moksal** | 목살 | Pork neck/shoulder | Leaner, juicier, chewier | Salt with sesame oil |
| **Dwaeji galbi** | 돼지갈비 | Marinated pork ribs | Sweet soy-garlic glaze | Already seasoned; eat with lettuce |
| **So galbi** | 소갈비 | Beef short ribs, often marinated | Sweet, tender, premium | Already seasoned |
| **Chadolbaegi** | 차돌박이 | Paper-thin beef brisket | Buttery, cooks in seconds | Soy-onion dipping sauce |
| **Hanwoo** | 한우 | Korean native beef (various cuts) | Marbled, top quality | Just salt |

Hanwoo is the most expensive option by far, often three or more times the price of pork. For a first BBQ, samgyeopsal and moksal are the classic, affordable pair.

## How ordering works

| Step | What happens | Tip |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Sit down** | Staff light the grill and bring side dishes | Banchan, lettuce and dipping sauces are included |
| **2. Order** | Choose a cut, minimum two servings (2인분) | Order more of the same cut later if you want |
| **3. Grill** | Staff often grill and cut the meat for you | At self-grill places, wait until it's golden before flipping |
| **4. Extras** | Add rice, a stew or cold noodles near the end | 된장찌개 (doenjang stew) and 냉면 (cold noodles) are popular |
| **5. Pay** | At the counter on the way out | No tipping; see [how paying works in Korea](/blog/en/k-patto/zero-tipping-split-bills-korea) |

## How to wrap ssam

1. **Take a lettuce or perilla leaf** in your palm.
2. **Add a piece of grilled meat** and a dab of ssamjang.
3. **Add grilled garlic, kimchi or green onion salad** if you like.
4. **Fold it into one bite** and eat it whole. Biting a wrap in half gets messy.

## Do's and Don'ts at the grill

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Ask for a fresh grill plate when it turns black
- Use the tongs and scissors for the grill, your chopsticks for eating
- Pour drinks for others and let them pour yours
- Ask for refills of lettuce and side dishes

</Do>
<Dont>

- Order a single serving at a place that requires two
- Flip meat that staff are grilling for you
- Double-dip a bitten wrap in the shared sauce
- Leave the grill on high and walk away

</Dont>
</DoDont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does Korean BBQ cost per person?**
At a typical pork BBQ restaurant, budget roughly 25,000–40,000 KRW per person with drinks and a stew. Beef, especially hanwoo, can cost several times that.

**Will the staff grill the meat for me?**
At many restaurants, yes, especially in busy areas. At others the grill is self-service. If staff start grilling, let them finish cutting before you dig in.

**What is the best drink with Korean BBQ?**
Soju and beer are the classics, and many people mix them as somaek. See our guide to [Korean alcohol](/blog/en/k-pantry/soju-makgeolli-cheongju-korea-guide).

**Are side dishes free?**
Yes. The banchan, lettuce and basic sauces come with the meal, and refills are usually free. Extra items like steamed egg or stew are ordered separately.
