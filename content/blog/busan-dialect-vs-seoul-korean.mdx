Watch enough Korean dramas and you'll meet a character whose Korean sounds different: the pitch jumps, and sentences end in sounds you haven't learned. That's usually **경상도 사투리**, the Gyeongsang dialect, and Busan is its biggest city.

## Busan dialect at a glance

Busan speech differs from Seoul Korean mostly in melody and sentence endings. Vocabulary changes less than you'd expect, so a few phrases cover most of what you'll hear in dramas.

| Seoul | Busan | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 진짜? *jinjja?* | 맞나? *manna?* | Really? |
| 왜 그래? *wae geurae?* | 와 그라는데? *wa geuraneunde?* | What's wrong? |
| 밥 먹었어? *bap meogeosseo?* | 밥 뭇나? *bap munna?* | Did you eat? |

맞나 is written with ㅈ but said *manna*: the ㅈ batchim turns into *n* before ㄴ.

## What 사투리 means

**사투리** (*saturi*) is regional dialect, as opposed to **표준어** (*pyojuneo*), standard Korean, based on Seoul speech and used in broadcasting and schools. Everyone understands 표준어, and dialect speakers shift toward it in formal settings.

| Region | Dialect |
|---|---|
| Seoul, Gyeonggi | 표준어 (standard) |
| Gyeongsang (Busan, Daegu) | 경상도 사투리 |
| Jeolla (Gwangju, Jeonju) | 전라도 사투리 |
| Chungcheong | 충청도 사투리 |
| Jeju | 제주어 |

Jeju is the outlier: different enough that many linguists treat it as a separate language, and UNESCO lists it as critically endangered.

## The melody

Standard Korean has fairly even pitch. Gyeongsang speech has pitch accent: syllables rise and fall much more, and pitch can tell words apart. That's why Busan speech can sound blunt to Seoul ears. It isn't aggression; it's a different melody, and the hardest part for outsiders to copy.

## Question endings

| Seoul | Busan | Type |
|---|---|---|
| 뭐 해? | 뭐 하노? | question word → -노 |
| 어디 가? | 어디 가노? | question word → -노 |
| 맞아? | 맞나? | yes/no → -나 |
| 가? | 가나? | yes/no → -나 |

The rule of thumb Gyeongsang speakers describe: **-노** when there's a question word (what, where, who), **-나** for yes/no questions.

## Words you'll hear

| Busan | Seoul | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 억수로 | 아주, 많이 | very, a lot |
| 아이가 | 아니야, 그렇지 | isn't it, right? |
| 와? | 왜? | why? |
| 마 | — | hey (attention-getter, filler) |

**마** is hard to translate: an attention-getter and intensifier that turns up constantly in Busan speech and in any drama character from there.

<Callout type="info" title="The famous sentence: 가가 가가?">

Four identical syllables, told apart by pitch. A common explanation: "is that kid (가 = 그 아이) the one from the Ga family (가가)?" Standard Korean would need a full sentence. It's quoted as proof of how much work pitch does in the dialect.

</Callout>

## Should you learn it?

Not as a beginner. Standard Korean works everywhere, including Busan. Dialect from a learner usually gets laughter rather than acceptance. And what you need is recognition: knowing 맞나 and 아이가 helps with dramas; producing them doesn't. The exception is living in the region, where it happens on its own.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Busan dialect rude?
No. It sounds blunt to Seoul ears because of its strong pitch and short endings, but the politeness is the same. Busan speakers use polite forms with strangers and elders like anyone else.

### Can Busan and Seoul people understand each other?
Almost always. Everyone learns standard Korean at school and hears it on TV, and Busan speakers shift toward it with people from elsewhere.

### Should I learn Busan dialect as a beginner?
No. Learn standard Korean first, and learn to recognize a few phrases like 맞나 and 아이가 so dramas are easier to follow.
