Step into an elevator in Seoul and you'll notice something within three seconds. Before even glancing at the floor numbers, a Korean's finger darts toward the **닫힘** (close) button. Welcome to Korea's famous **빨리빨리** (*ppalli-ppalli*, "hurry-hurry") lifestyle, where saving two seconds of door-closing time is an art form.

Here is what native speakers actually say in elevators, and how to ride one like a seasoned local.

## The exchange

**A:** 몇 층이야? 빨리!  
*(Which floor? Hurry!)*  
**B:** 너 층수 누르기도 전에 닫힘부터 눌렀어.  
*(You hit the close button before you even pressed your floor!)*  
**A:** 닫히면서 눌러야 시간 아끼지!  
*(You press it while the door's closing. That's how you save time!)*  
**B:** 진짜 한국인 그 자체다.  
*(You're literally Korean to the core.)*

## Polite or casual?

The two friends in the video use **반말** (casual speech). When you share an elevator with strangers, neighbors or colleagues, use the polite versions instead:

| Casual (friends) | Polite (neighbors, strangers) | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 몇 층이야? | 몇 층 가세요? | Which floor are you going to? |
| 눌러 줘. | 눌러 주시겠어요? | Could you press it for me? |
| 먼저 타. | 먼저 타세요. | Get in first. / After you. |
| 빨리! | 천천히 오세요. | Hurry! / Take your time. (to someone you're holding the door for) |

> **한국인 그 자체 (Korean to the core):**
> **~ 그 자체** literally means "the thing itself," the pure essence of something. Calling someone **한국인 그 자체** means their habits, like rushing to close the elevator, make them indistinguishable from a born-and-raised local.

## Elevator buttons and essential questions

Here is what to press, and what to ask when you're standing far from the control panel:

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning | Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **열림** | *yeollim* | Open | The button with two triangles pointing apart (◄ ►). |
| **닫힘** | *dachim* | Close | The button with two triangles pointing together (► ◄). |
| **7층 눌러 주시겠어요?** | *chil-cheung nulleo jusigesseoyo?* | Could you press 7, please? | When you're stuck behind people in a crowded elevator. |
| **올라가요, 내려가요?** | *ollagayo, naeryeogayo?* | Going up or down? | Checking before you step in. |

## What people in the elevator will tell you

Quick courtesies fly fast in Korean apartment buildings, subway stations and department stores:

| You'll hear | Romanization | Meaning | What to do |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **먼저 내리세요.** | *meonjeo naeriseyo* | Please get off first. | Step out with a slight nod. |
| **잠시만요, 내릴게요!** | *jamsimanyo, naerilgeyo!* | Excuse me, getting off! | Say it clearly when you're in the back corner. |
| **잡아 드릴게요.** | *jaba deurilgeyo* | I'll hold it for you. | Someone is holding the open button for you. |
| **감사합니다.** | *gamsahamnida* | Thank you. | Always say it when someone holds the door. |

> **잠시만요 is your elevator superpower:**
> When the elevator reaches your floor and four people block the doors, don't squeeze past in silence. A firm, polite **잠시만요, 내릴게요!** parts the crowd immediately.

## What to actually say

- Asking someone to press your floor: **7층 부탁드립니다.** (*7th floor, please.*)
- Thanking someone holding the door: **감사합니다!** (*Thank you!*)
- Getting through a crowded door: **잠시만요, 내릴게요!** (*Excuse me, getting off!*)
- Telling a friend to hurry: **빨리 타!** (*Get in, quick!*)
