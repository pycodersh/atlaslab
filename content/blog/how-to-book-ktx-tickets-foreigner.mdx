Korea's high-speed trains, the KTX, link Seoul with Busan, Gyeongju, Jeonju and other cities in a few hours. Since September 1, 2026, the former SRT trains from Suseo also run as KTX, and trains from both Seoul hubs are booked in the same place. Book online in English up to about a month ahead, show your e-ticket on your phone, and board: there are no ticket gates.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Seoul to Busan:** about 2.5–3 hours, roughly 50,000–60,000 KRW in standard class
- **Book:** the Korail website or the Korail+ app, which list trains from Seoul Station and Suseo together; counters and machines at stations
- **No gates:** show your e-ticket only if a conductor asks
- **Avoid:** waiting until the day for weekend or holiday travel; trains sell out

</KeyTakeaways>

## KTX and SRT: what changed in September 2026

Until August 2026, SRT was a separate high-speed service from Suseo with its own app and website. On September 1, 2026, it merged into KTX and its trains were renamed KTX-Sancheon. KTX fares were lowered to the old SRT level, about 10% on average (Seoul to Busan went from 59,800 to 54,400 KRW), and trains from both Seoul hubs are now searched and booked together on Korail+ and the Korail website ([Korail announcement](https://info.korail.com/info/selectBbsNttView.do?bbsNo=199&key=911&nttNo=27135), in Korean). What still matters is the station your train leaves from.

| | Seoul Station side | Suseo side |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Seoul stations** | Seoul Station, Yongsan, Cheongnyangni, Haengsin | Suseo (southeast Seoul, Gangnam side) |
| **Before Sept 2026** | KTX | SRT |
| **Now** | KTX | KTX (former SRT trains renamed KTX-Sancheon) |
| **Lines** | Most high-speed lines nationwide | Seoul–Busan and Honam lines, among others |
| **Booking** | Korail website and Korail+ app | Same as Seoul Station side |
| **Korail Pass** | Accepted | Not confirmed since the merger; check with [Korail](https://www.korail.com) |

Pick by departure station: if you're staying in Gangnam, a train from Suseo (the former SRT) may be closer; from downtown Seoul, Seoul Station is easier.

For bus and flight alternatives on the same routes, see the [Korea Intercity Transit Guide](/tools/intercity-transit).

## Seat types and luggage

| Seat type | Korean | What it is |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Standard** | 일반실 | Reserved 2+2 seats |
| **First class** | 특실 | Wider 2+1 seats, a bit more legroom |
| **Unreserved** | 자유석 | Specific cars, first come first served (on some routes) |
| **Standing** | 입석 | Cheaper ticket; stand or use free seats when available |

<Callout type="tip" title="Where to put your suitcase">
Large suitcases go on the racks at the ends of each car, and small bags on the overhead shelves. On busy trains the end racks fill up, so board early if you have big luggage.
</Callout>

## Boarding, step by step

1. **Check the departure board** for your train number and platform (타는 곳).
2. **Go to the platform** about 10 minutes early; there are no ticket gates.
3. **Find your car number** on the platform floor markings.
4. **Sit in your reserved seat** and stow your luggage.
5. **Show your e-ticket** only if a conductor asks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can foreigners book KTX tickets online?**
Yes. The Korail website has an English version and accepts foreign cards. Since SRT merged into KTX in September 2026, trains from Suseo are booked in the same places as other KTX trains: the Korail website and the Korail+ app. If you can't find a Suseo train on the English site, check with [Korail](https://www.korail.com).

**How far ahead can I book?**
Usually up to about a month before departure. Popular weekend and holiday trains can sell out quickly.

**Is there food on the KTX?**
Food service on board is limited. Buy snacks and drinks at the station before boarding; eating your own food on the train is fine.

**What if I miss my train?**
Go to the ticket counter right away. You may be able to change to a later train for a fee, depending on how late you are.
