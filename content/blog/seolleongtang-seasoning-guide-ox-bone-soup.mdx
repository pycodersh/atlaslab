Seolleongtang (설렁탕) and gomtang (곰탕) are slow-simmered beef soups served with rice, and both arrive deliberately unseasoned. Add salt, pepper and green onion at the table, and pour in a little radish kimchi juice if you want tang.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Seolleongtang:** milky white broth from long-boiled bones
- **Gomtang:** clearer, meatier broth from beef and some bones
- **Season yourself:** salt, pepper and green onion are on the table
- **Avoid:** judging the soup before seasoning; it tastes flat on purpose

</KeyTakeaways>

## Seolleongtang vs gomtang

| | Seolleongtang | Gomtang |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Broth** | Milky white, from bones boiled for many hours | Clearer, from meat and some bones |
| **Flavor** | Deep, mellow, rich | Clean, meaty |
| **Inside** | Sliced beef, thin wheat noodles (somyeon) | Sliced beef, sometimes tripe |
| **Famous for** | Seoul | Naju (Naju gomtang) |
| **Price** | Around 10,000–15,000 KRW | Around 11,000–16,000 KRW |

## Seasoning at the table

| Seasoning | How much | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Salt (소금)** | Start with a small pinch, taste, adjust | The broth comes with no salt |
| **Black pepper** | A light shake | Adds warmth |
| **Green onion (파)** | A generous spoonful | Freshness and color |
| **Kkakdugi juice** | A spoonful or two | Adds tang and a little heat |

<Callout type="tip" title="The kkakdugi trick">
Seolleongtang comes with radish kimchi (깍두기) and cabbage kimchi. Many locals spoon some of the kkakdugi liquid into the broth for a sour, spicy kick. Try it halfway through the bowl.
</Callout>

## How to eat it

1. **Taste the broth plain** first.
2. **Add salt and pepper gradually**, then green onion.
3. **Eat the noodles first** if your bowl includes somyeon.
4. **Add the rice** to the broth, or eat it spoonful by spoonful.
5. **Alternate bites** with kimchi and kkakdugi.

## Related beef soups

Seolleongtang and gomtang belong to a family of Korean beef soups. Once you know the two, the others on the menu are easy to read.

| Soup | Korean | What it is | Spice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Doganitang** | 도가니탕 | Beef knee cartilage soup, rich and gelatinous | None |
| **Galbitang** | 갈비탕 | Beef short rib soup, clear and meaty | None |
| **Yukgaejang** | 육개장 | Shredded beef with bracken in a spicy red broth | Hot |
| **Kkori gomtang** | 꼬리곰탕 | Oxtail soup, deep and premium | None |
| **Haejangguk** | 해장국 | "Hangover soup", often with beef blood or cabbage | Mild to medium |

If you're avoiding spice, every soup above except yukgaejang is a safe choice. Many seolleongtang restaurants serve several of these, so the menu board is a good place to try something new on a second visit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is seolleongtang so bland?**
It's served unseasoned so each person can adjust the salt to taste. Add salt, pepper and green onion at the table.

**What makes the broth white?**
Beef bones are boiled for many hours, releasing collagen and marrow that turn the broth milky.

**Is seolleongtang good for a hangover?**
Many Koreans think so. It's warm, mild and filling, and seolleongtang shops often open early in the morning.

**What does 특 mean on the menu?**
특 (*teuk*) means "special": a larger bowl with more meat, for a little more money.
