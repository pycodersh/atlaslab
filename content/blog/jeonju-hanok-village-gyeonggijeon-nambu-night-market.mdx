Jeonju is the city of hanok and bibimbap, and its old centre is small enough to cover on foot in a day. Start with the murals on the hillside at Jaman Village, walk down through the lanes of Jeonju Hanok Village, visit Gyeonggijeon Shrine, and end with street food at Nambu Market's night market.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **The route:** Jaman Mural Village → Jeonju Hanok Village → Gyeonggijeon Shrine → Nambu Night Market, under 2 km on foot
- **Getting there:** KTX from Seoul to Jeonju Station, then a 10–15 minute taxi to Hanok Village
- **Cost:** the village and murals are free; Gyeonggijeon charges a small fee
- **Best day:** Friday or Saturday, when the night market runs
- **Avoid:** midday weekend crowds on the main hanok street; explore the side lanes instead

</KeyTakeaways>

Jaman Village sits on a hillside just east of Hanok Village, across the main road. Its small houses and alleys are covered in murals, from cartoon characters to flowers, and the top of the hill looks back over the tiled roofs below. It's a quick visit, best in the morning before the light gets harsh.

People live here, so keep your voice down and don't go into courtyards or up private steps for photos.

Jeonju Hanok Village is the largest cluster of traditional houses in a Korean city: hundreds of hanok, many now cafes, craft shops, guesthouses and restaurants. The main street, Taejo-ro, is busy with visitors in rented hanbok. The quieter side lanes are where the village feels most like a neighbourhood.

Lunch here means bibimbap. Our [Jeonju bibimbap guide](/blog/en/k-pantry/jeonju-bibimbap-vs-regular-bibimbap) explains what makes the Jeonju version different and how much gochujang to add. <img src="https://eecvvgkihtcgfikaimao.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/blog-covers/jeonju-hanok-village-gyeonggijeon-nambu-night-market-gyeonggijeon.webp" alt="The red-pillared gate of Gyeonggijeon Shrine framed by large trees" loading="lazy" />

Photo: Mobius6 (<a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">CC BY-SA 4.0</a>), via <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jeonju_Gyeonggijeon_20230408_005.jpg" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Wikimedia Commons</a></figcaption>
</figure>

Gyeonggijeon was built in the early Joseon period to enshrine the portrait of King Taejo, the dynasty's founder, whose family came from Jeonju. The walled grounds are shaded by old trees and feel calm compared with the streets outside. The Royal Portrait Museum inside the grounds displays royal portraits, and its entry is included with the shrine ticket.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fee** | Small entry fee (between ₩1,000 and ₩3,000 depending on age, per the city of Jeonju) |
| **Hours** | 9 am to 8 pm in summer, last entry an hour before closing; shorter in other seasons |
| **Getting there** | On Taejo-ro, in the middle of Hanok Village |

## Nambu Market and the Night Market

<figure className="post-figure">
<img src="https://eecvvgkihtcgfikaimao.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/blog-covers/jeonju-hanok-village-gyeonggijeon-nambu-night-market-nambu.webp" alt="Pungnammun, Jeonju's old south gate, with a two-storey wooden pavilion on a stone base" loading="lazy" />
<figcaption>Pungnammun Gate, at the entrance to Nambu Market. Photo: Minseong Kim (<a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">CC BY-SA 4.0</a>), via <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pungnammun_Jeonju.jpg" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Wikimedia Commons</a></figcaption>
</figure>

Nambu Market is Jeonju's main traditional market, just past Pungnammun, the old city's south gate. In the daytime it's a working market known for kongnamul-gukbap (bean sprout soup with rice), and its upper floor holds a youth mall of small shops and cafes. On Friday and Saturday evenings, stalls set up in the central aisle for the night market, selling grilled skewers, dumplings and snacks from around the world.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Night market** | Fridays and Saturdays, from early evening until late (a 2026 report gave 5 pm to 11 pm) |
| **Getting there** | About 10 minutes on foot from Hanok Village |
| **Tip** | Arrive early; the aisles get very crowded after 8 pm |

<Callout type="warn" title="Check the dates">
The night market has paused and changed its schedule in past years. Check a recent post or map listing before you build your trip around it.
</Callout>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which days does the Nambu night market run?**
Fridays and Saturdays, from early evening. Check before you go, as the schedule has changed in the past.

**Is Jeonju Hanok Village free?**
Yes. The village is free to walk; only sights like Gyeonggijeon charge entry.

**How do I get from Jeonju Station to Hanok Village?**
About 10–15 minutes by taxi, or 20–25 minutes by city bus.

**Can I do Jeonju as a day trip from Seoul?**
Yes. The KTX makes it possible, though staying one night lets you see the night market without rushing.
