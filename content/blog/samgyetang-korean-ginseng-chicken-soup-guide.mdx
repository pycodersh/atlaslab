Samgyetang (삼계탕) is a whole young chicken stuffed with sticky rice, ginseng, garlic and jujube, simmered in a milky broth. Season it yourself with the salt and pepper on the table, pull the meat apart with chopsticks, and finish with the soft rice from inside.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **One bowl, one chicken:** each person gets a whole small chicken
- **Price:** around 16,000–22,000 KRW at a specialist restaurant
- **Season yourself:** the broth comes lightly salted; add salt and pepper to taste
- **Avoid:** queuing on boknal (the hottest summer days) unless you love crowds

</KeyTakeaways>

## What's in the bowl

| Ingredient | Korean | Why it's there |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Young chicken** | 영계 | Tender meat that falls off the bone |
| **Sticky rice** | 찹쌀 | Stuffed inside; turns into porridge-like rice |
| **Ginseng** | 인삼 | Earthy, slightly bitter; valued as a tonic |
| **Garlic** | 마늘 | Soft and sweet after simmering |
| **Jujube** | 대추 | A dried red date that adds sweetness |

## How to eat samgyetang

| Step | What to do |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Season** | Taste the broth, then add salt and pepper from the table |
| **2. Open the chicken** | Pull the chicken apart with chopsticks and a spoon |
| **3. Dip the meat** | Dip pieces in the small dish of salt and pepper |
| **4. Eat the rice** | Scoop out the sticky rice and mix it into the broth |
| **5. Side drink** | Many restaurants serve a small glass of ginseng liquor (인삼주) |

<Callout type="info" title="Why eat hot soup in summer?">
Koreans traditionally eat samgyetang on boknal, the three hottest days of summer (chobok, jungbok and malbok), following the idea of "fighting heat with heat" (이열치열). Restaurants have long queues on those days.
</Callout>

## Other Korean "boyangsik" (stamina foods)

Samgyetang is the best-known example of boyangsik (보양식), food eaten to restore energy, especially in summer. Restaurants often advertise it on boknal alongside other nourishing dishes.

| Dish | Korean | What it is |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Abalone porridge** | 전복죽 | Rich rice porridge with abalone |
| **Eel** | 장어구이 | Grilled freshwater eel, a summer favorite |
| **Duck** | 오리백숙 | Whole duck simmered with herbs, for groups |
| **Chicken porridge** | 닭죽 | Chicken and rice porridge, gentle and filling |

On the three boknal days, specialist samgyetang restaurants can have queues of an hour or more, so go early or pick a different day that week.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is samgyetang spicy?**
No. The broth is mild, lightly salted and milky. Kimchi on the side adds the only heat.

**Can I eat the ginseng root?**
Yes. It's soft after simmering, with an earthy, slightly bitter taste. Some people skip it.

**Is one samgyetang enough for one person?**
Yes, it's a full meal. Light eaters can order banyetang (a half chicken) where available.

**Why is samgyetang eaten in summer?**
Koreans believe hot, nourishing food restores energy lost to summer heat, especially on the three boknal days.
