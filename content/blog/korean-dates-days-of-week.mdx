Korean dates are one of the friendliest corners of the language. There are no month names to memorize. January is "month one," February is "month two," and so on. Dates work the same way, and the full date runs from big to small: year, month, day.

There are only two traps, and they're the two months everyone gets wrong at first.

## The months

Every month is a **Sino-Korean number + 월** (wol, "month").

| Month | Korean | Romanization |
|---|---|---|
| January | 일월 | irwol |
| February | 이월 | iwol |
| March | 삼월 | samwol |
| April | 사월 | sawol |
| May | 오월 | owol |
| **June** | **유월** | **yuwol** |
| July | 칠월 | chirwol |
| August | 팔월 | parwol |
| September | 구월 | guwol |
| **October** | **시월** | **siwol** |
| November | 십일월 | sibirwol |
| December | 십이월 | sibiwol |

June and October drop a final consonant to make them easier to say: 육 → **유**, 십 → **시**. You'll see them written 6월 and 10월 with digits, but they're always read 유월 and 시월.

## Dates and the full date

Dates are a **Sino-Korean number + 일** (il, "day"): 일일 (1st), 이일 (2nd), 십오일 (15th), 삼십일일 (31st).

A full date goes **from big to small**, the opposite of American order:

| Written | Said | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 2026년 11월 4일 | 이천이십육 년 십일월 사 일 | November 4, 2026 |
| 3월 1일 | 삼월 일 일 | March 1 |
| 12월 25일 | 십이월 이십오 일 | December 25 |

Asking the date uses **며칠** ("which day of the month"). It's spelled 며칠, not 몇일, even though it comes from 몇 ("how many").

- **오늘 며칠이에요?** — What's the date today?
- **생일이 몇 월 며칠이에요?** — When's your birthday? (literally "what month, what day")

## The days of the week

Every day ends in **요일** (yoil). Learn the first syllable and you have the day.

| Day | Korean | Romanization | Element |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | 월요일 | woryoil | moon (월) |
| Tuesday | 화요일 | hwayoil | fire (화) |
| Wednesday | 수요일 | suyoil | water (수) |
| Thursday | 목요일 | mogyoil | wood (목) |
| Friday | 금요일 | geumyoil | metal (금) |
| Saturday | 토요일 | toyoil | earth (토) |
| Sunday | 일요일 | iryoil | sun (일) |

In casual speech and on signs, the single syllable is often enough: 월 · 화 · 수 · 목 · 금 · 토 · 일. Opening hours like **월–금 10:00–19:00** use exactly that.

<Callout type="tip" title="Time words that come up constantly">

**어제** (yesterday), **오늘** (today), **내일** (tomorrow), **모레** (the day after tomorrow). **이번 주** (this week), **다음 주** (next week), **지난주** (last week), **주말** (the weekend). 어제 · 오늘 · 내일 need no particle (내일 만나요, "see you tomorrow"), but days and periods take **에**: 토요일에 ("on Saturday"), 주말에 ("at the weekend").

</Callout>

## Booking in Korean

A booking sentence stacks the date, the weekday and the time, big to small, then the verb. Hours use **native** numbers ([here's why](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-two-number-systems)), so 7 p.m. is 저녁 **일곱** 시.

- **11월 15일 토요일 저녁 일곱 시에 예약하고 싶어요.** — I'd like to book for Saturday, November 15, at 7 p.m.
- **다음 주 금요일 두 명이요.** — Two people, next Friday.
- **며칠까지 계세요?** — Until what date are you staying? (a hotel question)
- **월요일에 쉬어요?** — Are you closed on Mondays?

Many small restaurants close one weekday, often Monday, and post it as **매주 월요일 휴무** ("closed every Monday").

## Common mistakes

**육월 and 십월.** Say 유월 and 시월.

**English date order.** 4일 11월 sounds scrambled. Year, month, day: 11월 4일.

**Native numbers for dates.** 넷 일 or 열다섯 일 are wrong. Dates use Sino-Korean: 사일, 십오일. (Hours are the exception that uses native numbers.)

**Writing 몇일.** The standard spelling is 며칠.

## 2-minute recap

- Month = **Sino-Korean number + 월**, date = **number + 일**.
- The two irregulars: **유월** (June) and **시월** (October).
- Full dates go **big to small**: 2026년 11월 4일.
- Ask **오늘 며칠이에요?** and **무슨 요일이에요?**
- Days: **월 · 화 · 수 · 목 · 금 · 토 · 일 + 요일**.
- In bookings, the hour is native: **일곱 시** (7 o'clock).
