You've found the perfect jacket in a Seongsu boutique, but the one on the rack has been tried on all afternoon. In Korean fashion shops it's completely normal to ask for a fresh one from stock, and staff expect the question. Here is exactly how to say it, from the rack to the register.

## The exchange

**손님 (customer):** 혹시 입어본 거 말고 새 상품 있어요?  
*(Excuse me, do you have a new one instead of the display item?)*  
**직원 (staff):** 네, 서랍에 새 제품 있는지 확인해 볼게요.  
*(Yes, let me check the drawer to see if we have a new one.)*  
**직원:** 새 상품으로 준비해 드렸습니다.  
*(Here is a fresh new one for you.)*  
**손님:** 감사합니다, 이걸로 계산해 주세요.  
*(Thank you, I'll take this one, please.)*

## Why you can (and should) ask

In shopping streets like Myeongdong, Hongdae and Seongsu, the clothes on mannequins and racks are samples. They've been tried on, handled and sometimes marked with makeup. The same item usually sits folded and bagged in a drawer under the counter or in the stockroom (**창고**, *changgo*). Asking for it isn't fussy. It's what Korean shoppers do every day, especially for white tops, knitwear and anything you'll wear straight away.

The key is the soft opener **혹시** (*hoksi*), "by any chance." It turns a request into a gentle question and makes it easy for staff to say no if the display piece is the last one.

## Polite or casual?

You're talking to shop staff, so polite speech ending in **-요** is standard. The casual forms are for shopping with friends.

| Casual (friends) | Polite (to shop staff) | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 입어본 거 말고 새 거 있어? | 입어본 거 말고 새 상품 있어요? | Is there a new one, not the tried-on one? |
| 확인해 볼게. | 확인해 볼게요. | I'll check. |
| 이걸로 계산해 줘. | 이걸로 계산해 주세요. | Ring this one up, please. |

## Grammar breakdown

### 말고: "not this one, but…"

**N + 말고** means "not N, (but something else)." It always points to an alternative, so it's perfect for swapping one item for another.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **입어본 거 말고** | *ibeobon geo malgo* | Not the one that's been tried on |
| **이거 말고 저거요** | *igeo malgo jeogeoyo* | Not this one, that one |
| **검정 말고 흰색 있어요?** | *geomjeong malgo huinsaek isseoyo?* | Do you have white instead of black? |

**입어본 거** breaks down as **입어 보다** (to try on) + **-ㄴ 거** (the thing that was ~ed): "the thing someone tried on."

### -해 볼게요: "let me (try and) check"

**확인해 볼게요** combines three pieces: **확인하다** (to check) + **-아/어 보다** (to try doing) + **-(으)ㄹ게요** (I'll do it, for you). The **-ㄹ게요** ending is a small promise to the listener, which is why staff use it constantly: **찾아볼게요** (I'll look for it), **여쭤볼게요** (I'll ask someone). See how **-아/어 보다** works in our [guide to "have you ever…?"](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-have-you-ever-eo-boda).

### 이걸로 계산해 주세요: "ring this one up"

**이걸로** is **이거** (this) + **(으)로** (with / as), so "with this one." **계산하다** means to calculate or pay, and **-아/어 주세요** turns it into a polite request. Together: "Please do the payment with this one." It's the most natural line at the counter. For more on these two endings, see [(으)로](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-particle-euro-by-to-as) and [-아/어 주세요](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-requests-a-eo-juseyo).

## Key words

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **새 상품 / 새 제품** | *sae sangpum / sae jepum* | Brand new item, fresh stock |
| **입어본 거** | *ibeobon geo* | The one that's been tried on |
| **재고** | *jaego* | Stock, inventory |
| **진열 상품** | *jinyeol sangpum* | Display item |
| **확인해 볼게요** | *hwaginhae bolgeyo* | Let me check |
| **이걸로 계산해 주세요** | *igeollo gyesanhae juseyo* | I'll pay for this one, please |

## What staff might tell you

| You'll hear | Meaning | What to say back |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **새 상품으로 준비해 드렸습니다.** | Here's a new one for you. | **감사합니다.** (Thank you.) |
| **잠시만 기다려 주세요.** | Please wait a moment. | **네.** (Okay.) |
| **이게 마지막 하나예요.** | This is the last one. | **그럼 이걸로 할게요.** (Then I'll take this one.) |
| **재고가 없어요.** | We're out of stock. | **괜찮아요.** (That's okay.) |

> **준비해 드렸습니다:**
> Staff use the humble **드리다** (to give, for someone) and the formal **-습니다** ending. You don't need to copy it. Your polite **-요** form is exactly right as the customer.

## What to actually say

- Asking for a fresh one: **혹시 입어본 거 말고 새 상품 있어요?**
- The short version: **새 걸로 주세요.** (*A new one, please.*)
- Choosing the item: **이걸로 계산해 주세요.**
