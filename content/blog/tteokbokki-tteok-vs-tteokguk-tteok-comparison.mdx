Korean rice cakes for cooking (tteok, 떡) come in two main shapes: long cylinders for tteokbokki and thin oval slices for tteokguk (rice cake soup). They're made from similar dough but behave differently, so buying the right one makes your dish much better.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Tteokbokki tteok:** finger-thick cylinders, chewy, made for saucy stir-fries
- **Tteokguk tteok:** thin diagonal slices, soft, made for soups
- **Rice vs wheat:** rice tteok is chewier; wheat tteok is softer and holds sauce well
- **Avoid:** cooking hard, refrigerated tteok without soaking; it cracks

</KeyTakeaways>

## Tteokbokki tteok vs tteokguk tteok

| | Tteokbokki tteok (떡볶이 떡) | Tteokguk tteok (떡국 떡) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Shape** | Cylinders, about finger-thick | Thin oval slices cut on the diagonal |
| **Texture** | Very chewy, bouncy | Soft, tender |
| **Made from** | Rice flour or wheat flour | Rice flour (garaetteok, long white rice cake) |
| **Best for** | Tteokbokki, rabokki, skewers, budae jjigae | Tteokguk, mandu-guk, hot pots |
| **Cooking time** | Longer simmer in sauce | Quick, a few minutes in broth |
| **Holiday link** | Everyday snack food | Lunar New Year (Seollal) |

## Rice tteok vs wheat tteok

Tteokbokki tteok comes in two kinds. **Rice tteok (쌀떡)** is chewy and springy, with a clean rice flavor, and stays firm. **Wheat tteok (밀떡)** is softer and slightly lighter, and its surface absorbs sauce well, which is why many street stalls and nostalgic snack shops prefer it. Koreans debate which is better as passionately as pour vs dip for sweet and sour pork.

- **Rice tteok:** best for chewiness and soups, also gluten-free if pure rice
- **Wheat tteok:** best for saucy, sticky street-style tteokbokki
- **Fresh vs packaged:** fresh tteok from a rice cake shop is softer; vacuum-packed lasts longer

<Callout type="tip" title="Soften hard tteok first">
Packaged or refrigerated tteok gets hard. Soak it in warm water for 10–20 minutes before cooking so it softens evenly and doesn't crack.
</Callout>

## Other ways to cook rice cakes

Rice cakes appear in many Korean dishes beyond tteokbokki and tteokguk. Once you have a bag in the freezer, they're an easy way to make meals more filling.

- **Tteok-kkochi (떡꼬치):** rice cakes on skewers, fried and glazed with sweet-spicy sauce, a street snack
- **Gungjung tteokbokki (궁중떡볶이):** the royal-court version, stir-fried with soy sauce and beef, not spicy
- **Budae jjigae and ramyeon:** add a handful of cylinder tteok for chew
- **Tteok-galbi style:** sliced rice cakes grilled alongside meat at BBQ
- **Honey and toasted tteok:** grill plain rice cakes and dip them in honey for a simple snack

## Storing tteok

Fresh rice cakes harden quickly in the fridge. For short storage, keep them at room temperature for a day; for longer, freeze them in portions and soak or boil from frozen. Vacuum-packed tteok from supermarkets keeps for weeks at room temperature before opening, so check the label.

Fresh tteok from a rice cake shop (떡집) is softest and best eaten the day you buy it. Tteokguk tteok is often bought around Seollal, when eating rice cake soup symbolizes growing a year older. Tteokbokki tteok is sold year-round in supermarkets and convenience stores, often with a sauce packet.

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Use cylinder tteok for tteokbokki and slices for soup
- Soak hard tteok in warm water before cooking
- Freeze extra tteok in portions
- Try both rice and wheat tteokbokki

</Do>
<Dont>

- Refrigerate fresh tteok for days
- Overcook tteokguk slices; they turn mushy
- Use soup slices for tteokbokki; they fall apart in thick sauce
- Assume all tteok is gluten-free; wheat tteok isn't

</Dont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use tteokguk tteok for tteokbokki?**
You can, but thin slices soften quickly in thick sauce. Cylinder tteok holds up better.

**What's the difference between rice tteok and wheat tteok?**
Rice tteok is chewier and firmer; wheat tteok is softer and absorbs sauce more.

**Why is my tteok hard?**
Rice cakes harden when cold. Soak them in warm water or boil briefly to soften.

**Are Korean rice cakes gluten-free?**
Pure rice tteok usually is, but wheat tteok contains gluten, and sauces may too.
