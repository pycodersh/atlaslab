Sokcho puts mountains and sea side by side. Seoraksan's granite peaks rise just west of town, and the harbour and beach are minutes away. Watch the sunrise at Yeonggeumjeong, ride the cable car into Seoraksan, take a turn on the beach Ferris wheel and end with sweet fried chicken at the market.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **The route:** Yeonggeumjeong → Seoraksan Cable Car → Sokcho Eye → Sokcho Tourist and Fishery Market
- **Getting around:** bus 7 or 7-1 to Seoraksan, taxis or buses in town
- **Cost:** Yeonggeumjeong and the market are free; the cable car and Ferris wheel are paid
- **Best timing:** sunrise at the pavilion, the cable car early before the queues
- **Avoid:** autumn weekends at midday, when cable car waits run for hours

</KeyTakeaways>

Yeonggeumjeong is a group of rocks on the shore by Dongmyeong Port, with a pavilion on a hill and another reached by a short bridge over the water. Its name is linked to the sound of waves on the rocks, which locals likened to a stringed instrument. It's a favourite spot for sunrise over the East Sea, and free to visit.

## Seoraksan Cable Car

<figure className="post-figure">
<img src="https://eecvvgkihtcgfikaimao.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/blog-covers/sokcho-seoraksan-cable-car-fishery-market-yeonggeumjeong-seorak.webp" alt="Green forested slopes and jagged granite peaks of Seoraksan" loading="lazy" />
<figcaption>Seoraksan's granite peaks. Photo: Bernard Gagnon (<a href="http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/deed.en" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">CC0</a>), via <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Seoraksan_Cable_Car_06.jpg" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Wikimedia Commons</a></figcaption>
</figure>

The Seoraksan cable car climbs from the Seorak-dong entrance of Seoraksan National Park toward Gwongeumseong, an old fortress site on a granite ridge. The ride takes about 10 minutes. From the top station, a short but steep walk on rock leads to the peak, with views over the jagged ridges and down to the sea.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Tickets** | Round trip only, no one-way tickets; around ₩16,000 for adults in most recent listings |
| **Hours** | Posted daily on the official site, changing with the season and weather |
| **Getting there** | Bus 7 or 7-1 from Sokcho to Seorak-dong, the last stop |

<Callout type="warn" title="Go early in autumn">
In the autumn foliage season the cable car is very busy, and the wait to board can run to hours by late morning. Arrive when ticket sales open.
</Callout>

Sokcho Eye is a Ferris wheel about 65 metres tall at the edge of Sokcho Beach. A ride takes about 15 minutes, with views of the beach, the town and Seoraksan behind. It's also pretty lit up after dark.

Sokcho Tourist and Fishery Market, still widely known by its old name, Sokcho Jungang Market, is the town's main market. It's famous for dakgangjeong, sweet and sticky fried chicken sold in boxes to take away, and for squid sundae and fresh seafood. The basement has a fish market where you can choose seafood and have it prepared.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long is the Seoraksan cable car ride?**
About 10 minutes each way, plus a short, steep walk to the top.

**Can I buy a one-way cable car ticket?**
No. Tickets are sold as round trips only.

**What is Sokcho market famous for?**
Dakgangjeong (sweet fried chicken), squid sundae and fresh seafood.

**Is there a train to Sokcho?**
Not yet. Take an express bus from Seoul, which takes about two and a half hours.
