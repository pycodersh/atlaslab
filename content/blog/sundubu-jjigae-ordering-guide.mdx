Sundubu jjigae (순두부찌개) is a bubbling soft tofu stew served in an earthenware pot, often with a raw egg and rice in a hot stone bowl. Choose your spice level and flavor, crack the egg in while the stew is still boiling, and turn the scorched rice into nurungji at the end.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Price:** around 9,000–13,000 KRW, usually with rice and side dishes
- **Spice levels:** many shops let you choose, from mild (순한맛) to extra hot
- **The egg:** crack it in immediately, while the stew is still boiling
- **Avoid:** eating the first spoonful straight away; the stew arrives extremely hot

</KeyTakeaways>

## Choosing your spice and flavor

| Spice level | Korean | What to expect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mild / white** | 순한맛 · 하얀 순두부 | No chili; clear, savory broth |
| **Medium** | 보통 | Gentle heat most visitors handle well |
| **Hot** | 매운맛 | Properly spicy |
| **Extra hot** | 아주 매운맛 | Only for confirmed spice lovers |

| Flavor | Korean | What's in it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Seafood** | 해물 순두부 | Clams, shrimp, sometimes oysters |
| **Beef** | 소고기 순두부 | Minced or sliced beef |
| **Kimchi** | 김치 순두부 | Sour kimchi for extra depth |
| **Mixed** | 섞어 순두부 | Seafood and meat together |
| **Dumpling** | 만두 순두부 | Dumplings in the stew |

## How to eat it

| Step | What to do |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Crack the egg** | As soon as the pot arrives, crack the raw egg into the boiling stew |
| **2. Leave it** | Let it poach for a minute, or stir it in for a richer broth |
| **3. Scoop the rice** | Move the rice from the stone pot into your metal bowl |
| **4. Make nurungji** | Pour hot water or tea into the stone pot over the crust left behind |
| **5. Finish** | Eat the softened scorched rice (누룽지) at the end |

<Callout type="tip" title="Why the stone pot matters">
The rice is cooked in a hot stone pot (돌솥밥), which leaves a crispy layer stuck to the bottom. Covering it with hot water turns it into nutty, comforting nurungji, the classic way to finish the meal.

## What comes with it

Sundubu restaurants usually serve the stew as a set, and the sides change little from place to place. Refills of the side dishes are free.

| Item | What it is |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Stone-pot rice** | Rice cooked in a hot stone bowl, for nurungji later |
| **Raw egg** | In a basket on the table or served with the stew |
| **Banchan** | Kimchi, seasoned bean sprouts, fish cake, sometimes a small fish |
| **Seasoning** | Salt or soy sauce if you want the broth saltier |

## Sundubu vs other tofu dishes

| Dish | Tofu | Texture |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sundubu jjigae** | Uncurdled soft tofu | Silky, almost custard-like |
| **Doenjang jjigae** | Firm tofu cubes | Firm, holds its shape |
| **Dubu jorim** | Firm tofu, braised | Chewy and savory |

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Crack the egg in while the stew is still bubbling
- Choose mild or medium on your first visit
- Pour hot water over the stone-pot crust at the end
- Move rice to your own bowl before it all sticks

</Do>
<Dont>

- Touch the earthenware pot or the stone bowl
- Wait until the stew cools to add the egg; it won't cook
- Order extra hot just to test yourself
- Leave the nurungji; it's part of the meal

</Dont>
</DoDont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is sundubu jjigae always spicy?**
No. Many restaurants offer a mild or white version without chili. Ask for 순한맛 (*sunhanmat*).

**When should I add the raw egg?**
Immediately, while the stew is still boiling. It poaches in the heat; wait too long and it won't cook.

**What is nurungji?**
The crispy layer of rice stuck to the bottom of the stone pot. Add hot water or tea and it softens into a nutty porridge.

**Is sundubu vegetarian?**
Usually not; most versions use seafood or meat and anchovy stock. Some restaurants offer a vegetable version, so ask.
