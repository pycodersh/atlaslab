Ssambap (쌈밥) means "wrapped rice": you take a leaf, add rice, a bit of meat or side dish and a dab of ssamjang, then fold it into one bite. Ssambap restaurants serve baskets of fresh leaves with many small dishes, and it's one of the healthiest, most interactive Korean meals.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **What it is:** rice and side dishes wrapped in fresh leaves, eaten in one bite
- **Key sauce:** ssamjang, a mix of doenjang and gochujang
- **Where:** ssambap restaurants, often with grilled meat or fish
- **Avoid:** making wraps too big; one bite is the rule

</KeyTakeaways>

## How to build a wrap

1. **Take one or two leaves** in your palm; layer lettuce and perilla for more flavor.
2. **Add a spoonful of rice** and a piece of meat, fish or side dish.
3. **Add ssamjang** and, if you like, raw garlic or green chili.
4. **Fold it closed** and eat it in one bite.

Wash your hands or use the wet towel before you start, since you'll be eating with your fingers. Ssambap restaurants usually serve rice, a stew such as doenjang jjigae, grilled pork or mackerel, and many banchan alongside the leaf basket. Refills of leaves are often free.

<Callout type="tip" title="One bite only">
Koreans eat each wrap in a single bite, which is why they're kept small. Biting a wrap in half usually ends with the filling falling out.
</Callout>

## Making ssamjang at home

Ssamjang is easy to make, and homemade versions taste fresher than store-bought. Mix roughly two parts doenjang (soybean paste) with one part gochujang, then add minced garlic, chopped green onion, a little sesame oil, sesame seeds and a touch of honey or sugar. Some cooks add chopped walnuts, tofu or onion for texture.

Ssamjang isn't the only dip for wraps. Many restaurants also serve:

- **Gireumjang (기름장):** sesame oil with salt and pepper, popular with pork
- **Salted shrimp (새우젓):** a classic with boiled pork
- **Ganjang (간장):** soy-based dipping sauce with onion, common with thin-sliced beef
- **Raw garlic and green chili:** for extra heat and bite in each wrap

## Where to try ssambap

Ssambap restaurants are common in Seoul and around tourist areas, often specializing in "jeongsik" set meals. Gyeongju has many ssambap restaurants near its historic sites, where a full table of small dishes comes with the leaves. See [what Gyeongju ssambap is](/blog/en/k-pantry/gyeongju-ssambap-what-is-it).

- **Set meals:** rice, leaves, a stew and grilled meat or fish for one price per person
- **BBQ:** every Korean BBQ restaurant serves lettuce and perilla for wraps
- **Health appeal:** lots of fresh vegetables makes ssambap popular as a lighter meal

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is ssambap?**
Rice wrapped in fresh leaves with side dishes and ssamjang, eaten in one bite.

**What is ssamjang?**
A thick dipping paste made from doenjang and gochujang, often with garlic and sesame oil.

**Is ssambap healthy?**
It's full of fresh vegetables, so it's considered one of the lighter Korean meals.

**Do I need to use my hands?**
Yes. Ssambap is eaten with your hands, so wash them before eating; restaurants provide wet towels.
