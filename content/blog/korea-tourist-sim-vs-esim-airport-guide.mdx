Most visitors to Korea choose between a physical prepaid SIM, picked up at the airport, and an eSIM installed on the phone before departure. If your phone supports eSIM and you only need data, an eSIM is usually cheaper and quicker; choose a physical SIM if you need a Korean phone number for apps and deliveries.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Cheapest and fastest:** a data-only eSIM, installed before you fly
- **Need a Korean number?** Pick a SIM or eSIM plan that includes a 010 number
- **Where:** telecom counters in Incheon arrivals, or online with airport pickup
- **Avoid:** buying a plan without checking that your phone is unlocked

</KeyTakeaways>

## SIM vs eSIM compared

| | Physical prepaid SIM | Tourist eSIM |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **How you get it** | Airport counter or pickup booth | QR code by email; install before or after landing |
| **Price (rough)** | About 25,000–40,000 KRW for 5–10 days unlimited | About 15,000–25,000 KRW for 5–10 days unlimited |
| **Korean phone number** | Usually included | Some plans only; many are data-only |
| **Passport check** | At the counter | Online, if a number is included |
| **Swapping SIMs** | You remove your home SIM | Keep your home SIM active for texts |
| **Best for** | Long stays, needing a local number | Short trips, data only |

Prices change often and vary by provider, so compare the official carrier sites (SKT, KT, LG U+) and resellers before you buy.

## Why a Korean number matters

| You want to... | Needs a Korean number? |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Use Naver Map or KakaoMap** | No |
| **Message with KakaoTalk** | No (your home number works) |
| **Order on delivery apps** | Often yes |
| **Book restaurant waitlists by text** | Often yes |
| **Call a taxi with Kakao T** | Usually works with a foreign number |

<Callout type="info" title="Check your phone first">
Your phone must be unlocked (not tied to your home carrier) to use a Korean SIM or eSIM. eSIM also needs a compatible model; check your phone's settings before buying.
</Callout>

## Setting it up

| Step | Physical SIM | eSIM |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Buy** | Reserve online or walk up to the counter in arrivals | Buy online, receive a QR code |
| **2. Verify** | Show your passport at the counter | Upload passport details if the plan includes a number |
| **3. Install** | Staff insert and activate it | Scan the QR in phone settings, ideally on Wi-Fi |
| **4. Activate** | Ready within minutes | Turn on data roaming for the eSIM line if instructed |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is eSIM or SIM cheaper in Korea?**
Data-only eSIMs are usually cheaper. Physical SIMs with a phone number cost more but unlock apps that need a Korean number.

**Can I buy a SIM card in Seoul instead of at the airport?**
Yes, at carrier stores and some convenience stores, but the airport counters are the easiest for tourists, with English-speaking staff.

**What about pocket Wi-Fi?**
A rented pocket Wi-Fi device works for groups sharing data, but you have to carry, charge and return it. Most solo travelers find an eSIM simpler.

**Will my phone work with Korean networks?**
Most modern unlocked phones work with Korean 4G and 5G networks. Check your phone model on the provider's site to be sure.
