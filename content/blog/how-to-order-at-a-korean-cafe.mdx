A cafe is the easiest place to say your first Korean sentence out loud. The vocabulary is small, most drink names are borrowed from English, and the exchange is over in under a minute. The only surprise is the questions staff ask after you order.

## The order

**[drink] 주세요** (*juseyo*, "please give me") turns any drink name into a polite order. Add **하나** (one) or **두 개** (two) before 주세요 if you're ordering more than one.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 아이스 아메리카노 주세요. | aiseu amerikano juseyo | An iced americano, please. |
| 따뜻한 라떼 하나 주세요. | ttatteutan latte hana juseyo | One hot latte, please. |
| 이거 두 개 주세요. | igeo du gae juseyo | Two of these, please. |

Hot or iced comes first, before the drink name. **따뜻한** is the standard word for hot, though **핫** is understood too. **아아** is everyday slang for an iced americano.

## Sizes and extras

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 큰 사이즈로 주세요. | keun saijeuro juseyo | A large, please. |
| 샷 추가해 주세요. | syat chugahae juseyo | An extra shot, please. |
| 연하게 해 주세요. | yeonhage hae juseyo | Make it weaker, please. |
| 얼음 적게 주세요. | eoreum jeokge juseyo | Less ice, please. |
| 시럽 넣어 주세요. | sireop neoeo juseyo | Please add syrup. |
| 두유로 바꿔 주세요. | duyuro bakkwo juseyo | Soy milk instead, please. |

Chains use their own size names (톨, 그란데 at some); independent cafes usually just have regular and large. If you don't say a size, you get the standard one.

## What staff will ask you

| You'll hear | Meaning | Answer like this |
|---|---|---|
| 드시고 가세요? / 매장이세요, 포장이세요? | For here or to go? | 여기서 마실게요. / 포장이요. |
| 사이즈는요? | What size? | 큰 사이즈로요. |
| 적립하세요? / 포인트 있으세요? | Do you collect points? | 아니요, 괜찮아요. |
| 결제 도와드릴게요. | I'll take your payment now. | 카드로 할게요. |
| 영수증 드릴까요? | Would you like the receipt? | 버려 주세요. (please throw it away) |
| 진동벨 울리면 오세요. | Come up when the buzzer goes off. | 네. |

The points question surprises almost everyone. It's only a loyalty card, and **아니요, 괜찮아요** ("no, it's fine") ends it politely.

## Polite or casual?

| Who | How they sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Staff to you | honorific, with -시- | 드시고 가세요? |
| You to staff | polite -요 | 포장이요. / 카드로 할게요. |
| You with a friend | casual (반말) | 뭐 마실래? 난 아아. |

You never need the honorific yourself. Plain polite **-요** endings are right for every cafe counter.

## Putting it together

> **You:** 아이스 아메리카노 하나 주세요.  
> **Staff:** 드시고 가세요?  
> **You:** 포장이요.  
> **Staff:** 적립하세요?  
> **You:** 아니요, 괜찮아요.  
> **Staff:** 사천오백 원입니다.  
> **You:** 카드로 할게요.

You pay at the counter before you get your drink, and there's no tipping. The price on the menu is the price you pay; the [prices guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-prices-money-eolmayeyo) covers reading it out loud.
