Seollal (설날) is Korean Lunar New Year, a three-day family holiday of bows to elders, rice cake soup and traditional games. If you visit during Seollal, expect packed trains, quieter cities and some closed shops, and learn the greeting 새해 복 많이 받으세요.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **When:** the first day of the lunar calendar, usually late January or February, with a day either side off
- **Greeting:** 새해 복 많이 받으세요 (*saehae bok mani badeuseyo*), "Receive many blessings in the new year"
- **Traditions:** sebae (a deep bow to elders), sebaetdon (New Year money), tteokguk (rice cake soup)
- **Avoid:** last-minute train travel; tickets sell out as families head home

</KeyTakeaways>

## Main traditions

| Tradition | Korean | What it is |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **New Year's bow** | 세배 | Younger family members bow deeply to elders |
| **New Year money** | 세뱃돈 | Elders give children money in envelopes after the bow |
| **Rice cake soup** | 떡국 | Eating it symbolizes growing a year older |
| **Ancestral rites** | 차례 | Food offerings to ancestors, now simplified in many families |
| **Traditional dress** | 한복 | Some families wear hanbok |
| **Yut nori** | 윷놀이 | A traditional board game played with sticks |

## Seollal foods

| Food | Korean | What it is |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rice cake soup** | 떡국 | Sliced rice cakes in beef or anchovy broth, topped with egg and seaweed |
| **Jeon** | 전 | Pan-fried fritters of fish, vegetables and meat |
| **Japchae** | 잡채 | Stir-fried glass noodles with vegetables |
| **Galbijjim** | 갈비찜 | Braised short ribs, a holiday centerpiece |
| **Sikhye** | 식혜 | Sweet rice drink served after the meal |

Families often spend the day before Seollal cooking jeon together, a big part of the holiday atmosphere.

## Seollal vs Chuseok

| | Seollal (설날) | Chuseok (추석) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **When** | Lunar New Year (Jan–Feb) | Harvest festival (Sep–Oct) |
| **Signature food** | Tteokguk (rice cake soup) | Songpyeon (half-moon rice cakes) |
| **Main custom** | Sebae and sebaetdon | Visiting family graves, harvest thanks |
| **Holiday length** | About 3 days | About 3 days |

<Callout type="info" title="Traveling during Seollal">
Trains and buses out of Seoul sell out weeks ahead, highways jam, and some small shops and restaurants close. Palaces and many big attractions stay open, and some offer special holiday events, so Seoul itself can be pleasantly quiet.
</Callout>

## Planning a trip around Seollal

| Tip | Why |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Book trains a month ahead** | Holiday tickets go on sale early and sell out |
| **Check opening hours** | Small restaurants and markets may close for a few days |
| **Visit palaces** | Many hold holiday events and folk games |
| **Expect quieter streets** | Many residents leave Seoul to visit family |

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Learn the greeting 새해 복 많이 받으세요
- Bring a small gift if you're invited to a Korean home
- Try tteokguk at a restaurant during the holiday
- Book transport early

</Do>
<Dont>

- Expect every shop and restaurant to be open
- Plan road trips on the first and last days of the holiday
- Give sebaetdon in used, crumpled notes; new notes are preferred
- Forget to check palace and museum holiday hours

</Dont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When is Seollal?**
It falls on the first day of the lunar calendar, usually in late January or February, with the day before and after also public holidays.

**What do you say on Seollal?**
새해 복 많이 받으세요 (*saehae bok mani badeuseyo*), meaning "Receive many blessings in the new year."

**Is it a good time to visit Korea?**
Cities are quieter and palaces host events, but intercity travel is crowded and some shops close. Plan transport early.

**Why do Koreans eat tteokguk on Seollal?**
Eating tteokguk symbolizes starting the year fresh and growing one year older.
