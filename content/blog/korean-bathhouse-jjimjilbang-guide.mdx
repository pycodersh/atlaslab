A *jjimjilbang* (찜질방) combines gender-separated bathing areas with shared sauna rooms where everyone wears the provided clothes. Pay at the front, follow the locker-to-bath-to-sauna routine below, and remember the bathing area is nude while the shared areas are not.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Price:** around 12,000–18,000 KRW for a standard jjimjilbang, more for spa complexes
- **Bathing areas:** separated by gender and fully nude; shower before entering the baths
- **Shared areas:** everyone wears the provided T-shirt and shorts
- **Avoid:** wearing a swimsuit in the baths or skipping the shower first

</KeyTakeaways>

## Bathhouse, jjimjilbang or spa?

| Type | Korean | What it has | Rough price |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Neighborhood bathhouse** | 목욕탕 | Baths and showers only, gender-separated | About 8,000–10,000 KRW |
| **Jjimjilbang** | 찜질방 | Baths plus shared saunas, snack bar, sleeping areas | About 12,000–18,000 KRW |
| **Spa complex** | 스파 | Large pools and themed saunas, often family-friendly | About 20,000–35,000 KRW |

Many jjimjilbang are open 24 hours, and some travelers stay overnight on the floor mats, though it's not a quiet hotel.

## Step by step

| Step | What to do |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Pay and get a key** | At the front desk; you receive a locker key, towels and clothes |
| **2. Shoe locker** | Leave your shoes in the small locker by the entrance |
| **3. Changing room** | Store your belongings; men and women go to separate floors |
| **4. Shower first** | Wash thoroughly at the seated showers before entering any bath |
| **5. Baths** | Hot, warm and cold pools; no swimsuits, small towel stays out of the water |
| **6. Shared saunas** | Put on the provided clothes and head to the common floor |
| **7. Rest and snack** | Try sikhye (rice drink) and baked eggs, a jjimjilbang classic |

<Callout type="info" title="Tattoos and body scrubs">
Tattoo rules vary: many jjimjilbang allow small tattoos, while some upscale spas restrict them. A body scrub (세신, *sesin*) costs extra and is paid for at the bath area counter.
</Callout>

## The sauna rooms

The shared floor usually has several rooms at different temperatures, each with its own purpose. Rotate between hot and cool rooms, and rest on the heated floor in between.

| Room | Korean | Temperature | What it's for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Kiln sauna** | 불가마 | Very hot | A short, intense sweat; sit on a mat |
| **Salt room** | 소금방 | Warm | Relaxing on warm salt crystals |
| **Jade or charcoal room** | 옥방 · 숯방 | Warm | Lying down on the heated floor |
| **Ice room** | 얼음방 | Cold | Cooling off between hot rooms |
| **Sleeping room** | 수면실 | Comfortable | Napping or overnight stays |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do you have to be naked at a jjimjilbang?**
Only in the gender-separated bathing areas. In the shared sauna and rest areas everyone wears the provided clothes.

**Can I sleep at a jjimjilbang overnight?**
Many are open 24 hours and allow overnight stays on mats, sometimes with a small surcharge. It's cheap but noisy and not private.

**Are tattoos allowed?**
It depends on the venue. Many neighborhood jjimjilbang are relaxed, while some upscale spas restrict visible tattoos. Check before you go.

**What should I eat at a jjimjilbang?**
Baked eggs (맥반석 계란) and sikhye, a sweet rice drink, are the classic combination. Many places also serve ramyeon and simple meals.
