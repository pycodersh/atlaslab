In English, politeness lives in word choice: "could you possibly pass the salt" vs "pass the salt". In Korean it's built into the verb ending, and every sentence has to pick one. Dropping 요 is mechanically easy. Knowing when you're allowed to is the real skill.

<RuleCard badge="Critical rule" title="When can you drop the 요? Only when the relationship says so">
  <RuleAvoid ko="먹어" rom="meogeo" note="반말 with someone older, someone new, or service staff." warn="Stay with 먹어요 until you're invited." />
  <RuleAvoid ko="좋아요 → 좋아" rom="joayo → joa" note="Switching levels mid-conversation with the same person." warn="Pick one ending and keep it." />

## The mechanics

| 요 form | 반말 | Romanization |
|---|---|---|
| 먹어요 | 먹어 | *meogeo* |
| 좋아요 | 좋아 | *joa* |
| 가요? | 가? | *ga?* |
| 학생이에요 | 학생이야 | *haksaengiya* |
| 고마워요 | 고마워 | *gomawo* |

Most of the time you just drop 요. Nouns change a little (이에요 → 이야, 예요 → 야). The [politeness levels guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-politeness-levels-explained) shows all three levels side by side.

## When it's safe, and when it isn't

| Safe to use 반말 | Not safe |
|---|---|
| close friends your age, once agreed | anyone noticeably older, however friendly |
| children | someone you've just met |
| when the other person invites it | anyone senior to you at work |
| talking to yourself | service staff |

**The asymmetry matters.** An older person may speak 반말 to you while you answer with 요. That's normal, not an insult, and it doesn't mean you can switch.

## How the switch is offered

Switching to 반말 isn't just being casual: it's a statement about the relationship, so it's usually agreed out loud.

| You'll hear | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 말 놓으세요. | Please speak casually to me. |
| 말 편하게 해요. | Let's talk comfortably. |
| 우리 동갑이네요! | We're the same age! (often the cue) |

Once an older person offers, both sides switch. If you're the one asking, **말 놓아도 될까요?** ("may I speak casually?") is the polite way. Finding out you're the same age (동갑) is a common trigger: the [age guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-age-sal-man-nai) explains how people work that out.

## What happens if you get it wrong

Erring polite costs nothing. Erring casual costs a moment of awkwardness, and learners are usually forgiven. The one habit to avoid is **mixing levels with the same person**: ending one sentence with 요 and the next without sounds unsteady rather than friendly.
