Baechu kimchi (배추김치), made from napa cabbage, is the kimchi most people picture, while kkakdugi (깍두기) is crunchy cubed radish kimchi served with soups. Korea has hundreds of kimchi varieties, and knowing the main ones helps you understand what's on your table.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Baechu kimchi:** napa cabbage, the everyday kimchi, spicy and sour as it ages
- **Kkakdugi:** cubed radish, crunchy and juicy, classic with seolleongtang and gukbap
- **Non-spicy options:** baek kimchi (white kimchi) and mul kimchi (water kimchi)
- **Avoid:** throwing out sour kimchi; it's perfect for stews and fried rice

</KeyTakeaways>

## Main kimchi types

| Kimchi | Korean | Main vegetable | Taste | Served with |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Napa cabbage kimchi** | 배추김치 | Napa cabbage | Spicy, sour, savory | Almost everything |
| **Cubed radish kimchi** | 깍두기 | Korean radish | Crunchy, sweet-sour, spicy | Seolleongtang, gukbap, fried chicken |
| **Cucumber kimchi** | 오이소박이 | Cucumber | Fresh, crisp, mildly spicy | Summer meals, BBQ |
| **Green onion kimchi** | 파김치 | Green onion | Pungent, spicy | Ramyeon, grilled meat |
| **Young radish kimchi** | 열무김치 | Young radish greens | Refreshing, tangy | Summer noodles, bibimbap |
| **White kimchi** | 백김치 | Napa cabbage, no chili | Mild, clean, slightly sweet | Kids, those avoiding spice |
| **Water kimchi** | 물김치 | Radish or cabbage in brine | Cool, refreshing broth | Rich dishes, as a palate cleanser |

Restaurants usually serve one or two kinds, refilled for free. Kimchi from different restaurants can taste very different, and many diners judge a restaurant partly by its kimchi.

## How kimchi changes as it ages

Fresh kimchi (겉절이, *geotjeori*) is crisp and bright, almost like a spicy salad, and it's served at kalguksu and bossam restaurants. After a few days to weeks in the fridge it ferments, becoming sour and complex. Well-fermented, sour kimchi (묵은지 or 신김치) is the best for cooking: it gives kimchi jjigae, kimchi fried rice and kimchi pancakes their deep flavor.

<Callout type="tip" title="Kimjang season">
Each late autumn, Korean families gather to make a winter's worth of kimchi in an event called kimjang (김장). It's recognized by UNESCO as part of Korea's intangible cultural heritage.
</Callout>

## Cooking with kimchi

Kimchi isn't just a side dish; it's an ingredient in many favorite meals, especially once it turns sour.

- **Kimchi jjigae:** the classic stew, best with well-fermented kimchi and pork
- **Kimchi fried rice (김치볶음밥):** a quick, cheap favorite topped with a fried egg
- **Kimchi pancake (김치전):** crispy and tangy, great with makgeolli
- **Kimchi mandu:** dumplings with a spicy filling
- **Dubu kimchi (두부김치):** stir-fried kimchi with warm tofu, a classic drinking snack
- **Kimchi in ramyeon:** a handful in instant noodles adds depth

Kkakdugi juice is useful too: a spoonful in seolleongtang or gukbap adds tang, and leftover kimchi juice can season fried rice or stews.

## Storing and using kimchi

Keep kimchi in an airtight container in the fridge, pressed down so it's covered by its own juice. Use a clean utensil each time, and expect it to keep getting sourer. Many Korean homes have a separate kimchi fridge (김치냉장고) to control fermentation. For more, see our [guide to buying and storing kimchi](/blog/en/k-pantry/how-to-buy-store-and-use-kimchi).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the most common kimchi?**
Napa cabbage kimchi (baechu kimchi), served at almost every Korean meal.

**What is kkakdugi?**
Cubed radish kimchi: crunchy, juicy and slightly sweet, often served with soups.

**Is there kimchi that isn't spicy?**
Yes. White kimchi (baek kimchi) and water kimchi (mul kimchi) are made without chili.

**Is kimchi vegan?**
Usually not. Most kimchi contains fish sauce or salted shrimp, though vegan versions exist.
