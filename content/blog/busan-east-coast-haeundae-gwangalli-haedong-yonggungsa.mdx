Busan's east coast packs its three best-known sights into one easy day. Start at Haedong Yonggungsa, a temple built right on the sea rocks, spend the afternoon on Haeundae Beach, and finish at Gwangalli, where Gwangan Bridge lights up and a drone show fills the sky every Saturday night.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **The route:** Haedong Yonggungsa → Haeundae Beach → Gwangalli Beach, about 13 km by road
- **Getting around:** bus from Haeundae Station to the temple, then subway Line 2 between Haeundae and Gwangalli
- **Cost:** all three are free; only the temple car park charges
- **Best day:** Saturday, for the Gwangalli drone show at 7 pm and 9 pm (October to February)
- **Avoid:** going to the temple at midday on weekends, when the stone steps are packed

</KeyTakeaways>

Most Korean temples sit in the mountains. Haedong Yonggungsa sits on the rocks at the edge of the sea, so waves break below its halls and pagodas. The way in leads down a long stone stairway, and the best views of the whole temple are from the rocks to one side of the main hall.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fee** | Free; the car park is paid |
| **Hours** | Opens at 4:30 am; closing times differ between official sources, so arrive by early evening |
| **Getting there** | Bus 181 from Haeundae Station, or Osiria Station on the Donghae Line, then bus 139 and about a 15-minute walk |

Come early. By late morning on weekends the stairs and the narrow paths around the main hall fill with tour groups. The temple is a working place of worship, so keep your voice down near the halls.

Haeundae is Korea's best-known city beach: a long curve of sand backed by hotels and some of the country's tallest towers. It's about a 10-minute walk from Haeundae Station (Line 2). In summer it fills with parasols; out of season it's a pleasant place to walk, with the Dongbaekseom island path at the western end and the rest of Haeundae's restaurants a few streets back.

For lunch, try Busan's own cold noodles. Our [milmyeon guide](/blog/en/k-pantry/busan-milmyeon-vs-naengmyeon) explains how they differ from naengmyeon and how to order them.

Gwangalli faces Gwangan Bridge, which lights up after dark, and the beachfront is lined with cafes, bars and seafood restaurants. It's near Gwangan Station on Line 2.

Every Saturday, Suyeong-gu runs the **Gwangalli M Drone Light Show** over the water, with about 1,100 drones.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **When** | Saturdays at 7:00 pm and 9:00 pm from October to February; 8:00 pm and 10:00 pm from March to September |
| **Length** | About 12 minutes per show |
| **Cost** | Free; watch from anywhere along the beach |

<Callout type="warn" title="Check on the day">
Shows can be cancelled or delayed for weather or signal problems, and special holiday shows can run at different times. Check the official drone show site or Suyeong-gu's social channels before you go.
</Callout>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Haedong Yonggungsa free?**
Yes. Entry is free; only the temple car park charges.

**Which day is the Gwangalli drone show?**
Every Saturday evening, with two shows: 7 pm and 9 pm from October to February, and 8 pm and 10 pm from March to September. Weather can cancel a show.

**How far is Haeundae from Gwangalli?**
A few stops on subway Line 2, or about 5 km by road.

**Can I do this route without a car?**
Yes. Use the bus to the temple and subway Line 2 between the two beaches. A taxi between the temple and Haeundae saves time if you're in a hurry.
