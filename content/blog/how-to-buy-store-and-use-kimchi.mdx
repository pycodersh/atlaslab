Kimchi is alive: it keeps fermenting in your fridge, getting more sour over time. Buy a small jar first, keep it sealed and pressed under its juice, and when it turns too sour to eat fresh, cook with it.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Fresh kimchi:** crisp and bright, best as a side dish
- **Sour kimchi:** perfect for stews, fried rice and pancakes
- **Storage:** airtight container, pressed under the juice, in the coldest part of the fridge
- **Avoid:** using a dirty spoon in the jar; it speeds up spoilage

</KeyTakeaways>

## Buying kimchi

Outside Korea, look for kimchi in the refrigerated section of Asian grocery stores or larger supermarkets. In Korea, supermarkets, markets and convenience stores sell kimchi in tubs and pouches, and many restaurants sell their own. Check the date: fresher kimchi gives you more time before it turns sour. Small packs are best until you know which brand you like.

- **Labels:** 배추김치 (cabbage), 깍두기 (radish), 총각김치 (young radish)
- **Vegan options:** look for 비건 or "no fish sauce" labels
- **Bloating packs:** puffed-up packs are normal; fermentation releases gas

<Callout type="tip" title="Stop the smell">
Kimchi smell travels in the fridge. Use a tightly sealed glass or kimchi container, keep it pressed down under its juice, and wipe the rim after each use.
</Callout>

## Making a small batch at home

If you can't find kimchi you like, a small quick batch is easier than you think. Cut napa cabbage into bite-sized pieces, salt it for an hour or two, then rinse and drain well. Mix a paste of gochugaru, garlic, ginger, fish sauce (or soy sauce for vegan kimchi), a little sugar and a spoon of rice flour paste, then toss it with the cabbage, green onion and radish. Pack it into a jar, leave it at room temperature for a day, then move it to the fridge.

- **Quick version:** fresh kimchi (geotjeori) can be eaten the same day
- **Salting:** the cabbage should bend without snapping before you season it
- **Gloves:** wear food-safe gloves; gochugaru stings
- **Patience:** flavor develops over the first week in the fridge

## Storing kimchi

Keep kimchi in the coldest part of the fridge in an airtight container, pressed down so the cabbage stays under the juice. Always use clean, dry chopsticks or tongs. Kept this way, cabbage kimchi lasts for months, becoming more sour week by week. White film on top can be yeast from fermentation; scrape it off if it's small, but throw kimchi away if it's fuzzy, moldy or smells rotten rather than sour.

## Cooking with sour kimchi

Sour kimchi is a treasure for cooking. Simmer it with pork and tofu for **kimchi jjigae**, fry it with day-old rice for **kimchi fried rice**, mix it into batter for **kimchi pancakes**, or stir-fry it with pork belly for **dubu kimchi**. It also adds depth to instant ramyeon and budae jjigae.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does kimchi last in the fridge?**
Properly stored cabbage kimchi lasts for months, getting more sour over time.

**Is sour kimchi bad?**
No. Sour kimchi is just more fermented and ideal for cooking. Discard it only if it's moldy or smells rotten.

**Why is my kimchi jar bubbling?**
Fermentation produces gas. It's normal; open the jar carefully and leave space at the top.

**Can I freeze kimchi?**
You can, though it softens. Frozen kimchi works well for cooking stews and fried rice.
