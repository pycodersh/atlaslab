Staff walk past your table and nobody stops. You haven't done anything wrong. In most Korean restaurants staff leave you alone until you call them, so every request, from ordering to the bill, starts with you.

## The exchange

**A:** 저기요!  
*(Excuse me!)*  
**B:** 네, 부르셨어요?  
*(Yes, did you call?)*  
**A:** 주문할게요. 김치찌개 하나 주세요.  
*(I'd like to order. One kimchi stew, please.)*

You speak polite **-요** forms; staff answer with the honorific **-시-** (부르셨어요). Neither side uses casual speech (반말) here.

## Two ways to call

**The button.** Many tables have a small call button on the edge or the wall. Press it once and wait: a chime shows your table number at the counter. Pressing it again and again is the one thing that reads as rude.

**Your voice.** With no button, say **저기요** (*jeogiyo*, "excuse me") clearly enough to carry across a busy room. If nobody hears, say it again at the same volume. **여기요** (*yeogiyo*, literally "over here") works the same way.

## After you call

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 주문할게요. | jumunhalgeyo | I'd like to order. |
| 이거 주세요. | igeo juseyo | This one, please. (pointing) |
| 하나 주세요. / 둘 주세요. | hana juseyo / dul juseyo | One, please. / Two, please. |
| 물 좀 주세요. | mul jom juseyo | Water, please. |
| 계산할게요. | gyesanhalgeyo | I'd like to pay. |

Pointing at the menu with **이거 주세요** works everywhere and needs no vocabulary. Quantities use native numbers: 하나, 둘, 셋.

## What staff will say to you

| You'll hear | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 네, 부르셨어요? | ne, bureusyeosseoyo? | Yes, did you call? |
| 잠시만요. | jamsimanyo | Just a moment. |
| 주문하시겠어요? | jumunhasigesseoyo? | Ready to order? |
| 더 필요한 거 있으세요? | deo piryohan geo isseuseyo? | Anything else? |

**잠시만요** means they heard you and are on the way, so there's no need to call again.

## Other ways people call

| Word | Romanization | When you'll hear it |
|---|---|---|
| 사장님 | sajangnim | to the owner, literally "boss"; warm at small family places |
| 이모 | imo | to older women staff at casual places, literally "aunt" |

**이모** is affectionate and very common, but it's better to hear it used a few times before you try it. **저기요** is safe everywhere.

## What to actually say

- Calling: **저기요!**
- Ordering: **이거 주세요.**
- Asking for the bill: **계산할게요.**
