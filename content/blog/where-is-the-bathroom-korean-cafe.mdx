You ask where the restroom is, get pointed toward the door, walk out into a hallway, and find a keypad lock. Cafes in multi-floor buildings usually share a restroom with the whole building, and the door is often locked.

## The exchange

**A:** 화장실이 어디에 있어요?  
*(Where's the restroom?)*  
**B:** 밖으로 나가서 오른쪽에 있어요.  
*(Go outside and it's on the right.)*  
**A:** 혹시 비밀번호 눌러야 하나요?  
*(Do I need to enter a code?)*  
**B:** 네, 1234예요.  
*(Yes, it's 1234.)*

**화장실** literally means "makeup room". It's the ordinary word, used everywhere.

## Asking

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 화장실 어디예요? | hwajangsil eodiyeyo? | Where's the restroom? |
| 화장실이 어디에 있어요? | hwajangsiri eodie isseoyo? | Where is the restroom? |
| 혹시 비밀번호 눌러야 하나요? | hoksi bimilbeonho nulleoya hanayo? | Do I need to enter a code? |
| 화장실 열쇠 있어요? | hwajangsil yeolsoe isseoyo? | Is there a key for the restroom? |

Ask about the code before you walk off. It saves a trip back from the hallway.

## What you'll hear back

| You'll hear | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 밖으로 나가서 오른쪽에 있어요. | bakkeuro nagaseo oreunjjoge isseoyo | Go outside, it's on the right. |
| 2층에 있어요. | icheunge isseoyo | It's on the second floor. |
| 복도 끝에 있어요. | bokdo kkeute isseoyo | At the end of the hallway. |
| 그냥 들어가시면 돼요. | geunyang deureogasimyeon dwaeyo | You can just go in. |
| 비밀번호는 영수증에 있어요. | bimilbeonhoneun yeongsujeunge isseoyo | The code is on your receipt. |

Two words carry most directions: **오른쪽** (right) and **왼쪽** (left). Some places hand you a key instead, often tied to something large so it doesn't leave with you.

## Polite or casual?

| Polite (staff, strangers) | Casual (friends) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 화장실 어디예요? | 화장실 어디야? | Where's the restroom? |
| 화장실 좀 다녀올게요. | 화장실 좀 갔다 올게. | I'll just go to the restroom. |

<Callout type="tip" title="Using the keypad">

Codes are usually four digits. Some locks need you to press **\*** or **#** after the number, or to touch the panel first to wake it. If the code doesn't work, go back and ask: **비밀번호가 안 돼요** ("the code isn't working").

</Callout>

## Signs on the door

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 남자 / 남 | namja / nam | Men |
| 여자 / 여 | yeoja / yeo | Women |

Public restrooms in subway stations, department stores and large buildings are free and generally clean, and easier than a locked cafe door if you're nearby.

## What to actually say

- Asking: **화장실 어디예요?**
- The code: **혹시 비밀번호 눌러야 하나요?**
- It's not working: **비밀번호가 안 돼요.**
