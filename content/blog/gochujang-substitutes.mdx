Gochujang (고추장) gives heat, sweetness, fermented depth and thick body all at once, which is why single-ingredient swaps like sriracha fall flat. The closest homemade substitute is a quick mix of miso, gochugaru and honey.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Best mix:** 2 tbsp miso + 1 tbsp gochugaru + 1 tbsp honey + 1 tsp soy sauce
- **No gochugaru?** Miso, a small pinch of cayenne and sugar
- **Works for:** marinades, stir-fries and soups where gochujang is one of several flavors
- **Avoid:** substituting in tteokbokki, bibimbap sauce or gochujang jjigae

</KeyTakeaways>

## What gochujang does in a dish

A good substitute has to cover at least three of gochujang's four jobs:

1. **Heat:** moderate and slow, not sharp
2. **Sweetness:** from fermented glutinous rice rather than added sugar
3. **Umami and funk:** from fermented soybean
4. **Body:** thick enough to cling to rice cakes and coat meat

Sriracha covers only the first, and its vinegar and garlic pull the flavor elsewhere.

## Gochujang substitutes ranked

| Rank | Mix (makes about 3 tbsp) | Covers | Best for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Closest** | 2 tbsp red or white miso + 1 tbsp gochugaru + 1 tbsp honey or corn syrup + 1 tsp soy sauce | Heat, sweetness, umami, body | Stews, marinades, bibimbap-style bowls |
| **2. No gochugaru** | 2 tbsp red miso + ½ tsp cayenne or paprika mix + 1 tbsp sugar + 1 tsp water | Sweetness, umami, body, some heat | Marinades, stir-fries |
| **3. Sambal mix** | 2 parts sambal oelek + 1 part miso + 1 tsp sugar per tbsp | Heat, umami, some body | Spicy stir-fries, dipping sauces |
| **4. Pinch only** | Sriracha + a little honey, less vinegar elsewhere | Heat only | Marinades where other flavors dominate |

Red miso gets closer than white for every mix. For mix 1, let it rest ten minutes so the gochugaru hydrates and loses its dusty taste. For mix 2, keep the cayenne small; it's far hotter than Korean chili, and a one-for-one swap will overwhelm the dish. Avoid smoked paprika, which tastes like barbecue rather than Korean food.

<Callout type="tip" title="The taste test">
Real gochujang tastes sweet first, then warm, never sharp. If your mix tastes hotter or more sour than that, add a little more miso and honey rather than more chili.
</Callout>

## What doesn't work

- **Harissa:** the chili base is fine, but caraway, coriander and cumin read clearly North African in a Korean dish.
- **Ketchup and chili flakes:** a common internet tip. The sweetness is tomato-sharp and too acidic, so the result is edible but not Korean.
- **Chili garlic sauce on its own:** no sweetness, no fermented depth and too thin.

## Dishes where you shouldn't substitute

Some dishes are essentially gochujang delivery systems. When gochujang is the main flavor rather than one ingredient among many, a substitute won't read as the dish.

- **Tteokbokki:** the sauce is mostly gochujang
- **Bibimbap sauce (비빔장):** the same
- **Gochujang jjigae:** the name is the ingredient

![Gochujang Jjigae](https://eecvvgkihtcgfikaimao.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/pantry-recipe-images/gochujang-jjigae-done.png)

For these, wait until you can buy a tub. It costs a few dollars and lasts a year or more in the fridge. Substitutes are perfectly fine in grilled-meat marinades, stir-fried vegetables, and soups that use gochujang alongside other seasonings.

## A note on heat and batch size

Gochujang sold abroad is usually milder than people expect. Korean tubs often show a heat level from 1 to 5, and the common everyday kind sits around 2 or 3 (보통 매운맛, medium hot). If your substitute is much spicier than the finished dish should be, you've overcorrected.

Homemade mixes keep about a week in the fridge, but the gochugaru dulls once wet. Make three tablespoons at a time rather than a jar. If you find yourself mixing it more than twice, buy gochujang; you've already spent more on the components.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best substitute for gochujang?**
A mix of miso, gochugaru, honey and a little soy sauce. It covers heat, sweetness, umami and body.

**Can I use sriracha instead of gochujang?**
Only in a pinch for marinades. It's thin, vinegary and lacks gochujang's sweetness and depth.

**Can I make tteokbokki without gochujang?**
You can, but it won't taste like tteokbokki. It's worth buying the real paste for it.

**How long does homemade gochujang substitute keep?**
About a week in the fridge. Make small batches, since the chili flavor fades once wet.
