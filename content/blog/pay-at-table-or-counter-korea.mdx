You've finished eating and asked for the bill, and nobody comes. Nothing is wrong: in most Korean restaurants you pay at the counter by the door, and the bill is already on your table.

## The exchange

**A:** 계산할게요.  
*(I'd like to pay.)*  
**B:** 카드요, 현금이요?  
*(Card or cash?)*  
**A:** 카드요. 영수증 주세요.  
*(Card. A receipt, please.)*

Look for a slip of paper on your table. It's usually placed there when the food arrives. Take it to the counter on your way out and say **계산할게요** (*gyesanhalgeyo*).

## At the counter

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 계산할게요. | gyesanhalgeyo | I'd like to pay. |
| 카드요. / 현금이요. | kadeuyo / hyeongeumiyo | By card. / In cash. |
| 따로 계산해 주세요. | ttaro gyesanhae juseyo | Separate payments, please. |
| 같이 계산할게요. | gachi gyesanhalgeyo | I'll pay for everyone. |
| 영수증 주세요. | yeongsujeung juseyo | A receipt, please. |

**계산서 주세요** ("the bill, please") is the textbook phrase. It isn't wrong, but in Korea it usually gets you pointed to the counter.

## What staff will say to you

| You'll hear | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 카드요, 현금이요? | kadeuyo, hyeongeumiyo? | Card or cash? |
| 몇 번 테이블이세요? | myeot beon teibeuriseyo? | Which table were you? |
| 같이 하세요, 따로 하세요? | gachi haseyo, ttaro haseyo? | Together or separately? |
| 영수증 드릴까요? | yeongsujeung deurilkkayo? | Would you like the receipt? |
| 맛있게 드셨어요? | masitge deusyeosseoyo? | Did you enjoy your meal? |

The last one isn't a survey. **네, 잘 먹었습니다** ("yes, I ate well") is the natural answer.

## Polite or casual?

| Polite (staff) | Casual (friends) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 계산할게요. | 내가 낼게. | I'll pay. |
| 따로 계산해 주세요. | 각자 내자. | Let's each pay our own. |

Splitting is common among younger people, and one person paying for the group is common too, especially when they invited you. Say how you're paying before they ring anything up.

<Callout type="info" title="No tipping">

The menu price is the full price, tax included. Leave money behind and someone may chase you to return it. Card works almost everywhere, including small family places; foreign cards occasionally fail at very small shops, so a little cash is a sensible backup.

</Callout>

## Where it works differently

- **Cafes and fast food:** you pay first, when you order.
- **Upscale and hotel restaurants:** the bill often comes to your table.
- **Table tablets:** some newer chains let you order and pay on a screen at your seat.

## What to actually say

- At the counter: **계산할게요.**
- Paying: **카드요.**
- On the way out: **잘 먹었습니다.**
