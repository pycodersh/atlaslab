Central Seoul fits a market, a shopping street, a mountain-top tower and a modern landmark into one day. Start at Namdaemun Market, walk over to Myeongdong, ride up Namsan to N Seoul Tower, then finish at Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP) and the Cheonggyecheon stream once the lights come on.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **The route:** Namdaemun Market → Myeongdong → N Seoul Tower → DDP & Cheonggyecheon
- **Getting around:** on foot for the first three, then a short taxi or subway ride to DDP
- **Cost:** the market, Myeongdong, DDP's outdoor plazas and the stream are free; the tower observatory and the Namsan cable car are paid
- **Best timing:** markets in the morning, the tower around sunset, DDP after dark
- **Avoid:** Namdaemun on a Sunday, when most shops are closed

</KeyTakeaways>

Namdaemun is Korea's best-known traditional market: a tangle of lanes selling clothes, kitchenware, ginseng, souvenirs and street food, right next to Sungnyemun Gate. Come for the knife-cut noodle alley, the braised hairtail (galchi-jorim) alley and hot snacks from the stalls, and for souvenirs at lower prices than in Myeongdong.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Getting there** | Hoehyeon Station (Line 4), one stop from Myeongdong |
| **When** | Mornings and early afternoons on weekdays and Saturdays |
| **Watch out** | Most shops close on Sundays |

Cash still helps at the smaller stalls. Our guide to [shopping at a Korean traditional market](/blog/en/k-patto/shopping-at-a-korean-traditional-market) covers how to ask prices and pay.

## N Seoul Tower

<img src="https://eecvvgkihtcgfikaimao.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/blog-covers/seoul-n-seoul-tower-myeongdong-namdaemun-ddp-namsan.webp" alt="N Seoul Tower lit up at dusk above a traditional pavilion on Namsan" loading="lazy" />
<figcaption>N Seoul Tower above a Namsan pavilion at dusk. Photo: Eugene Lim from Hong Kong (<a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">CC BY 2.0</a>), via <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:N_Seoul_Tower_(13952097192).jpg" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Wikimedia Commons</a></figcaption>
</figure>

N Seoul Tower stands on top of Namsan, the mountain in the middle of the city, so the view takes in all of Seoul. The plaza at the foot of the tower, with its walls of love locks, is free. The observatory at the top needs a ticket.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Getting up** | Namsan cable car (separate ticket) or a local bus; walking takes 30–40 minutes uphill |
| **Fee** | Plaza free; observatory ticket required, and prices differ between sellers, so check the official site |
| **Best time** | Late afternoon into sunset, when it's also busiest |

Cable car queues get long around sunset and on weekends. Go up an hour before sunset and you'll see the city in daylight, at dusk and after dark.

Dongdaemun Design Plaza, designed by Zaha Hadid, is a long curving building of metal panels with no straight lines. Walking around and over it is free. Exhibitions and shops inside have their own hours and tickets. Its silver surface looks best after dark.

From DDP it's a short walk north to Cheonggyecheon, a stream restored through the middle of downtown, with paths below street level that you can follow west for a quiet evening walk.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Namdaemun Market open on Sundays?**
Mostly no. A few stalls open, but most shops close on Sundays, so go on a weekday or Saturday.

**Do I have to pay to visit N Seoul Tower?**
Only for the observatory. The plaza around the tower is free, and the Namsan cable car is a separate ticket.

**How far is Myeongdong from Namdaemun Market?**
About 1 km, an easy walk, or one stop on subway Line 4.

**Is DDP free?**
Walking around and over the building is free. Exhibitions inside are ticketed.
