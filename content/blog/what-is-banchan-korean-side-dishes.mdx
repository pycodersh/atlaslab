Banchan (반찬) are the small side dishes that arrive with almost every Korean meal: kimchi, seasoned vegetables, braised potatoes and more. They're included in the price, usually refillable for free, and meant to be shared by the whole table.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Free:** banchan come with the meal at no extra cost
- **Refills:** usually free too; ask staff or use the self-service bar
- **Shared:** everyone picks from the same small plates
- **Avoid:** piling banchan onto your own plate and leaving it uneaten

</KeyTakeaways>

The number and type of banchan depend on the restaurant: a kimbap shop may serve just kimchi and pickled radish, while a set-meal restaurant (백반집) might bring a dozen plates.

## Why banchan exist

A Korean meal is built around rice, soup and side dishes eaten together, rather than one big plate per person. Banchan balance flavors and textures across the table: something spicy, something sweet, something fresh, something fermented. Restaurants include them as part of the meal, and generous banchan are a point of pride.

<Callout type="tip" title="Self-service banchan bars">
Many restaurants have a self-service bar (셀프 코너) with kimchi and side dishes. Take small amounts and go back for more rather than filling a plate at once.
</Callout>

## Banchan at home

In Korean homes, banchan are made in batches and kept in the refrigerator in small containers, then brought out at each meal alongside freshly cooked rice and soup. Many families have a dedicated kimchi refrigerator (김치냉장고) to keep kimchi at the right temperature. Making several banchan on the weekend is a common routine, and markets and supermarkets sell ready-made banchan by weight.

The main cooking methods give banchan their variety:

- **Muchim (무침):** vegetables tossed with seasoning, fresh or blanched
- **Bokkeum (볶음):** stir-fried, like anchovies or fish cake
- **Jorim (조림):** braised in soy sauce until glossy, like potatoes or lotus root
- **Jeon (전):** small pan-fried fritters
- **Kimchi:** fermented vegetables, the constant at every meal

## Banchan etiquette

Rice is the center of the meal, and banchan are eaten bite by bite alongside it rather than as a separate course. Mixing flavors in each mouthful is the point. Banchan plates are shared, so take a little at a time with your chopsticks and eat it before reaching for more. Try a bit of everything; it's part of the meal. When a plate is empty, it's fine to ask for more, especially kimchi.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are banchan free in Korea?**
Yes. They come with the meal, and refills are usually free as well.

**How many banchan will I get?**
It varies: from two at a simple snack shop to a dozen or more at set-meal restaurants.

**Can I ask for more banchan?**
Yes. Ask staff with 반찬 좀 더 주세요, or use the self-service bar if there is one.

**Are banchan vegetarian?**
Many are vegetable-based, but kimchi often contains fish sauce and some sides include anchovies or fish cake. Ask if it matters to you.
