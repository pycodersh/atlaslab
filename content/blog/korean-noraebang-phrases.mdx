노래방 is not karaoke in a bar. It is a private room you rent by the hour, with a song machine, two microphones, and a tambourine that someone will hand you within about four minutes.

The Korean involved is short, and most of it happens in the first thirty seconds at the front desk.

---

## Two kinds of noraebang

**일반 노래방** — you rent a room by the hour. Usually 15,000 to 25,000 won for an hour, split across however many people are in the room.

**코인 노래방** — you pay per song, typically 500 to 1,000 won for two or three songs. Smaller rooms, often just two seats, and increasingly popular for solo practice. No staff interaction at all beyond the machine.

If you are alone and want to practice singing in Korean, 코인 노래방 is the low-pressure option.

---

## At the front desk

**몇 분이세요?** — *How many people?*

- 두 명이요. — Two.

**몇 시간 하실 거예요?** — *How many hours?*

- 한 시간이요. — One hour.
- 두 시간이요. — Two hours.

That is the entire booking. You get a room number and go.

---

## 서비스: the free extra time

When your time runs out, the screen often keeps going anyway. That is **서비스** — free extra time the owner adds, usually without saying anything.

You can also ask:

- 서비스 좀 주세요. — Could we get some extra time?

This is a normal request, not cheeky, and it usually works — especially on a quiet weeknight.

As covered in [the article on Konglish](/blog/en/k-patto/konglish-words-when-english-becomes-korean), 서비스 in Korean means *something given free*, not customer service. Noraebang is where most learners first meet the word.

## Working the machine

The remote is in Korean, and these four buttons cover almost everything.

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| 예약 | queue a song |
| 취소 | cancel |
| 간주점프 | skip the instrumental intro |
| 시작 | start |

**간주점프** is the one worth knowing. Korean ballads often have a thirty-second intro, and this button jumps straight to the singing.

To find a song, search by number if you have it, or by title using the Korean keyboard on the machine. Most machines also have an English search mode.

## Phrases inside the room

- 이 노래 같이 불러요. — Let's sing this one together.
- 신청곡 있어요? — Any requests?
- 한 곡 더 해요. — One more song.
- 저 노래 잘 못해요. — I'm not good at singing.
- 목소리가 안 나와요. — I'm losing my voice.

That third one — 한 곡 더 — is what ends every noraebang session, usually several times before anyone actually leaves.

---

## Ordering food and drinks

Most rooms have a phone or a call button.

- 맥주 두 병 주세요. — Two beers, please.
- 음료수 주세요. — Soft drinks, please.
- 과자 주세요. — Snacks, please.

---

## A note on song choice

If you are with Koreans and want to try a Korean song, older ballads are more forgiving than recent K-pop — slower, clearer, and everyone knows them. 벚꽃 엔딩 and 좋은 날 both come up constantly.

Singing badly in Korean earns more goodwill than singing well in English. The attempt is the point.
