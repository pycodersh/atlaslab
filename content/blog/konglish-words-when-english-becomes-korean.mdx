Konglish is English-based vocabulary used in Korean, often with a different meaning or form from the original. Learn the common ones below so "service" and "one-room" don't confuse you, and use them freely; Koreans will understand you instantly.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **What it is:** English words adapted into Korean, sometimes with new meanings
- **Useful:** Konglish words are often easier for Koreans to understand than standard English
- **Confusing:** "service" means a free extra, and "one-room" means a studio apartment
- **Avoid:** correcting people's Konglish; it's normal Korean vocabulary

</KeyTakeaways>

## Shortened Konglish

Korean loves to shorten long English phrases into two or three syllables. These forms can be hard to recognize even for fluent English speakers.

| Konglish | Short for | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **에어컨 (aircon)** | Air conditioner | Air conditioner |
| **리모컨 (remocon)** | Remote control | TV remote |
| **셀카 (selca)** | Self camera | Selfie |
| **SNS** | Social network service | Social media |
| **파이팅 (fighting)** | — | A cheer: "you can do it!" |
| **오피스텔 (officetel)** | Office + hotel | Studio-style apartment building |
| **콘셉트 (concept)** | Concept | A theme or style, as in K-pop "concepts" |

## Why Konglish exists

English words entered Korean through media, business and education, and many were shortened or reshaped to fit Korean pronunciation and grammar. Some came through Japanese, and a few, like arbeit, come from German. The result is a set of words that look English but work as Korean vocabulary. Pronunciation shifts too: Korean has no "f" or "th" sounds, so "fighting" becomes 파이팅 (*paiting*) and "health" becomes 헬스 (*hellseu*).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is Konglish?**
English-based words used in Korean, often shortened or with new meanings, like 핸드폰 (handphone) for cell phone.

**Is Konglish wrong English?**
It's not English at all; it's Korean vocabulary that came from English. Within Korean, it's perfectly correct.

**What does "service" mean in a Korean restaurant?**
A free extra, such as a complimentary side dish or drink from the owner.

**Why do Koreans say "fighting"?**
It's a cheer meaning "you can do it" or "good luck", used before exams, games or hard tasks.
