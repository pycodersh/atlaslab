Foreign Visa and Mastercard cards work at most shops, restaurants, cafes and taxis in Korea, so you need only a little cash. Change a small amount at the airport, get the rest from a Myeongdong exchange booth or a Global ATM, and always pay in won.

<KeyTakeaways title="Quick summary">

- **Cards:** foreign Visa and Mastercard work almost everywhere; always choose **KRW**, not your home currency, on the terminal
- **Cash:** keep a little for market stalls, food carts and topping up a T-money card
- **Exchange:** a small amount at the airport, the rest in Myeongdong or from a Global ATM
- **Subway gates:** foreign cards and phone wallets can't be tapped at the gates; use a T-money, Climate Card or WOWPASS

</KeyTakeaways>

## Where to exchange money

Airport counters are convenient but usually give the weakest rates, so change only what you need for the first day.

| Option | Rate | Best for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Airport bank counters** | Usually the weakest | A small amount for the train and a transit card |
| **Myeongdong exchange booths** | Usually the best for cash | Changing larger amounts of USD, EUR, JPY and other major currencies |
| **Global ATMs** | Your bank's rate plus an operator fee | Travelers with a low-fee travel debit card |
| **WOWPASS kiosks** | Competitive | Turning foreign cash into a prepaid card balance |

Rates vary day to day, so compare the posted buy rates at two or three booths before you change a large amount.

<Callout type="tip" title="Bring clean banknotes">

Exchange booths check notes carefully and may refuse bills that are torn, marked or very worn. Large, crisp notes ($100 or €100) usually get the best rate.

</Callout>

## Three money rules for visitors

**1. Always pay in won.** When a terminal or ATM offers your home currency (USD, EUR, GBP), choose **KRW** instead. Paying in your own currency, known as dynamic currency conversion, usually adds a markup of several percent. Let your own bank do the conversion.

**2. WOWPASS has two separate balances.** The payment balance, used for shopping and eating, is loaded with foreign cash at WOWPASS kiosks. The transit balance is a T-money chip, topped up separately, usually with Korean cash at station machines or convenience stores.

**3. Look for "Global ATM".** Not every ATM takes foreign cards. Look for machines labeled **Global ATM** (글로벌 ATM) at airports, big stations, bank branches and some convenience stores. Expect an operator fee of a few thousand won per withdrawal, so take out larger amounts less often. The [foreign cards and Global ATM guide](/blog/en/k-patto/foreign-credit-cards-global-atm-korea) has more detail.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does Apple Pay work everywhere in Korea?**
No. With a foreign card, Apple Pay works wherever the terminal accepts contactless payment, which includes most big chains and convenience stores. Many small shops and market stalls don't have contactless readers, so carry the physical card too.

**How much cash should I bring for a 5-day trip?**
Around 70,000–100,000 KRW per person is usually enough for street food, markets and transit top-ups. Pay for hotels, restaurants and shopping by card.

**Can I pay for taxis with a foreign card?**
Yes. Licensed taxis have a card reader between the front seats that takes foreign credit cards, and most also accept T-money.

**Is WOWPASS worth it?**
It's useful if you'd rather load cash onto one card than carry notes, since it combines a prepaid card with T-money. If your bank card has low foreign fees, a normal card plus a T-money card does the same job.
