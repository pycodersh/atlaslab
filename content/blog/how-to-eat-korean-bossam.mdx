Bossam (보쌈) is tender boiled pork belly served sliced, with fresh kimchi, salted shrimp and leaves for wrapping. Build each bite as a wrap with a slice of pork, a little kimchi and sauce, and share the platter with your table.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **What it is:** pork belly simmered in spices, sliced and served with wraps
- **Price:** around 30,000–45,000 KRW for a medium platter for two to three people
- **Eat it as a wrap:** leaf, pork, kimchi, sauce, one bite
- **Avoid:** confusing it with jokbal, which is braised pig's trotters

</KeyTakeaways>

## Bossam vs jokbal

| | Bossam (보쌈) | Jokbal (족발) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cut** | Pork belly or shoulder | Pig's trotters |
| **Cooking** | Boiled with spices, soft and pale | Braised in soy and spices, glossy and dark |
| **Texture** | Tender, juicy | Chewy, gelatinous skin |
| **Served with** | Fresh bossam kimchi, salted shrimp, napa cabbage leaves | Salted shrimp, lettuce, garlic |
| **Best for** | First-timers, mild flavors | Fans of chewy textures |

Many restaurants sell both, and some offer a combo platter.

## Building a bossam wrap

Take a leaf of salted napa cabbage or lettuce, add a slice of pork, a little bossam kimchi (a sweet, spicy radish and cabbage mix), and a dab of salted shrimp (새우젓) or ssamjang. Fold it and eat it in one bite. Raw garlic and chili are optional for extra kick.

## How bossam is cooked

Bossam pork is simmered slowly in water with aromatics that remove any strong smell and add depth. Common additions include doenjang (soybean paste), garlic, ginger, green onion, onion, black peppercorns, and sometimes instant coffee or bay leaves. After simmering for about an hour, the pork rests briefly and is sliced thick, so each piece has a layer of soft fat and tender meat.

The kimchi is just as important. Bossam kimchi is usually made fresh, with radish strips, chestnuts, jujubes and oysters in winter, and it's sweeter and less fermented than everyday kimchi. Salted napa cabbage leaves for wrapping are soft and lightly salty, which balances the rich pork.

- **Kimjang season:** late autumn, when families make winter kimchi, is the traditional time to eat bossam with fresh kimchi
- **At home:** a pressure cooker or slow cooker makes very tender bossam
- **Leftovers:** slice cold bossam thin and pan-fry it, or add it to kimchi stew

## Ordering

Bossam is sold by platter size, usually small, medium and large. A small platter suits two people, a medium three, and a large four or more. It's also one of the most popular delivery foods, especially for group nights in, and restaurants often add a free soup or steamed egg to larger orders. Many specialist restaurants also sell bossam set meals at lunchtime, a cheaper way to try it alone.

Makguksu (cold buckwheat noodles) is a popular side with bossam, its cool, tangy flavor balancing the rich pork. Soju or makgeolli is the usual drink.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is bossam?**
Boiled pork belly or shoulder, sliced and eaten wrapped in leaves with kimchi and sauces.

**Is bossam spicy?**
The pork isn't spicy. The bossam kimchi served with it is sweet and moderately spicy.

**What's the difference between bossam and jokbal?**
Bossam is boiled pork belly; jokbal is pig's trotters braised in soy sauce and spices.

**Can I get bossam delivered?**
Yes. It's one of the most popular delivery dishes in Korea, usually arriving with all the sides.
