Korean rice (밥, *bap*) is short-grain rice cooked until glossy, slightly sticky and tender, so it's easy to eat with chopsticks and a spoon. Rinse it well, soak it, use the right amount of water, and let it rest before serving.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Rice:** short- or medium-grain (Korean, Japanese or Calrose style)
- **Ratio:** roughly 1 part rice to 1.1–1.2 parts water by volume for a pot
- **Steps:** rinse until clear, soak 20–30 minutes, cook, rest 10 minutes
- **Avoid:** long-grain rice like basmati; it won't have the right texture

</KeyTakeaways>

## Types of rice Koreans eat

| Rice | Korean | What it is | Texture |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **White rice** | 쌀밥 · 흰밥 | Polished short-grain rice | Soft, sticky, glossy |
| **Multigrain rice** | 잡곡밥 | White rice mixed with beans, barley, millet or black rice | Chewy, nutty |
| **Black rice** | 흑미밥 | White rice with a little black rice, turns purple | Slightly chewy |
| **Brown rice** | 현미밥 | Unpolished rice | Chewy, needs longer soaking |
| **Barley rice** | 보리밥 | Rice mixed with barley | Bouncy, hearty |
| **Stone-pot rice** | 돌솥밥 | Rice cooked in a stone pot | Crispy crust (nurungji) |

## How to cook rice in a pot

1. **Rinse** the rice in cold water, swirling and draining 3–4 times until the water is mostly clear.
2. **Soak** for 20–30 minutes (longer for multigrain or brown rice).
3. **Add water** at about 1.1–1.2 times the volume of rice.
4. **Boil** on medium-high, then turn to low, cover and cook for about 12–15 minutes.
5. **Rest** off the heat, covered, for 10 minutes, then fluff gently.

In a rice cooker, rinse and add water to the line for "white rice" (백미). Many Korean rice cookers have settings for multigrain, brown rice and porridge.

<Callout type="tip" title="The hand trick">
Many Koreans measure water by resting a flat hand on the rice: the water should come up to just over the knuckles. It's a handy guide when you don't have a measuring cup.
</Callout>

## Rice in Korean culture

Rice is so central to Korean life that the word for cooked rice, bap (밥), also means "meal". A common greeting is "Have you eaten?" (밥 먹었어요?), and "Let's eat sometime" (언제 밥 한번 먹어요) is a friendly way to suggest meeting up. Rice cookers are a standard appliance in almost every Korean home, and many families keep rice warm all day.

Korea grows its own short-grain rice, and newly harvested rice (햅쌀) each autumn is prized for its sweetness and aroma. Rice also becomes many other foods: rice cakes (tteok), rice drinks like sikhye, rice wine like makgeolli, and the scorched rice that turns into nurungji.

- **Rice cookers:** Korean pressure rice cookers make especially glossy, sticky rice
- **Instant rice:** microwavable rice packs (햇반) are sold everywhere for quick meals
- **Rice and soup:** a bowl of rice with soup and kimchi is the simplest Korean meal

## How Koreans eat rice

Rice is the center of a Korean meal, served in individual bowls with soup and banchan. It's eaten with a spoon, not lifted to the mouth. Leftover rice becomes kimchi fried rice, rice balls or porridge, and the crispy crust from stone pots is turned into nurungji with hot water. Cooked rice keeps a day at room temperature in a rice cooker's warm setting, a few days in the fridge, or longer in the freezer in portions.

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Use short- or medium-grain rice
- Rinse and soak before cooking
- Let rice rest after cooking
- Freeze leftover rice in portions

</Do>
<Dont>

- Use long-grain rice for Korean dishes
- Lift the lid while the rice is cooking
- Skip the rinse; the rice turns gummy
- Leave cooked rice out overnight

</Dont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What rice do Koreans use?**
Short- or medium-grain rice. Japanese or Calrose rice works well abroad.

**What's the water ratio for Korean rice?**
About 1 part rice to 1.1–1.2 parts water for a pot, or follow your rice cooker's lines.

**Do I need to soak rice?**
Soaking for 20–30 minutes gives a softer, more even texture, especially in a pot.

**What is japgokbap?**
Multigrain rice: white rice mixed with beans, barley, millet or black rice.
