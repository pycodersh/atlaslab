At many Korean restaurants, the meal isn't finished until staff turn the leftover sauce and scraps into fried rice (볶음밥) right on your grill or pan. Order it when you're nearly done with the meat, let staff make it, and wait for the crispy layer at the bottom.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **When:** near the end of Korean BBQ, dakgalbi, jjukkumi, gopchang and some stews
- **What:** rice, kimchi, seaweed and sesame oil fried in the leftover sauce or fat
- **Price:** usually around 2,000–4,000 KRW per portion
- **Avoid:** scraping it up too early; let the bottom crisp first

</KeyTakeaways>

## Where fried rice finishes the meal

| Dish | What the fried rice is made with | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Samgyeopsal / pork BBQ** | Pork fat, kimchi, rice, seaweed | Often made on the grill plate or a separate pan |
| **Dakgalbi** | Leftover spicy sauce, rice, seaweed, cheese | The classic finish |
| **Jjukkumi (baby octopus)** | Spicy sauce, rice, seaweed | Very popular finish |
| **Gopchang / makchang** | Rich fat and sauce, rice, chives | Hearty and savory |
| **Haemul-tang / jeongol** | Leftover broth reduced with rice | Similar idea as porridge (juk) |

## How staff make it

When you order fried rice, staff clean most of the leftover meat off the pan or swap the grill plate, then add rice, chopped kimchi, seaweed flakes, sesame oil and sometimes cheese or an egg. They chop and stir it with spatulas, then press it flat into a thin layer so the bottom crisps. Some restaurants shape it into a heart or a smiley face.

- **Cheese:** a popular add-on, melted over the top
- **Egg:** sometimes cracked in the middle for a soft center
- **Crispy layer:** the scorched rice (누룽지) at the bottom is the prize

<Callout type="tip" title="Wait for the crust">
After staff press the rice flat, leave it on the heat for a few minutes. Scrape from the edges when the bottom is golden and crunchy.
</Callout>

## Why Koreans finish with rice

In Korean dining, rice often closes a meal, especially after meat. Turning the last of the sauce into fried rice makes sure nothing goes to waste and fills everyone up. It's a social moment too: the table watches as staff chop, stir and shape the rice, and the crispy bottom is usually fought over.

The same idea appears with soups and hot pots, where noodles or rice are added to the last of the broth. After spicy seafood stew, many restaurants make juk (porridge) in the pot, and after shabu-shabu, noodles followed by porridge are common.

- **Noodles first, rice last:** some places offer udon or ramyeon noodles before the fried rice
- **Share:** one or two portions of fried rice are usually enough for a group
- **Ask staff:** they know the best moment to start

## Making it at home

After grilling pork belly or cooking dakgalbi at home, keep a spoonful of fat or sauce in the pan. Add cold cooked rice, chopped kimchi, a little gochujang or soy sauce, and seaweed flakes. Stir-fry, then press it flat and leave it to crisp on medium heat. Finish with sesame oil and sesame seeds.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is fried rice free at Korean BBQ?**
Usually not; it's a small extra per portion, often a few thousand won.

**When should I order fried rice?**
When you're nearly finished with the meat, so staff can make it in the leftover sauce or fat.

**What is in Korean BBQ fried rice?**
Rice, kimchi, seaweed flakes, sesame oil and the pan's leftover sauce or fat, often with cheese.

**Can I order fried rice alone?**
At most BBQ restaurants it's meant as a finish after meat, not a separate dish.
