Kimchi jjigae and doenjang jjigae are the two everyday stews of Korean home cooking and lunchtime restaurants. Order kimchi jjigae if you like sour heat, doenjang jjigae if you want something savory and mild, and eat either one spooned over rice.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Kimchi jjigae:** sour, spicy, rich, usually with pork or tuna
- **Doenjang jjigae:** earthy, salty, mildly sweet from vegetables, rarely spicy
- **Price:** around 8,000–11,000 KRW per serving, with rice and side dishes
- **Avoid:** drinking the stew like soup; it's meant to be eaten with rice

</KeyTakeaways>

## The two stews compared

| | Kimchi jjigae (김치찌개) | Doenjang jjigae (된장찌개) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Broth base** | Aged kimchi and its juice, often with anchovy stock | Fermented soybean paste in anchovy or vegetable stock |
| **Flavor** | Sour, spicy, deep | Savory, earthy, gently sweet |
| **Spice** | Medium to hot | Mild (green chili optional) |
| **Usually inside** | Pork belly or tuna, tofu, onion, green onion | Tofu, zucchini, potato, onion, mushrooms, sometimes clams or beef |
| **Best with** | Rice and a fried egg | Rice and grilled fish or BBQ |
| **Good for** | Spice lovers, cold days | First-timers, a calm lunch |

Many BBQ restaurants serve a small doenjang jjigae near the end of the meal, and it's often the dish people remember.

## What comes with your stew

| Item | Note |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Rice (공깃밥)** | Included, or about 1,000 KRW extra |
| **Banchan** | Kimchi, seasoned vegetables, sometimes fish cake or egg; refills free |
| **Shared pot** | For two or more, the stew may arrive in one pot on a burner |
| **Extras (사리)** | Ramyeon noodles, extra tofu or ham can be added to kimchi jjigae |

<Callout type="tip" title="How locals eat it">
Spoon a little stew over your rice, mix, and eat it together. When the pot is shared, use the ladle or ask for a small plate (앞접시) rather than dipping your own spoon in.
</Callout>

## Where to eat them

Both stews are on the menu almost everywhere, but some places do them especially well.

| Place | What to expect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Baekban restaurants** | A set meal with stew, rice and many side dishes |
| **Kimchi jjigae specialists** | Big shared pots, often with ramyeon noodles to add |
| **Korean BBQ restaurants** | Doenjang jjigae at the end of the meal |
| **Office lunch spots** | Quick, cheap single portions |

## Cooking them at home

Both stews are simple to make if you have the key ingredients.

| Step | Kimchi jjigae | Doenjang jjigae |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Base** | Fry pork and well-fermented kimchi in a little oil | Dissolve doenjang in anchovy stock |
| **2. Simmer** | Add water or stock and kimchi juice; simmer 15–20 min | Add potato and onion; simmer until soft |
| **3. Finish** | Add tofu and green onion | Add zucchini, tofu, green chili |
| **Tip** | Older, sour kimchi tastes best | Don't boil the paste too hard or it turns bitter |

See our guides to [anchovy stock](/blog/en/k-pantry/korean-anchovy-stock-guide) and [buying and storing kimchi](/blog/en/k-pantry/how-to-buy-store-and-use-kimchi).

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Eat the stew with rice, a spoonful at a time
- Use the ladle or a small plate for shared pots
- Add ramyeon noodles to kimchi jjigae for a heartier meal
- Ask for less spice if you're unsure

</Do>
<Dont>

- Drink the stew straight like soup
- Dip your own spoon in a shared pot without asking
- Use fresh kimchi at home; it lacks depth
- Boil doenjang hard for a long time

</Dont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which is spicier, kimchi jjigae or doenjang jjigae?**
Kimchi jjigae is clearly spicier. Doenjang jjigae is usually mild, unless the cook adds green chili.

**Is doenjang the same as miso?**
They're related but different. Doenjang is fermented longer, chunkier and stronger than most Japanese miso.

**Are these stews vegetarian?**
Usually not. Both often use anchovy stock, and kimchi jjigae typically contains pork or tuna. Ask before ordering if it matters.

**Can I order one stew for two people?**
Some restaurants sell stews per person, others in two-person pots. Check the menu for 1인분 (one serving) or 2인 (for two).
