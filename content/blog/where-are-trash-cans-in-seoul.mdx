Seoul has very few street trash cans, so locals carry their rubbish until they reach a station, a convenience store or their home. Keep a small bag with you, use the spots in the table below, and sort cups, cans and general waste into the right slots.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Why so few bins:** households pay for official trash bags, and street bins were being used to dodge that
- **Reliable spots:** subway stations, convenience stores where you bought something, cafes and malls
- **Basic sorting:** liquids out, then plastic, cans and bottles, paper, and general waste separately
- **Avoid:** leaving cups on walls or benches; littering can bring a fine

</KeyTakeaways>

## Why the bins disappeared

In 1995 Korea introduced a pay-as-you-throw system: households buy official volume-based trash bags (종량제 봉투), and recyclables are collected separately. Street bins quickly filled with household rubbish from people avoiding the bag fee, so many districts removed them. Some areas have added bins back near bus stops and busy streets, but they're still rare compared with many other cities.

## Where you can throw things away

| Place | What you can drop | Tip |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Subway stations** | General waste and recyclables | Bins are usually near the gates or platforms, sometimes only inside |
| **Convenience stores** | Rubbish from items you bought there | Bins are inside or by the door; ask politely for anything else |
| **Cafes** | Your cup and tray | Use the return shelf and liquid sink |
| **Department stores and malls** | General waste | Bins near restrooms and food courts |
| **Bus stops in busy areas** | Cups and small items | Some districts have special cup bins |
| **Your accommodation** | Everything | Follow the building's sorting rules |

<Callout type="tip" title="Carry a small bag">
A spare plastic or zip bag in your pocket makes a day of street food and takeout coffee painless. Locals do the same, and many carry their empty cups home rather than hunting for a bin.
</Callout>

## Basic sorting

| Item | Korean label | How |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Takeout cups** | 플라스틱 (plastic) | Pour out the drink and ice, put the lid and straw in plastic or general, cup in plastic |
| **Cans and bottles** | 캔 · 병 | Empty them; glass and cans may share or have separate slots |
| **Paper cups and boxes** | 종이 (paper) | Flatten boxes; wet or greasy paper goes in general waste |
| **Food scraps and broth** | 음식물 | The food-waste bin, not the general bin |
| **Wrappers, napkins, skewers** | 일반쓰레기 (general) | Everything that isn't recyclable |

## Asking a store for help

If you need to throw away something you didn't buy at that store, ask nicely. Most clerks will help with a small item, especially if you buy something.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will I be fined for littering in Seoul?**
Littering can bring a fine, and some districts patrol popular areas. Carrying your rubbish to a bin avoids the issue entirely.

**Why do street food stalls take back sticks and cups?**
Because there are few public bins, many vendors keep a bin or a cup for used skewers. Hand them back when you finish.

**Can I use a convenience store bin for anything?**
Bins are meant for customers' rubbish from that store. For a small item, ask politely, and consider buying something.

**Where do I throw away rubbish at my Airbnb?**
Follow the host's instructions. Most buildings sort general waste in official bags, recyclables by type and food waste separately.
