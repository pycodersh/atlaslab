Myeongdong's street food stalls set up in the late afternoon and line the shopping streets into the evening. The food is fun but priced for tourists, so pick two or three snacks and set a budget before you start.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **Getting there:** Myeongdong Station (Line 4); exits 5 to 8 open onto the shopping streets
- **When:** stalls open around 4 to 5 pm and are busiest from about 7 pm
- **How long:** about 2 hours with some shopping
- **Cost:** a few thousand won for classic snacks; premium seafood costs much more
- **Avoid:** buying the first thing you see; the same snack is priced differently stall to stall

</KeyTakeaways>

## What to eat and what it costs

| Snack | What it is | Rough price |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **계란빵** egg bread | sweet bun with a whole egg baked on top; cheap and classic | 2,000–3,000 won |
| **회오리감자** tornado potato | spiral-cut potato fried on a stick; share it | 5,000–6,000 won |
| **떡볶이 · 어묵** tteokbokki, fish cake | spicy rice cakes, fish cake in broth; the local staple | 4,000–6,000 won |
| **탕후루** tanghulu | fruit in a hard candy shell; very sweet | 4,000–7,000 won |
| **랍스터구이** grilled lobster | lobster tail with cheese; a small splurge | 15,000–25,000 won |
| **딸기모찌** strawberry daifuku | rice cake around a fresh strawberry | 4,000–6,000 won |

Prices are rough guides and rise every year, so check the sign before you order. Neighborhood markets and areas like [Hongdae](/blog/en/k-patto/hongdae-street-food-guide) are usually cheaper for the same snacks.

## Paying at the stalls

| Method | Works? | Tip |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Card** | Most stalls | Contactless foreign cards are widely accepted |
| **Cash** | Everywhere | Keep 10,000 KRW in small notes for cash-only carts |
| **Bank transfer** | Locals only | Needs a Korean bank account |

<Callout type="tip" title="A simple anti-overspend plan">

- Walk one full loop first and note prices before buying
- Choose one sweet, one savory and one "splurge" at most
- Share big items like tornado potato and lobster with a friend
- Set a cash budget and leave the card in your pocket

</Callout>

## A two-hour evening plan

| Time | Plan |
| :--- | :--- |
| **6:00 pm** | Arrive at exit 6 and walk the main street once to compare prices |
| **6:30 pm** | Start savory: tteokbokki, fish cake or a tornado potato |
| **7:15 pm** | Browse the beauty stores while the crowds peak at the stalls |
| **8:00 pm** | Finish with something sweet, like egg bread or strawberry daifuku |

## How to order

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 이거 얼마예요? | igeo eolmayeyo? | How much is this? |
| 하나 주세요. | hana juseyo | One, please. |
| 카드 돼요? | kadeu dwaeyo? | Do you take card? |

You'll hear the price, like **오천 원이요** ("5,000 won"), and **여기 찍으세요** ("tap here"). The [street food phrases guide](/blog/en/k-patto/ordering-korean-street-food) covers spice levels and portions.

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Eat next to the stall and return sticks and cups to it
- Check the price sign before ordering
- Go on a weekday evening for thinner crowds
- Carry your trash until you find a bin or hand it back

</Do>
<Dont>

- Stop in the middle of the crowd flow to eat
- Assume the most expensive snack is the best
- Buy everything at the first busy corner
- Expect public bins on every street

</Dont>
</DoDont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Myeongdong street food expensive?**
It's pricier than neighborhood markets, especially premium items like lobster and wagyu skewers. Classic snacks such as egg bread and tteokbokki are still affordable.

**What time is best to visit?**
Around 6–8 pm, when nearly all stalls are open. After 9 pm some stalls start packing up.

**Is the food safe?**
Stalls cook to order and turn over quickly. Choose busy stalls and food cooked in front of you.

**Can I find vegetarian snacks?**
Egg bread, hotteok, tornado potato and fruit snacks are usually vegetarian. Tteokbokki and fish cake broth often contain fish stock.
