You're sitting. An elderly passenger gets on, and there are no free seats. What happens next is one of the most Korean moments you'll see on public transport.

## The exchange

**A:** 어르신, 여기 앉으세요!  
*(Ma'am / Sir, please have a seat here!)*  
**B:** 어머, 학생 고마워요.  
*(Oh, thank you, young man.)*  
**A:** 아니에요, 편하게 앉아서 가세요.  
*(Not at all, please sit comfortably.)*  
**B:** 학생도 손잡이 꼭 잡아요.  
*(Make sure you hold the handrail tight too.)*

**어르신** (*eoreusin*) is the respectful word for an elderly stranger, more formal than 할머니 or 할아버지. **어머** is a soft "oh my!", mostly used by women. **학생** literally means "student", but older people use it for any young person, and it's warm, not condescending.

## Words to know

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 어르신 | eoreusin | elderly person (respectful) |
| 여기 앉으세요 | yeogi anjeuseyo | please sit here |
| 편하게 | pyeonhage | comfortably |
| 손잡이 | sonjabi | handrail |
| 노약자석 | noyakjaseok | priority seat |

## What you'll hear

| You'll hear | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 고마워요, 학생. | Thank you, young one. |
| 아이고, 괜찮아요. | Oh, I'm fine. |
| 곧 내려요. | I'm getting off soon. |
| 학생도 조심해요. | You be careful too. |

Sometimes the answer is a polite refusal, **괜찮아요** or **곧 내려요**. Offering once more is natural; if they still say no, sit back down. No one is offended.

## Answering thanks

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 아니에요. | anieyo | Not at all. |
| 별말씀을요. | byeolmalsseumeullyo | Don't mention it. (more formal) |
| 괜찮아요. | gwaenchanayo | It's no problem. |

Here **아니에요** means "don't mention it", not "no". Saying nothing after a thank-you can come across as cold, and one of these closes the moment warmly.

## Priority seats

Subway cars have priority seats (**노약자석**) at each end of the car, and pink seats in the middle set aside for pregnant passengers. Buses have priority seats near the front. Even on a regular seat, standing up for an older passenger is common. You don't need to announce it: stand, gesture toward the seat, and say **어르신, 여기 앉으세요**. The [subway etiquette guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-subway-etiquette-guide) covers the rest of the unwritten rules.

## Polite or casual?

Older passengers often speak to young people in casual or half-polite speech (**잡아요**, **고마워**). That's normal from them. You keep to polite speech with **-세요**: **앉으세요**, **가세요**.

## What to actually say

- Offering: **어르신, 여기 앉으세요.**
- After thanks: **아니에요, 편하게 앉아서 가세요.**
- If they decline: **네, 알겠습니다.**
