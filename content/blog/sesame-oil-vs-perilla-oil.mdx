Korean stores shelve two similar amber oils side by side: 참기름 (*chamgireum*, toasted sesame oil) and 들기름 (*deulgireum*, perilla oil). Sesame oil is the everyday default, while perilla oil gives certain vegetable dishes an herbal flavor that sesame oil can't copy.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Sesame oil:** nutty and roasted, used in bibimbap, most namul, marinades and dips
- **Perilla oil:** grassy and herbal, best on wild greens, roasted seaweed and perilla seed soups
- **Both are finishing oils:** add them off the heat, by the teaspoon
- **Avoid:** keeping opened perilla oil in the cupboard; it goes rancid quickly

</KeyTakeaways>

## Is perilla oil the same as sesame oil?

No. They're pressed from different seeds and taste clearly different. Toasted sesame oil is nutty and goes into almost everything, while perilla oil is earthy and herbal and belongs mainly on vegetable dishes. Both are finishing oils, not frying oils.

| Feature | Toasted Sesame Oil (참기름) | Perilla Seed Oil (들기름) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Made from** | Toasted sesame seeds | Perilla seeds (들깨) |
| **Flavor & Aroma** | Deep, nutty, toasted, rich | Earthy, herbal, rustic, slightly grassy |
| **Color** | Deep amber | Lighter, greenish-gold |
| **Best Used For** | Marinades (Bulgogi), Bibimbap, dipping sauces | Namul (wild greens), Makguksu, tofu seasoning, roasted gim |
| **Cooking Heat** | Finishing oil; high heat burns off the aroma | Finishing oil only; never deep-fry |
| **Key Nutrient** | Sesamol (antioxidant) | Very high plant-based Omega-3 (ALA) |
| **After opening** | Months in a cool, dark cupboard | Refrigerate; use within a couple of months |
| **If you buy one** | Start here | Add later |

## Both are finishing oils

This matters more than choosing between them. Neither is a cooking oil. Both are aromatic oils added off the heat, at the end, usually a teaspoon at a time. Heating them hard destroys the aroma you paid for, and perilla oil turns bitter.

When a recipe says "stir-fry in sesame oil," it usually means cooking in a neutral vegetable oil and finishing with a little sesame oil. Korean home cooks use ordinary vegetable oil for the actual frying.

<Callout type="warn" title="Refrigerate perilla oil">
Perilla oil is high in unsaturated fat, so it oxidizes much faster than sesame oil. Keep an opened bottle in the fridge, buy the smallest size available, and check the production date rather than only the expiry date.
</Callout>

## Where sesame oil goes

Sesame oil is the default. It goes into bibimbap, most namul, bulgogi marinade, dipping sauces for dumplings and pancakes, and a drizzle over rice. It's also brushed on seasoned gim. It's used more freely than in Chinese cooking and more often than in Japanese cooking, and it's a big part of why food tastes specifically Korean.

![Sesame Oil](https://eecvvgkihtcgfikaimao.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/pantry-ingredients/sesame-oil.png)

Buy small bottles. Sesame oil keeps well, but its aroma fades once opened, and a large bottle goes flat before you finish it.

## Where perilla oil goes

Perilla oil is the specialist, and in its own dishes it's hard to replace.

- **Wild and dried greens:** namul made from 취나물 (aster greens), 고사리 (bracken) and other dried vegetables
- **Roasting gim:** seaweed sheets brushed with perilla oil and salt before toasting
- **Perilla seed dishes (들깨):** soups and stews thickened with ground perilla seeds, where the oil reinforces the flavor
- **Some buckwheat noodle and cold dishes**

If you've eaten a Korean vegetable side dish with an herbal note you couldn't place, it was probably perilla oil.

## The rancidity problem

Left in a warm cupboard, an opened bottle of perilla oil can turn within a couple of months. Rancid perilla oil smells like old paint or crayons rather than fresh grass. People often assume that's simply how it tastes and stop buying it, when the bottle was just old. Fresh perilla oil should smell clean, nutty and herbal.

## Can you substitute one for the other?

Functionally, yes; both are aromatic finishing oils. In flavor, they're clearly different. Sesame oil in a perilla dish gives you something that works but tastes more ordinary. Perilla oil in a sesame dish stands out more, since its grassy note clashes with sweet or meat-based dishes. If you own only one, make it sesame oil, and add perilla oil once you cook vegetable side dishes regularly.

## Buying tips

Pressed Korean sesame oil labeled 참기름 100% is stronger than the blended sesame oils in many general supermarkets, some of which are mostly soybean oil with sesame flavor. Check the ingredient list; it should list just one ingredient. At Korean markets, small mills press both oils fresh to order, and the smell of roasting seeds fills the street.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the difference between sesame oil and perilla oil?
Sesame oil is nutty and roasted; perilla oil is grassy and herbal, and goes rancid faster.

### Can I substitute perilla oil for sesame oil?
Yes, but it changes the dish. Both are finishing oils, so the swap works technically. Perilla oil's grassy note stands out in sweet or meat-based dishes like bulgogi, so use a little less. Going the other way, sesame oil in a perilla dish works but tastes more ordinary.

### Is perilla oil healthy?
It's one of the richest plant sources of omega-3 fat (ALA), which makes up more than half of the oil. Those same fats are why it oxidizes quickly, so it's only a healthy choice when it's fresh: keep it in the fridge and don't heat it.

### Can I cook with sesame oil?
It's best as a finishing oil. Cook in neutral oil and add sesame oil at the end.

### Does perilla oil need to be refrigerated?
Yes, after opening. It oxidizes quickly at room temperature.
