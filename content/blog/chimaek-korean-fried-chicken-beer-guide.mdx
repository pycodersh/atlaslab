Chimaek, short for chicken and maekju (beer), is Korea's favorite night-out and stay-in meal. Choose a flavor and bone-in or boneless from the tables below, and every order comes with pickled radish cubes to cut through the grease.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Price:** a whole chicken costs about 20,000–30,000 KRW at most chains
- **Can't decide?** Order 반반 (*banban*): half fried, half sauced
- **Free with every order:** pickled radish (치킨무); extra sauces may cost a little
- **Avoid:** expecting fast service at peak times; Friday nights and big sports games mean long waits

</KeyTakeaways>

## Flavors compared

| Flavor | Korean | What it is | Spice | Best for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fried** | 후라이드 | Plain, extra-crispy fried chicken | None | First-timers, pairing with beer |
| **Yangnyeom** | 양념 | Glazed in sweet-spicy red sauce | Mild to medium | Sweet-and-spicy fans |
| **Soy-garlic** | 간장 | Glazed in sweet soy and garlic | None | Anyone who wants flavor without heat |
| **Half-and-half** | 반반 | Half of one flavor, half of another | Depends | Groups and indecisive eaters |
| **Spicy** | 매운맛 · 핫 | Chili-heavy sauce or seasoning | Hot | Heat lovers |

## Boneless or bone-in

| Option | Korean | Pros | Cons |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bone-in** | 뼈 | Juicier, the classic choice, often cheaper | Messier to eat |
| **Boneless** | 순살 | Easy to share, no gloves needed | Usually 1,000–3,000 KRW more, a little drier |

<Callout type="tip" title="Pickup is often cheaper">
Ordering 포장 (*pojang*, takeout) and collecting it yourself can be a little cheaper than delivery, which adds a delivery fee. A pickup order makes a good Han River picnic; see our [Han River guide](/blog/en/k-patto/han-river-parks-seoul-guide).
</Callout>

## Where to eat chimaek

You can eat chimaek in three ways, and each has its own feel. **Chicken pubs (호프)** are casual bars with draft beer, sports on TV and plastic gloves on every table; they're the classic place for a group night out. **Franchise chicken shops** such as the big national chains focus on takeout and delivery but usually have a few tables. **Outdoors**, picking up chicken and eating it in a park, especially by the Han River on a summer evening, is a local ritual.

| Setting | Best for | What to expect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chicken pub (호프)** | Groups, sports nights | Draft beer pitchers, loud and lively, open late |
| **Chain store** | Quick meals, takeout | Consistent menus, set prices, delivery-focused |
| **Park picnic** | Summer evenings | Pick up the chicken, bring a mat, sort your trash after |

## Delivery and extras

| Item | What to know |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Delivery apps** | Baemin and Coupang Eats usually need a Korean phone number; see our [delivery app guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-delivery-apps-tourists) |
| **Delivery fee** | Usually a few thousand won on top of the menu price |
| **Extra radish or sauce** | Ask for 무 추가 or 소스 추가; sometimes free, sometimes 500 KRW |
| **Side dishes** | Cheese balls, fries and rice cake skewers are popular add-ons |

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Use the plastic gloves provided for bone-in chicken
- Order draft beer (생맥주) in a pitcher for groups
- Try half-and-half on your first visit
- Ask for 덜 맵게 (less spicy) if the spicy menu worries you

</Do>
<Dont>

- Expect ranch or ketchup; dipping sauces are Korean-style
- Leave bones on the table; use the bone bowl
- Order the hottest flavor without trying a medium one first
- Assume delivery works without a Korean number

</Dont>
</DoDont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is Korean fried chicken so crispy?**
It's usually fried twice, with a thin batter, so the skin stays crunchy even under sauce.

**What beer goes with chimaek?**
Light Korean lagers or draft beer are the classic match. Some restaurants also serve soju or highballs.

**Is one chicken enough for two people?**
For a snack with beer, yes. As a full meal for two hungry people, add a side dish or a second half-and-half.

**Do chicken places open late?**
Most stay open until late evening, and many deliver until after midnight, especially near universities and nightlife areas.
