If your Korean soups taste thin next to restaurant versions, the missing piece is usually the stock, not a seasoning. Anchovy and kelp stock (멸치육수, *myeolchi yuksu*) takes about 20 mostly hands-off minutes and sits under most Korean soups and stews.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Ratio:** 10–15 large dried anchovies (about 20g) and one 10cm piece of kelp per 1 liter of cold water
- **Buy:** the large "for soup" anchovies (국물용), not the tiny ones for side dishes
- **Two rules:** remove the anchovy guts, and pull the kelp out after about 10 minutes
- **Avoid:** a hard rolling boil; it turns the stock cloudy and bitter

</KeyTakeaways>

## Stock ingredients and what they add

| Ingredient | Amount per 1 liter water | What it adds | Required? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Large dried anchovies (국물용 멸치)** | 10–15, about 20g | Savory seafood depth | Yes |
| **Dried kelp (다시마)** | One 10cm square | Clean umami | Yes |
| **Korean radish (무)** | A few slices | Clean sweetness | Common |
| **Onion** | Half | Sweetness, rounds sharp edges | Common |
| **Dried shiitake** | 1–2 | Earthy depth, good for doenjang jjigae | Optional |
| **Green onion roots** | A few | Mild aroma | Optional |
| **Dried shrimp** | A small handful | Sharper seafood note | Optional |

Add one or two optional items, not all of them. The most standard home version is anchovy, kelp, radish and onion.

## Buy the right anchovies

Korean stores sell dried anchovies in several sizes. The small ones (볶음용, 2–3cm) are for stir-fried side dishes and give very little stock. You want the large ones labeled 국물용 (for broth), about 6–8cm long. They come in bigger bags and are usually cheaper per gram. Keep them in the freezer so their oils don't go rancid.

## The two mistakes that make stock bitter

**1. Leaving the guts in.** Large dried anchovies have a dark strip of innards along the belly, and it's bitter. Pinch off the head, split the body with your thumb and flick the black strip out. Cooks disagree about the heads; many keep them for depth, some remove them for a cleaner taste. The guts, though, should always go. A whole batch takes about two minutes, and you can prep a week's worth at once.

**2. Boiling the kelp.** Kelp releases its umami quickly, then starts releasing slime and bitterness. Take it out after about 10 minutes, as the water approaches a boil. This is the single most common reason homemade stock tastes vaguely bitter and looks cloudy.

<Callout type="tip" title="Toast first for more flavor">
Dry-toast the cleaned anchovies in a pot for a minute before adding water. It drives off fishy moisture and gives a rounder, nuttier stock.
</Callout>

## The method

1. Put the anchovies and kelp in 1 liter of **cold** water. A cold start pulls more flavor from the kelp.
2. Heat over medium until it simmers gently, about 10 minutes.
3. **Remove the kelp** just before the water boils.
4. Simmer the anchovies, uncovered, for another 10–15 minutes.
5. Strain.

You'll end up with about 800ml of pale golden stock. Keep the heat gentle the whole time; a rolling boil makes it cloudy and pushes bitterness out of the anchovies.

## Storage, shortcuts and a vegetarian version

Refrigerated stock lasts about three days. For longer, freeze it flat in zip bags or in ice cube trays for small amounts; it keeps two to three months and goes straight into a hot pot. Many Korean home cooks make two liters on the weekend and freeze it in portions for the week's soups.

For busy nights, Korean grocery stores sell stock packets (육수팩): large tea bags filled with anchovy, kelp and aromatics. Drop one into boiling water for about ten minutes, then remove it. The stock is a little flatter than homemade, but it's a fair trade on a weeknight. There are also concentrated stock cubes and liquid bases that dissolve instantly.

For a vegetarian stock, use kelp, dried shiitake, radish and onion with the same method, and remove the kelp at the same point. It's lighter and slightly sweeter, works well in doenjang jjigae, and is noticeable mainly in soups where the seafood note carries the dish.

Swap water for anchovy stock in doenjang jjigae, kimchi jjigae, tteokguk, sundubu or any noodle soup and the dish gains a savory base it was missing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you make Korean anchovy stock?**
Simmer about 20g large dried anchovies and a 10cm piece of kelp in 1 liter of cold water, remove the kelp after 10 minutes, simmer 10–15 more minutes and strain.

**Why is my anchovy stock bitter?**
Usually the anchovy guts were left in, the kelp boiled too long, or the stock boiled too hard.

**Which anchovies should I buy for stock?**
Large dried anchovies labeled 국물용 (for broth), about 6–8cm long.

**How long does anchovy stock keep?**
About three days in the fridge, or two to three months frozen.
