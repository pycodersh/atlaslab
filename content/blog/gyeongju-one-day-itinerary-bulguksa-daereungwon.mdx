Gyeongju was the capital of the Silla kingdom for almost a thousand years, and its best sights sit close together. Spend the morning at Bulguksa Temple, then walk downtown from the cafe street of Hwangnidan-gil past grassy royal tombs and an ancient stone observatory, and finish at Wolji Pond as the palace lights come on.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **Getting there:** about 2 to 2¾ hours from Seoul by KTX to Gyeongju Station, or about 3½ hours by express bus to Gyeongju Bus Terminal
- **The walk:** Hwangnidan-gil → Daereungwon → Cheomseongdae → Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond, about 2.7 km on foot
- **Cost:** Bulguksa, Daereungwon and Cheomseongdae are free; Donggung and Wolji costs 3,000 KRW
- **Best timing:** Bulguksa in the morning, the downtown walk in the afternoon, Wolji Pond at dusk
- **Avoid:** arriving at Wolji Pond right before last entry on a weekend evening; lines can stop you getting in

</KeyTakeaways>

## Bulguksa Temple

<img src="https://eecvvgkihtcgfikaimao.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/blog-covers/gyeongju-one-day-itinerary-bulguksa-daereungwon-bulguksa.webp" alt="The stone staircases and wooden halls of Bulguksa Temple on their raised stone terrace" loading="lazy" />
<figcaption>The stone staircases of Bulguksa lead up to the temple halls. Photo: Basile Morin (<a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">CC BY-SA 4.0</a>), via <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bulguksa_temple_entrance_gate_stairs_flower_bed_and_blue_sky_in_Gyeongju_South_Korea.jpg" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Wikimedia Commons</a></figcaption>
</figure>

Bulguksa is the temple on every Gyeongju postcard: stone staircases, stone pagodas and wooden halls built on a raised terrace in the 8th century. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site together with the Seokguram Grotto on the mountain above it.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fee** | Free since May 4, 2023 |
| **Hours** | 9:00 to 5:00 pm (last entry) from November to January, to 5:30 pm in February and October, to 6:00 pm from March to September |
| **Getting there** | City bus 10 or 11 from downtown Gyeongju, about 35 to 45 minutes |

Bulguksa sits outside the city center, so go first while you're fresh and the courtyards are quiet. Allow about two hours, or longer if you add Seokguram, which needs another bus or taxi up the mountain.

Back downtown, Hwangnidan-gil is a street of low hanok-style buildings full of cafes, bakeries and small shops. It's about a 10-minute walk from Gyeongju Bus Terminal and the natural place for lunch. Look for **gyeongju-ppang** (경주빵), the small red-bean pastries the city is known for, and the long lines outside the newest cafes.

## Daereungwon Tomb Complex

<figure className="post-figure">
<img src="https://eecvvgkihtcgfikaimao.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/blog-covers/gyeongju-one-day-itinerary-bulguksa-daereungwon-daereungwon.webp" alt="Grass-covered Silla royal tomb mounds behind a lily pond at Daereungwon" loading="lazy" />
<figcaption>Royal tomb mounds and a lily pond at Daereungwon. Photo: Bernard Gagnon (<a href="http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/deed.en" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">CC0</a>), via <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hwangnamdaechong_Tomb.jpg" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Wikimedia Commons</a></figcaption>
</figure>

Right next to Hwangnidan-gil, Daereungwon is a park of huge grass-covered mounds: the tombs of Silla kings and nobles. Paths wind between them, and one spot frames two mounds behind a single magnolia tree that has become one of the city's best-known photos.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fee** | Free; Cheonmachong, the one tomb you can walk inside, costs 3,000 KRW |
| **Hours** | 9:00 am to 10:00 pm, last tickets 9:30 pm |

Step inside **Cheonmachong** (천마총) to see a cut-away of how the tombs were built and a replica of the painted saddle flap that gave the tomb its name, the "Heavenly Horse Tomb."

<img src="https://eecvvgkihtcgfikaimao.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/blog-covers/gyeongju-one-day-itinerary-bulguksa-daereungwon-cheomseongdae.webp" alt="Cheomseongdae, a bottle-shaped stone observatory tower standing in an open field" loading="lazy" />
<figcaption>Cheomseongdae, one of the oldest surviving observatories in Asia. Photo: Christophe95 (<a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">CC BY-SA 4.0</a>), via <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cheomseongdae_in_2018.jpg" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Wikimedia Commons</a></figcaption>
</figure>

A short walk east, Cheomseongdae is a bottle-shaped stone tower from the 7th century and one of the oldest surviving observatories in Asia. It stands alone in an open field, which makes it easy to photograph from every side. Entry is free. In late spring and autumn the fields around it fill with flowers, and the tower is lit up after dark.

End the day at Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond, once a detached palace of the Silla crown prince. The rebuilt pavilions are reflected in the pond, and the view is at its best just after sunset when the lights come on. You may also see it signposted as **Anapji**, its older name.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fee** | 3,000 KRW for adults |
| **Hours** | Usually 9:00 am to 10:00 pm, last tickets 9:30 pm |

<Callout type="warn" title="Check before a night visit">
Gyeongju city warns that the gates can get too crowded to enter when you arrive close to last entry, especially on weekend evenings. Hours can also change during repair work, so check the Gyeongju city tourism site or call 054-750-8655 before you plan the evening around it.
</Callout>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is one day enough for Gyeongju?**
One full day covers Bulguksa and the downtown sights in this guide. With two days you can add Seokguram Grotto, the Gyeongju National Museum and a slower evening on Hwangnidan-gil.

**Do I need to book tickets in advance?**
No. Bulguksa, Daereungwon and Cheomseongdae are free, and Donggung and Wolji tickets are sold at the gate. Book your KTX or express bus from Seoul ahead on weekends.

**How do I get from Gyeongju Station to downtown?**
Gyeongju Station, the KTX stop formerly called Singyeongju, is about 11 km from downtown. City buses reach the bus terminal area in about 20 minutes, and a taxi is quicker.

**When is the best season to visit?**
Spring, for cherry blossoms around the tombs and Bomun Lake, and autumn, when the fields by Cheomseongdae and the maples at Bulguksa turn color. Summer evenings are pleasant for the night walk.
