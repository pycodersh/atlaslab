Korea sorts rubbish strictly, and the rules depend on where you stay: hotels handle it for you, while Airbnbs and apartments expect you to sort it yourself. Separate food waste, recyclables and general waste, use the official bags, and leave the place tidy at checkout.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Hotels:** leave rubbish in the room bin; staff sort it
- **Airbnbs and apartments:** sort food, recyclables and general waste yourself
- **Official bags:** general waste goes in volume-based bags (종량제 봉투) sold at convenience stores
- **Toiletries:** many larger hotels no longer give free disposable toothbrushes and razors

</KeyTakeaways>

## Where you stay changes the rules

| | Hotel | Airbnb / guesthouse | Apartment (long stay) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Who sorts** | Housekeeping | You, following the host's guide | You |
| **Where to take it** | Room bin | A building collection point or a bin in the unit | The building's recycling area, often on set days |
| **Trash bags** | Provided | Usually provided by the host | Buy official bags yourself |
| **Food waste** | Room bin is fine | Separate bin or bag | Separate food waste bin, sometimes card-operated |

## The basic categories

| Category | Korean | Examples | How |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General waste** | 일반쓰레기 | Tissues, wrappers, dirty packaging | Official volume-based bag |
| **Food waste** | 음식물 쓰레기 | Leftovers, peels, rice | Separate bin or yellow food bag; no bones, shells or tea bags |
| **Plastic** | 플라스틱 | Bottles, cups, containers | Rinse, remove labels if possible |
| **Vinyl / film** | 비닐 | Plastic bags, wrappers | Clean and dry |
| **Paper** | 종이 | Boxes, paper | Flatten boxes |
| **Cans and glass** | 캔 · 병 | Drink cans, bottles | Empty and rinse |

<Callout type="info" title="The toiletries rule">
Korea restricts free single-use toiletries at larger lodgings, so many hotels no longer put out disposable toothbrushes, toothpaste, razors or small shampoo bottles. Bring your own, or buy them at the front desk or a convenience store.
</Callout>

## Reading the bin labels

Collection points in buildings use simple Korean labels. These are the words you'll see most often.

| Label | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **일반쓰레기** | General waste |
| **음식물** | Food waste |
| **플라스틱** | Plastic |
| **비닐** | Plastic film and bags |
| **캔 · 고철** | Cans and metal |
| **유리병** | Glass bottles |
| **종이 · 박스** | Paper and boxes |

## Checking out of an Airbnb

| Task | Why |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Sort and take out rubbish** | Most hosts ask guests to use the building's collection point |
| **Empty the fridge** | Leftover food becomes food waste the host has to sort |
| **Wash dishes** | Often listed in house rules |
| **Return keys or codes** | Follow the host's checkout message |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to sort rubbish in a Korean hotel?**
No. Leave it in the room bin and housekeeping will sort it. Sorting matters most in Airbnbs and apartments.

**Where do I buy official trash bags?**
Convenience stores and supermarkets sell them. They're specific to each district, so buy them near where you're staying.

**Why don't hotels give free toothbrushes?**
Korea restricts free single-use toiletries at larger lodgings to cut waste. Many hotels sell them at the front desk instead.

**What goes in food waste?**
Leftover food, fruit and vegetable scraps and rice. Bones, shells, tea bags and fruit pits go in general waste.
