Self-service laundromats (셀프빨래방, *selpeu ppallaebang*) are everywhere in Korean cities, many open 24 hours and unstaffed. Pay at the machine or a kiosk, check whether detergent is added automatically, and allow about an hour and a half for a wash and dry.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Cost:** roughly 4,000–5,000 KRW per wash and a similar amount per dry
- **Payment:** coins from a change machine, cards, or a kiosk at the entrance
- **Detergent:** many washers add it automatically; others sell it from a vending machine
- **Avoid:** leaving clothes in a machine long after the cycle ends; others will be waiting

</KeyTakeaways>

## Washers and dryers compared

| | Washer (세탁기) | Dryer (건조기) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rough price** | About 4,000–5,000 KRW per load; more for large machines | About 4,000–5,000 KRW per 30–40 minutes, or coins per few minutes |
| **Time** | About 30–40 minutes | About 30–40 minutes, more for heavy items |
| **Detergent** | Often automatic (자동세제투입); otherwise buy a pack | Dryer sheets optional, from the vending machine |
| **Big items** | Large washers fit bedding and duvets | Large dryers for bedding |
| **Settings** | Standard, delicate, bedding | Temperature: low, medium, high |

Prices vary by shop and machine size, so check the sticker on each machine.

## Paying

| Method | How it works |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Coins** | Change 1,000 KRW notes into 500 KRW coins at the change machine |
| **Card** | Tap or insert at the machine's card reader |
| **Central kiosk** | Choose your machine number on the kiosk, then pay |
| **App or QR** | Some chains use their own apps; not necessary for visitors |

<Callout type="tip" title="Check for automatic detergent">
Look for 세제 자동 투입 (automatic detergent) on the washer. If you see it, don't add your own or the machine may over-foam. If not, buy a detergent pack from the vending machine.
</Callout>

## Step by step

| Step | What to do |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Load** | Put clothes in the washer; don't overfill |
| **2. Choose a course** | Standard (표준) for most clothes, bedding (이불) for duvets |
| **3. Pay** | Coins, card or the kiosk |
| **4. Start** | Press start (시작); the screen shows the time left |
| **5. Move to the dryer** | Choose the temperature and pay for the time |
| **6. Collect and fold** | Folding tables are usually provided |

## Laundry options compared

| Option | Best for | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Self-service laundromat** | Travelers with a big load | Cheap, fast, 24 hours at many shops |
| **Hotel laundry service** | Business trips, shirts | Expensive, priced per item |
| **Guesthouse washing machine** | Long stays | Ask the host about drying racks |
| **Dry cleaner (세탁소)** | Suits, delicate fabrics | Takes a day or more |

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Check each machine's price and capacity sticker
- Bring 1,000 KRW notes for the change machine
- Use the bedding course for duvets and blankets
- Stay nearby or set a timer for when the cycle ends

</Do>
<Dont>

- Add detergent to machines with automatic dispensing
- Overload washers or dryers
- Dry wool or delicate items on high heat
- Leave clothes in machines after the cycle ends

</Dont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does a coin laundromat cost in Korea?**
Usually around 4,000–5,000 KRW to wash and a similar amount to dry a normal load. Large machines for bedding cost more.

**Do Korean laundromats accept cards?**
Many do, either at the machine or a central kiosk. Keep some 1,000 KRW notes for the change machine just in case.

**Are laundromats open 24 hours?**
Many are, and they're often unstaffed, with CCTV and a contact number on the wall.

**Do I need to bring detergent?**
Usually not. Many washers add detergent automatically, and vending machines sell detergent and dryer sheets.
