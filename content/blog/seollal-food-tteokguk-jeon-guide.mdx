The essential Seollal (설날) dish is tteokguk (떡국), a clear beef broth with sliced rice cakes, eaten on the morning of Korean Lunar New Year. Around it, families serve jeon (pan-fried savory bites), braised short ribs, japchae and sweet rice drinks.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Must-eat dish:** tteokguk, rice cake soup, on New Year's morning
- **When:** Lunar New Year; in 2027 it falls on February 6, with a holiday of about three days around it
- **Most work:** jeon, which families fry in huge batches the day before
- **Avoid:** going to a restaurant on the day itself; many are closed

</KeyTakeaways>

## What goes on the Seollal table

Family tables vary, but these dishes appear in most homes.

| Dish | What it is | Why it's there |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tteokguk (떡국)** | Sliced rice cakes in beef or anchovy broth, topped with egg and seaweed | A fresh, white start to the year |
| **Jeon (전)** | Fish, zucchini, meat patties and vegetables dipped in flour and egg, then pan-fried | Festive and made in big batches |
| **Galbi-jjim (갈비찜)** | Soy-braised beef short ribs | The special-occasion main dish |
| **Japchae (잡채)** | Glass noodles stir-fried with vegetables and beef | Long noodles, colorful and loved by all ages |
| **Namul (나물)** | Seasoned vegetables, often in three colors | Simple sides that balance the rich dishes |
| **Sikhye (식혜)** | Sweet, chilled rice drink | Dessert and digestive after a heavy meal |

Families from the north, and many Seoul families, make tteok-mandu-guk, the same soup with dumplings added.

## Why tteokguk matters

The long white rice cake rods (가래떡) used for tteokguk stand for a long life, and the white color for a clean start. The coin-shaped slices are said to look like old coins, a wish for wealth in the new year.

Koreans used to say that you turn one year older by eating a bowl of tteokguk. Korea switched to international age for legal and official purposes in 2023, but the joke survives: ask a Korean how many bowls they've eaten and they may answer with their age.

## The jeon marathon and the ancestral table

In many families, the day before Seollal is spent frying jeon for hours. Some families also set a charye (차례) table to honor ancestors on the morning itself, with dishes arranged by traditional rules. Charye food is usually seasoned without red pepper or strong garlic.

That work has become a familiar point of debate in Korea. More families now buy jeon from markets, shrink the table, or skip charye altogether and simply eat together.

<Callout type="info" title="Seollal for travelers">
Seoul empties out as people travel to their hometowns. Palaces and many museums stay open, but small restaurants and markets often close for one or two days. Convenience stores, hotel restaurants and chains are the safest bets.
</Callout>

## Making a Seollal meal at home

Tteokguk itself is easy: about 30 minutes with store-bought sliced rice cakes. Buy the oval coin slices labeled for soup, not the thin tteokbokki rods.

1. **Soak** the sliced rice cakes in cold water for 20 minutes.
2. **Simmer** thinly sliced beef in water or broth with garlic and soup soy sauce for 15 minutes.
3. **Add** the rice cakes and cook until soft and floating, about 5 minutes.
4. **Stir** in a beaten egg, then top with green onion and crumbled roasted seaweed.

For the rest of the table, our recipes for [galbi-jjim](/kpantry/en/recipes/galbi-jjim), [japchae](/kpantry/en/recipes/japchae) and [hobak-jeon](/kpantry/en/recipes/hobak-jeon) cover the classics.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What do Koreans eat on Lunar New Year?**
Tteokguk, rice cake soup, is the must-eat dish, usually with jeon, galbi-jjim, japchae, namul and sikhye.

**Why do Koreans eat tteokguk on New Year's Day?**
The long white rice cakes stand for long life and a clean start. Eating it traditionally meant turning a year older.

**What's the difference between tteokguk and tteok-mandu-guk?**
Tteok-mandu-guk is tteokguk with dumplings added, a style common in northern and Seoul families.

**Are restaurants open in Korea during Seollal?**
Many small restaurants close for one or two days. Chains, hotel restaurants and convenience stores usually stay open.
