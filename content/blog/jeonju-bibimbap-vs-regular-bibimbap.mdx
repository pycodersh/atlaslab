Bibimbap (비빔밥) means "mixed rice": a bowl of rice topped with seasoned vegetables, egg and often meat, stirred together with gochujang. Choose a stone-pot (dolsot) version if you love crispy rice, and add the chili paste a little at a time.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Price:** around 9,000–14,000 KRW; Jeonju-style is often more
- **Dolsot bibimbap:** served sizzling in a hot stone bowl with a crispy rice crust
- **Gochujang:** add a little, mix, taste, then add more
- **Avoid:** eating it unmixed; stirring everything together is the point

</KeyTakeaways>

## Types compared

| Type | Korean | What's different | Best for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Regular** | 비빔밥 | Room-temperature bowl, vegetables, egg, gochujang | A quick, everyday meal |
| **Dolsot (stone pot)** | 돌솥비빔밥 | Sizzling stone bowl; rice crisps on the bottom | Crispy rice lovers |
| **Jeonju** | 전주비빔밥 | Rice cooked in beef broth, many toppings, often raw beef (yukhoe) and gingko nuts | A special-occasion meal |
| **Yukhoe** | 육회비빔밥 | Topped with seasoned raw beef | Adventurous eaters |
| **Sanchae (mountain vegetable)** | 산채비빔밥 | Wild mountain greens, often near temples and hiking trails | Vegetarian-leaning diners |

## How to mix it

| Step | What to do |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Add sauce** | Start with half the gochujang provided |
| **2. Add sesame oil** | A drizzle if there's a bottle on the table |
| **3. Mix thoroughly** | Use your spoon to fold everything together, scraping the bottom |
| **4. Taste** | Add more gochujang if you want more heat or sweetness |
| **5. Dolsot only** | Press rice against the hot bowl for a minute to crisp it |

<Callout type="tip" title="Not a fan of spice?">
Ask for the gochujang on the side (고추장 따로 주세요), or use soy sauce or doenjang instead. Some restaurants offer a soy-based sauce.
</Callout>

## What makes Jeonju bibimbap special

Jeonju, in North Jeolla Province, is famous as the home of bibimbap. The city's version cooks the rice in beef bone broth for flavor and a better texture, and tops it with a long list of seasonal vegetables, gingko nuts, pine nuts and often raw beef. A visit to Jeonju's Hanok Village is a common day trip for food lovers.

| | Jeonju bibimbap | Regular bibimbap |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rice** | Cooked in beef broth | Plain steamed rice |
| **Toppings** | 10 or more | Around 5–7 |
| **Price** | Often 13,000–20,000 KRW | Around 9,000–12,000 KRW |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is bibimbap spicy?**
Only as spicy as the gochujang you add. Use less, or ask for it on the side.

**What's the difference between dolsot and regular bibimbap?**
Dolsot bibimbap is served in a sizzling stone bowl, so the rice crisps at the bottom. Regular bibimbap is served in a normal bowl.

**Can vegetarians eat bibimbap?**
Often, yes. Ask for no meat (고기 빼 주세요) and check the egg. Mountain vegetable (sanchae) bibimbap is a good choice.

**Is Jeonju bibimbap worth the trip?**
If you enjoy food, yes. It's richer and more elaborate than the everyday version, and Jeonju's Hanok Village makes a good day out.
