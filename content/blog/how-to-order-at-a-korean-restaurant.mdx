A Korean restaurant meal has four moments that need words: getting a table, ordering, asking for something mid-meal, and paying. Each one is short, and the staff side of the conversation is very predictable once you know it.

## Getting a table

| You'll hear | Meaning | Answer like this |
|---|---|---|
| 몇 분이세요? | How many people? | 두 명이요. / 혼자예요. |
| 편한 데 앉으세요. | Sit anywhere you like. | 네. |
| 잠시만 기다려 주세요. | Please wait a moment. | 네. |

**명** counts people: 한 명, 두 명, 세 명. Staff use the polite **분** about you; you use 명 about yourself. If nobody greets you, ask **자리 있어요?** ("is there a table?").

## Calling staff

Staff usually won't come to your table until you call them. Press the call button if there is one, or say **저기요!** (*jeogiyo*) clearly across the room. It's normal, not rude. [How to call a waiter](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-restaurant-call-bell) covers the button, the voice and the other titles you'll hear.

## Ordering

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 이거 주세요. | igeo juseyo | This one, please. (pointing) |
| 김치찌개 하나 주세요. | gimchijjigae hana juseyo | One kimchi stew, please. |
| 삼겹살 2인분 주세요. | samgyeopsal iinbun juseyo | Two servings of pork belly, please. |
| 덜 맵게 해 주세요. | deol maepge hae juseyo | Less spicy, please. |
| 고기 빼고 주세요. | gogi ppaego juseyo | Without the meat, please. |

Dishes take native numbers (**하나, 둘**); grilled meat is ordered by the portion, **인분**, with Sino-Korean numbers (**이인분**), usually two minimum. The [counters guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-counters-gae-myeong-jan-byeong) explains both.

## Mid-meal

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 반찬 좀 더 주세요. | banchan jom deo juseyo | More side dishes, please. |
| 물 좀 주세요. | mul jom juseyo | Water, please. |
| 공기밥 하나 추가해 주세요. | gonggibap hana chugahae juseyo | One more bowl of rice, please. |
| 앞접시 주세요. | apjeopsi juseyo | A small plate, please. |
| 가위 주세요. | gawi juseyo | Scissors, please. |

Side dishes and refills are free, and so is water, often from a self-serve dispenser. Spoons and chopsticks are usually in a drawer under the table: **수저** is the word for the pair.

## What staff will say to you

| You'll hear | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 주문하시겠어요? | Are you ready to order? |
| 반찬은 셀프예요. | Side dishes are self-serve. |
| 잠시만요. | Just a moment. |
| 맛있게 드세요. | Enjoy your meal. |
| 카드요, 현금이요? | Card or cash? |

## Polite or casual?

| Who | How they sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Staff to you | honorific, with -시- | 주문하시겠어요? |
| You to staff | polite -요 | 이거 주세요. / 계산할게요. |
| You with friends | casual (반말) | 뭐 먹을래? 이거 먹자. |

## Paying

Most restaurants take payment at the counter by the door, not at the table. Take the bill slip with you and say **계산할게요** ("I'd like to pay"). For splitting, say **따로 계산해 주세요** before they ring it up. There's no tipping. The [paying guide](/blog/en/k-patto/pay-at-table-or-counter-korea) has the details.
