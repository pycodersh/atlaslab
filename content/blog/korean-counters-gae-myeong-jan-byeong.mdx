In English you can say "two coffees" and be done. Korean won't let a number stand next to a noun on its own. It adds a small word that says what kind of thing you're counting: cups, bottles, people, sheets of paper. These are **counters**, and you need one almost every time you order, buy, or book something.

There are dozens in the dictionary. Ten of them do nearly all the work.

## The word order

Korean counting has a fixed shape:

**noun + number + counter**

| English | Korean | Romanization |
|---|---|---|
| two coffees | 커피 두 잔 | keopi du jan |
| three people | 세 명 / 사람 세 명 | se myeong / saram se myeong |
| one bottle of soju | 소주 한 병 | soju han byeong |
| four tickets | 표 네 장 | pyo ne jang |

The noun can drop out when everyone knows what you mean. Pointing at a pastry and saying **세 개 주세요** ("three, please") is completely natural.

## Why 하나 becomes 한

Counters use **native Korean numbers** (하나, 둘, 셋…), and the first four change shape when a counter follows them. So does 스물 (20).

| Number | On its own | Before a counter | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 하나 | **한** | 한 개 |
| 2 | 둘 | **두** | 두 잔 |
| 3 | 셋 | **세** | 세 명 |
| 4 | 넷 | **네** | 네 병 |
| 20 | 스물 | **스무** | 스무 개 |

Everything else stays the same: 다섯 개, 여섯 명, 열 잔. Combined numbers only change the last part: 열한 개 (11), 스물두 명 (22).

If the native and Sino-Korean systems are still blurry, the [guide to Korea's two number systems](/blog/en/k-patto/why-korean-has-two-number-systems) covers when each one is used.

## The ten counters that cover a trip

| Counter | Used for | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **개** (gae) | general things, pieces | 사과 두 개 | two apples |
| **명** (myeong) | people | 학생 세 명 | three students |
| **분** (bun) | people, politely | 몇 분이세요? | how many in your party? |
| **잔** (jan) | cups, glasses | 커피 한 잔 | a cup of coffee |
| **병** (byeong) | bottles | 물 두 병 | two bottles of water |
| **그릇** (geureut) | bowls | 밥 한 그릇 | a bowl of rice |
| **마리** (mari) | animals, whole fish or chicken | 치킨 한 마리 | a whole fried chicken |
| **장** (jang) | flat things: tickets, paper, photos | 표 두 장 | two tickets |
| **권** (gwon) | books | 책 세 권 | three books |
| **대** (dae) | cars, machines | 택시 한 대 | one taxi |

**개 is the safety net.** If you can't remember the right counter for an object, 개 works for most small things and people will understand you. It never works for people, though: use 명.

## At a restaurant: 명, 분 and 인분

The first question at most restaurants is about your group:

- Staff: **몇 분이세요?** — How many people? (polite, with 분)
- You: **두 명이요.** — Two people.

Staff use 분, the honorific counter, about you. You use 명 about yourself, since you don't honor your own group.

Barbecue and some set dishes are ordered by the portion, with **인분** (inbun, "servings"). 인분 is the exception on this page: it takes **Sino-Korean** numbers.

- 삼겹살 **2인분** 주세요. — *samgyeopsal i-inbun juseyo* — Two servings of pork belly, please.
- 갈비 **3인분**이요. — *galbi sam-inbun-iyo* — Three servings of galbi.

<Callout type="tip" title="Asking 'how many?'">

Put **몇** (myeot, "how many") in the number's place and keep the counter: **몇 개예요?** (how many items?), **몇 명이에요?** (how many people?), **몇 잔 드릴까요?** (how many cups shall I give you?). You'll hear the last one at every cafe counter.

</Callout>

## Common mistakes

**Using the full number.** 하나 개, 둘 잔 and 셋 명 sound broken. Shorten them: 한 개, 두 잔, 세 명.

**Putting the number first.** 두 커피 is English word order. Say 커피 두 잔.

**Using Sino-Korean numbers.** 이 개 and 삼 명 are wrong with these counters. 인분 is the main exception you'll meet on a trip, along with money, minutes and dates.

**Counting people with 개.** 사람 두 개 sounds like you're counting objects. People take 명 (or 분 when you're being polite).

## 2-minute recap

- Order: **noun + number + counter**: 커피 두 잔, 맥주 한 병.
- Counters take **native Korean numbers**, and 1–4 and 20 shorten: **한 · 두 · 세 · 네 · 스무**.
- The core ten: **개, 명, 분, 잔, 병, 그릇, 마리, 장, 권, 대**.
- 개 is the fallback for things, never for people.
- Restaurant portions use **인분** with Sino-Korean numbers: 2인분 (이인분).
- Ask with **몇**: 몇 개예요? 몇 명이에요?
