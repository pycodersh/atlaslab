Korean won't put a number straight next to a noun. It adds a small word for the kind of thing you're counting: cups, bottles, people, tickets. These are **counters**, and you need one almost every time you order, buy or book something. Ten of them cover nearly every trip.

  <RuleAvoid ko="하나 개" rom="hana gae" note="Leaving 하나 in its full form before a counter." warn="Say 한 개. Likewise 두, 세, 네, 스무." />
  <RuleAvoid ko="두 커피" rom="du keopi" note="Putting the number in front, English-style." warn="The noun comes first: 커피 두 잔." />
  <RuleInstead lead="Noun first, then a native Korean number, then the counter. 하나 · 둘 · 셋 · 넷 · 스물 shorten to 한 · 두 · 세 · 네 · 스무.">
    <RuleExample ko="커피 두 잔 주세요." rom="keopi du jan juseyo" en="Two coffees, please." />
    <RuleExample ko="두 명이에요." rom="du myeongieyo" en="There are two of us." />
    <RuleExample ko="맥주 한 병 주세요." rom="maekju han byeong juseyo" en="One bottle of beer, please." />

The noun can drop when it's obvious: pointing at a pastry and saying **세 개 주세요** ("three, please") is completely natural.

## Why 하나 becomes 한

Most counters take **native Korean numbers**, and five of them shorten before a counter.

| On its own | Before a counter | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 하나 (1) | **한** | 한 개 |
| 둘 (2) | **두** | 두 잔 |
| 셋 (3) | **세** | 세 명 |
| 넷 (4) | **네** | 네 병 |
| 스물 (20) | **스무** | 스무 개 |

The rest stay the same: 다섯 개, 여섯 명, 열 잔. In combined numbers only the last part changes: 열한 개 (11), 스물두 명 (22).

## The ten counters for a trip

| Counter | Used for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **개** (gae) | things in general | 사과 두 개: two apples |
| **명** (myeong) | people | 학생 세 명: three students |
| **분** (bun) | people, politely | 두 분: two people |
| **잔** (jan) | cups, glasses | 커피 한 잔: a cup of coffee |
| **병** (byeong) | bottles | 물 두 병: two bottles of water |
| **그릇** (geureut) | bowls | 밥 한 그릇: a bowl of rice |
| **마리** (mari) | animals, whole chicken or fish | 치킨 한 마리: a whole fried chicken |
| **장** (jang) | flat things: tickets, paper | 표 두 장: two tickets |
| **대** (dae) | cars, machines | 택시 한 대: one taxi |
| **인분** (inbun) | servings of a dish | 삼겹살 2인분: two servings of pork belly |

**개 is the safety net.** If you forget the right counter for an object, 개 works for most small things. It never works for people: use 명.

**인분 is the odd one out:** it takes Sino-Korean numbers. 2인분 is read **이인분** (*iinbun*), and barbecue usually has a two-serving minimum.

## Native or Sino-Korean?

The counter decides which number system you use. This is where most learners slip.

| Counting | Numbers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 개, 명, 잔… | native | 커피 두 잔 *keopi du jan* |
| hours (시) | native | 세 시 *se si* |
| age (살) | native | 스무 살 *seumu sal* |
| minutes (분) | Sino-Korean | 삼십 분 *samsip bun* |
| money (원) | Sino-Korean | 오천 원 *ocheon won* |
| servings (인분) | Sino-Korean | 이인분 *iinbun* |
| floors, dates | Sino-Korean | 삼 층 *sam cheung*, 오월 오일 *owol oil* |

Watch the two 분s: **두 분** (native) is "two people", **이 분** (Sino-Korean) is "two minutes". The [guide to Korea's two number systems](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-two-number-systems) covers the rest.

## What you'll hear

| You'll hear | Meaning | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 몇 분이세요? | How many in your party? | 두 명이요. |
| 몇 개 드릴까요? | How many shall I give you? | 세 개 주세요. |
| 몇 인분 드릴까요? | How many servings? | 2인분 주세요. |
| 몇 잔 드릴까요? | How many cups? | 두 잔이요. |
| 병으로 드릴까요? | By the bottle? | 네, 병으로 주세요. |

Staff use **분** about you; you use **명** about your own group. **몇** (*myeot*, "how many") takes the number's place and keeps the counter. Before 명 it's pronounced *myeon*: 몇 명이에요? sounds like *myeon myeongieyo*.

## Common mistakes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 삼 명, 이 개 | 세 명, 두 개 | These counters take native numbers. |
| 두 인분 | 2인분 (이인분) | 인분 takes Sino-Korean numbers. |
| 사람 두 개 | 두 명 | People take 명 (or 분). |
