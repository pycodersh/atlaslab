Kimjang (김장) is the day in late autumn when Korean families gather to make enough kimchi to last the winter. It usually falls between late November and mid-December, and UNESCO added it to its intangible cultural heritage list in 2013.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **When:** late November to mid-December, earlier in the north and later in the south
- **What:** dozens of salted napa cabbages rubbed with a red pepper filling
- **Why it matters:** the work is shared, and so is the kimchi
- **Avoid:** making a full-size batch your first time; start with one cabbage

</KeyTakeaways>

## How a kimjang day works

Kimjang is less a recipe than a production line. Families split the jobs, and the whole process usually takes two days.

| Step | What happens | Who usually does it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Salting** | Cabbages are halved and soaked or packed with coarse sea salt overnight | The host family, the day before |
| **2. Rinsing** | Salted cabbages are rinsed several times and drained | Anyone with strong arms |
| **3. Filling (소)** | Julienned radish is mixed with gochugaru, garlic, ginger, jeotgal and green onion | The most experienced cook, who seasons it |
| **4. Rubbing** | Filling is spread between every leaf | Everyone, sitting around a big tub |
| **5. Packing** | Cabbages are folded and pressed into containers | The family who'll store it |

The timing is practical. Napa cabbage and Korean radish are at their best in late autumn, and cold weather lets the kimchi ferment slowly. Today most families store it in a kimchi refrigerator (김치냉장고), a separate appliance that keeps kimchi just above freezing.

## What goes into kimjang kimchi

The filling changes by region and family, but the core is the same: salted napa cabbage, a radish-based filling and red pepper powder. Southern regions use more jeotgal (salted seafood) and stronger seasoning; northern-style kimchi is lighter and less salty. Many families add Asian chives, mustard greens, pear or fresh oysters.

<Callout type="tip" title="The kimjang day meal">
The cook's reward is bossam: boiled pork belly wrapped in a salted cabbage leaf with fresh filling. Freshly made, unfermented kimchi (겉절이) is eaten the same day too.
</Callout>

## Kimjang as a social event

Kimjang has always been about sharing. Neighbors used to take turns helping at each other's houses, and finished kimchi goes to relatives who couldn't come, newlyweds and elderly neighbors. Companies, churches and local governments still hold large charity kimjang events in November, where volunteers make kimchi for families who need it.

If you're in Korea in late autumn, ask a Korean friend or coworker about their family's kimjang. Many will happily tell you whose filling is the best.

포기 (pogi) is the counter for heads of cabbage. Thirty is a normal family batch; some families make more than a hundred.

## Trying a small kimjang at home

You don't need thirty cabbages. One napa cabbage fills a large jar and teaches you the whole process. Our [homemade kimchi recipe](/kpantry/en/recipes/homemade-kimchi) walks through the salting, paste and packing, and [musaengchae](/kpantry/en/recipes/musaengchae) is a quick radish side you can make with the leftover radish.

1. **Salt** a halved napa cabbage for about 2 hours, turning it once.
2. **Rinse** it three times in cold water and drain it cut-side down.
3. **Mix** the paste: gochugaru, garlic, ginger, fish sauce, a little sugar and green onion.
4. **Rub** the paste between the leaves, fold each half and pack it tightly into a jar.
5. **Ferment** a day at room temperature, then refrigerate.

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Use coarse sea salt, not table salt
- Wear gloves when rubbing the filling
- Taste the filling before it goes on the cabbage
- Leave headspace in the jar for fermentation gas

</Do>
<Dont>

- Rinse the cabbage only once; it will be too salty
- Use iodized salt, which can turn kimchi bitter
- Pack the jar to the brim
- Skip the bossam if you've made the effort

</Dont>
</DoDont>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When is kimjang season in Korea?**
Usually late November to mid-December. Families pick a weekend when the cabbage is good and the weather has turned cold.

**Can tourists join a kimjang?**
Some cooking schools and cultural centers run kimchi-making classes year-round, and local festivals sometimes open kimjang events to visitors. Check current schedules.

**How long does kimjang kimchi last?**
Stored cold, it lasts through winter and into spring, getting more sour over time. Sour kimchi is ideal for kimchi jjigae.

**Why is kimjang on the UNESCO list?**
UNESCO recognized the practice of making and sharing kimchi together, not the food itself, for its role in community life.
