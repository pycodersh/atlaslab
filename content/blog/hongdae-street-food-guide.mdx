Hongdae's street food is best in the evening, when stalls line the streets around Hongik University Station and buskers draw crowds. Start with fish cake and tteokbokki, add one sweet snack, and carry a card plus a little cash.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **Getting there:** Hongik University Station (Line 2, the Airport Railroad and the Gyeongui-Jungang Line); exit 9 leads to the main streets
- **When:** 6 pm to late, busiest on Friday and Saturday nights
- **How long:** 1 to 2 hours
- **Cost:** classic snacks cost a few thousand won each; no booking
- **Avoid:** walking off with a skewer stick; put it back in the cup at the stall

</KeyTakeaways>

## What to eat in Hongdae

| Food | What it is | Typical price |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **어묵** fish cake skewer | fish cake on a stick in hot broth | 1,000–2,000 won each |
| **떡볶이** tteokbokki | rice cakes in sweet-spicy sauce | 4,000–6,000 won a portion |
| **핫도그** Korean corn dog | sausage or mozzarella in batter, rolled in sugar | 3,000–5,000 won |
| **호떡** hotteok | pancake filled with brown sugar and nuts | 2,000–3,000 won |
| **계란빵** egg bread | sweet bread with a whole egg on top | 2,000–3,000 won |
| **닭꼬치** chicken skewer | grilled chicken, sweet or spicy glaze | 3,000–5,000 won |

<Callout type="tip" title="The free broth">
At fish cake stalls, the hot broth is free with your skewer. Use the small paper cup and ladle at the stall; locals sip it between bites, especially in winter.
</Callout>

## How to order and pay

Check the price board, point, say how many (하나, 두 개), and pay when you order or when you finish, depending on the stall. At fish cake stalls you just pick a skewer and start eating, then say how many you had.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 떡볶이 1인분 주세요. | tteokbokki irinbun juseyo | One portion of tteokbokki, please. |
| 어묵 두 개 먹었어요. | eomuk du gae meogeosseoyo | I had two fish cakes. |
| 안 매운 거 있어요? | an maeun geo isseoyo? | Do you have a non-spicy one? |

You'll hear **어묵도 드릴까요?** ("fish cake too?") and the total, like **칠천 원이요** ("7,000 won"). Tteokbokki can be hotter than it looks: the [tteokbokki guide](/blog/en/k-pantry/what-is-tteokbokki-how-spicy) explains the heat, and the [street food phrases guide](/blog/en/k-patto/ordering-korean-street-food) has more stall Korean.

## Street food etiquette

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Put used skewer sticks back in the cup at the stall
- Step aside after ordering so others can reach the stall
- Carry your trash until you find a bin, or hand it back to the vendor
- Keep 10,000 KRW in cash for the occasional cash-only cart

</Do>
<Dont>

- Double-dip a bitten skewer in a shared sauce pot
- Wander far away eating from the stall's dishes
- Photograph vendors up close without asking
- Expect bins on every corner; Seoul has few public trash cans

</Dont>
</DoDont>

Public bins are rare; the [trash can guide](/blog/en/k-patto/where-are-trash-cans-in-seoul) explains why. There's no tipping at stalls.

**A simple route:** from exit 9, fish cake and tteokbokki first, a corn dog while you watch the buskers, then hotteok or egg bread before a cafe or bar.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Hongdae street food available during the day?**
Some stalls open in the afternoon, but most set up in the early evening and stay open late. Weekend nights have the most stalls and the liveliest atmosphere.

**Can I pay by card at street stalls?**
Most Hongdae stalls take cards or mobile payment. A few small carts prefer cash, so keep a 10,000 KRW note just in case.

**Is street food in Hongdae safe to eat?**
Yes. Stalls cook food fresh and turn it over quickly because of the crowds. Pick busy stalls with a steady stream of locals.

**Are there vegetarian options?**
Hotteok, egg bread and plain corn dogs made with cheese instead of sausage are common vegetarian picks. Tteokbokki sauce and fish cake broth usually contain fish stock, so ask if that matters to you.
