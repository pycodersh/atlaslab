Hongdae's street food is at its best in the evening, when stalls line the streets around Hongik University Station and buskers draw crowds. Start with fish cake skewers and tteokbokki, then add one sweet snack, and carry a card plus a little cash.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Where:** the streets between Hongik University Station (Line 2, exit 9) and the main busking street
- **When:** 6 pm to late, busiest on Friday and Saturday nights
- **Budget:** around 10,000–15,000 KRW gets you three or four snacks
- **Avoid:** walking off with a skewer stick; put it back in the cup at the stall

</KeyTakeaways>

## What to eat in Hongdae

Hongdae mixes old-school snacks with whatever is trending on social media. The classics below are always around, and they're the best value.

| Food | Korean | What it is | Typical price |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fish cake skewer** | 어묵 (eomuk) | Fish cake on a stick in hot broth | 1,000–2,000 KRW each |
| **Tteokbokki** | 떡볶이 | Rice cakes in sweet-spicy sauce | 4,000–6,000 KRW per portion |
| **Korean corn dog** | 핫도그 | Battered sausage or mozzarella, rolled in sugar | 3,000–5,000 KRW |
| **Hotteok** | 호떡 | Pan-fried pancake filled with brown sugar and nuts | 2,000–3,000 KRW |
| **Egg bread** | 계란빵 (gyeranppang) | Sweet bread with a whole egg baked on top | 2,000–3,000 KRW |
| **Chicken skewer** | 닭꼬치 (dakkochi) | Grilled chicken glazed with sweet or spicy sauce | 3,000–5,000 KRW |

<Callout type="tip" title="The free broth">
At fish cake stalls, the hot broth is free with your skewer. Use the small paper cup and ladle at the stall; locals sip it between bites, especially in winter.
</Callout>

## How to order and pay

Street stalls work fast and with few words. Point, say how many, and pay when you finish or when you order, depending on the stall.

| Step | What to do | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Look** | Check the price board on the stall | Prices are usually per piece or per portion |
| **2. Order** | Point and say the number, such as 하나 (one) or 두 개 (two) | For fish cake, just pick a skewer and start eating |
| **3. Eat** | Stand by the stall or step to the side | Some stalls have a small counter or stools |
| **4. Pay** | Tell them how many skewers you had, or pay up front | Cards are common; a few carts prefer cash |

## How spicy is it?

Tteokbokki and spicy chicken skewers can be hotter than they look, but most stalls offer a milder option. Ask 안 매운 거 있어요? (an maeun geo isseoyo?, "do you have a non-spicy one?"). Our guide to [what tteokbokki is and how spicy it gets](/blog/en/k-pantry/what-is-tteokbokki-how-spicy) explains the heat levels.

Sweet snacks like hotteok and egg bread are a good way to cool down. A cup of fish cake broth helps too.

## Street food etiquette

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Put used skewer sticks back in the cup at the stall
- Step aside after ordering so others can reach the stall
- Carry your trash until you find a bin, or hand it back to the vendor
- Keep 10,000 KRW in cash for the occasional cash-only cart

</Do>
<Dont>

- Double-dip a bitten skewer in a shared sauce pot
- Wander far away eating from the stall's dishes
- Photograph vendors up close without asking
- Expect bins on every corner; Seoul has few public trash cans

</Dont>
</DoDont>

Public bins are rare, and our guide on [where to find trash cans in Seoul](/blog/en/k-patto/where-are-trash-cans-in-seoul) explains why. There's no tipping at stalls either; see [how paying works in Korea](/blog/en/k-patto/zero-tipping-split-bills-korea).

## A street food route

1. **Start at exit 9** of Hongik University Station and walk toward the main street.
2. **Fish cake and tteokbokki first** at one of the stalls along the way.
3. **Watch the buskers** on the main busking street while you eat a corn dog.
4. **Finish with something sweet**, such as hotteok or egg bread, before heading to a cafe or bar.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Hongdae street food available during the day?**
Some stalls open in the afternoon, but most set up in the early evening and stay open late. Weekend nights have the most stalls and the liveliest atmosphere.

**Can I pay by card at street stalls?**
Most Hongdae stalls take cards or mobile payment. A few small carts prefer cash, so keep a 10,000 KRW note just in case.

**Is street food in Hongdae safe to eat?**
Yes. Stalls cook food fresh and turn it over quickly because of the crowds. Pick busy stalls with a steady stream of locals.

**Are there vegetarian options?**
Hotteok, egg bread and plain corn dogs made with cheese instead of sausage are common vegetarian picks. Tteokbokki sauce and fish cake broth usually contain fish stock, so ask if that matters to you.
