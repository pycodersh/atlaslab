In Korean cafes it's common to see laptops, phones and bags left on tables while their owners order or use the restroom. Theft is rare thanks to social norms and CCTV, but use common sense with valuables, and follow the cafe's rules on seating and time limits.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Leaving items:** common for short breaks; theft is rare but not impossible
- **Saving seats:** placing a bag or item on a table claims it
- **Working:** laptop work is welcome in most cafes; buy a drink and refill after a few hours
- **Avoid:** leaving passports or cash unattended, or camping at a tiny cafe during the lunch rush

</KeyTakeaways>

## Why it feels safe

| Reason | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **CCTV** | Cameras are common in cafes and on streets |
| **Social norms** | Taking someone's belongings is strongly frowned upon |
| **Busy spaces** | Lots of people around act as informal witnesses |
| **Lost-and-found culture** | Lost items are often handed to staff or police |

Even so, short absences are the norm; long ones invite trouble anywhere. If something does go missing, tell staff right away: they can check the CCTV, and lost items are often handed in to the counter or a nearby police box. Small habits help too: keep your bag zipped, sit where you can see your table from the counter, and take your phone with you to the restroom.

## Seat-saving and staying

| Habit | Etiquette |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Saving a table** | Put a bag or item down before ordering; others will respect it |
| **How long to stay** | A few hours is usually fine; order again if you stay much longer |
| **Peak times** | Lunch hours fill fast; free up big tables for groups |
| **Outlets** | Ask before unplugging others; some cafes limit charging |
| **Calls** | Step outside or keep your voice low |

<Callout type="tip" title="Study cafes">
For a full day of work, try a study cafe (스터디카페): quiet desks paid by the hour, with fast Wi-Fi and outlets, built for long sessions.
</Callout>

## Where to work in Seoul

If you plan to work for a few hours, choose the space to fit the job. Big chain cafes and study cafes are the most laptop-friendly.

| Place | Good for | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Big chain cafes** | A few hours of laptop work | Outlets along walls; busy at lunch |
| **Study cafes** | All-day focus | Paid by the hour, silent, fast Wi-Fi |
| **Library cafes and bookstores** | Reading and light work | Some limit laptop use |
| **Coworking spaces** | Calls and meetings | Day passes available |
| **Small independent cafes** | Short visits | Limited seats; be considerate at busy times |

## What's okay to leave and what isn't

| Item | Leave it for a short break? |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Laptop or tablet** | Many locals do, briefly |
| **Phone** | Common, but consider taking it |
| **Jacket or book** | Fine for saving a seat |
| **Wallet, passport, cash** | No; keep them with you |
| **Camera gear** | Better to take it |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is it safe to leave a laptop in a Korean cafe?**
Theft is uncommon, and many locals leave laptops briefly. It's still smart to take valuables like passports, wallets and cameras with you.

**Can I work on my laptop in Korean cafes?**
Yes. Laptop work is common. Order a drink, and order again if you stay for a long time.

**What is a study cafe?**
A quiet workspace paid by the hour, with desks, Wi-Fi and outlets, popular with students and remote workers.

**How do I save a seat?**
Leave a bag, jacket or book on the table before ordering. It's widely understood as "taken".
