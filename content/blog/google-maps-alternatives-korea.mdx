Google Maps shows public transit in Korea but offers little or no walking and driving navigation, because Korea restricts exporting detailed map data. Install Naver Map or KakaoMap before you arrive: both work in English and give accurate walking, bus and subway routes.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Install before landing:** Naver Map (English mode) or KakaoMap
- **Why Google struggles:** map-data rules limit walking and driving directions
- **Search tip:** paste the Korean name or address for the most reliable results
- **Avoid:** relying on Google walking times; they're often missing or wrong

</KeyTakeaways>

## The three apps compared

| | Naver Map | KakaoMap | Google Maps |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **English interface** | Yes | Partial | Yes |
| **Walking directions** | Yes | Yes | Limited or none |
| **Subway and bus routes** | Detailed, with exits and car numbers | Detailed | Basic |
| **Real-time bus arrivals** | Yes | Yes | Limited |
| **Restaurant reviews** | Huge Korean review base | Good | Fewer local reviews |
| **Best for** | All-round navigation | Pairing with Kakao T taxis | Saving places from home |

## Why Google Maps doesn't work well

Korea limits exporting high-precision map data overseas for security reasons. Google can show places and transit routes, but turn-by-turn walking and driving directions are largely unavailable. Korean apps use local data and are far more accurate.

<Callout type="tip" title="Search in Korean for best results">
English searches work for big landmarks, but small restaurants and cafes are often listed only in Korean. Copy the Korean name or address from a website or review and paste it into the app.
</Callout>

## Features worth knowing

Both Korean apps do much more than directions. A few features make daily travel noticeably easier, especially in big stations and busy restaurant streets.

| Feature | Where | Why it helps |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Exit numbers** | Subway route details | Tells you which exit is closest to your destination |
| **Fast-transfer car** | Naver Map transit details | Shows which train car stops nearest the transfer stairs |
| **Real-time bus arrival** | Bus stop pages | Shows how many minutes and stops away the bus is |
| **Opening hours and break times** | Place pages | Avoids arriving during a restaurant's afternoon break |
| **Waiting lists and bookings** | Some restaurant pages | Lets you join a queue or reserve (may need a Korean number) |
| **Street view** | Both apps | Lets you check what the entrance looks like before you go |

## Following a route

| Step | What to do | Tip |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Set English** | Switch the language in the app settings | Naver Map has a full English mode |
| **2. Search** | Type or paste the place name | Choose the result with the right district |
| **3. Choose transport** | Pick subway, bus, walking or a mix | Fastest and fewest transfers are both offered |
| **4. Follow details** | Note the exit number and transfer stations | Naver shows the best car for quick transfers |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does Google Maps work at all in Korea?**
It shows places and public transit routes, but walking and driving navigation are limited. Use it as a backup, not your main map.

**Is Naver Map available in English?**
Yes. Switch the language in settings; most menus and many place names appear in English.

**Can I use KakaoMap without a Korean number?**
Yes, you can browse maps and routes without logging in. Some features like saving places need an account.

**Which app is better for buses?**
Both show real-time bus arrivals and stop locations. Many visitors find Naver Map's English mode easier to follow.
