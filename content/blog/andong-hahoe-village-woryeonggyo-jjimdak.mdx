Andong is the heartland of Korea's Confucian tradition, and the best place in the country to see a clan village that people still live in. Spend the day at Hahoe Folk Village, come back to town for Andong's famous braised chicken, and finish on Woryeonggyo, the long wooden footbridge that glows over the river at night.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **The route:** Hahoe Folk Village → Andong Jjimdak Alley → Woryeonggyo Bridge
- **Getting there:** KTX-Eum from Seoul to Andong Station
- **Getting around:** bus or taxi to Hahoe, which is about 40 minutes outside town; taxi to the bridge
- **Cost:** Hahoe charges a small entry fee; the bridge is free
- **Avoid:** going to Hahoe late in the day, when you'll have to rush

</KeyTakeaways>

## Hahoe Folk Village

<figure className="post-figure">
<img src="https://eecvvgkihtcgfikaimao.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/blog-covers/andong-hahoe-village-woryeonggyo-jjimdak-hahoe-aerial.webp" alt="Tiled and thatched roofs of Hahoe Folk Village among trees, with the Nakdong River behind" loading="lazy" />
<figcaption>Hahoe Folk Village from above, with the Nakdong River behind</figcaption>
</figure>

Hahoe is a village of the Ryu clan, set inside a loop of the Nakdong River (hahoe means "river turning round"). Tiled-roof homes of the yangban gentry stand among thatched farmhouses, and many are still lived in. In 2010 Hahoe and Yangdong Village in Gyeongju were listed together by UNESCO as the Historic Villages of Korea.

<img src="https://eecvvgkihtcgfikaimao.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/blog-covers/andong-hahoe-village-woryeonggyo-jjimdak-hahoe-2.webp" alt="A large traditional wooden house with a tiled roof inside Hahoe Folk Village" loading="lazy" />
<figcaption>One of Hahoe's gentry houses. From the ticket office, a shuttle bus takes you to the village entrance. Walk the lanes, then cross to the river sands for a view up to Buyongdae, the cliff opposite. The village is also home to the Hahoe mask dance, performed on a regular schedule.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fee** | Small entry fee; check the current price on the official site |
| **Hours** | Open daily, from morning until early evening, shorter in winter |
| **Tip** | Check the mask dance schedule if you want to see a performance |

Residents live in many of these houses. Stay out of courtyards marked private and keep your voice down.

Andong jjimdak is chicken braised with vegetables and glass noodles in a dark, sweet-savoury soy sauce, with a little chilli heat. It's said to have started in the city's old market (gu-sijang), where a whole alley of restaurants now serves it. Portions are large and sized for two or more, so come hungry or with company.

## Woryeonggyo Bridge

<figure className="post-figure">
<img src="https://eecvvgkihtcgfikaimao.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/blog-covers/andong-hahoe-village-woryeonggyo-jjimdak-woryeonggyo.webp" alt="Woryeonggyo, a long wooden footbridge with a pavilion in the middle, over the Nakdong River" loading="lazy" />
<figcaption>Woryeonggyo over the Nakdong River. Photo: Bernard Gagnon (<a href="http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/deed.en" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">CC0</a>), via <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Weolyeonggyo_Bridge_01.jpg" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Wikimedia Commons</a></figcaption>
</figure>

Woryeonggyo, opened in 2003, is a wooden footbridge 387 metres long across the Nakdong River below Andong Dam, often called the longest of its kind in Korea. A pavilion stands in the middle. After dark the bridge is lit up and reflected in the water, making it a popular evening walk. It's free.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How far is Hahoe Folk Village from Andong?**
About 40 minutes by bus or 30 minutes by taxi from downtown, depending on traffic.

**Is Hahoe a UNESCO World Heritage Site?**
Yes. It was listed in 2010 with Yangdong Village as the Historic Villages of Korea.

**Is Andong jjimdak spicy?**
Mildly. It's soy-based and sweet-savoury, with a little chilli.

**How long is Woryeonggyo Bridge?**
387 metres, often called Korea's longest wooden footbridge.
