Seoul's two great Joseon palaces sit about a kilometer apart, with the hanok lanes of Bukchon in between. Start at Gyeongbokgung for the guard ceremony, climb through Bukchon while the Red Zone is open to visitors, take an afternoon tour of Changdeokgung's Secret Garden, and end in the cafe alleys of Ikseon-dong.

<KeyTakeaways title="At a glance">

- **The walk:** Gyeongbokgung → Bukchon Hanok Village → Changdeokgung → Ikseon-dong, about 3 km on foot
- **Cost:** Gyeongbokgung 3,000 KRW; Changdeokgung 3,000 KRW plus 5,000 KRW for the Secret Garden; palace grounds are free if you wear hanbok
- **Closed days:** Gyeongbokgung on Tuesdays, Changdeokgung on Mondays, so do this walk Wednesday to Sunday
- **Bukchon rule:** sightseeing in the Red Zone is limited to 10 am to 5 pm
- **Book ahead:** Secret Garden tickets go on sale online six days before, and spring and autumn dates sell out

</KeyTakeaways>

Gyeongbokgung was the main royal palace of the Joseon dynasty. Walk through Gwanghwamun Gate to the throne hall, Geunjeongjeon, then on to Gyeonghoeru, the banquet pavilion that seems to float on its pond.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fee** | 3,000 KRW for adults (foreign visitors aged 19 to 64); free for under-19s, over-65s and anyone in hanbok |
| **Hours** | 9:00 am to 5:00 pm from November to February, to 6:00 pm from March to May and in September and October, to 6:30 pm from June to August; last entry one hour before closing |
| **Closed** | Tuesdays (open if a Tuesday is a public holiday, closed the next weekday instead) |

The **Royal Guard Changing Ceremony** takes place at 10:00 am and 2:00 pm around Heungnyemun Gate and lasts about 20 minutes. It doesn't run on Tuesdays. Arrive for the 10:00 am ceremony and you'll be inside before the midday crowds.

<Callout type="tip" title="Free entry in hanbok">
Hanbok rental shops line the streets around the palace. To get in free, wear a full outfit: a top (jeogori) with a skirt or trousers. A jeogori over jeans, or a hanbok skirt with a T-shirt, doesn't count.
</Callout>

Bukchon is a hillside neighborhood of tiled-roof hanok houses, and people still live in them. The busiest lanes around Bukchon-ro 11-gil, where most of the classic photos are taken, are now a **Red Zone** with visiting hours.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Red Zone hours** | Sightseeing allowed 10:00 am to 5:00 pm |
| **Fine** | Officers ask you to leave first; a 100,000 KRW fine applies only if you stay |
| **Not affected** | Residents, shop and cafe customers, guests staying in the area, and people simply passing through |
| **Nearest station** | Anguk Station (Line 3) |

The limits have applied with fines since March 1, 2025. Outside the Red Zone, Bukchon's other lanes stay open, but keep your voice down and don't step into courtyards or doorways. A few guesthouses in the area let you sleep in a hanok; our [hanok stay guide](/blog/en/k-patto/hanok-stay-korea-booking-etiquette) explains how to book one.

</figure>

Changdeokgung is the palace the Joseon kings actually preferred to live in, built to follow the hills instead of a strict grid, and it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Behind it lies **Huwon**, the Secret Garden: ponds, pavilions and old trees that you can only see on a timed tour.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Palace fee** | 3,000 KRW for adults |
| **Secret Garden** | 5,000 KRW for adults, plus the palace ticket |
| **English garden tours** | 10:30 am, 11:30 am and 2:30 pm from December to February; a 3:30 pm tour is added from March to November |
| **Closed** | Mondays (open if a Monday is a public holiday, closed the next weekday instead) |

Secret Garden tickets go on sale online at 10:00 am six days before your visit, and the rest are sold at the palace from 9:00 am on the day. April, May, October and November sell out fast, so book the 2:30 pm English tour as soon as your date opens.

Finish in Ikseon-dong, a pocket of narrow hanok alleys next to Jongno 3-ga Station (Lines 1, 3 and 5) that has filled with cafes, bakeries, small bars and restaurants. It's at its best in the early evening, when the alleys light up. Weekends get very crowded, so expect a wait at the popular places.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I visit Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung on the same day?**
Yes, on any day from Wednesday to Sunday. Gyeongbokgung closes on Tuesdays and Changdeokgung on Mondays, so check the day before you plan this walk.

**Is Bukchon Hanok Village closed in the evening?**
Only the Red Zone limits sightseeing, from 5 pm to 10 am. You can still pass through or visit shops and cafes there, and the rest of Bukchon stays open.

**Do I need to book Gyeongbokgung tickets?**
No. Buy them at the gate. Only the Changdeokgung Secret Garden tour needs booking, and it often sells out in spring and autumn.

**Is there a combined palace ticket?**
Yes, an integrated palace pass covers several palaces and Jongmyo Shrine, but it doesn't include the Secret Garden tour, which needs its own ticket.
