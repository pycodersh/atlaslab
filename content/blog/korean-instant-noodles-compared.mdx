Korean instant noodles (ramyeon, 라면) range from mild and brothy to dry and fiery. Pick a classic like Shin Ramyun or Jin Ramen first, try Buldak only if you love heat, and cook them the Korean way with an egg and green onion.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Most famous:** Shin Ramyun, spicy beef-and-mushroom broth
- **Mildest classics:** Jin Ramen (mild), Ansung Tangmyun, Neoguri (mild version)
- **Very spicy:** Buldak stir-fried noodles; start with a smaller or milder variety
- **Avoid:** following Western instructions; Korean ramyeon uses less water and a hotter boil

</KeyTakeaways>

## Popular ramyeon compared

| Ramyeon | Korean | Style | Spice | Flavor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Shin Ramyun** | 신라면 | Soup | Medium-hot | Spicy beef and mushroom |
| **Jin Ramen (mild/spicy)** | 진라면 | Soup | Mild or medium | Savory, balanced |
| **Ansung Tangmyun** | 안성탕면 | Soup | Mild-medium | Soybean paste base |
| **Neoguri** | 너구리 | Soup, thick noodles | Mild or spicy | Seafood with kelp |
| **Chapagetti** | 짜파게티 | Dry, black bean | None | Sweet-savory jjajang |
| **Jjapaguri** | 짜파구리 | Chapagetti + Neoguri mix | Mild | Made famous by the film Parasite |
| **Buldak** | 불닭볶음면 | Dry stir-fried | Very hot | Sweet, fiery chicken flavor |
| **Bibimmyeon** | 비빔면 | Cold, dry | Medium | Sweet, tangy chili sauce |

Most packets come with a powdered soup base and a sachet of dried vegetables, and some include an oil or sauce packet. Spicy noodles often list a Scoville-style heat level on the package, which helps you compare before buying.

## How Koreans cook ramyeon

Korean ramyeon is cooked in a pot, not just soaked. Boil about 500–550 ml of water, add the soup base and noodles together, and cook for around 4 minutes. In the last minute, crack in an egg and add sliced green onion. Many people eat it straight from the pot lid, which cools the noodles faster.

## Ramyeon culture in Korea

Korea is one of the world's biggest eaters of instant noodles per person, and ramyeon is woven into daily life. Students eat it during late-night study sessions, office workers grab a cup at the convenience store at lunch, and families keep multi-packs in the cupboard. Saying "Would you like to come in for ramyeon?" (라면 먹고 갈래?) is even a famous flirty line from a Korean film.

New flavors launch all the time, from cheese and carbonara Buldak to limited editions. Convenience stores often stock exclusive versions, so browsing the noodle aisle is part of the fun.

- **Cup vs pouch:** cups are for convenience-store eating; pouches taste better cooked in a pot
- **Kettle pots:** small yellow aluminum pots (양은냄비) are the classic way to cook and serve ramyeon
- **Souvenir idea:** multi-packs of popular flavors are cheap, light and easy to pack

## Where to buy and eat

Every convenience store sells cup and pouch ramyeon, with hot water dispensers so you can eat on the spot. Han River parks have ramyeon cooking machines, and supermarkets sell multi-packs. Bunsik snack shops cook ramyeon to order with egg and cheese for a few thousand won.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the most popular Korean ramyeon?**
Shin Ramyun is the best-known worldwide, with a spicy beef and mushroom broth.

**Which Korean ramyeon is the least spicy?**
Jin Ramen mild, Chapagetti and mild Neoguri are good low-spice choices.

**What is Jjapaguri?**
A mix of Chapagetti and Neoguri noodles, made internationally famous by the film Parasite.

**How long should I cook Korean ramyeon?**
Around 4 minutes in boiling water, with the soup base added at the start.
