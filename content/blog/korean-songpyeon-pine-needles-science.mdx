Songpyeon (송편) are small half-moon rice cakes filled with sesame, beans or chestnut, made by families for Chuseok, the autumn harvest festival. They're steamed on a bed of pine needles, which gives them a faint forest aroma and helps keep them fresh.

<KeyTakeaways>

- **What they are:** chewy rice-flour dough shaped into half-moons with sweet or nutty fillings
- **When:** Chuseok (harvest festival) in September or October
- **Pine needles:** add aroma and have natural antibacterial properties
- **Avoid:** expecting very sweet desserts; songpyeon are gently sweet

</KeyTakeaways>

## Why pine needles?

Songpyeon gets its name from pine (송, *song*) and the rice cake itself (편, *pyeon*). Steaming the rice cakes on fresh pine needles keeps them from sticking, leaves a subtle pine scent and faint needle marks on the surface, and helps them last longer. Pine needles contain natural compounds with antibacterial properties, which mattered in the days before refrigeration, when families made big batches for the holiday.

- **Fresh needles:** washed before steaming; they're not eaten
- **Sesame oil finish:** cakes are brushed with sesame oil after steaming for shine
- **Shape:** the half-moon shape is linked to wishes for growth and good fortune

<Callout type="info" title="A family tradition">
On the eve of Chuseok, families often gather to make songpyeon together. A popular saying claims that whoever makes beautiful songpyeon will have a beautiful child or meet a good partner.
</Callout>

## Songpyeon and Chuseok

Chuseok is Korea's harvest festival, held on the 15th day of the eighth lunar month. Families travel to their hometowns, hold memorial rites for ancestors (차례), visit family graves and share food. Songpyeon made from the new rice harvest are placed on the memorial table and eaten together. Other Chuseok foods include jeon (pan-fried fritters), japchae and galbijjim.

## Making songpyeon at home

Songpyeon dough is made from rice flour mixed with hot water, kneaded until smooth. Pinch off a small ball, press a well into it with your thumb, add a little filling, then seal it into a half-moon or a rounded shape. Lay the cakes on a bed of washed pine needles in a steamer and steam for about 20–25 minutes, then brush them with sesame oil.

- **Dough:** rice flour with hot water; add mugwort or other powders for color
- **Filling:** sesame seeds ground with sugar and honey is easiest
- **Size:** keep them small, about the size of a walnut
- **No pine needles?** Use parchment paper or a cloth; you'll lose the aroma but not the texture

## Where to buy songpyeon

Rice cake shops (떡집) sell fresh songpyeon all year, but especially in the weeks before Chuseok, when lines can be long. Supermarkets and markets also sell packaged songpyeon. Fresh rice cakes harden after a day or two, so buy them fresh or freeze them and steam or microwave briefly before eating.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is songpyeon?**
A small, half-moon rice cake with sweet or nutty fillings, made for Chuseok.

**Why are songpyeon steamed on pine needles?**
The needles stop them sticking, add a pine aroma and help preserve them.

**When do Koreans eat songpyeon?**
Mainly at Chuseok, the autumn harvest festival, though rice cake shops sell them all year.

**How should I store songpyeon?**
Eat them fresh within a day, or freeze them and reheat by steaming or microwaving briefly.
