Planning a winter trip to South Korea between December and February? You don't need to travel far from Seoul to experience real snow, night skiing, and full-service resort villages. However, because Korean ski resorts sit in the mountainous Gangwon Province—and public buses require multiple transfers—choosing the right resort and booking a direct shuttle package from Seoul makes all the difference.

Below is a practical comparison of South Korea’s three best-known ski resorts for international visitors: **Vivaldi Park**, **Yongpyong Resort**, and **High1 Resort**.

## 1. Vivaldi Park: Best for Day Trips & First-Timers

Located in Hongcheon about **90 minutes east of Seoul**, Vivaldi Park is the most convenient ski resort for international travelers on a tight schedule. Because of its proximity to the capital, it is the easiest resort to visit as a single-day trip without booking an overnight hotel room.

* **Why Choose Vivaldi Park**: The beginner slopes are wide and forgiving, and many Seoul day-tour packages can add a basic lesson. Even if someone in your group doesn't ski, **Snowy Land**—a snow sledding and tubing park inside the resort—keeps kids and non-skiers entertained for hours.
* **Night Skiing**: Vivaldi Park is popular with Seoul locals for late-night skiing, with sessions that have run well past midnight in recent seasons. Hours change with the weather and the season, so check the current schedule before you go.
* **Practical Tip**: Weekends get crowded with local day-trippers. If your schedule allows, book a weekday shuttle package for shorter lift lines and smoother rental fittings.

## 2. Yongpyong Resort: Best for Olympic Slopes & Scenic Views

Situated in Pyeongchang—the host region of the **2018 Winter Olympics**—Yongpyong Resort is one of South Korea’s oldest and most iconic alpine destinations. Sitting at a higher elevation than Vivaldi Park, it receives heavier natural snowfall and features longer, steeper runs suited for skiers who already know how to turn and stop.

* **Why Choose Yongpyong**: With 28 trails ranging from gentle cruisers to the steep Rainbow runs, Yongpyong feels like a true alpine village. K-drama fans will also recognize it: scenes from *Winter Sonata* were filmed here, and the Balwangsan cable car appears in *Guardian: The Lonely and Great God (Goblin)*.
* **Must-Do for Non-Skiers**: Ride the **Balwangsan Cable Car** to the summit skywalk for panoramic views of snow-covered ridges—no ski gear required.
* **Getting There**: Take a direct winter ski shuttle from Seoul, or ride the KTX from Seoul Station to **Jinbu Station** (roughly 1.5–2 hours) and continue to the resort by shuttle or a short taxi ride.

## 3. High1 Resort: Best for Long Beginner Runs & Resort Comfort

Tucked deeper in the mountains of Jeongseon, High1 Resort takes about **3 to 3.5 hours from Seoul**, making it a better fit for a **1-night or 2-night winter stay** than a rushed day trip. Its high elevation keeps snow conditions reliable through the core of the season.

* **Why Choose High1**: At many resorts, beginners are limited to short, flat hills at the bottom of the mountain. High1 stands out for its long beginner run (the Zeus course), which starts high on the mountain and winds gently down—letting beginners enjoy real summit views at a comfortable pace.
* **Resort Amenities**: High1 has hotel towers, spacious condominiums, and a wide selection of Korean BBQ and international restaurants inside the complex, so you rarely need to leave once you arrive.

## Essential Booking Tips for Foreign Visitors

1. **Book All-in-One Packages**: Buying lift tickets, poles, boots, jackets, and pants separately at the counter usually costs more than a bundle. Packages typically include clothing rental (jacket + pants), but bring or buy your own **waterproof gloves, neck warmer, and ski socks** for hygiene reasons.
2. **Check Seoul Pickup Zones**: Most ski shuttles leave early (around 6:30–7:30 AM). Common pickup areas include **Hongik Univ. Station**, **Myeongdong**, and **Dongdaemun History & Culture Park**—the exact exit depends on your package, so check the voucher. Staying near one of these hubs makes early departures much easier; see our [Where to stay in Myeongdong: L'Escape Hotel review](/blog/en/k-patto/lescape-hotel-seoul-review).
3. **Traveler Korean Tip**: At the rental shop, staff will ask for your shoe size in **millimeters (mm)** rather than US/UK sizes (e.g., US Men's 9 ≈ `270mm`, US Women's 7 ≈ `240mm`). Practice asking for sizes and ordering food with [**K-PATTO**](/kpatto) before your trip.
