From the street, Park Hyatt Seoul barely announces itself: a slim, 24-storey column of dark glass at the corner of Teheran-ro, directly opposite COEX. Inside, the mood drops several decibels. Japanese design studio Super Potato built the interiors from rough granite, oak and burnished metal, and the result feels less like a corporate Gangnam hotel than a private residence suspended above the traffic.

The hotel is small — 185 rooms — and the lobby sits on the top floor, so arrival is a quiet elevator ride straight up to the skyline. It is the stay for travelers who care about design, a serious bathroom and a short walk to almost everything in Samseong-dong.

## Location & Getting There

<StayMap id="park-hyatt-seoul" />

Park Hyatt stands right beside **Exit 1 of Samseong Station (Line 2)** — you are on the platform within a couple of minutes of leaving your room. COEX, Starfield Library and Hyundai Department Store sit just across Yeongdong-daero.

* **COEX Mall & Aquarium**: The mall entrance is across the street; the aquarium at the far end of the complex is about 15 minutes on foot (1.2 km).
* **Bongeunsa Temple**: A 1,200-year-old Buddhist temple facing COEX across Bongeunsa-ro, perfect for an early-morning walk.
* **Lotte World Tower & Jamsil**: About 15 minutes by taxi, or two stops east on Line 2 then a short transfer.
* **From Incheon Airport**: Airport limousine buses serve the COEX / Samseong area (about 70–90 minutes); a private transfer drops you at the door.

## Rooms, Dining & Facilities

### The Rooms

Every room has floor-to-ceiling windows, timber floors and a muted palette of stone and wood. The bathrooms are the headline: **granite-clad spa bathrooms** with a deep tub and walk-in rain shower, often placed so you look out over Gangnam while you soak.

* **Park Rooms**: The entry category, with the same materials and bathroom concept as the suites. Ask for a higher floor facing COEX or the Han River direction.
* **Park Suites**: Add a separate living area and dining table, useful for longer business stays.
* **Best For**: Design lovers, couples and solo travelers who value quiet.

{/* IMAGE PLACEHOLDER: Park Hyatt Seoul granite bathroom with a soaking tub beside a full-height window */}

### Dining & Drinks

* **The Timber House**: One of Gangnam's best-known hotel bars, a warren of wood-panelled rooms with live jazz, a strong whisky list and Korean small plates.
* **Cornerstone**: The hotel's Italian restaurant, built around an open kitchen and a wood-fired oven.
* **The Lounge**: On the top floor beside the lobby, serving breakfast, afternoon tea and all-day plates with a skyline view.

### Wellness

The fitness centre and indoor pool sit high in the tower with city views, and the hotel's spa offers Korean and Western treatments. The pool is calm and lap-friendly rather than a resort-style splash zone.

## The Verdict: Honest Pros & Cons

### What We Loved
* **The Subway at Your Door**: Samseong Station Exit 1 is beside the entrance, and COEX is across the street.
* **Bathrooms Worth the Price**: Granite, oak and full-height glass make them some of the best in Seoul.
* **Small and Quiet**: With 185 rooms, the hotel never feels like a convention machine, despite its location.

### What to Consider
* **Older Building**: The tower dates from 2005; the 2022 refresh helped, but some details feel less new than Gangnam's latest openings.
* **City Noise**: A few guests report hearing traffic and construction from lower and middle floors; request a high floor.
* **Not for Pool Days**: There is a pool, but no resort-style leisure deck for families.
