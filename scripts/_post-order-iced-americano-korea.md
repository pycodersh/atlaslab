Korea runs on iced americano. It outsells everything else by a wide margin, it's ordered year-round including in January, and there's a nickname for it — 아아 (*a-a*), short for 아이스 아메리카노.

Ordering one is simple. What catches people out is what the barista asks back.

## The order itself

**아이스 아메리카노 하나 주세요** — *a-i-seu a-me-ri-ka-no ha-na ju-se-yo* — "One iced americano, please"

That's the whole thing. Most drink names are borrowed from English and pronounced the Korean way, so you barely need vocabulary:

| Korean | Romanization | Drink |
|---|---|---|
| 아메리카노 | a-me-ri-ka-no | Americano |
| 라떼 | ra-tte | Latte |
| 카푸치노 | ka-pu-chi-no | Cappuccino |
| 바닐라 라떼 | ba-nil-la ra-tte | Vanilla latte |
| 녹차 라떼 | nok-cha ra-tte | Green tea latte |

Say the drink, add **하나 주세요** (one, please), and you're done.

## The two questions you'll get

**아이스로 드릴까요, 따뜻하게 드릴까요?** — iced or hot?

Answer with one word:

- **아이스요** — *a-i-seu-yo* — iced
- **따뜻하게요** — *tta-tteu-ta-ge-yo* — hot

If you already said 아이스 in your order, they'll skip this.

**사이즈는요?** — *sa-i-jeu-neun-yo?* — what size?

You don't need to know the size names. Two words cover it:

- **큰 사이즈로 주세요** — *keun sa-i-jeu-ro ju-se-yo* — large
- **작은 사이즈로 주세요** — *ja-geun sa-i-jeu-ro ju-se-yo* — small

Most Korean cafes have two or three sizes, often labeled in English on the menu board anyway.

## Sweetness is not assumed

An americano in Korea has no sugar and no syrup unless you ask. If you want it sweet:

**시럽 넣어 주세요** — *si-reop neo-eo ju-se-yo* — "Please add syrup"

Many cafes also have a self-serve station with syrup pumps, sugar, and stirrers near the pickup counter.

## Paying comes first

Unlike restaurants, cafes take payment **before** you get your drink. You order at the counter, pay right there, then wait.

**카드요** — *ka-deu-yo* — "By card" — is all you need to say when asked.

There's no tipping, and the price on the menu includes tax.

## What happens after you order

The barista will say something like:

**나오면 불러드릴게요** — *na-o-myeon bul-leo-deu-ril-ge-yo* — "I'll call you when it's ready"

At smaller cafes they'll call your order out loud. At most chains you'll get a small buzzer instead, which vibrates when your drink is ready.

Either way you collect it yourself. Nobody brings drinks to your table.

## Why iced, even in winter

The 아아 phenomenon is real. Ordering iced coffee in freezing weather is common enough to have its own joke — **얼죽아**, short for "I'd rather freeze to death than drink it hot."

You'll see people carrying iced americanos through snow. Nobody finds it strange.

## What to actually do

1. Order at the counter: **아이스 아메리카노 하나 주세요**
2. Answer the size question: **큰 사이즈로 주세요**
3. Pay right away with **카드요**
4. Wait for your name or the buzzer
5. Pick it up yourself

If you only learn one cafe phrase in Korean, make it the first one. It works at every cafe in the country.
