Ordering coffee in Korea is easier than most people expect, because the drinks are already in English. 아메리카노, 라떼, 카푸치노 — say them the Korean way and you're understood.

The words that trip people up are the ones around the drink. These ten cover a whole cafe visit.

## At the counter

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 따뜻한 | tta-tteu-tan | hot |
| 사이즈 | sa-i-jeu | size |
| 큰 / 작은 | keun / ja-geun | large / small |
| 포장 | po-jang | to go |
| 머그컵 | meo-geu-keop | mug |

**따뜻한** is the one worth drilling. Iced is 아이스, which sounds like English, but hot is not "핫" — it's 따뜻한, and you'll be asked which one every time.

**사이즈는요?** is the second question you'll get. You don't need to know Korean size names; **큰 사이즈로 주세요** (large) or **작은 사이즈로 주세요** (small) handles it.

**포장** means takeout, and it's the answer to the question every barista asks: 드시고 가세요, 아니면 가져가세요? If you're staying, you get a **머그컵** — a real ceramic mug, because single-use cups are restricted for in-store drinks.

## At your table

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 콘센트 | kon-sen-teu | power outlet |
| 와이파이 | wa-i-pa-i | wifi |
| 화장실 | hwa-jang-sil | restroom |

**콘센트** is the least guessable word on this list. It comes from "concentric plug," and it's the only word Koreans use for a wall socket. Ask **콘센트 자리 있어요?** and you'll be pointed toward a wall.

**와이파이** is English, but the Korean pronunciation flattens it enough that it's worth hearing once. The password is usually posted somewhere — check the counter and your receipt before asking.

**화장실** literally means "makeup room." It's the standard word, not a euphemism. In many buildings the restroom is shared and locked, so it's worth also knowing: **비밀번호 눌러야 하나요?** — "Do I need a code?"

## On the way out

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 반납대 | ban-nap-dae | return station |
| 또 오세요 | tto o-se-yo | come again |

**반납대** is where you take your cup. Korean cafes expect you to clear your own table, and the return station is usually near the register with a tray rack and a bin for lids.

**또 오세요** is what you'll hear as you leave. You don't need to reply — a nod is enough — but recognizing it means you'll know someone just said something friendly rather than instructional.

## The ones you don't need

Skip the drink names. 아메리카노, 라떼, 모카, 에스프레소, 바닐라 라떼 — all borrowed, all close enough to English that pronunciation is the only adjustment.

Same with **아이스**. It sounds like what it is.

Learning the ten above gets you further than memorizing a menu.

## Putting them together

Three sentences built from this list cover a normal cafe visit:

**따뜻한 라떼 하나 주세요** — One hot latte, please
**콘센트 자리 있어요?** — Is there a seat with an outlet?
**컵은 어디에 반납할까요?** — Where do I return the cup?
