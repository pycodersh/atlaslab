You put two cans of beer on the counter. The staff scans one, then stops and says something before handing it back.

Nothing is wrong. They need to check your ID.

## The exchange

**주류는 구입할 때 신분증 확인이 필요합니다**
*ju-ryu-neun gu-ip-hal ttae sin-bun-jeung hwag-in-i pil-yo-ham-ni-da*
"We need to check ID for alcohol purchases"

**주류** (*ju-ryu*) is the formal word for alcohol. You'll see it on store signs and shelf labels.

Shorter versions you might hear:

**신분증 보여주세요** — *sin-bun-jeung bo-yeo-ju-se-yo* — "Please show me your ID"
**신분증 확인할게요** — "I'll check your ID"

If you're a visitor, this is the useful follow-up:

**신분증이나 여권 보여주세요**
*sin-bun-jeung-i-na yeo-gwon bo-yeo-ju-se-yo*
"Please show me your ID or passport"

## Words to know

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 신분증 | sin-bun-jeung | ID |
| 여권 | yeo-gwon | Passport |
| 주류 | ju-ryu | Alcohol |
| 확인 | hwag-in | Check, verification |
| 여기요 | yeo-gi-yo | Here you go |
| 미성년자 | mi-seong-nyeon-ja | Minor |

## What counts as ID

Bring your **passport**. It's the one document that works everywhere.

If you live in Korea, your **외국인등록증** (*oe-gug-in-deung-nok-jeung*, alien registration card) works too.

A photo of your passport on your phone usually won't be accepted, and a foreign driver's license often gets turned down because staff can't verify it. Carry the physical passport if you plan to buy alcohol.

## They're not being suspicious of you

Selling alcohol to a minor carries a real penalty for the store and the staff member personally, so many stores check everyone regardless of how old they look.

**미성년자에게는 판매하지 않습니다** — "We don't sell to minors"

Korea's drinking age is 19, counted by birth year rather than your exact birthday. If you're clearly an adult, you have nothing to worry about — just show the passport.

## Answering

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 아 네, 여기요 | a ne, yeo-gi-yo | Oh sure, here you go |
| 여권이요 | yeo-gwon-i-yo | It's my passport |
| 잠시만요 | jam-si-man-yo | One moment |
| 두고 왔어요 | du-go wass-eo-yo | I left it behind |

If you don't have it, they can't sell to you. There's no talking around it, so the fix is to go get it.

## Good news about the hours

Unlike many countries, Korean convenience stores sell alcohol around the clock. There's no late-night cutoff, no separate liquor store trip, and no weekend restriction.

You can't drink in the store seating area at some branches, but buying is available any time the store is open, which for most branches is always.

## What to actually do

1. Carry your passport if you might buy alcohol
2. When the staff says **신분증 확인할게요**, hand it over
3. Say **아 네, 여기요** and pass it across the counter
4. They'll scan or glance at it and hand it straight back
5. Pay as normal — the whole check takes a few seconds

One document in your pocket turns this from a dead end into a five-second pause.
