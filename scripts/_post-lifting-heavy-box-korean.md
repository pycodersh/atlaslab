A box arrives. It's heavy. Mom tries, can't budge it. Then a small pair of hands reaches in.

This is one of those everyday moments that happens in Korean exactly the way it happens at home — just with different words.

## The exchange

**와, 상자가 너무 무겁네! 엄마는 못 들겠어!**
*wa, sang-ja-ga neo-mu mu-geom-ne! eom-ma-neun mot deul-ge-sseo!*
"Wow, this box is so heavy! Mommy can't lift it!"

**~네** at the end of a sentence expresses a spontaneous reaction — something you just noticed or realized. **무겁네!** isn't "it is heavy" as a fact; it's "oh wow, it's heavy!" as a feeling. You'll hear **~네** constantly in natural Korean speech.

**못 ~겠어** is "I can't / I won't be able to." **못 들겠어** — "I can't lift it." Swap the verb and the phrase travels everywhere: **못 먹겠어** (can't eat it), **못 자겠어** (can't sleep).

**내가 들어볼게! 영차!**
*nae-ga deu-reo-bol-ge! yeong-cha!*
"Let me try! Heave-ho!"

**~어볼게** means "I'll try doing ~" — literally "I'll try and see." **들어볼게** = "I'll try lifting it." A softer, more natural way to volunteer than just **들게** ("I'll lift it").

**영차** is the Korean effort sound — what you say when you're about to heave something heavy. The equivalent of "heave-ho" or "one, two, three!" It shows up in children's songs, moving day, and every gym where someone is about to attempt a personal record.

**번쩍 들었네! 와, 우리 아기 힘세다!**
*beon-jjeok deu-reot-ne! wa, u-ri a-gi him-se-da!*
"You lifted it right up! Wow, you are so strong, sweetie!"

**번쩍** is a vivid descriptive word — it captures something happening in a flash, all at once. **번쩍 들었네** isn't just "you lifted it" — it's "you lifted it just like that, in one swoop!"

**우리 아기** — "our baby" — is how Korean parents address young children affectionately, even when they're not babies anymore. **힘세다** is "strong" as an exclamation. Combine them: the warmest possible reaction to a small person doing something impressive.

## Words to know

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ~네 | -ne | Spontaneous reaction ("oh, it's ~!") |
| 못 ~겠어 | mot ~ge-sseo | Can't do ~ |
| ~어볼게 | -eo-bol-ge | I'll try ~ |
| 영차 | yeong-cha | Heave-ho! (effort sound) |
| 번쩍 | beon-jjeok | In a flash, all at once |
| 우리 아기 | u-ri a-gi | Our baby / sweetie |
| 힘세다 | him-se-da | Strong! |

## Why Korean has so many vivid sound-words

**번쩍** belongs to a category of words linguists call mimetic words — Korean is full of them. They're not quite onomatopoeia (which imitates sounds) but rather words that mimic the feel of a motion or state.

A few you'll hear all the time:

**반짝반짝** — sparkling, twinkling (think Christmas lights or stars)
**통통** — bouncy, chubby (used affectionately for round-cheeked babies)
**폴짝** — hopping, jumping lightly
**쭉** — straight, all the way through ("쭉 가세요" = "go straight")

These words show up constantly in children's speech, songs, and cartoons. If you're learning Korean with kids or for kids, they're worth knowing early.

## Encouragement phrases for everyday moments

Korean parents have a short repertoire of go-to praise phrases:

**잘했어!** (*jal-hae-sseo*) — "Good job! / Well done!"
**최고야!** (*choe-go-ya*) — "You're the best!"
**역시!** (*yeok-si*) — "As expected! / That's my kid!"
**대단해!** (*dae-dan-hae*) — "Amazing! / Impressive!"

And the combination that works for almost anything a child does well:

**우리 아기 최고야!** — "You're the best, sweetie!"

## What to actually say

When something is heavier than expected:
**이거 너무 무겁네** — "Wow, this is really heavy"

When volunteering to try:
**내가 해볼게** — "Let me try"

When someone does something impressive in one go:
**번쩍 들었네!** — "You lifted it just like that!"

When praising a child (or anyone):
**우리 [이름] 힘세다!** — "You're so strong, [name]!"
