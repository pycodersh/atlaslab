Noryangjin Fisheries Market works on one simple idea: you buy live fish downstairs, then carry it to a restaurant upstairs that slices, cooks, and serves it. You pay twice, once to the vendor for the fish and once to the restaurant for the table. Here is how to do it without overpaying.

| Step | Where | What you pay |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. Choose your fish | Ground-floor stalls | The vendor, for the fish |
| 2. Have it sliced | The stall, or the restaurant | Ask whether slicing is included |
| 3. Sit down and eat | Restaurant upstairs | A table-setting fee, reported around 5,000 KRW per person; confirm before you sit |
| 4. Turn the bones into soup | Same restaurant | A cooking fee for the stew |

## How Noryangjin Works

The market is a large building filled with stalls selling live seafood from tanks, with restaurants on the upper floor. Entry is free. The retail floor is reportedly open around the clock, while the overnight auction is for wholesale buyers. The market closes on January 1, Lunar New Year, and Chuseok, so check before a holiday visit.

Getting there is easy: take Line 1 or Line 9 to **Noryangjin Station** and walk over the footbridge.

Prices change daily with the species, the size, and the season, and several guides say you can negotiate. The vendor you buy from will usually point you to a restaurant they work with.

## Step by Step

### Step 1: Walk the Whole Floor First

Don't buy from the first stall. Do a full lap and compare prices on the same fish, because the same species can be priced differently from one stall to the next. Some visitors report being quoted high prices, so a lap and a polite no-thank-you is your best defense.

### Step 2: Ask the Total Price Before You Agree

Fish are priced by weight or by the piece. Before anything goes into a bag, ask for the full price, then ask whether slicing and packing are included.

### Step 3: Choose What to Order

| Korean | Romanization | English |
|---|---|---|
| 광어 | gwang-eo | Olive flounder, a mild, firm, all-purpose choice |
| 우럭 | u-reok | Rockfish, chewy and clean |
| 도미 | do-mi | Sea bream |
| 방어 | bang-eo | Amberjack, a winter favorite |
| 전복 | jeon-bok | Abalone |
| 해삼 | hae-sam | Sea cucumber |
| 멍게 | meong-ge | Sea squirt |
| 산낙지 | san-nak-ji | Live octopus |
| 대게 | dae-ge | Snow crab |

If you've never eaten raw fish in Korea, start with **광어** or **우럭**. If you try **산낙지**, cut it into small pieces and chew thoroughly, because the tentacles keep moving and can stick to your throat.

### Step 4: Go Upstairs

The vendor will either bring your fish to a restaurant or send you there. At the table you'll be charged a **상차림** (*sang-cha-rim*, table-setting) fee per person, and that covers side dishes, sauces, and leaf wraps. Ask what's included before you sit, and ask about extra cooking fees too.

### Step 5: Eat It the Korean Way

You'll get a plate of sliced fish and a set of small dishes. Here's the routine:

1. **Pick up a lettuce or perilla leaf** and put a slice of fish on it.
2. **Add a little sauce.** Choose **초장** (*cho-jang*, sweet and sour chili paste) or soy sauce with wasabi.
3. **Add garlic and a sliver of green chili** if you like heat.
4. **Fold it up and eat it in one bite.**
5. Pair it with soju or beer, which is the classic combination.

When the fish is finished, ask for **매운탕** (*mae-un-tang*), a spicy fish stew made from the bones and head. It's the reason locals order a whole fish.

## Order-Ready Phrases

Show these on your phone or read the romanization aloud.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 이거 얼마예요? | i-geo eol-ma-ye-yo? | How much is this? |
| 전부 얼마예요? | jeon-bu eol-ma-ye-yo? | How much is it in total? |
| 회로 떠 주세요. | hoe-ro tteo ju-se-yo. | Please slice it as sashimi. |
| 포장해 주세요. | po-jang-hae ju-se-yo. | Please pack it to go. |
| 매운탕 끓여 주세요. | mae-un-tang kkeu-ryeo ju-se-yo. | Please make spicy fish stew. |
| 카드 돼요? | ka-deu dwae-yo? | Can I pay by card? |

## Tips

* **Go with a group.** One large fish is better shared, and the cost per person drops.
* **Don't feel pressured.** Walking away from a stall is normal.
* **Report problems.** If you believe you were overcharged, the market has a customer service center, which reportedly sits on the second floor.
* **Winter is the season for amberjack.** If you visit in the colder months, ask for **방어**.
