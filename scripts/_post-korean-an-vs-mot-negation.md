In English, "I don't eat meat" and "I can't eat meat" are different sentences, but Korean makes you choose between them in the very first syllable. The choice is 안 or 못, and picking the wrong one can turn a polite refusal into a rude one, or an excuse into a boast.

---

## The split

Both words go right before the verb, and both mean "not." The difference is why.

- **안** — you don't, by choice or habit, or simply because it isn't so.
- **못** — you can't, because of ability or circumstance.

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 안 가요. | an gayo | I'm not going. (I choose not to.) |
| 못 가요. | mot gayo | I can't go. (Something stops me.) |
| 안 먹어요. | an meogeoyo | I don't eat it. (My choice, or my habit.) |
| 못 먹어요. | mon meogeoyo | I can't eat it. (Allergy, too spicy, no appetite.) |

Notice that 못 먹어요 sounds like "mon meogeoyo." The final ㅅ in 못 turns into an n sound before ㅁ and ㄴ. You'll hear it constantly.

---

## 안: choice, habit, and plain facts

안 covers everything that isn't about ability.

- 저는 고기를 안 먹어요. — I don't eat meat.
- 커피를 안 마셔요. — I don't drink coffee.
- 안 매워요. — It isn't spicy.
- 안 예뻐요. — It isn't pretty.

The last two matter: **adjectives only take 안.** You can't say 못 매워요, because an adjective can't be an ability.

For 이다 ("to be"), the negative is not 안 이에요. Use 아니에요 instead:

- 학생이 아니에요. — I'm not a student.

---

## 못: when something stops you

못 is about being unable.

- 오늘은 못 가요. — I can't go today. (I'm busy, I'm sick, there's no time.)
- 매운 음식을 못 먹어요. — I can't eat spicy food.
- 땅콩을 못 먹어요. — I can't eat peanuts. (Allergy.)
- 수영을 못 해요. — I can't swim.

Whether it's a skill you lack or a situation that blocks you, 못 is the word.

---

## The same verb, two meanings

| 안 | 못 |
|---|---|
| 안 갔어요. I didn't go. | 못 갔어요. I couldn't go. |
| 안 봤어요. I didn't watch it. | 못 봤어요. I couldn't see it. |
| 안 왔어요. He didn't come. | 못 왔어요. He couldn't come. |
| 한국어를 안 해요. I don't speak Korean (by choice). | 한국어를 못 해요. I can't speak Korean. |

The last row is the one that matters most for beginners. If you tell someone 한국어를 안 해요, they'll hear that you choose not to speak Korean, which can sound like a refusal. 한국어를 못 해요 is what you want. See also how the [past tense](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-past-tense-explained) attaches to both.

---

## Why 못 is the polite refusal

Koreans often decline invitations with 못 rather than 안, even when they simply don't feel like going.

- 안 갈래요. — I don't want to go. (A flat no that states your own wish.)
- 오늘은 못 가요. — I can't go today. (A soft no, with no explanation required.)

Saying you *can't* sounds gentler than saying you *won't*, because it removes the idea that the decision is yours. If you want to turn down an invitation without friction, 못 is the safer word.

---

## Where it goes with 하다 verbs

Verbs ending in 하다 split in two. The negative goes **between** the noun and 하다.

- 공부 안 해요. — I don't study.
- 운동 못 해요. — I can't exercise.
- ✗ 안 공부해요 — wrong.

This is the single most common error in written Korean from learners. The reason is that 공부하다 is really 공부 (study) plus 하다 (do), and the negative slides in the middle.

The exception is when 하다 is built into the word as a single verb, such as 좋아하다 (like) or 싫어하다 (dislike). Those take 안 in front: 안 좋아해요.

---

## The longer forms: -지 않다 and -지 못하다

You'll meet these in writing, in formal speech, and in announcements.

| Short | Long |
|---|---|
| 안 먹어요 | 먹지 않아요 |
| 못 먹어요 | 먹지 못해요 |

The long forms mean the same thing and sound more formal. They also let you negate long verbs without awkwardness: 도착하지 못했어요 (I couldn't arrive).

---

## Common mistakes

**못 with an adjective.** 못 예뻐요 is not Korean. Use 안 예뻐요.

**안 where you mean 못.** 한국어 안 해요 means you choose not to speak Korean. Say 한국어 못 해요.

**안 before a 하다 verb.** 안 공부해요 is wrong. Say 공부 안 해요.

**안 이에요.** Say 아니에요.

---

## 2-minute recap

- **안** = don't (choice, habit, or fact). **못** = can't (ability or circumstance).
- Both go right before the verb: 안 가요, 못 가요.
- **Adjectives** only take 안: 안 매워요, never 못 매워요.
- With **하다 verbs**, the negative goes in the middle: 공부 안 해요, 운동 못 해요.
- 못 is the softer, more polite refusal.
- Formal forms: -지 않다 and -지 못하다.
