You walk up to the front desk. The staff smiles and asks one question.

Everything after that follows a simple pattern.

## The exchange

**어서 오세요! 체크인 도와드릴까요?**
*eo-seo o-se-yo! che-keu-in do-wa-deu-ril-kka-yo?*
"Welcome! May I help you check in?"

**어서 오세요** is the standard Korean greeting in any service setting — shops, restaurants, hotels. You'll hear it the moment you walk through any door.

**네, 체크인 부탁드립니다.**
*ne, che-keu-in bu-tak-deu-rim-ni-da.*
"Yes, I'd like to check in, please."

**부탁드립니다** is a polite way to make a request — more formal than **부탁해요**, appropriate for a hotel front desk.

**예약자분 성함 알려주시겠어요?**
*ye-yak-ja-bun seong-ham al-lyeo-ju-si-ge-sseo-yo?*
"May I have your name for the reservation?"

**성함** is the formal word for "name" — used when the other person is a customer or guest. The everyday word is 이름.

**김석진으로 예약했어요.**
*gim-seok-jin-eu-ro ye-yak-haess-eo-yo.*
"Under 'Kim Seokjin'."

The pattern is **[name]으로 예약했어요** — "I made a reservation under [name]." Swap in your own name and you're done.

**네, 확인되었습니다!**
*ne, hwak-in-doe-eoss-seum-ni-da!*
"Perfect, you're all set!"

## Words to know

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 체크인 | che-keu-in | Check-in |
| 부탁드립니다 | bu-tak-deu-rim-ni-da | Please (formal request) |
| 예약자 | ye-yak-ja | Person who made the reservation |
| 성함 | seong-ham | Name (formal) |
| ~으로 예약했어요 | -eu-ro ye-yak-haess-eo-yo | I have a reservation under ~ |
| 확인되었습니다 | hwak-in-doe-eoss-seum-ni-da | It's confirmed, you're all set |

## The one phrase that handles check-in

**[name]으로 예약했어요.**

That's it. Say your name, add **으로 예약했어요**, and the staff will find the booking. You don't need to spell it out unless asked — they'll ask if they need clarification.

If your name is hard to pronounce in Korean, show your booking confirmation on your phone. Staff at Korean hotels deal with foreign names constantly and will work it out.

## What to have ready

Korean hotels typically ask for:
- Your **passport** — have it out before they ask
- A **credit card** for incidentals (임시 보증금, *im-si bo-jeung-geum*)
- Your **booking confirmation** if you didn't book directly

**여권 보여주시겠어요?** (*yeo-gwon bo-yeo-ju-si-ge-sseo-yo?*) — "May I see your passport?" — comes right after the name check. Hand it over without waiting to be asked twice.

## Useful phrases at check-in

**몇 시에 체크아웃이에요?** — "What time is checkout?"
**짐을 맡길 수 있을까요?** — "Can I store my luggage?"
**와이파이 비밀번호가 뭐예요?** — "What's the Wi-Fi password?"
**엘리베이터가 어디에 있어요?** — "Where is the elevator?"

The last two you'll use every single stay.

## Late arrival

If you're arriving late, it's worth calling ahead:

**오늘 늦게 도착할 것 같아요** — "I think I'll arrive late today"
**체크인은 몇 시까지 가능해요?** — "Until what time can I check in?"

Most Korean hotels have 24-hour front desks, but smaller guesthouses and hanok stays may not. A quick message through the booking app handles this.

## What to actually do

1. Walk up and say **네, 체크인 부탁드립니다**
2. When asked for your name, say **[your name]으로 예약했어요**
3. Have your passport ready — they'll ask for it
4. Ask **와이파이 비밀번호가 뭐예요?** before you go to the room

One sentence to start, one sentence for your name — the rest takes care of itself.
