## Past Midnight: What Happens If You Run Late

T-Locker follows station hours, which third-party guides put at roughly 05:00 to midnight. Plan to collect your bag well before the last train, not after it.

Past the 4-hour base, every extra hour adds a charge. On Seoul's published weekday rates that is 500 KRW (small), 800 KRW (medium) or 1,000 KRW (large) per hour, so a bag left from the afternoon into the evening costs noticeably more than a short stop.

We could not find a published rule for how an overnight stay is billed. If you think you might be late, open the T-Locker app and check your running balance before you walk away, and keep the printed receipt with your compartment number. A photo of it on your phone is a good backup.

## When the Lockers Are Full: Hongdae, Myeongdong and Busy Stations

Large compartments sell out first, especially in the afternoon at busy stations. Work through these in order:

1. **Check the app.** The T-Locker app shows live availability, so look at the stations on either side before you start walking.
2. **Go to the staffed counter.** Hongik University Station has a T-Luggage counter in the Airport Railroad connecting passage, and Myeongdong Station has one inside Exits 1, 9 and 10.
3. **Try your hotel or a shop.** Hotels often hold bags even for guests who have not checked in — see [how to ask at the front desk](/blog/en/k-patto/hotel-luggage-storage-korea). App-booked shops are the other fallback.
4. **Heading to the airport?** Skip the locker and send the bag on with a delivery service instead.
