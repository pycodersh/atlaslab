Yes. Korea is generally considered one of the safer places to travel, and most visitors never need more than directions. But if something does go wrong, you want the right number ready before you land. Save these four.

| Number | Who answers | Call it for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **112** | Police | Crime, theft, assault, or any situation where you feel in danger |
| **119** | Fire and ambulance | Medical emergencies, fires, accidents |
| **1330** | Korea Travel Helpline | Non-life-threatening problems: directions, interpretation, complaints, finding the nearest tourist police |
| **1345** | Immigration Contact Center | Visa and length-of-stay questions, in multiple languages, during weekday office hours |

You dial these like any phone number, with no area code. 112 and 119 are for emergencies only. 1330 is the line to call when you need help but nobody is in danger.

## How Safe Is Korea, Really?

Violent crime against visitors is uncommon, and city streets stay busy and well lit late into the evening. Most problems tourists report are ordinary ones:

* **Crowded places.** As in any big city, keep your phone and wallet in a front pocket on packed trains and in busy markets.
* **Traffic you don't expect.** Delivery motorcycles sometimes cut across sidewalks and crosswalks. Look both ways, even on a green light.
* **Weather.** Summers are hot and humid, with a typhoon season that runs through late summer and early autumn. Winters bring ice on hills and steps.
* **Prices that aren't posted.** In regular shops and restaurants the price is on the menu. Where prices are negotiable, such as at a fish market, ask the total before you agree. Our [Noryangjin guide](/blog/en/k-pantry/noryangjin-fish-market-guide-how-to-eat-hoe) shows how.

Many people travel alone in Korea, including solo women. The usual city precautions apply: stay aware of your surroundings, don't leave drinks unattended, and let someone know your plans.

## What to Do in an Emergency

1. **Call 112 or 119.** Say which one you need first: police (**경찰**) or ambulance (**구급차**).
2. **Give your location.** The station name and exit number, the building name, or a nearby landmark works best. You can read it off a map app.
3. **Stay on the line.** If you don't speak Korean, keep holding. The police run an interpretation service, and an interpreter joins the call.
4. **Need a translator?** Call **1330**. It runs around the clock in several languages including English, and it can reportedly connect a three-way call with 119.

## Common Situations

**Lost wallet, phone, or bag.** Go to the nearest police station or neighborhood police box and ask for a report (**분실 신고**). Police run a lost-property search site called Lost112, and subway stations keep their own lost-and-found at the station office. If your passport is gone, you will also need your embassy in Seoul, so keep a photo of your passport on your phone.

**Left something in a café.** Our guide to [Korean café culture](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-cafe-laptop-safety-rules) explains the local habits around unattended belongings.

**Taxi trouble.** Use the app or a metered taxi and keep the receipt. See our [taxi guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korea-taxi-survival-guide).

**Getting sick at night.** Convenience stores sell emergency medicine around the clock, and walk-in clinics are fast. See our guide to [late-night medicine and English clinics](/blog/en/k-patto/late-night-medicine-korea-clinics-guide).

**Bad air days.** Spring and winter can bring fine dust. Check the apps and pack a mask — see our [fine dust guide](/blog/en/k-patto/korean-fine-dust-apps-mask-guide).

**A loud alert on your phone.** Korean phones sound a loud emergency alert for severe weather and other warnings, and the message is often in Korean. Take a screenshot and paste it into a translation app, then follow the instructions.

## Before You Go

* Save **112**, **119**, and **1330** in your phone.
* Buy travel insurance that covers medical care.
* Keep your passport photo and your embassy's contact details somewhere you can reach offline.
* Learn the five phrases above. If you can say **도와주세요**, most people will find a way to help.
